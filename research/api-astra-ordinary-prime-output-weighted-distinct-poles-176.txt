## Result

Let \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{elig}}\) be the actual eligible-prime family from the selected near-critical block, and let \(p\to\infty\) through \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{elig}}\). Write
\[
Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,\qquad n=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad
G=\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_p}g(z)=O(\sqrt p),
\]
with the original
\[
g=|f|^2,\qquad w=g(1-g).
\]
The physical mark is \(v\neq0\), with
\[
\rho=\chi(v),\qquad \sigma=-\rho,
\]
and all terms below retain the original square reconstruction of \(x,y\), the \(b\in Q\) condition, the exclusions, and all four original \(g\)-factors.

I obtain a target-scale bound for the distinct-\(b\) block localized to the pinned output sector \(B_-\), but not for the full output-weighted distinct-\(b\) block.

### 1. Exact distinct-\(b\) block

For a retained physical line \(\ell\) at slope \(s\), write its affine parameters as \((r,b)\) and its four physical arguments as \(A,C,Y,D\). Keep the full line weight
\[
\lambda(\ell)
 =g(A)g(C)g(Y)g(D)
 =[g(A)g(C)]\,[g(Y)g(D)].
\]
Thus neither input pair is discarded.

Let \(\varepsilon(\ell)\) be the indicator of the three exact favorable tests
\[
\chi(1-A)=\rho,\qquad
\chi(1-Y)=\rho,\qquad
\chi(A-Y)=\rho\,\chi(r),
\]
together with the physical square and exclusion conditions. Define
\[
P_s=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_s}\lambda(\ell)
\]
and the positive distinct-pole contribution
\[
\mathcal D_s^{\neq b}
 =
 \sum_{\substack{\ell_1,\ell_2\in\mathcal L_s\\ b_1\neq b_2}}
 \varepsilon(\ell_1)\varepsilon(\ell_2)
 \lambda(\ell_1)\lambda(\ell_2).
\]
Since this is a positive subblock of the full physical diagonal,
\[
0\le \mathcal D_s^{\neq b}\le P_s^2.
\]

The already checked two-role and three-role pins give, after the priced endpoint removal,
\[
\sup_s P_s\le 2G^2,\qquad
\sum_sP_s\le G^3.
\]
Consequently
\[
\sum_sP_s^2\le 2G^5,\qquad
\sup_sP_s^2\le4G^4.
\tag{1}
\]

### 2. Output in the pinned sector \(B_-\)

Put
\[
B_-=\{s\in Q\setminus\{1\}:\chi(1-s)=\sigma\}.
\]
The actual pin gives, uniformly on the selected block,
\[
f(s)=c+e(s)\quad(s\in B_-),
\]
with
\[
|c|=O(p^{-1/2}),\qquad
\|e\|_{\ell^2(B_-)}^2
 =O\!\left(n^{-2/3}T^{-4/9}\right).
\]
Since \(0\le w\le g\),
\[
w(s)\le 2|c|^2+2|e(s)|^2.
\]
Therefore, using (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\in B_-}w(s)\mathcal D_s^{\neq b}
&\le
2|c|^2\sum_sP_s^2
+2\|e\|_{\ell^2(B_-)}^2\sup_sP_s^2\\
&\ll
p^{-1}G^5
+n^{-2/3}T^{-4/9}G^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
With \(G=O(p^{1/2})\) and \(n\asymp p\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s\in B_-}w(s)\mathcal D_s^{\neq b}
 =
O\!\left(p^{3/2}+p^{4/3}T^{-4/9}\right)
 =O(p^{3/2})
}
\tag{2}
\]
uniformly for admissible \(T\ge1\). The previously priced endpoint terms are also \(O(p^{3/2})\).

This estimate keeps both input weights on both lines until the final use of the positive pins. It does not invoke a Jacobi factor, and it does not mix in the common-\(b\) block.

## Why (2) does not close the full distinct-\(b\) question

The three exact sign tests imply an additional exact output relation. Since
\[
1-s=\frac{r(A-Y)}{(1-r)CY},
\]
and \(C,Y\in Q\), we get
\[
\chi(1-s)
 =\chi(r)\chi(A-Y)\chi(1-r)
 =\rho\,\chi(1-r).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus
\[
s\in B_-
\iff \chi(1-r)=-1,
\qquad
s\in B_+
\iff \chi(1-r)=1,
\]
where
\[
B_+=\{s:\chi(1-s)=\rho\}.
\]

The sign tests therefore do not force the output into \(B_-\). They leave the genuine surviving block
\[
\mathcal D_{+,s}^{\neq b}
=
\mathcal D_s^{\neq b}\,
1_{B_+}(s),
\]
equivalently the part with \(\chi(1-r)=1\). On \(B_+\), the wrong-sector pin is unavailable. The elementary positive estimate gives only
\[
\sum_{s\in B_+}w(s)\mathcal D_s^{\neq b}
\le
\sum_sP_s^2
=O(G^5)
=O(p^{5/2}).
\]
The current output-first incidence estimate inherited from the full diagonal improves this to at most
\[
O\!\left(p^{37/16}(1+\log p)\right),
\]
but still not to \(O(p^{3/2})\).

Hence the checked conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The pinned }B_-\text{ portion of the actual distinct-}b\text{ block is }O(p^{3/2}).
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{The full output-weighted distinct-}b\text{ block remains open; the surviving }B_+\text{ block is the precise gap.}
}
\]

This is not an asymptotic refutation. The obstruction is only that the available pin is localized to \(B_-\), while (3) shows that the exact sign conditions permit a separate \(B_+\) sector.

## What would decide the target

It is sufficient to prove the coupled estimate
\[
\sum_{s\in B_+}w(s)\mathcal D_s^{\neq b}
=O(p^{3/2}).
\tag{4}
\]
Together with (2), this would settle the full favorable distinct-\(b\) contribution at the required scale.

### Ranked next tests

1. **Highest priority: two-pole incidence/energy estimate.**  
   Parameterize two retained lines at the same output \(s\) by
   \[
   s=b+\frac rE=b'+\frac{r'}{E'},\qquad b\neq b',
   \]
   retain the three sign indicators and the full product
   \[
   [g(A)g(C)] [g(Y)g(D)]
   [g(A')g(C')] [g(Y')g(D')],
   \]
   and seek an output-set estimate for the positive kernel on \(B_+\). A bound of the form (4), or any uniform power saving sufficient to offset the \(B_+\) output mass, would close the distinct-pole target without touching the common-pole terms.

2. **Character test for the surviving \(r\)-sector.**  
   Use (3) to insert the exact factor
   \[
   1_{B_+}(s)=\frac12\bigl(1+\rho\,\chi(1-r)\bigr)
   \]
   into the two-pole expansion, while retaining \(w(s)\) and all original amplitudes. Test whether the nontrivial \(\chi(1-r)\) component supplies square-root cancellation in the distinct-\(b\) sum. The trivial component must still be controlled by a genuine coupled two-pole estimate; an individual Jacobi bound or a common-\(b\) subnorm multiplied by a Jacobi factor would not address the surviving positive block.
