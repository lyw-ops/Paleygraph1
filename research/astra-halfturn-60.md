# Prime-index restrictions on transport within the true optimal faces

Write $G=\widehat Q$, $n=|G|=(p-1)/2$, $D_s=\{a\in Q:\chi(1-a)=-s\}$, and $q_s=\theta_s/n$ for the root probability on the sign-$s$ optimal face. Keep the cold scores $s\sqrt n v_t$, their [common-row quantile objective](astra-tail-53.md) $M_t$, and the energy charge $E_t/4$. The results below apply at every time in the actual growing window $D(p)/2\le t\le D(p)$. They constrain the available rows independently of time. A zero change in the rows gives exactly the reference pair's charged value.

## Fourier restriction for every prime-index quotient

Let $H\le G$ have prime index $\ell$. For a change $h$ between two true sign-$s$ rows, suppose each $H$-coset retains its mass and every changed coset is disjoint from the support of a chosen opposite optimum $\nu$, including its root. Site-dependent routing within those cosets is arbitrary. The hypothesis holds simultaneously for **each** opposite optimum for the [common-displacement distributions generating $H$](astra-flow-59.md), with arbitrary donor selection and no root donor. For half-turn moves, it holds for each opposite optimum by ordinary exposure and pair conservation alone.

Fix a nontrivial character $k$ of $H$. If the $k$-Fourier component of $h$ on even one coset is nonzero, the $k$-Fourier component of $\nu$ on **every** coset vanishes. Indeed extend $k$ to a character $a\in Q$; with $C$ running over $G/H$, form
$$
 h_a(C)=\sum_{i\in C}h_i i(a),\qquad N_a(C)=\sum_{i\in C}\nu_i i(a).
$$
Their supports in the prime quotient are disjoint. Their length-$\ell$ Fourier transforms are the original spectra on $aH^\perp$. On this coset the forbidden sets for the two signs partition all $\ell$ positions since the coset omits the identity. A nonzero length-$\ell$ vector with $b$ prescribed Fourier zeros must occupy at least $b+1$ sites. Thus if both vectors were nonzero they would jointly occupy at least $\ell+2$ sites.

For completeness, the prime Fourier submatrices used here are nonsingular. Given $b$ rows and columns, evaluate the $b$ monomials $z^{u_j}$ at the corresponding distinct $\ell$-th roots. Replace evaluations by successive Newton divided differences. The latter are integral polynomials in the nodes, and at reduction modulo $1-\zeta_\ell$ the $r$-th divided difference becomes $\binom{u_j}{r}$. The resulting determinant for $0\le r<b$ is the ordinary Vandermonde on distinct $u_j\bmod\ell$, divided by $\prod_{r<b}r!$, hence nonzero modulo $\ell$. The complex evaluation determinant was nonzero before the invertible divided-difference change.

In particular, if one site in any one coset carries strictly more than half its $\nu$-mass, **all** its components are nonzero by the triangle inequality. All nontrivial components of $h$ then vanish, and the trivial one vanishes by mass conservation. This excludes any changed row even if the exposed support leaves many other whole cosets available for routing. The argument applies on every prime for every prime divisor $\ell\mid n$, and requires one such opposite optimum for the exclusion. For common-displacement plans the row can in principle be unchanged under a circulation supported by the donor law. These conclusions concern the resulting row.

## An exact dichotomy for the half-turn on $p=4\ell+1$, $\ell$ prime

Take $d=n/2$, where $n/2=\ell$ is prime, and call a sign movable if some two distinct true optimal rows have the same half-turn pair masses. If neither sign is movable, the optimum over the half-turn dictionary at every time equals the reference cost, for any chosen reference pair. Otherwise:

* Only one sign $s$ is movable. Every true opposite optimum is the same $d$-invariant probability.
* Every true sign-$s$ row has the same pair masses. Thus from **any** reference on that side the half-turn realizes the entire sign-$s$ face. The minimum charged cost in the half-turn dictionary is the actual full-face minimum at every time.
* With $e_s,f_s$ the counts of even and odd forbidden positional exponents, the real dimension of this entire face is at most
$$
 e_s-f_s=\frac{-1-s\lambda_d}{2}\le \frac{\sqrt p-1}{2},
 \qquad \lambda_d=\sum_{a\in Q}\chi(1-a)d(a).
$$
A nonzero dimension therefore requires $s\lambda_d\le-3$. The arithmetic sign test is available before identifying either optimum. These assertions include noneven rows; the scores themselves are even.

To prove the claims, apply the preceding result to the single nontrivial character of $H=\{0,d\}$. A changed pair must have both sites zero in each opposite row: at any changed site at least one of the two true rows is positive and the root pair is fixed. Hence all opposite optima satisfy $\nu_i=\nu_{i+d}$, including $\nu_d=q_{-s}$.

The folded probabilities $W_s(i)=\mu_i+\mu_{i+d}$ for $i\in G/H$ obey the even-frequency forbidden equations. These are exactly the two sector problems for the induced graph on the fourth powers $Q^2\subset Q$. Write their ordinary theta values as $\theta_s^{(4)}$, with product $\ell$. Splitting each quotient mass equally between its two lifts gives a feasible original probability with identical theta objective, so $\theta_u\ge\theta_u^{(4)}$ for both signs. For the invariant opposite optimum its fold has objective $\ell\,(2q_{-s})=\theta_{-s}$, giving equality. For arbitrary true $\mu$ of sign $s$, exposure against this optimum gives $\mu_d=0$. Its fold has root $q_s$ and is optimal, since $\ell q_s=\theta_s/2=\theta_s^{(4)}$ by the two complementary products.

On a prime-order sector problem with any complementary symmetric masks, each optimal row has support of size exactly $e_u+1$, and both rows are unique. There are $e_u$ prescribed Fourier zeros, giving the support lower bound. The opposite row has at least $e_{-u}+1$ sites, and the two supports meet only at the root, with $e_u+e_{-u}=\ell-1$. Thus each lower bound is sharp and their union is the quotient. Fixing the opposite row determines the allowed support of the first. The forbidden rows have full rank $e_u$ there by the prime Fourier argument, leaving one line fixed by normalization. This holds for arbitrary positive optima without an inversion assumption.

It follows that $W_s$ is fixed and the opposite invariant optimum is unique. Writing $\Delta_i=\mu_i-\mu_{i+d}$, the support of $\Delta$ is contained in the $e_s+1$ sites of $W_s$. Its odd-frequency equations form a prime Fourier matrix up to a diagonal twist. Its complex kernel dimension there is $e_s+1-f_s$, since $\Delta_0=q_s\ne0$ and all minors of feasible sizes are nonsingular. Fixing this nonzero root reduces the dimension by one. The real solution set has dimension at most the resulting complex dimension because real linearly independent real vectors remain complex linearly independent. Finally $e_s-f_s=\sum_{a\in D_s} d(a)$, and $\sum_{a\ne1}d(a)=-1$. Expanding the sign indicator off the identity gives the displayed formula and the Paley Jacobi bound supplies its inequality.

In particular, a necessary condition for an $s$-move is the **exact equality** $\theta_{-s}=\theta_{-s}^{(4)}$. It suffices to have feasible theta lower bounds $L_u,L_v^{(4)}$ with $L_{-s}L_s^{(4)}>\ell$ to exclude such a move. This strict product can be certified without enumerating either optimal support. The equality alone gives an invariant opposite optimizer by lifting, without proving that every opposite optimizer is invariant.

## Directed paths with arbitrary jump lengths

There is a further obstruction on every eligible prime, with no condition on $n/2$. Fix a generator $g$ of $G$. Represent each edge of an arbitrary source-conditioned bulk plan as a forward path of $1\le L<n$ single $g$-steps. Let $F_e$ be its mass and $J_x$ the total mass of paths crossing the unit edge $x\to x+g$. If the plan's divergence $h$ is a true face change, then for **every** translate $a$ of any opposite optimum,
$$
 \sum_e F_e\!\sum_{x\text{ a step origin on }e}\nu(x-a)
       =\frac1n\sum_e F_e L_e .
$$
Indeed $h_x=J_{x-g}-J_x$; the generating difference has no nonconstant kernel and is diagonal in the same Fourier basis, so $J-\bar J\mathbf1\in V_s$. Every translated opposite optimum has constant pairing with $J$, equal to its mean. At $a=0$ both endpoints of every bulk edge are zero sites of $\nu$ by true exposure for the source and target of the plan when only excess mass is sent to deficit sites. More generally it suffices to stipulate those endpoint zeros. Hence a nonempty forward plan must **jump across** a positive opposite site. If every permitted path stays inside an unexposed interval, positive movement is impossible even with different jump distributions at each donor. If every path visits at most $k$ positive opposite sites, its flow-weighted mean length is at most $nkq_{-s}=k\gamma_{-s}\sqrt n$, by the true point cap. These statements allow arbitrary numbers of unequal and non-generating individual jumps. They do not obstruct general bidirectional flow using the opposite arc or jumps crossing enough exposed mass.

## Replay and remaining charged bound

The [checker](astra-halfturn-60-check.py) gives [six records](astra-halfturn-60-checks.jsonl). At $p=13$ the nonconstant branch has real dimension one, attaining the Jacobi dimension bound. The two distinct extremal trials have the same evenization. At $53,173,317$ both cross products are strictly greater than $\ell$ by rational certificates, so neither face admits *any* half-turn change at any depth. This covers noneven plans and every true reference. For the stated certified complementary references at $53,173$, Arb384 gives, respectively, the minimum of $(M_t-E_t/4)/t$ over the seventeen integer times $16\le t\le32$ in $[.056313435,.056313436]$ and $[.047728354,.047728355]$. By rigidity these are the exact dictionary minima up to enclosure, evaluated with a single row per sign at every threshold. In this finite calculation the references have square invertible even support systems; the score laws depend only on evenization. The times are a fixed-field diagnostic. The prime-index theorem and the directed-path obstruction apply throughout genuine growing windows, without supplying any uniformly positive normalized lower bound there. No infinitude is assumed for primes $p=4\ell+1$ with $\ell$ prime.

On a rigid branch test the reference charged objective itself; on a movable branch use the unique folded pair and the remaining odd equations on its exactly $e_s+1$ sites. A charged $o(t)$ upper trial at one late time on every eligible prime is sufficient, including the composite-quotient cases which require separate analysis. A lower bound $\eta t$ above $E_t/4$ for the best dictionary value throughout the late window on **unbounded primes** rejects the specified reference/dictionary approach.

Retain permitted edges crossing opposite mass when those tests force them. To advance the limit, evaluate the one-time energy-charged objective on the surviving common rows, instead of treating the absence of motion as a positive objective lower bound.
