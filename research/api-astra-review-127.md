# Review of the sparse-adjoint arithmetic response

The [raw draft](api-astra-overflow-127.txt) preserves the two genuine stationary factors and the exact output mask in the coefficient of the backward adjoint. Its critical sparse bounds are inherited from Round 125. They give \(O(1)\) for the scaled signed pairing, while its example only shows that the direct term can reach that order for unrestricted data. The upper bound on \(Kr\) cannot show a corresponding lower on the transported term.

There is a simple generic example for the **combined** test with the actual \(K\). Take \(S\) of size \(\lfloor c\sqrt n\rfloor\), \(r=n^{-1/2}1_S\), \(v=1_S-|S|/n\), and \(d=1_S\), with \(c>0\) sufficiently small. The Jacobi entry bound gives \(|Kr|\le c\sqrt3\,n^{-1/2}\), so \(r-\epsilon Kr\ge(1-c\sqrt3)n^{-1/2}\) on \(S\). Then \(n\langle v,d(r-\epsilon Kr)\rangle_{2,n}\) is bounded below by a positive constant, with \(v\) mean zero and at the critical normalized second-norm scale. These choices impose no actual cap secant or repair slopes. Thus an unrestricted support and norm statement would be false, whereas the actual signed estimate remains open. The unused support supremum in the draft's (F) also does not prescribe the actual relation between these fields.

The signed finite-prefix estimate requires a separately controlled lower-height endpoint and the known tail. The independent [support and loss calculation](real-cap-drift-localization-127.md) replaces the transported term with an exact sample of the next genuine cap increment and localizes the direct term by cap saturation and actual signed repair drift. It also gives an actual strict cap-contraction loss test for any order-one increment at critical cap height. None of these gives the missing prime upper.
