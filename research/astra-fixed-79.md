## Two circle derivatives on the compact Mellin model

Work first in any fixed finite list of the trace objects in the calculation, including its quadratic lifts, the necessary sources, outputs and tensor words. Use arithmetic semisimplifications of weight zero. Write \(G\) for the compact geometric group and \(gG\) for its arithmetic coset, with probability Haar measure. Full-character lift averages are represented by averaging the two traces before viewing them as functions on the coset. Products are then ordinary products. The fieldwise coset moment estimate holds with error \(o(1)\) as the prime grows for each fixed word, with bounded complexity and coefficients. [Fieldwise moment argument](astra-centered-78.md#a-square-with-positive-variance-at-unbounded-primes)

For \(\alpha=1,\chi\) use a geometric weight circle \(c_\alpha(\theta)\) whose integer weights on every block are the fiber weights at \(\alpha\). Here and below the angle is in radians. To construct it, take the semisimple Frobenius of the corresponding fiber functor and diagonalize it. The map sending an eigenvalue to its weight respects every multiplicative relation among eigenvalues. Thus the diagonal entries \(e^{iw\theta}\) satisfy the relations defining its algebraic torus closure (also for relations with value a root of unity, by taking powers). They lie in the arithmetic group. The circle fixes every geometric invariant tensor because the invariant part is a sum of geometrically punctual units carrying a pure weight-zero arithmetic action, on which every weight is zero. The tensor-stabilizer description puts the circle in the geometric group. Conjugate it into the chosen maximal compact group, independently for the two fibers. This preserves all spectra, including on summands. The construction works on a joint direct sum and then projects to all its blocks. For the quadratic fiber use the tensor-compatible quadratic-twist fiber functor, identified over the algebraic closure of the coefficient field with the usual fiber functor. [Katz, chapters 3, 5 and 16, especially Theorem 16.1](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf) · [Two-fiber weight and rank formulas](astra-feedback-52.md#reading-rank-from-two-mellin-characters)

Let \(\partial_\alpha\) differentiate left multiplication by \(c_\alpha(\theta)\), and put \(\mathcal E(f)=\frac12\sum_\alpha\|\partial_\alpha f\|_2^2\) in Haar norm. Two inequalities on trace polynomials drive the proof:
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(f)\le\mathcal E(f),\qquad
 \mathcal E(\mathcal K f)\le\mathcal E(f),\quad
 \|\mathcal Kf\|_2^2=\operatorname{Var}(f)\quad(f\text{ of positive type}).          \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\mathcal K\) discards the punctual part and quadratically twists the generic positive local system by \(\chi(1-x)\). It represents the actual operator on even polynomials of negative fields in normalized second norm up to \(o(1)\) at each fixed complexity. Its output has zero mean. The variance inequality applies to trace polynomials of arbitrary parity. All constituents used in it are tame with only the interior singularity one, both boundaries have characters in \(\{1,\chi\}\), and the only possible punctual constituent is the unit at one. For mixed tensor words the finite singularity and tameness statements follow from local convolution. The boundary-character assertion follows by tensor compatibility at every other twist, where all weights vanish. Subobjects inherit these properties. [Local convolution and actual operator](iteration-parity.md) · [Boundary closure](astra-feedback-52.md)

For details of (2), an irreducible geometric block \(M\) fixed up to isomorphism by arithmetic Frobenius contributes a twisted character \(u\mapsto\mathrm{tr}(\rho_M(u)A_M)\) on \(G\), using a unitary intertwiner and a scalar coefficient accounting for the arithmetic trace on multiplicities. Orbits of distinct permuted blocks contribute zero. Schur orthogonality gives squared norm one for each such character and orthogonality for distinct geometric simples, also after left differentiation. The squared derivative norm is \(d_M^{-1}\sum_i w_{\alpha,i}^2\), where \(d_M=\dim\rho_M\). This follows by applying matrix-coefficient orthogonality to the trace with its differentiated matrix. For a nonpunctual simple of generic rank \(r_M\), the pooled sum of absolute weights is \(r_M\). Its convolution dimension \(d_M\) is the drop at one, at most \(r_M\). Since the weights are integers, the pooled sum of their squares divided by \(d_M\) is at least one. The unit gives exactly the constant part. This proves the first inequality.

On a positive nonpunctual simple, ordinary twisting preserves geometric simplicity and distinguishes inequivalent generic local systems. It is defined over the base field, preserves the scalar action on arithmetic multiplicities and sends a permuted orbit to a permuted orbit. One can choose the target arithmetic structure induced from the source, so the unitary character coefficients have the same moduli. The two-fiber pooled nonzero weights are preserved because the zero-boundary blocks stay in place and the infinity-boundary blocks exchange their two fibers. The output is negative, hence has convolution dimension \(r_M\). Replacing the denominator \(d_M\) by \(r_M\) can only decrease the derivative energy. Dropping punctual constants costs zero energy and gives the stated norm identity. Quadratic lifts commute with this ordinary twist. Thus the proof includes identifications between lift summands, by collecting each geometric simple with its arithmetic coefficient before evaluating either side.

## Limits of finite rows and the analytic circle used for backward uniqueness

Projection commutes with Hausdorff limits of compact sets. The resulting inverse limit has a compact geometric group, Haar coset, and two continuous circle actions with the limiting block matrices. The compact polynomial norms, derivative norms, and means pass to the limit by Haar convergence on each finite projection. Empirical physical moments agree in the limit at each fixed complexity. ## The centered remainder and the exact correction

Continue in one subsequential model. Write \(W=U_j\), \(D=F'_t(W)\), and \(H=(I-\mathcal K D)^{-1}R\), meaning multiplication by the odd function \(D\) followed by positive-to-negative transport in the Neumann sum. Each summand is a real negative-type limit of trace polynomials. The finite-prefix caps give \(\|D\|_\infty\le d<1\) and both \(\|\partial_\alpha D\|_\infty\) are bounded. For the latter use the chain rule and the compact real range of \(W\). Approximation of \(F_t'\) by odd polynomials with uniformly approximated first derivative on that range puts \(D\) in the uniform first-derivative closure with odd parity. For \(X_m=(\mathcal K D)^m R\), (2) and the product rule give
\[
 \|X_m\|_2\le d^m\|R\|_2,\qquad
 \mathcal E(X_{m+1})^{1/2}\le d\,\mathcal E(X_m)^{1/2}+C\|X_m\|_2,
 \quad C=\max_\alpha\|\partial_\alpha D\|_\infty .
\]
First apply these inequalities to parity-correct trace approximations and take limits. The terminal \(R=S+\mathcal K F_t(W)-W\) belongs to the first-derivative closure by (2) and even polynomial approximation of \(F_t\) with its first derivative on the bounded range of \(W\). Hence the Neumann sum converges there by summing \(d^m\mathcal E(R)^{1/2}+Cm d^{m-1}\|R\|_2\). In particular \(EH=0\), \(H\ne0\), and \(H\) has weak circle derivatives in \(L^2\). Here \(E\) is Haar expectation. The derivative estimates use the energy contraction in (2).

For a smooth real function \(s\) with bounded derivative, \(s(H)\) is in the same Sobolev closure and \(\partial_\alpha s(H)=s'(H)\partial_\alpha H\). To verify the domain statement, approximate \(H\) in the graph norm by real trace polynomials. For each approximant, polynomially approximate \(s\) and \(s'\) on its compact range by approximating the derivative and integrating. For the graph limit use boundedness and continuity of \(s'\), convergence in measure of the approximants and the fixed integrable density \(|\partial_\alpha H|^2\). Thus (2) applies also to \(s(H)\).

The real bulk remainder \(B'=F_t(W+H)-F_t(W)-DH\) is nonnegative and satisfies \(0\le B'\le2|H|\), by real convexity and the global Lipschitz constant one. If its centered norm vanished, \(B'=b\) almost everywhere. Strict convexity excludes \(b=0\) because \(H\ne0\). For \(b>0\) we have \(|H|\ge b/2\) almost everywhere. Choose a smooth saturated sign \(s\) equal to \(\pm1\) on the two ranges \(\pm x\ge b/2\), with derivative zero there. Its energy is zero. Inequality (2) forces \(s(H)\) constant, whereas the real, nonzero, mean-zero \(H\) takes both signs on sets of positive measure. This proves \(\operatorname{Var}(B')>0\) in every subsequential model. No continuity of \(H\) on the compact coset is asserted.

For precision concerning the normalized physical limit, approximate the real unmarked Neumann sum by a fixed number of terms, uniformly using the real cap and \(d<1\). For each term first approximate its previous factor in normalized second norm by a bounded finite negative trace combination. Then approximate the bounded base and its derivative accurately enough for multiplication by this bounded approximant, with even/odd polynomial parity before transport. The same approximants work on the finite vectors and on the limiting coset, by fixed-word moment matching and contraction. This realizes \((W,H)\) jointly in second norm, including uniform integrability of the physical squared corrections. The real remainder is 2-Lipschitz in the correction. For a bounded approximant to the correction, perturbing the base costs at most twice its norm difference plus the norm of the derivative difference times that bound. Thus its mean and centered second norm converge to those of \(B'\). To restore the mark, the actual and unmarked base rows differ by \(O(n^{-1/2})\) in normalized second norm at each fixed height, by iteration of the real Lipschitz map and the bounded bracket on \(Ke_0\). At the nine-center starting height take the identical finite-field starting row. The finite preimage identities carry bounded exceptional-column coefficients, already absorbed by fixed-expression approximation. Subtract the two Newton equations, using the bounded actual inverse. The multiplier derivative difference is bounded and tends to zero in normalized second norm, so its product with the unmarked correction tends to zero by bounded approximability. The mark itself contributes \(o(1)\) to the normalized remainder since the correction is boundedly approximable and the real remainder is at most twice its modulus at either floor. This gives the physical centered norm in (1) after multiplying by \(\sqrt n\). The argument by arbitrary violating sequences, including varying floors and the finitely many heights, gives a uniform positive constant.
