## Why degrees one and two require no blanket debit

In an intermediate price treat the new inputs as six separately colored residuals, leaving their boundary convolution outside the collected \(J_j\). At fixed actual list, number and unordered multiset of new inputs, the signed old multiplier is identical for all orderings. This reproduces the intermediate price simply by multiplication by the multinomial coefficient. At degree one each new standard produces its own derived type. At degree two, inputs on different factors leave their ordered factor signatures. At a single special-linear factor the three possibilities have nontrivial parts
\[
 (2\omega_1,\omega_2),\quad \omega_1+\omega_6,\quad
 (2\omega_6,\omega_5)
\]
for two standards, a standard and its dual, or two duals. At a single orthogonal factor the square has a traceless symmetric square and an exterior square in addition to the scalar. These are the usual distinct irreducibles in dimension eight. All listed nontrivial outputs identify their unique input multiset and are disjoint from degree zero and one. On the actual arithmetic group each such summand has its canonical extension from the given tensor product. Tensoring with old irreducibles preserves distinctness for that fixed summand: restrict to the new derived group and apply its multiplicity-space functor, or cancel its common character on the connected arithmetic torus. No signed old coefficient or original boundary multiplicity is lost. In particular this reasoning keeps the Jacobi coordinate on the old multiplier; it never equates two determinant shifts. No independence of the *determinants* is necessary. The two special-linear determinants are \(-m^3\), with inverse phases on duals.

After setting aside the scalar components of degree two and the entirety of degrees three and higher, all remaining terms thus have exactly their intermediate price. Reintroducing the set-aside terms costs at most twice their full intermediate price, boundary bin by boundary bin, by the triangle inequality. This pays both for their original price and for any cancellation of competitors, even if the latter include a lower-degree standard. The scalar terms in degree two have total original amplitude bounded by \(C_N=4\mathrm{cap}_U^2+2\mathrm{cap}_D^2\), with actual unit phases. In degree \(k\ge3\) use the full unsigned boundary power of the six inputs. This also pays every determinant return, from both cosets, without truncating its degree.

## Two downward substitutions and all previous charges

Let \(B\) be the retained lower boundary, and \(T_O,T_N\) the exact positive boundary measures of old and new tags. By exchange symmetry their summed marginals are the same for either lift. The true untagged boundary dominates \(R=(B-T_O-T_N)_+\), and \(R\le B\). Substituting this \(R\) at the beginning gives a lower bound for the *final* price, because the fully pooled multiplier on each residual list has nonnegative boundary. For the **intermediate** price the pseudo-residual boundary is \(R+T_N\), which dominates \((B-T_O)_+\). Thus the old-only comparison can use the latter smaller pseudo-residual boundary and its upper bound \(B\), while the final independent-factor collision debit uses the original surrogate \(R\le B\). This order avoids ever asserting \(R+T_N\le B\).

More precisely one may restrict the intermediate output to pseudo-residual counts \(j\le9\). The old-only comparison with the nine-center lower map on \(B\) then retains all of its original arities \(\le9\), applying the old-tag loss estimate at every such \(j\). At fixed actual count \(r\le9\), losing the discarded degree-two scalars and \(k\ge3\) can interfere with any of these retained terms. Charge them at **all** \(k\), even when \(r+k>9\). Retained safe terms with \(r+k>9\) may also simply be dropped: after setting aside the dangerous terms they are disjoint from every smaller multiset. Lists with \(r>9\) carry their own nonnegative final price.
