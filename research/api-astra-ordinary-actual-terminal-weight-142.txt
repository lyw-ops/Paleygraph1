## Report

### Checked reduction

Fix a prime family \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\), with
\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
and retain the physical marked coordinate \(0\). Fix \(0<\tau\) first, use the actual zero-start Picard orbit with floor \(w_0=1\), \(w_i=\tau/n\) for \(i\neq0\), and choose
\[
T=T(p)\to\infty
\]
inside the proved simultaneous marked window, including \(T+1\). Only after the prime limit is taken is \(\tau\downarrow0\).

For sign \(\sigma\), let \(F\) be a genuine nonnegative mean-one optimizer in the opposite sector, with
\[
F_0=q_*=\theta_{-\sigma},
\qquad
KF=-\sigma(F-\mathbf 1).
\]
Let
\[
g_T=\phi_T-\phi_0\ge0
\]
be the actual terminal activation, and use normalized counting measure
\[
\mathbb E h=\frac1n\sum_i h_i.
\]
The exact terminal numerator is
\[
N_T(F):=\mathbb E[1_{\{i\ne0\}}F_i g_T(i)].
\]

The accepted endpoint identity gives
\[
1-\frac{q_*a}{nM}
\ge
-\frac{N_T(F)}{2TM}
-O_{\tau,B}(T^{-B-1}),                                      \tag{A}
\]
where \(a=X_0\) is the actual physical mark and \(M=\mathbb E X\). Thus the required sufficient estimate is exactly
\[
\boxed{\frac{\sqrt n}{T}N_T(F)=o(1)}.                       \tag{B}
\]
The equivalent signed form is
\[
N_T(F)
=
\mathbb E g_T+\sigma\mathbb E(F\Delta v)
-\frac{q_*}{n}g_T(0),\qquad
\Delta v=v_{T+1}-v_1.                                      \tag{C}
\]
Inside the marked window, the first and last terms in (C) are already lower-order after multiplication by \(\sqrt n/T\). Hence the remaining target is equivalently
\[
\frac{\sqrt n}{T}\sigma\mathbb E(F\Delta v)=o(1).
\]

### A precise support-sensitive reduction

The Jacobi eigenvector bound used in the dual analysis gives, on the bulk,
\[
0\le F_i\le C_p:=1+\sqrt p.                                \tag{D}
\]
Therefore, for every threshold \(0\le\alpha\le C_p\), with
\[
A_\alpha(F)=\{i\neq0:F_i>\alpha\},
\]
positivity of \(g_T\) yields the exact level-set bound
\[
N_T(F)
\le
\alpha\,\mathbb E g_T
+
\frac{C_p}{n}\sum_{i\in A_\alpha(F)}g_T(i).                 \tag{E}
\]
Consequently, a nontrivial prime-specific route would be to prove, for a selectable \(\alpha=\alpha_p\),
\[
\alpha_p\,\frac{\sqrt n}{T}\mathbb E g_T=o(1)
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sqrt n\,C_p}{nT}
\sum_{i\in A_{\alpha_p}(F)}g_T(i)=o(1).
}                                                           \tag{F}
\]
This is a genuine reduction to a weighted terminal high-set estimate for the **actual** opposite optimizer and the **actual** physical terminal source. It does not replace \(F\) by an abstract \(l^2\) vector or move the mark away from coordinate \(0\).

The existing opposite-autocorrelation estimate can potentially constrain the geometry of \(A_\alpha(F)\): for a supercritical level set \(A\), the supplied prime-specific inequality
\[
\frac{\sqrt n}{|A|}
+
\frac{n}{|A|^2}
\max_{h\ne0}
\frac{R_A(h)}{1+L/\operatorname{ord}(h)}
\ge c_\delta D,\qquad
L\asymp\log p,                                               \tag{G}
\]
forces either \(|A|\) to be small or \(A\) to have substantial repeated differences. However, (G) contains no information about the placement of the terminal high set
\[
\{i:g_T(i)\text{ large}\}
\]
relative to \(A_\alpha(F)\). Thus it does not imply (F).

### What is established and what is not

The prime-specific evidence is real but insufficient:

1. The actual opposite optimizer has the Jacobi support equation and the pointwise cap (D).
2. Prime Fejér constructions provide opposite feasible rows whose autocorrelations price many difference classes, including a logarithmic gain on selected character arcs.
3. These estimates restrict possible supercritical support geometries of \(F\).
4. The actual terminal source \(g_T\) is generated by the nonlinear Picard orbit, and no proved estimate connects its high-value locations or difference correlations to the support geometry forced by (G).

The square diagnostic demonstrates that no estimate of the form (B) can hold uniformly over all eligible orders. On the true square minus side,
\[
\liminf\frac{\sqrt n}{T}
\mathbb E[1_{\{i\ne0\}}F_i g_T(i)]
\ge
2\bigl(\sqrt2-\sqrt{1+\tau}\bigr)>0
\]
through the same growing marked window. This is a genuine obstruction for squares, but it does not refute (B) on ordinary primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\).

Accordingly, the proposed ordinary-prime terminal estimate remains **unproved**. The precise gap is:

> Existing prime Jacobi autocorrelation bounds control repeated differences inside high sets of the opposite optimizer, while the required estimate controls the weighted intersection of that optimizer with the nonlinear terminal activation \(g_T\). A prime-uniform transfer from difference geometry of \(F\) to weighted placement of \(g_T\) is missing.

No conclusion about the original asymptotic follows yet.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Prove a support-sensitive terminal correlation lemma

For the actual prime orbit and actual \(F\), attempt to prove a bound of the form
\[
\sum_{i\in A}g_T(i)
\le
o\!\left(\frac{nT}{\sqrt n\,C_p}\right)
\]
for every level set \(A=A_{\alpha_p}(F)\) satisfying the opposite-sector Jacobi constraints, or directly prove the stronger bilinear estimate
\[
\sup_{F\in\mathcal F_{-\sigma}^{\rm opt}}
\frac{\sqrt n}{T}\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}(Fg_T)=o(1).
\]
The natural arithmetic input is (G), combined with a difference-correlation estimate for the terminal high set of \(g_T\). This is the most direct test of the requested mechanism.

### 2. Optimize \(F\) inside the true opposite optimal face

Define the secondary finite-dimensional problem
\[
\min\left\{
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}(F g_T):
F\ge0,\ \mathbb EF=1,\ F\in1+V_{-\sigma},\ F_0=\theta_{-\sigma}
\right\}.
\]
Test whether its minimizer has a support profile compatible with the prime Fejér autocorrelation bounds. If the minimum is \(o(T/\sqrt n)\) uniformly for \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), this would establish the terminal route without requiring the estimate for every optimizer. If the minimum remains of order \(T/\sqrt n\) on an infinite ordinary-prime family, that would give a precise arithmetic obstruction to the selectable-\(F\) approach.
