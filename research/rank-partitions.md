# The exterior partition in the random-weight expansion

Use the notation of the [rank expansion](random-weight-ranks.md), with weights of total one and positive support $S$. For $I$ in the frequency group, write

$$\ell_\sigma(I)=\dim\big(V_\sigma\cap\mathbb R^I\big),$$

where $\mathbb R^I$ consists of vectors supported on $I$. Equivalently, $\ell_\sigma(I)$ is the multiplicity of the exact eigenvalue $\sigma$ in $K_{II}$. A unit vector attaining this signed Rayleigh quotient also attains it for $K$ on the whole group, since $\|K\|\le1$.

For almost every weight on each support $S$, there is a disjoint partition $S^c=I_+\sqcup I_-$ with the following properties. One may choose optimal spectra $P,Q$ such that both are positive on $S$, and exactly the indicated sign is positive at each coordinate of $I_+$ or $I_-$. Each partition set records the largest possible support of its sign outside $S$. The projected face dimensions obey

$$r_+(w)-r_-(w)=-1+|I_+|-|I_-|+2\big(\ell_-(I_-)-\ell_+(I_+)\big).$$

This expresses the summands of the expansion through the signs of the exterior spectra and exact localized eigenspaces. They can be measured with positive-weight smoothing and a rank calculation on the resulting coordinate sets.

## Complementarity gives the partition

Approximation by positive weights and compactness give feasible nonnegative $P,Q$ that optimize at $w$ and satisfy $P_iQ_i=n w_i$. All optimizers of each sign have the same restriction to $S$ by strict concavity. Write $c_i=Q_i$ on $S$ and zero elsewhere. The linear program $\max_{X\in\mathcal P_+}c^{\mathsf T}X$ has value $n$, as $c\le Q$ and every feasible $X$ has $X^{\mathsf T}Q=n$. The dual minimizes $\sum_i Y_i$ over $Y\in U_-$ with $Y\ge c$. At optimum $\overline Y=1$, and $Y_S=c_S$ by complementary slackness against the spectrum $P$, positive on $S$. Thus all dual optima belong to the minus log-optimum fiber over $Q_S$, and all members of that fiber are dual optima.

For almost every $w$ the normal $(w_i/P_i)_{i\in S}$ exposes precisely the minimal face of $P_S$ in the projected polytope. Indeed, if that minimal face has dimension $r$, its normal cone in $\mathbb R^S$ has dimension $|S|-r$. Normals exposing a larger face lie on its relative boundary. Weights generated by such a pair, using $w_i=P_i(w_i/P_i)$, lie in the locally Lipschitz image of a set of dimension at most $r+(|S|-r-1)$. Take the union over the finitely many faces. Exceptional sets are conic, giving the null statement on the weight simplex as well. The same argument applies to the minus sign.

Apply [Goldman–Tucker strict complementarity](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.41.12.1922) to the linear program and its dual. There is a primal optimizer and a dual optimizer whose primal variable and dual slack have complementary supports. The primal optimizer may have a different restriction to $S$. Its projection belongs to the exposed projected face, and $P_S$ is in that face's relative interior. A convex combination with a suitable other point in that face has projection $P_S$ and keeps positive every coordinate that was positive in the primal optimizer. Lift the other point to the full exposed face to make the same combination there. This gives a log-optimal pair positive on $S$ for both signs and on complementary sets outside $S$. Cross products between any plus and minus log optimizers sum to $n$ on $S$ alone, so neither sign can gain positivity on the other sign's set.

## From supports to dimension and back to smoothing

The exposed full plus face consists of feasible spectra vanishing on $I_-$. It has a relative-interior point positive exactly on $S\cup I_+$. Its tangent is $V_+\cap\mathbb R^{S\cup I_+}$. The projection of this tangent to $S$ has kernel $V_+\cap\mathbb R^{I_+}$. The rank of coordinate restriction of $V_+$ to $I_-$ is $|I_-|-\dim(U_-\cap\mathbb R^{I_-})$. Any vector in $U_-$ supported on $I_-$ has zero mean, since its inner product with the plus optimum $P$ is zero and equals the sum of its coordinates. Consequently

$$r_+=\dim V_+-|I_-|+\ell_-(I_-)-\ell_+(I_+).$$

Interchanging signs gives the other dimension, and $\dim V_+-\dim V_-=-1$ gives the claimed difference.

To read the partition from positive weights, let $w^\varepsilon=w+\varepsilon b$ with $b_i>0$ on $S^c$ and $b_i=0$ on $S$. Set $B=\sum_i b_i$ and $W_\varepsilon=1+\varepsilon B$. Use any strictly complementary pair $P^*,Q^*$ at the original weights. The identities $\langle P^*,Q^\varepsilon\rangle=\langle Q^*,P^\varepsilon\rangle=n$ and $P_i^\varepsilon Q_i^\varepsilon=nw_i^\varepsilon/W_\varepsilon$ give

$$\sum_{i\in S}w_i\left(\frac{P_i^*}{P_i^\varepsilon}+\frac{P_i^\varepsilon}{P_i^*}\right)
+\frac1n\sum_{i\notin S}(W_\varepsilon P_i^*Q_i^\varepsilon+Q_i^*P_i^\varepsilon)=1+W_\varepsilon.$$

The first sum is at least $2$. On $I_+$ this yields $Q_i^\varepsilon\le n\varepsilon B/(W_\varepsilon P_i^*)$ and $P_i^\varepsilon\ge b_iP_i^*/B$. On $I_-$ it gives $P_i^\varepsilon\le n\varepsilon B/Q_i^*$ and $Q_i^\varepsilon\ge b_iQ_i^*/(W_\varepsilon B)$. Thus the signs of $P_i^\varepsilon-Q_i^\varepsilon$ recover the partition for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ at each such weight. The threshold depends on the positive entries of the limiting optimal pair.

The log objective itself has a uniform floor error. The values $L_\sigma=\sum_{i\in S}w_i\log P_{\sigma,i}^\varepsilon$ are feasible lower bounds on $F_\sigma(w)$. Their sum is $F_+(w)+F_-(w)-\log W_\varepsilon$ by the product and entropy identities. The two nonnegative gaps therefore sum to $\log W_\varepsilon$, giving

$$\left|F(w)-\sum_{i\in S}w_i\log(P_i^\varepsilon/Q_i^\varepsilon)\right|\le\log W_\varepsilon.$$

In particular a common small floor mass gives the same error bound at every weight and field order, even when the partition needs a smaller floor to be resolved.

## Exact localization through prime orders

For prime $p$, identify the frequency group with $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, $n=(p-1)/2$. Let $I$ have size $m$. If $I$ avoids an interval of length $L$ in any generator direction, then

$$\ell_\sigma(I)\le (m-L)_+.$$

Translation of the frequency support preserves the dimension. Dilation by a unit modulo $n$ also preserves it. The equations for $V_\sigma$ are rows of an evaluation matrix with entries $\zeta_n^{ij}$ at positions with forbidden signs, and dilation applies a cyclotomic Galois automorphism to these rows. Thus we can take the avoided interval to start at zero and have step one.

Choose a generator $\beta$ of $Q$ and reduce the evaluation matrix at $\zeta_n\mapsto\beta$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Reduction cannot increase rank. A vector in the reduced kernel gives $f(X)=\sum_{i\in I}v_iX^i$ with

$$\big((1-X)^n-\sigma\big)f(X)\equiv0\pmod{X^n-1},$$

by Euler's criterion at all roots in $Q$. Let $a=\min(L,m)$. For $j=0,\ldots,a-1$ the coefficient equation is, up to row and column signs, $\sum_{i\in I}\binom{n}{i-j}v_i=0$. All $i$ lie at or above $L$, and reducing the product modulo $X^n-1$ uses the index $n+j-i$ in the binomial expansion. For any $a$ columns, divide by the nonzero $\binom n i$ and multiply by $\prod_{h=1}^{a-1}(n-i+h)$. The rows are then polynomials of degree at most $a-1$ evaluated at the distinct column indices $i$:

$$T_j(i)=i(i-1)\cdots(i-j+1)\prod_{h=j+1}^{a-1}(n-i+h).$$

They are a polynomial basis over $\mathbb F_p$. Evaluations at $0,\ldots,a-1$ are triangular with nonzero diagonal, as all nonzero factors are smaller than $p$. Evaluation of this basis at the selected distinct column indices is invertible. Hence the reduced kernel has dimension at most $m-a$, which also bounds the original dimension. The real dimension equals the complex one since the spectral subspace of the real $K$ complexifies to the same Fourier eigenspace.

More generally, for any interval $J$ of length $L$ in a generator direction, impose zero on the $|I\cap J|$ entries and apply the gap bound to the remaining support. This gives

$$\ell_\sigma(I)\le\max\{|I\cap J|,m-L\},\qquad
\ell_\sigma(I)\le \min\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac{m^2}n\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\frac{m^2}{n+m}\right\rceil\right).$$

The second bound uses averaging over translations of $J$, first with $L=m$ and then with $L=\lfloor mn/(n+m)\rfloor$. In particular every nonzero exact eigenmode of sign $\pm1$ has support at least $\sqrt n$. At equality every interval of length $m=\sqrt n$ in step one contains exactly one support element. Comparing adjacent intervals then makes the support a coset of the order-$m$ subgroup. The prime-field proof bounds exact eigenspace dimensions. Quantitative gaps for near-unit compression eigenvalues still require the separate [coset progression argument](coset-progression-gap.md).

## The densely sampled regime

Draw $k$ random labels as in the expansion and let $T=S^c$ be the missed labels. For $k/n\to c\ge\log2$, at either prime or square field orders,

$$\mathbb E\ell_\sigma(T)=o(n).$$

First replace $T$ by an independent Bernoulli set of density $\rho=e^{-c}$. Both missed-set laws are uniform conditional on size, and $|T|/n\to\rho$ in probability and in mean. For the occupancy model this follows from $\Pr(i\in T)=(1-1/n)^k$ and $\Pr(i,j\in T)=(1-2/n)^k$. Couple both sets as initial segments of a common random permutation. Their expected symmetric difference is $o(n)$, and each changed coordinate alters $\ell_\sigma$ by at most one.

For the Bernoulli set let $R$ be its coordinate projector. Put $B_\sigma=(I-J+\sigma K)/2$, the deterministic projection onto $V_\sigma$. It has normalized trace tending to $1/2$, and its centered entries are $O(n^{-1/2})$ by the Jacobi bound. The matrices $R$ and $B_\sigma$ converge in expected normalized mixed moments to free projections of traces $\rho$ and $1/2$. In fact, expand an alternating centered trace of length $2h$ using the independent diagonal entries of $R-\rho I$. Only index tuples in which every label occurs at least twice survive expectation, so at most $O_h(n^{h/2})$ tuples remain. The deterministic factors contribute $O_h(n^{-h/2})$, and normalization gives $O_h(n^{-1})$. All centered polynomials in a projection are scalar multiples of the centered projection. For alternating words with matching first and last type, cyclically combine the end factors, split into centered and scalar parts, and use shorter words; this verifies the full moment criterion.

The expected empirical eigenvalue measure of $R B_\sigma R$ therefore converges to the product law of these two free projections. Its mass at $1$ is $(\rho+1/2-1)_+$ by [Collins, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0406560). The compact spectral interval $[0,1]$ makes moment convergence sufficient for weak convergence, which bounds the limit superior of mass at the closed singleton $\{1\}$ by zero for $\rho\le1/2$. At finite $n$ this mass is exactly $\ell_\sigma(T)/n$. This argument controls the expected dimension, allowing isolated compression eigenvalues at $1$.

Since $I_\sigma\subseteq T$, the same $o(n)$ bound applies to the averaged correction $\ell_-(I_-)-\ell_+(I_+)$. It holds uniformly over $k\ge\lceil n\log2\rceil$ by coupling the samples as prefixes of one sequence. The total contribution of these correction terms to the rank expansion over that range is $o(1)$, by summing $1/[k(k-1)]$. The dense part is thus governed by the expected difference of the two exterior sign counts. This reduction works over square orders too. A comparison through primes still needs cancellation of the sign counts and control of the sparser regime.

## Independent input for the remainder

For $t>0$, let $Z_i$ be independent compound Poisson variables, each the sum of a $\operatorname{Poisson}(t/n)$ number of independent mean-one exponentials. Let $G_n(t)=t^{-1}\mathbb E F(Z)$, with the homogeneous extension $F(0)=0$. Then, for $N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(t)$,

$$G_n(t)=\mathbb E a_{N+1},\qquad
-G_n'(t)=\mathbb E\left[\frac{d_{N+2}}{(N+1)(N+2)}\right].$$

Poisson splitting gives the law of $Z$ by sending $N$ jumps to independent uniform labels. Conditional on $N=m>0$, the normalized exponential sizes have the Dirichlet law and are independent of the total jump mass, of mean $m$. Homogeneity gives $\mathbb E[F(Z)\mid N=m]=m a_m$. The Poisson size-bias identity gives the first formula. Differentiating the Poisson series and using $a_{m+1}-a_m=-d_{m+1}/[m(m+1)]$ gives the second formula. Thus $G_n(0)=a_1$ by continuity and $G_n(t)\to0$ for fixed $n$ at infinity. This provides a continuous version of the rank expansion.

The rank bound $|d_{m+1}|\le m$ gives $|a_{m+1}-a_m|\le1/(m+1)$, uniformly in $n$. For an integer $k\ge1$, split $N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(k)$ at $N=k/2$. Above the split the harmonic sum bounding $|a_{N+1}-a_k|$ is at most $2(|N-k|+1)/k$. Below it, the bound is $1+\log k$ and the event has exponentially small probability in $k$. Consequently

$$|G_n(k)-a_k|=O(k^{-1/2})$$

with a constant independent of the field order. This allows independent weights at every growing sample scale, including $k/n\to0$. The exact derivative also gives $|G_n'(t)|\le\mathbb E[1/(N+2)]=(t-1+e^{-t})/t^2$, with value $1/2$ at zero. In particular $|G_n(t)-a_1|\le t/2$. The [FFT comparisons](poisson-smoothing.md) test both $a_k$ and $G_n(t)$ directly.

## An extensive defect at a single mark over square fields

For $q=r^2$ and weight $e_0$, the [constant minus witness](square-order-control.md) is optimal and its spectrum at a nontrivial frequency is $(r-\lambda_i)/(r+1)$, with $\lambda=Fs$. Hence every plus optimum is supported on $\{0\}\cup\{i:\lambda_i=r\}$ by complementarity. The latter equality set has size $O(n/\sqrt r)$. For a nontrivial frequency, $\lambda_i/r$ is the real part of the unit Jacobi phase $J(\psi,\chi)/r$ at either extension of the frequency to $\mathbb F_q^\times$. The Gauss quotient and [Katz's moment bound, Theorem 9.6](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/Katz-GKM.pdf) give empirical complex moments of nonzero order $h$ bounded by $O(|h|/r+1/q)$. The positive test $M^{-2}|1+z+\cdots+z^{M-1}|^2$ majorizes the indicator of the phase being one and has average $O(1/M+M/r)$. Use $M=\lfloor\sqrt r\rfloor$ and include the two exceptional characters separately.

Thus $|I_+|=O(n/\sqrt r)$ and $|I_-|=n-1-O(n/\sqrt r)$. Both projected face ranks at one mark are zero. The dimension formula and $0\le\ell_+(I_+)\le|I_+|$ then force

$$n/2-O(n/\sqrt r)\le\ell_-(I_-)\le n/2.$$

In particular the exterior sign imbalance at a single mark over square fields is of order $n$ and cancels with a dimension correction of the same order. This provides a sparse regime where the dense-sampling bound cannot be extended uniformly across field orders.

## Computational comparisons

The [random-weight probe](../rank_partitions.py) solves the positive log problem with a sequence of decreasing floor masses, reads the exterior signs, and estimates both localized dimensions from dense compressions. At small orders it also enumerates feasible spectra of both signs and measures the dimensions of the faces exposed by the weighted log gradients. Four comparisons near a face transition needed a smaller floor and search tolerance. The [81 rows](../data/rank-partitions.csv) cover 3,888 draws at five prime orders through $401$ and four square orders through $361$. The sign partition passed the two-floor stability and separation check for 3,845 draws. All 2,158 direct vertex-rank cross-checks agreed after refinement. Partition averages use the resolved draws, whereas log objective averages include every draw. These are floating-point comparisons.

At one label, $q=361$ gives exterior count difference $-161$ and minus localized dimension $81$. At $p=401$ they are $1$ and $0$. The projected rank difference is zero in both cases. At 256 labels the two orders gave zero localized dimensions on the entire missed set in every draw. The averaged log differences were $-0.0031$ and $-0.0058$ respectively, with standard errors $0.0077$ and $0.0071$.

The prime-field polynomial argument was also checked separately. Eight prime orders through $521$ gave 800 full-rank binomial minors under exact elimination modulo $p$ and 1,600 signed compression-dimension checks on randomly translated intervals and supports, with every numerical dimension obeying the proven bound.
