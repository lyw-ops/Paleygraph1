## Checked result

Let \(p\) range through the original eligible ordinary-prime family, with \(p\to\infty\). Let \(Q\subset \mathbb F_p^\times\) be the physical square set, let \(g=|f|^2\) be the original weight, and put
\[
G=\sum_x g(x)=O(\sqrt p).
\]

The physical affine line attached to \((Y,D)\) is
\[
r=\frac{Y-D}{1-D},\qquad
b=\frac{D(1-Y)}{Y(1-D)},
\]
with the original endpoint exclusions and the square mask \(b\in Q\). Its weight is
\[
\mu_0(r,b)
=
g(Y)g(D)\,
1_{\{Y,D\text{ physical}\}}\,
1_{\{b\in Q\}}.
\]

For \(h=(a,c)\), the exact right action is
\[
(r,b)\mapsto (r',b')=(ar,b+cr).
\]
The second line is retained only when its reconstructed physical labels
\[
Y'=\frac{r'}{1-b'},\qquad
D'=\frac{b'r'}{(1-r')(1-b')}
\]
satisfy the same endpoint conditions and \(b'\in Q\).

Define the genuinely generic family
\[
\mathcal H_{\rm gen}
=
\{(a,c):a\neq0,\ c\neq0,\ a\neq1\},
\]
and restrict further to pairs for which
\[
Y,D,Y',D'
\]
are all distinct. The corresponding correlation is
\[
\Delta_{\rm gen}(a,c)
=
\sum_{\substack{(Y,D)\ {\rm physical}\\
b,b'\in Q\\
Y,D,Y',D'\ {\rm pairwise\ distinct}}}
g(Y)g(D)g(Y')g(D').
\]
Thus the actual four-copy weight, including both \(b\)-square masks and all physical exclusions, is retained.

### Rigorous bound

The all-level weighted affine-energy estimate from the vertical-fiber argument gives
\[
E_w(\mu_0)
:=
\sum_{a\neq0}\sum_c
\left|
\sum_{(r,b)}\mu_0(r,b)\mu_0(ar,b+cr)
\right|^2
=
O\!\left(p^{11/4-\delta_*/2}\right),
\]
where
\[
\varepsilon_*=\frac1{1280},
\qquad
\delta_*=\frac1{640}=2\varepsilon_*.
\]
Hence
\[
E_w(\mu_0)=O\!\left(p^{11/4-1/1280}\right).
\]

Since the generic correlation is a nonnegative restriction of the actual correlation,
\[
0\le \Delta_{\rm gen}(a,c)\le \Delta(a,c),
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{(a,c)\in\mathcal H_{\rm gen}}
\Delta_{\rm gen}(a,c)^2
\le
E_w(\mu_0)
=
O\!\left(p^{11/4-1/1280}\right).
}
\]

This is uniform over the eligible prime family and uses the original \(g(Y)g(D)g(Y')g(D')\), the physical marking, both \(b\)-square masks, and all weight levels.

The norm order is important: for each fixed \(p\), the affine fourth-root energy norm is estimated first; layer cake is then applied through Minkowski to the four actual \(g\)-weights, and the resulting fourth-root estimate is finally raised to the fourth power. The threshold is \(\tau=p^{-\varepsilon_*}\), with the heavy and light pieces combined before taking the final square. No \(p\)-limit is taken before these finite-field norm estimates.

## What is and is not improved

The heavy-level contribution has fourth root
\[
O\!\left(p^{11/16-\delta_*/8}\right),
\]
which yields the displayed
\[
O\!\left(p^{11/4-\delta_*/2}\right)
\]
energy estimate. Thus there is a genuine power saving over the crude affine-energy scale, but it is far above the desired \(O(p^2)\) generic energy.

The physical repeated-label portions have already been removed at cost
\[
O(p^2)
\]
in squared norm. The pure families remain separate:
\[
\sum_{c\neq0}\Delta(1,c)^2
=
O\!\left(p^{5/2-3\varepsilon_*}\right),
\]
and
\[
\sum_{a\neq1}\Delta(a,0)^2
=
O\!\left(p^{5/2-2\varepsilon_*}\right).
\]
These are not part of \(\mathcal H_{\rm gen}\), but they remain independent obligations for the full affine bound. The identity element is also separate, with squared contribution \(O(p^2)\).

Consequently, the proposed target-scale statement
\[
\sum_{(a,c)\in\mathcal H_{\rm gen}}
\Delta_{\rm gen}(a,c)^2=O(p^2)
\]
is not proved. The present argument gives only
\[
O\!\left(p^{11/4-1/1280}\right).
\]
No obstruction to the \(O(p^2)\) claim has been established either.

The zero-match contribution is likewise not controlled by this right-energy estimate and must remain a separate obligation.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Generic restricted affine energy with the output factor retained

Redo the level-set affine-energy argument after imposing all four-copy distinctness and retaining the fourth factor \(g(Y')g(D')\), rather than bounding the output factor positively or passing to the unrestricted energy.

A sufficient result would be
\[
\left(
\sum_{(a,c)\in\mathcal H_{\rm gen}}
\Delta_{\rm gen}(a,c)^2
\right)^{1/4}
=O(p^{1/2}),
\]
equivalently
\[
E_{\rm gen}=O(p^2).
\]
Relative to the current fourth-root exponent \(11/16\), this requires a saving of \(p^{3/16-o(1)}\). The most promising place is the incidence estimate after deleting the four coincidence diagonals and using the actual output \(g(Y')g(D')\), not merely \(g(Y)g(D)\).

### 2. Direct two-level correlation estimate for \(a,c\neq0\), \(a\neq1\)

For fixed generic \((a,c)\), use
\[
Y'=\frac{aY}{1-cY}
\]
and the corresponding Möbius formula for \(D'\), then exploit the two-level affine estimate simultaneously in the \(Y\)- and \(D\)-variables. The test should target a summed estimate of the form
\[
\sum_{\substack{a,c\neq0\\a\neq1}}
\left|
\sum_{Y,D}
g(Y)g(D)g(Y')g(D')
\,1_{\rm phys}\,1_{b,b'\in Q}
\right|^2
\ll p^2.
\]
The distinctness conditions should be imposed before Cauchy–Schwarz. A bound obtained only after discarding \(g(Y')g(D')\), or only for fixed \(a,c\), is insufficient for the actual weighted energy.
