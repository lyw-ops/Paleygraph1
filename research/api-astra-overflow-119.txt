## Result: a uniform bound exists, but it does not vanish

Let \(K\) be the genuine multiplicative-frequency operator, \(T=T_\epsilon\), and
\[
S=(\epsilon KT-z_+)_+.
\]
Since \(S\le (\epsilon KT)_+\),
\[
\|S\|_{2,n}^2\le \|KT\|_{2,n}^2
=\|T\|_{2,n}^2-(\mathbb E_nT)^2
\le \alpha,
\]
where \(\alpha=\nu^2-\mu^2\) is the actual cap deficit. The stationary factor is
\[
f_\epsilon=\frac{b+\epsilon Y}{2},\qquad \mathbb E_n f_\epsilon=\frac{\nu}{2}.
\]

The Jacobi entry bound applied to the genuine \(Y=Kb\) gives, uniformly in the coordinate,
\[
0\le f_{\epsilon,i}\le |Y_i|+\sqrt t
\le \nu\sqrt p+\sqrt t=:L_{n,t}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|f_\epsilon\|_{2,n}^2
\le \|f_\epsilon\|_{\infty}\mathbb E_n f_\epsilon
\le \frac{\nu}{2}L_{n,t}.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz yields the exact prime-uniform estimate
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon S)
\le
\left(\frac{\nu L_{n,t}}2\right)^{1/2}\alpha^{1/2}.
\;} \tag{A}
\]

For fixed \(t\) and \(M=10\), \(p=n+1\), so \(L_{n,t}=O_t(\sqrt n)\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon S)=O_t(n^{1/4}\sqrt\alpha). \tag{B}
\]

### Consequences

1. **Rate-free cap:** the available general bound \(\alpha\le 4(1-t/n)\) gives only
   \[
   \mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon S)=O_t(n^{1/4}),
   \]
   which is useless for the required \(o(1)\) criterion.

2. **Critical cap rate:** even under the additional assumption
   \[
   \alpha\le Bn^{-1/2},
   \]
   (A) gives only
   \[
   \mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon S)
   \le
   \left(\frac{\nu(\nu\sqrt p+\sqrt t)}2\right)^{1/2}
   \sqrt{B}\,n^{-1/4}
   =O_t(\sqrt B), \tag{C}
   \]
   not \(o(1)\).

Thus the critical cap rate controls the \(L^2\)-size of the adjusted overshoot but does not control its correlation with the stationary factor.

## Why this is a genuine obstruction rather than a loose entry estimate

The factor \(f_\epsilon\) may carry \(L^2\)-mass of order \(n^{1/4}\):
\[
\|f_\epsilon\|_{2,n}^2\le \|f_\epsilon\|_\infty\mathbb E_n f_\epsilon
=O_t(\sqrt n).
\]
At the critical rate, \(\|S\|_{2,n}=O(n^{-1/4})\). Their product is therefore naturally \(O(1)\). This scaling is compatible with \(S\) being \(O(1)\) on an \(O(\sqrt n)\)-sized high-factor set, precisely the receiver geometry forced by a persistent stationary mark. Neither positivity nor the eigensector identity excludes that alignment.

The exact signed transport identity makes the missing input explicit:
\[
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon\,\epsilon KT)
=
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon T)-\frac{\nu}{2}\mathbb E_nT.
\]
Writing
\[
\epsilon KT=z_+ + S-R,\qquad
R=(z_+-\epsilon KT)_+,
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon S)
=
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon T)
-\frac{\nu}{2}\mathbb E_nT
-\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon z_+)
+\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon R).
\;} \tag{D}
\]
All terms on the right are genuine actual-row quantities. The signed identity controls \(S-R\), not \(S\) alone. A prime-uniform estimate forcing the positive and negative transport parts to cancel on the \(f_\epsilon\)-weighted receiver set is absent.

Conversely, the prior stationary pairing gives, for \(d=z_0/\sqrt n\) and \(\ell=(\epsilon KT)_0^+/\sqrt n\),
\[
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon S)
\ge
(d-\ell)\left(e^{\epsilon\gamma}-\frac{\nu^2}{4}\right)
-\frac{\nu^2}{4}(2-d). \tag{E}
\]
Hence if \(\epsilon\gamma\ge\delta>0\), \(t<e^\delta-1\), and the fixed-cap mark limits apply, the weighted overshoot has a positive lower bound (subject to the displayed \(\ell\)-term). Equations (C) and (E) show that the critical rate is not incompatible with a persistent weighted mark; it merely confines the obstruction to order-one scale.

## Conclusion

No rate-free proof of
\[
\mathbb E_n(f_\epsilon S)=o(1)
\]
is obtained. The new bound (A) is prime-uniform and uses the actual \(K,T,z_+,f_\epsilon\), but it exposes a precise gap:

> \(L^2\)-small adjusted transport does not imply stationary-weighted smallness because the true stationary factor has \(L^2\)-norm of order \(n^{1/4}\), and the high-factor receiver set can have exactly the scale needed for order-one weighted mass.

### Ranked next tests

1. **Highest priority:** prove a genuine prime-uniform decorrelation estimate for the actual receiver set
   \[
   \{i:f_{\epsilon,i}\asymp\sqrt n,\ S_i>\eta\},
   \]
   or equivalently show that its \(S\)-energy is \(o(n^{-1/2})\). This would defeat the order-one scaling in (C).

2. **Under the explicit added assumption \(\alpha=O(n^{-1/2})\):** retain the bounded-source/top-\(\sqrt n\) transport rectangle and test whether the actual multiplicative operator forbids simultaneous high \(f_\epsilon\) and positive \(S\) with a fixed margin. Without such a receiver-set exclusion, the weighted criterion remains unresolved.
