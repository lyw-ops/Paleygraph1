## Checked obstruction: the fixed-factor pairing is strictly positive

Assume the persistent critical-rate obstruction on a prime subsequence, and let \(q=h_j\) be any actual ordinary negative row in the fixed late window
\[
j_0\le j\le 2c_*\sqrt n,
\]
with \(j_0\) fixed sufficiently large. Retain \(q(0)=0\), both true factors \(g,g_-\), and the deleted output.

Define
\[
S(y):=\sum_x\omega^-_x\,k(x-y)^2.
\]
Since
\[
w_y=\frac{q_y g_y}{n\,\mathbb E_n(qg)},
\]
the fixed-factor statistic in (1) is exactly
\[
\mathcal L(q)
 =\sum_{x,y}\omega^-_xw_y k(x-y)^2
 =\frac{\mathbb E_n\!\left[q\,g\,S\right]}
        {\mathbb E_n(qg)}.
\]
Consequently the main term in the distinct output-masked cubic is the linear functional
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathscr F_n(q)
 :=p\mu\,\mathbb E_n(qg)\mathcal L(q)
 =p\mu\,\mathbb E_n\!\left[q\,g\,S\right].
\;} \tag{A}
\]

This identity keeps the actual capped fixed-point seed through the actual row \(q\), and keeps both true factors: \(g\) appears explicitly and \(g_-\) is retained in the positive measure \(\omega^-\) defining \(S\).

By the uniform paired-spreading estimate already established for the actual row class, there is a constant \(c_{\mathrm{pair}}>0\), independent of \(n\), the prime, and \(j\) in the persistent window, such that
\[
\mathcal L(q)\ge c_{\mathrm{pair}}.
\]
The marked lower bound gives
\[
\mathbb E_n(qg)\ge c_{\mathrm{mark}}\,n^{-1/2},
\]
while \(\mu\) and \(p\) have the fixed normalization used in (1). Hence
\[
\mathscr F_n(q)\ge c_0>0 \tag{B}
\]
for a constant \(c_0\) depending only on the fixed obstruction parameters.

Substituting (A) into the genuine distinct expansion gives
\[
n\,T(q)
=\mathscr F_n(q)+O\!\left(\eta_q+n^{-1/4}\right), \tag{C}
\]
where
\[
\eta_q=n^{1/4}\|q-\epsilon Kq\|_{2,n}.
\]
For the actual ordinary orbit,
\[
\eta_q=O((j+1)^{-1/2}).
\]
Choose \(j_0\) so large that the implied fixed bound satisfies
\[
\sup_{j\ge j_0}\eta_{h_j}\le \frac{c_0}{4C_{\mathrm{err}}},
\]
where \(C_{\mathrm{err}}\) is the constant in (C). Then, for all sufficiently large primes in the obstruction subsequence,
\[
n\,T(h_j)\ge \frac{c_0}{2},
\qquad j_0\le j\le 2c_*\sqrt n.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\;
T(h_j)\ge \frac{c_0}{2n}
\;}
\]
uniformly throughout that fixed-depth portion of the genuine ordinary orbit.

### Consequence

Under the stated critical cap-rate marked obstruction, a genuine fixed-depth bound
\[
T(h_{j_0})=o(n^{-1})
\]
through primes is false. The obstruction is not a failure to find sufficiently strong prime arithmetic: the retained fixed-factor pairing is a positive linear functional of the actual repair row, with a uniform positive lower bound.

In particular, any arithmetic replacement \(r\) of \(q\) that is intended to preserve the fixed-factor statistic must satisfy
\[
\mathscr F_n(r)
=-\mathscr F_n(q)+o(1)
\]
if it is to prove \(nT(q)=o(1)\). A genuine approximation \(r\approx q\) in the pairing sense instead gives
\[
\mathscr F_n(r)=\mathscr F_n(q)+o(1),
\]
which has the same positive sign and cannot yield the desired upper bound. The exact residual that must be controlled is
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathscr R_n(q-r)
=p\mu\,\mathbb E_n\!\left[(q-r)gS\right].
\;}
\]
Thus an \(L^2\) approximation is relevant only after proving a prime-uniform operator estimate for the multiplier \(gS\). An unquantified \(o(n^{-1/4})\) row error does not suffice by itself.

## Remaining gap

The conclusion is conditional on the persistent obstruction and on the established expansion (C). The unresolved issue is whether that obstruction can actually occur for the capped fixed-point seed along infinitely many primes. Arithmetic analysis should therefore target exclusion of the obstruction itself, not an \(o(n^{-1})\) estimate for \(T(h_{j_0})\) while the obstruction hypotheses remain valid.

## Ranked next tests

1. **Prime-uniform residual test.** Prove or disprove
   \[
   \inf_{\substack{q\ \text{actual persistent row}}}
   p\mu\,\mathbb E_n(qgS)\ge c_0>0
   \]
   with all normalizations explicit. Any failure must come from a genuine loss in the paired-spreading estimate, not from output deletion.

2. **Seed-to-row obstruction test.** Use the capped fixed-point equation and the actual alternating ordinary updates to test whether rows satisfying simultaneously
   \[
   \mathbb E_n(qg)\asymp n^{-1/2},\qquad
   \|q-\epsilon Kq\|_{2,n}=O(n^{-1/2})
   \]
   can persist for \(j\asymp\sqrt n\) along infinitely many primes. Excluding this near-eigenrow window would remove the conditional lower bound; arithmetic cancellation inside the fixed-factor pairing would not.
