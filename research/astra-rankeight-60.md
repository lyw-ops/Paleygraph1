# The cross Legendre source has full independent symplectic group in dimension eight

Let \(N\) be the determinant-one twisted Legendre perverse sheaf, let \(N^\chi\) be its ordinary quadratic Kummer twist in the coordinate \(a\), and put \(M=N\star N^\chi\), \(R=\mathcal T(M)\). Here \(\star\) is middle multiplicative convolution and \(\mathcal T\) twists the generic ordinary local system by \(\chi(1-a)\) and takes middle extension. For each of the eligible prime and prime-square fields, the geometric and arithmetic Mellin groups of \(R\) are both \(Sp_8\), in their defining representation. This factor is independent of the full old group, the two new \(Sp_4\)'s and the new quadratic Legendre \(SL_4\). Its trace therefore has conditional mean zero, variance one and fourth moment three in the compact model, including after conditioning on all the preceding fields. Its determinant and quadratic contraction have arithmetic phase \(+1\).

At the real floor \(2031/128\), a disjoint-two-level grouping that retains the entire old part of *every power* of the newly tagged rank-four and rank-eight sources, together with the full previous unary \(C_2\) grouping, still has unbounded packet mass. The first certified lower mass above 21 is at height 2146. This applies to the specified propagation only. The all-prime theta limit remains open.

## Arithmetic self-duality and its sign

The independent geometric Legendre factors make \(M\) simple. It has convolution dimension four. Its two boundary lists are each \((-2,-2,2,2)\). Its generic rank is eight. The local convolution theorem makes it tame, lisse away from one in the torus, with unipotent inertia at one. The twist that forms \(R\) gives a simple negative object, with both generic rank and convolution dimension eight. Both its lists are \((-2,-2,0,0,0,0,2,2)\). The equality \(M^\chi\simeq M\) follows arithmetically by interchanging the two convolution factors. Both factors are arithmetically self-dual. Under multiplicative inversion the twisting line \(\chi(1-a)\) acquires \(\chi(a)\), since \(\chi(-1)=1\). It follows that \(R^\vee\simeq R^\chi\simeq R\) arithmetically. [Local types](iteration-parity.md) · [Two-twist lists](astra-ranknext-58.md#a-two-node-noncollision-test)

For the sign, fix the base field and let \(u\) run through its extension sizes. Use the norm-one group \(B\subset\mathbb F_{u^2}^\times\) and its quadratic character \(\xi\). Off the identity in \(B\), one has \(\chi(1-x)=-\xi(x)\). Indeed \((1-x)^{u-1}=-x^{-1}\) there and \(u\equiv1\pmod 4\). Every character \(\beta\) of \(\mathbb F_{u^2}^\times\) extending \(\xi\) satisfies \(\beta^u=\beta\chi\) and both characters are good, as the bad characters \(1,\chi\) restrict trivially to \(B\). Frobenius invariance gives the same Legendre Mellin trace at \(\beta\) and \(\beta\chi\). Put \(S=\mathrm{Sym}^2_\star N\). At each of these characters the Mellin trace of \(M\) thus equals the square of the standard trace, or \(1\) plus the Mellin trace of \(S\). Averaging over the extensions of \(\xi\) and writing \(t_U\) for the perverse trace gives the exact equality
\[
 \sum_{x\in B}\xi(x)t_M(x)=1+\sum_{x\in B}t_{[2]^*S}(x)-\sum_{x\in B}t_S(x).
\]
The last difference counts squares in \(B\) with their two square roots.

The geometrically irreducible \(S\) has an arithmetic orthogonal form as the symmetric square of the \(SL_2\) standard. The étale pullback \([2]^*S\) is geometrically irreducible. Indeed the projection formula gives \([2]_*[2]^*S=S\oplus S^\chi\), and \(S,S^\chi\) differ geometrically by their two boundary lists \((-4,0,4)\), \((0,0,0)\). Adjunction and geometric semisimplicity give a one-dimensional endomorphism space on pullback. Pullback commutes with inversion and Verdier duality here, giving an arithmetic convolution self-duality. Finite pushforward along a group homomorphism is a symmetric tensor functor for convolution. It carries that form to one on \(S\oplus S^\chi\) with the same sign. The two distinct orthogonal summands each have a nondegenerate restriction, proving the positive sign on the pullback. By the norm-one indicator formula, the last two sums both tend to \(+1\). Thus \(\sum_B\xi t_M\to1\). The norm-one sum of \(R\) equals its negative plus \(t_M(1)=O(1/u)\), the stalk of a nonpunctual middle extension of weight zero. The indicator is \(-1\). This proves arithmetic symplecticity and fixes the determinant phase over every base. [Katz, Chapters 8–10](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf)

For an exact check let \(q=u^2\), \(\ell(a)=\chi(1-a)\) and \(n=\ell(\ell*\ell)\) for ordinary finite cyclic convolution. The unnormalized \(M\) row is \(n*(\chi n)\) with denominator \(q^2\). At a bad character one factor has weights \(-2,2\) with eigenvalues \(q^{-1},q\), and the other has two weight-zero eigenvalues. The compact-support replacement of the positive eigenvalue by \(q^{-1}\) distributes over the weight-zero factor, so the good-character raw identity holds there too. The \(S\) row is \(n*n+2(q+1)-q^2\delta_1\) with the same denominator, by the symmetric-square correction. The checker tests the two norm-one identities exactly for \(u=5,9,13,25\). These finite sums can have either sign. The indicator above follows by extension in each fixed characteristic. ## The connected group

Geometric tensor words in \(R\) and its dual have tame middle-extension constituents lisse away from one and possible punctual summands only at one. For arbitrary lengths, pair factors in even words and apply the positive-positive local convolution closure. Odd length words can be treated by the same finite-singularity support theorem, independent of signs. Hence the only geometric simple punctual object in the generated category is \(\delta_1\). The geometric convolution group is connected by Katz's connectedness criterion. It acts irreducibly since \(R\) is simple, and its connected center is trivial since scalar symplectic transformations are finite. [Katz, Corollary 6.7](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf) · [Rojas-León, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1398) · [Local convolution closure](iteration-parity.md)

Over an algebraically closed coefficient field of characteristic zero, the dimension-eight irreducibles of a simple Lie algebra, up to diagram automorphism, are the standard of \(A_7\), \(\mathrm{Sym}^7\) of \(A_1\), the adjoint of \(A_2\), the spin of \(B_3\), the standard of \(C_4\), and the vector and half-spins of \(D_4\). This small list follows from the Weyl dimension formula, using monotonicity in dominant-weight coordinates. The two-dimensional simple standard is symplectic. The four-dimensional simple possibilities are \(A_1\) symmetric cube and \(C_2\) standard, both symplectic, and the non-self-dual \(A_3\) standard and dual. Thus the two-factor decomposition \(2\times4\) cannot give an alternating irreducible representation. Of the simple eight-dimensional possibilities, the \(A_2,B_3,D_4\) cases are orthogonal (the three \(D_4\) cases are interchanged by triality) and the \(A_7\) standard is non-self-dual. The \(A_1\) seventh power would have eight distinct weights on every nonzero cocharacter, or all zero. This contradicts the displayed boundary list. The possibilities left are the defining \(Sp_8\) and the product of three \(SL_2\) standards modulo its central kernel.

For the last exclusion, suppose the geometric group is the triple tensor. Its normalizer in \(Sp_8\) has finite quotient over it, since connected automorphisms are inner and the centralizer in \(Sp_8\) is scalar finite. Extend the constants to remove any arithmetic permutation of the three factors. Each factor adjoint is then an actual geometric simple summand of \(R^{\star2}\) definable over a finite extension. All weights below are relative to the new field size, so this operation preserves their numerical values. The absolute-value cocharacter of each bad-character Frobenius lies in the connected torus of the joint algebraic group; taking a common power removes any finite component. In the triple tensor its weights have the form \(\pm a\pm b\pm c\), where \(a,b,c\ge0\) are allowed rational in a simply connected cover. Exactly two weights reach the maximum \(2\), forcing exactly one of \(a,b,c\) to vanish. The four nonextremal weights all vanish, forcing the other two numbers to be equal. Thus up to reordering each twist has \((a,b,c)=(0,1,1)\).

Pick the factor inactive at the first twist. Its adjoint has first list \((0,0,0)\) and second list either \((0,0,0)\) or \((-2,0,2)\). The other boundary eigencharacters are absent: all other twists of \(R\) have only zero weights, so the same holds for any summand of its square. The generic rank of this simple adjoint is the pooled absolute first moment, hence at most two. It is a nonpunctual simple object of convolution dimension three. Tameness and lissity away from one give by Euler–Poincaré that its convolution dimension equals the drop at one, which is bounded by the generic rank. This contradiction excludes the triple tensor in every eligible field. Therefore geometric and arithmetic groups are both \(Sp_8\). Their common group has no simple quotient in common with the old \(A_1,\ldots,A_6\) and \(C_2\) factors. Goursat gives independence, also relative to the Jacobi torus. [Two-boundary rank formula](astra-feedback-52.md#reading-rank-from-two-mellin-characters) · [Katz, Chapters 13 and 16](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf)

A second exact independence check uses both normalized boundary polynomials \(2(1+x^2)^2\). If an old simple factor acted in this irreducible dimension-eight representation, its factor representation would have dimension dividing eight and its polynomial on each twist would divide this polynomial with nonnegative integer quotient, after subtracting the smallest exponent. The checker excludes the old \(SL_2\) standards, third and seventh powers, the \(SL_3\) adjoints, all four old \(SL_4\) standards and duals, and the three new four-dimensional factors by this separate test. No other old simple representation has suitable dimension. In the independent \(Sp_8\) factor the odd moments vanish by its central minus identity. Its tensor square decomposes as the unit, primitive exterior square and symmetric square, three inequivalent self-dual representations. This gives the fourth moment three and the second moment one.

## All old returns on the three tagged sources

Use the fixed limiting coefficients and root kernels at \(t=2031/128\). On a disjoint pair of Picard levels tag the arity-zero inner characters \(m^{\pm4},\mathrm{Sym}^2 A,\mathrm{Sym}^2 B,AB\), with signed coefficients \(v,w,z\) respectively, assigning \(v\) to each Jacobi exponent and \(w\) to each individual symmetric square. After the inner activation their surviving trace is
\[
 Z=v(U+U')+w(D+D^\vee)+zR.
\]
Here \(U,U'\) are the independent symplectic four-dimensional standards and \(D,D^\vee\) the arithmetic dual four-dimensional standards. Global signs on these three real pairs do not affect the estimates below. Keep the previous \(m^{\pm2}\) unary grouping. Additionally, whenever *all* children of the outer vertex belong to \(Z\), project their product onto the sectors trivial on the new derived factors, multiply by the full signed old arity kernel, and pool all such terms, at every arity, with all direct old labels and the previous unary grouping. Keep remaining contributions separately with the round-58 charge. This prescription excludes mixtures with residual untagged children, new nontrivial labels across arities and overlapping pairs of levels.

The quadratic old projection is exactly the arithmetic unit with coefficient \(C=2v^2+2w^2+z^2\). This uses symplectic self-pairings, the two ordered \(D,D^\vee\) contractions, and independence. The determinant of \(D\) is \(L^{\star4}\) with phase \(+1\); its quadratic contraction introduces no determinant shift. Every odd projection vanishes since each new group contains its central minus identity. At every even arity at least four charge the full unsigned tagged product, which also covers all determinant phases and shifts in its old projection. For the full original \(Z\) its boundary-measure mass is at most \(b_Z=8(|v|+|w|+|z|)<0.028\), by the certified root coefficients. Let \(U_j(k)\) be the upper arity-\(j\) preactivation kernel per signed boundary weight from the degree-28 polynomial, with its full analytic tail. The triangle inequality bounds any reduction caused by pooling a new group by twice its unsigned charge. Thus the additional per-sign loss at weight \(k\) is at most \(2CU_2(k)+2E\), with \(E<10^{-9}\). For \(E\), take the complete unsigned mass through arities four, six and eight times \(b_Z^j\). For arities through eight the discarded polynomial-degree tail is counted once in total mass, since it was bounded before distributing among weights. Applying this debit at even levels to the positive lower kernels on 28 packets, nine arities and shifts through 18, the certified mass first exceeds 21 at height 2146.
