## Result: the available two-step data do not yet close an invariant ball

Let \(T=F_t=K\Phi_t\), and use normalized counting norm \(\|\cdot\|_{2,n}\). For a starter \(V_m\), write
\[
R_m=T(V_m)-V_m,\qquad r_m=\sup_{Q}\|R_m\|_{2,n}.
\]

A sufficient moving-starter closure with derivative counting factor \(q<1\), coordinate-sheet margin \(d\), and curvature constant \(C\) is
\[
\frac{r_m}{1-q}<d-\eta,
\qquad
n^{1-\beta}r_m^2=o(1),
\qquad
\sup_{j\ge0}\|(KD_m)^j(I-KD_m)^{-1}K\|_{\max}=O(n^{-\beta}).
\tag{A}
\]
The second condition is exactly the nonlinear error threshold in the orbit-transfer argument. With \(\beta=0.467\), it requires
\[
r_m=o\!\left(n^{-(1-0.467)/2}\right)
=o(n^{-0.2665}).
\tag{B}
\]

### Quantitative failure at the certified height-600 input

The actual certified price for
\[
R_{600}=V_{601}-V_{600}
\]
is \(<0.010955\), and the price dominates the coordinate norm and the normalized counting norm. Hence the currently certified input only gives
\[
r_{600}<0.010955.
\]
This does **not** establish (B). Indeed, substituting the certified bound into the nonlinear term gives
\[
n^{1-0.467}r_{600}^2
\le 0.010955^2\,n^{0.533},
\]
which diverges with \(n\). Thus the existing height-600 certificate cannot serve as a growing-\(n\) starter for the \(\beta=0.467\) closure. This is a failure of the sufficient invariant-ball test, not a lower bound showing that the actual residual is large.

For scale, at \(n=10^{30}\), the threshold \(n^{-0.2665}\) is approximately \(1.0\times10^{-8}\), whereas the certified residual upper bound is \(1.1\times10^{-2}\). A moving height \(m=m(n)\), or a substantially sharper signed residual estimate, is therefore essential.

## What the genuine two-step calculation does prove

The actual outgoing estimates give, on \(Q\),
\[
\|R_{601}\|_\infty<0.012,
\]
and the two consecutive counting factors satisfy approximately
\[
\|R_{601}\|_{2,n}\le 0.515\,\|R_{600}\|_{2,n},
\qquad
\|R_{602}\|_{2,n}\le 0.518\,\|R_{601}\|_{2,n},
\]
so that
\[
\|R_{602}\|_{2,n}\lesssim 0.267\,\|R_{600}\|_{2,n}.
\tag{C}
\]
This is a genuine signed/physical two-step estimate at the displayed transition, with the two half-step sheets confined along those two actual segments.

However, (C) is not yet an invariant-ball statement. It controls the two particular outgoing residuals, not all secants
\[
T^2(U)-T^2(W)
\]
for \(U,W\) in a proposed ball. Nor does it provide a growing-depth estimate uniform in \(m\). Consequently it cannot be iterated to obtain \(r_{600+2k}\to0\), nor can it imply the residual scale (B).

A second issue is the cycle obstruction. A \(T^2\)-contraction alone permits a two-cycle. The present \(0.515\) and \(0.518\) bounds are directional estimates along the actual residual segments; they are not a common one-step secant bound on the whole candidate ball. Therefore they do not yet rule out a distinct pair \(U\ne W\) with \(T(U)=W\) and \(T(W)=U\).

## Precise block criterion that would close the argument

Suppose there is a ball \(\mathcal B_m\) around \(V_m\) such that:

1. both half-step physical sheets are defined on \(\mathcal B_m\cup T(\mathcal B_m)\);
2. for all \(U,W\in\mathcal B_m\),
   \[
   \|T^2(U)-T^2(W)\|_{2,n}\le q_2\|U-W\|_{2,n},
   \qquad q_2<1;
   \]
3. for all \(U,W\in\mathcal B_m\),
   \[
   \|T(U)-T(W)\|_{2,n}\le q_1\|U-W\|_{2,n},
   \qquad q_1<1;
   \]
4. \(T^2(\mathcal B_m)\subseteq\mathcal B_m\), and the shifted-resolvent bound at \(V_m\) has exponent \(\beta\).

Then \(T^2\) has a unique fixed point \(U_*\) in \(\mathcal B_m\). The one-step secant condition gives
\[
\|T(U_*)-U_*\|_{2,n}
=\|T(T^2U_*)-T(U_*)\|_{2,n}
\le q_1\|T^2U_*-U_*\|_{2,n}=0,
\]
so \(U_*\) is a fixed point of \(T\), not merely a point on a two-cycle. If the ball is centered at \(V_m\), a basic radius condition is
\[
\frac{\|T^2(V_m)-V_m\|_{2,n}}{1-q_2}<\rho_m,
\]
together with a coordinate-sheet margin for the entire ball and its image.

The present records establish only the analogue of the numerator estimate at \(m=600\), with approximate \(q_2=0.267\) along one orbit, not hypotheses 1–4 uniformly on a ball or for growing \(m\).

## Ranked next tests

1. **Highest priority:** certify a common physical ball around \(V_{601}\) or \(V_{600}\) with all-pair one-step and two-step secant bounds. In particular, test whether the existing curvature estimate \(<0.248\) upgrades the directional \(0.515/0.518\) bounds to a uniform \(q_1<1\) and \(q_2<1\) on a specified radius. This would settle the cycle issue.

2. **Residual-growth test:** replay the signed residual recurrence for heights \(m=m(n)\), retaining the product before absolute pricing, and seek
\[
r_m\le n^{-0.2665-\delta}
\]
for some \(\delta>0\), together with the shifted-resolvent estimate at the same moving starter. A fixed-height two-step contraction is insufficient unless it is upgraded to a uniform block contraction through growing depth.
