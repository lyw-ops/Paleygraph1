## Status

**Inconclusive, with a precise obstruction to the proposed Gauss-quotient argument.** I obtain the exact quotient identity, but the near-critical Jacobi cap controls the quotient only on the original lifted support. The third factor evaluates the quotient at *ratios* of independently sampled lifted characters, where no corresponding cap information is available.

Moreover, if the previously claimed \(\omega(p)\) lower for the same actual law is valid, then the requested \(O(p^{-1/2})\) phase upper is impossible: all nonregular and fixed-relation contributions are only \(O(p)\).

## Exact regular phase

For lifted variables \((\gamma,U,V,U',V')\), write
\[
J_1=J(\gamma/V,U/\gamma),\qquad
J_2=J(\gamma/V',U'/\gamma),
\]
and
\[
B=V/V',\qquad C=\frac{U/V}{U'/V'}.
\]
After the full-field lift and the \(u\in Q\setminus\{1\}\) sum, the regular contribution is
\[
p^{3/2}\,\Re A_p,
\]
where, after removing the prescribed finite list of fixed relations,
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_p
={}&\sum_{\mathcal R}
 w_\gamma w_Uw_Vw_{U'}w_{V'}\,
 \zeta_{\gamma,V,V'}\,
 \frac{J_1}{\sqrt p}\,
 \overline{\frac{J_2}{\sqrt p}}\\
&\hspace{35mm}\times
 \frac12\left(
 \frac{J(B,C)}{\sqrt p}
 +
 \frac{J(B\chi,C)}{\sqrt p}
 \right),
\end{aligned}
\]
with the physical marking phase
\[
\zeta_{\gamma,V,V'}
=(\gamma/V)(-1)\,
 \overline{(\gamma/V')(-1)}.
\]
The factor \(\zeta\) cannot be discarded by averaging \(\gamma\) uniformly: \(\gamma\) has the actual weight \(w\).

The two-trivial Jacobi terms and every fixed algebraic relation contribute \(O(p)\), using \(\max w_\eta=O(p^{-1/2})\). Thus the desired bound is genuinely
\[
\Re A_p=O(p^{-1/2}).
\]

## What the Gauss quotient actually gives

On jointly regular characters, with normalized Gauss sums \(\tau(\alpha)=g(\alpha)/\sqrt p\),
\[
J(A,D)=\frac{\tau(A)\tau(D)}{\tau(AD)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{J(B\chi,C)}{J(B,C)}
 =
 \frac{\tau(B\chi)}{\tau(B)}
 \frac{\tau(BC)}{\tau(B\chi C)}.
\]
Define
\[
R(X)=\frac{\tau(X\chi)}{\tau(X)}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\frac{J(B\chi,C)}{J(B,C)}
 =\frac{R(B)}{R(BC)}
 =\frac{R(V/V')}{R(U/U')}.
}
\]

This is the key obstruction. The near-cap Jacobi information concerns a supported lift \(\eta\):
\[
\frac{J(\eta,\chi)}{\sqrt p}
=\frac{\tau(\eta)\tau(\chi)}{\tau(\eta\chi)}.
\]
Since the high signed phase is near \(\sigma\in\{\pm1\}\), it gives, on the high set carrying essentially all actual mass,
\[
R(\eta)=\frac{\tau(\eta\chi)}{\tau(\eta)}
 \approx \sigma\,\tau(\chi).
\]
Thus \(R\) is controlled on the physical lifted support.

But the third-factor quotient samples
\[
R(V/V')\quad\text{and}\quad R(U/U'),
\]
which are ratios of two independently sampled supported lifts. They need not lie in the supported set, and the near-cap hypothesis supplies no estimate for \(R\) on these ratio sets.

There is even a direct positivity obstruction in a relaxed model. Let
\[
\mu(B)=\sum_{V/V'=B}w_Vw_{V'}
\]
be the actual ratio law. Ignoring the first two factors and the marking phase, the quotient part has average
\[
\sum_{B,D}\mu(B)\mu(D)R(B)\overline{R(D)}
=
\left|\sum_B\mu(B)R(B)\right|^2.
\]
So the Gauss quotient is not intrinsically cancellatory. A phase argument must control the *actual ratio law* and its coupling to \(J_1\overline{J_2}\zeta\); individual near-cap phases are insufficient.

This is a specific failure of the quotient-only route. It does not yet disprove the full ordinary-prime estimate, because the first two Jacobi factors and the physical marking phase may still produce cancellation.

## Conditional incompatibility with the known lower

Let \(\mathcal M_p\) denote the actual moment, with the same \(b,\nu,w\), the same physical mark, and the same order of finite-\(p\) floor choices. The factorization and the \(O(p)\) regularity exclusions give
\[
\mathcal M_p=p^{3/2}\Re A_p+O(p).
\]

The restricted/full comparison in R153 changes the moment only by
\[
\left(\frac52+o(1)\right)p,
\]
and all degenerate or fixed-relation terms are \(O(p)\). Therefore, if the claimed critical lower for the actual law is indeed
\[
\mathcal M_p=\omega(p)
\]
along ordinary primes, then necessarily
\[
\Re A_p=\omega(p^{-1/2}).
\]
Hence an upper \(\Re A_p=O(p^{-1/2})\) would contradict that lower.

This is not an unconditional refutation, because the existence of the actual near-critical ordinary-prime branch together with the \(\omega(p)\) lower is precisely part of the unresolved problem. But it shows that the requested upper cannot be proved simultaneously with that lower; one of the two claims must fail.

## Two ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: exact ratio-law diagnostic on the actual ordinary-prime sequence

For each finite-\(p\) actual \(b\), compute separately:

\[
S_p=\sum_B\mu(B)R(B),
\]
and the fully coupled quantities
\[
\sum_{\mathcal R}w^5\,
\zeta\,\Phi_1\overline{\Phi_2}R(V/V')\overline{R(U/U')},
\]
together with the same expression without the quotient replacement.

Use only ordinary primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), retain the actual \(b_0=0\), actual anchor law, and actual finite-\(p\) floors. Record \(p^{1/4}|S_p|\) and \(p^{1/2}\Re A_p\), after removing exactly the allowed fixed relations.

- If \(p^{1/4}|S_p|\) grows or stays bounded away from zero, the Gauss-quotient route is decisively inadequate.
- If the coupled expression exhibits \(p^{-1/2}\) decay while \(S_p\) does not, cancellation must come from \(J_1\overline{J_2}\zeta\), identifying the correct object for an analytic theorem.

This is diagnostic only, not an asymptotic proof.

### 2. Analytic test: prove a ratio-rigidity or coupled trace estimate

The weakest useful new theorem would control the actual ratio law, for example
\[
\left|\sum_B\mu(B)R(B)\right|\ll p^{-1/4}
\]
with the same near-critical \(b\), physical marking, and ordinary-prime limit. The stronger—and probably necessary—statement is the coupled estimate
\[
\left|\sum_{\mathcal R}w^5\,
\zeta\,\Phi_1\overline{\Phi_2}
R(V/V')\overline{R(U/U')}\right|
\ll p^{-1/2},
\]
plus the analogous term without the quotient.

A proof would need a genuine ordinary-prime trace/large-sieve input for ratios of the actual lifted support. The existing near-cap and coset-spread results do not imply it.
