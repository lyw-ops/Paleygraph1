# Signed prices of sparse deletions on the original short affine probes

Let \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) be prime, \(Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2\), \(n=(p-1)/2\), and take any genuine pair of optimal rows at zero, with real positional values \(X=\Re F, Y=\Re R\), spectral marks \(a=q_0, b=r_0\), and evenized probabilities \(q^e,r^e\). These probabilities have point caps \(a,b\ll p^{-1/2}\). Write \(D_\pm=\{x\in Q:\chi(1-x)=\pm1\}\). In the product experiment, \(D=Y(y)-X(x)\) for independent uniform strict sector points. In the actual experiment, \(D_z=Y(v^2(z+1))-X(u^2z)\) on the strict matches, with independent uniform rational primes \(u,v\le(\log p)^A, A>6\). The third coordinate \(z+2\) lies in one of the \(d\) cosets of a predetermined subgroup of \(Q\). Write \(M_C(I)\) for the matches in coset \(C\) and the cyclic interval \(I\), \(\alpha=|I|/p, M_0=\alpha p/(32d)\), and \(E_A=(1+\log p)(\log p)^{1/2-A/4}\). All actual prices are sums divided by \(\sqrt p\).

## Optimal deletion and its complementary price on short primes

For a sign \(s=\pm1\), let \(Q_s^+(v)\) be the decreasing quantile of \((sD)_+\) in the product experiment. In a chosen collection of bins, allow at most \(k_C\) deleted sites in each, chosen after the probe draw. Put \(k_C=0\) outside the collection. The maximum oriented deleted price has simultaneous approximation
\[
 \sum_C {M_0\over\sqrt p}\int_0^{\min(1,k_C/M_0)} Q_s^+(v)\,dv .       \tag{1}
\]
A zero target mass contributes zero. For any \(0<\tau\le1\) and integer \(m\ge2\), the absolute error is bounded by \(\tau\sum_C k_C/\sqrt p+Z\), with one nonnegative random \(Z\) valid for both signs, every interval and all the budgets. For an absolute \(C_0>1\),
\[
 \mathbb E Z\ll_A {1\over m\tau^2}
       +{C_0^m\over\tau}\left(\sqrt d\,E_A+p^{-1/2}\right).             \tag{2}
\]
In particular, for bounded \(d\) and **any** deterministic \(K_p=o(\sqrt{p\log\log p})\), (1) has error \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) for all choices with \(\sum k_C\le K_p\). Take \(m=\lfloor c_A\log\log p\rfloor\) with \(c_A>0\) sufficiently small and choose \(m^{-1/2}\ll\tau\ll\sqrt p/K_p\), with the first inequality in the sense \(\tau\sqrt m\to\infty\) and \(\tau K_p/\sqrt p\to0\). Such a cutoff exists also for bounded or zero \(K_p\), when one may cap it at one.

For a union of bins and interval, let \(H\) be its **intact** price and \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(H)\) if nonzero. Subtract the maximum deleted \(s\)-price from \(|H|\) and take the positive part. This gives the minimum absolute price on its ordinary complementary selections up to \(2/\sqrt p\). If the maximum reaches \(|H|\), successively delete the positive sites of a maximizing configuration until the first crossing. If it does not, that configuration is optimal. Consequently one may use (1) in this test with (2), including on data-chosen bins and intervals. On a macroscopic union on a fixed-gap sequence the intact price has the asymptotic signed value \(\alpha\beta n(b-a)/(8\sqrt p)\) from the short affine theorem, where \(\beta\) is the fraction of bins in the union and \(\sqrt d E_A=o(1)\). These statements determine which side of the original gap a sparse deletion pays.

Here is the nonlinear transfer. The [short-prime two-axis theorem](astra-fiber-91.md#total-variation-on-the-third-quotient) applied to \(V(x,y)=\sum v_{hk}h(x)k(y)\) compares \(p^{-1/2}\sum_{M_C(I)}D_z V(x,y)\) to \(M_0\mathbb E[D V]/\sqrt p\) with summed bin error \(O_{L^1}(\|v\|_1(\sqrt d E_A+p^{-1/2}))\) simultaneous over intervals. Indeed, on the strict minus sector
\(\sum Y k=n r^e_{-k}-1\), and on the strict plus sector \(\sum Xh=n q^e_{-h}-1\). Their sizes are \(n/2,(n-2)/2\), respectively. The strict-sector average of a nontrivial character is \(O(p^{-1/2})\) by the same distinct-root character bound, and the trivial average is one. With the point caps, these identities change the two-axis main term into the stated product main term with cost \(O(\|v\|_1/(d\sqrt p))\) per bin. Absolute mode errors permit an envelope in each bin separately. First take the absolute value for each bin and each fixed Fourier mode, and then sum over bins, modes and the common interval envelopes. The mixed complete columns used here satisfy the distinct-root estimate at nonzero additive frequency. [Lai, Marino, Robinson and Wan, Theorem 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10941333/)

For \(-2\le h\le2, t\ge\tau\) put \(g_{s,t}(h)=(s h-t)_+/h^2\), with value zero at zero. Its supremum is at most \(1/(4\tau)\) and its Lipschitz constant is \(O(\tau^{-2})\). The even function \(g_{s,t}(2\cos\theta)\) has a cosine polynomial approximation of degree \(2m\), uniform error \(O(1/(m\tau^2))\), with all coefficients bounded by \(O(1/\tau)\). For a direct construction convolve with the probability kernel proportional to \((\sin(m\theta/2)/\sin(\theta/2))^4\). Its integral before normalization is comparable to \(m^3\) by integration on \(|\theta|\le1/m\) and the upper \(O(\min(m^4,|\theta|^{-4}))\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\). Its normalized first absolute moment is \(O(1/m)\). Replacing \(\cos(j\theta)\) by the Chebyshev polynomial \(T_j(h/2)\) gives a polynomial \(P_{s,t}(h)\). With \(h=D=Y-X\), the Fourier coefficient norm of \(D/2\) is at most one. The Chebyshev recurrence thus gives norm at most \((1+\sqrt2)^j\) for each \(T_j(D/2)\), with an absolute coefficient envelope independent of \(s,t\). The multiplier \(V=D P_{s,t}(D)\) therefore admits an envelope of total mass \(O(C_0^m/\tau)\).

The polynomial prices have error (2) because the approximation error for each hinge is bounded by \(O(D^2/(m\tau^2))\). Deterministically \(p^{-1/2}\sum_{C,M_C}D_z^2\ll1\) by injectivity of both affine maps and \(\sum_Q X^2+\sum_QY^2\le n(a+b)\). Also \(\sqrt p\,\mathbb E D^2\ll1\). This proves (2) for the sum over bins of the maximum hinge-price error in each bin, for \(\tau\le t\le2\), even with a different threshold in each bin. The maximum empirical deleted \(s\)-price in a bin is exactly the infimum over \(0\le t\le2\) of
\[
 k_C t/\sqrt p+p^{-1/2}\sum_{M_C(I)}(sD_z-t)_+ .
\]
Use its product analogue to obtain the integral in (1). Restricting each infimum to \(t\ge\tau\) increases it by between zero and \(k_C\tau/\sqrt p\), by moving a smaller minimizing threshold to \(\tau\). This proves (1)–(2). The [long-prime quantile theorem](astra-price-92.md#the-signed-difference-law-at-all-retention-fractions-on-longer-prime-probes) covers arbitrary deletion budgets on its longer family.

### Moving the threshold gives a larger deletion budget

The same independent quantile formula (1) holds with error \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) up to **any \(K_p=o(\sqrt p\,\log\log p/\log\log\log p)\)** for bounded \(d\). More generally there is a simultaneous bound, for large integer \(m\),
\[
 O\!\left({\log m\over m}{\sum k_C\over\sqrt p}\right)+Z_m,\qquad
 \mathbb E Z_m\ll_A m^{-2}+m C_1^m(\sqrt d E_A+p^{-1/2}).              \tag{2a}
\]
The upper for the whole stop-loss curve in (2) and the upper for optimized deletions in (2a) use different approximations.

Put \(r=\lceil 10\log m\rceil,\ L=\lfloor m/r\rfloor\) and normalize the nonnegative trigonometric polynomial \(|\sin(L\theta/2)/(L\sin(\theta/2))|^{2r}\) to have integral one. Its degree is at most \(m\). Before normalization its integral is \(\gg1/(L\sqrt r)\), since on \(|\theta|\le1/(L\sqrt r)\) the ratio is at least \(1-O(1/r)\) by the sine expansion. Outside \(|\theta|\le 8\pi/L\) the absolute ratio is at most \(1/8\) on the circle. The normalized outer mass is therefore \(O(L\sqrt r\,8^{-2r})=O(m^{-4})\). Set \(\delta=16\pi/L\). Convolve \(g_{s,t}(2\cos\theta)\) with this kernel to obtain the polynomial \(P_{s,t}(h)\) as above. On the core \(|h-h'|\le\delta\), where \(h,h'\) are the old and new cosine coordinates.

For \(t\ge12\delta\) and \(|h-h'|\le\delta\),
\[
 (s h-t-5\delta)_+\le h^2g_{s,t}(h')\le (s h-t+5\delta)_+ .
\]
If \(s h'\le t\), the left side vanishes. Otherwise write \(x=s h',\ y=s h\), so \(x>t\) and \(0<y/x\le1+\delta/t\). The difference \(y^2(x-t)/x^2-(y-t)\) equals \((y-x)[y/x-t(y+x)/x^2]\) and has modulus at most \(5\delta\). On the outer part of the kernel use \(g_{s,t}\le1/(4t)\) and \((s h-t-5\delta)_+\le h^2/(4t)\). Hence the polynomial price \(h^2 P_{s,t}(h)\) lies between the two shifted hinges with additive error \(O(m^{-4}h^2/t)\). Its multiplier \(D P_{s,t}(D)\) has a common Fourier envelope of norm \(O(C_1^m/t_{\min})=O(m C_1^m)\) for all \(t\ge t_{\min}=12\delta\), including \(t>2\), where the convolution is zero. The squared-price bounds from the previous proof make the total outer-kernel cost \(O(m^{-2})\).

In each bin let \(t_*\in[0,2]\) minimize the product top-budget dual. Test the empirical dual at \(t_*+17\delta\) and use the polynomial at \(t=t_*+12\delta\). The empirical hinge there at \(t_*+17\delta\) is at most the empirical polynomial plus its outer error. Transport the polynomial and then bound it above by the product hinge at \(t_*+7\delta\), hence by that at \(t_*\), plus its outer error. Thresholds above two are admissible in the dual. This proves the upper with extra cost \(17\delta k_C/\sqrt p\); interchanging the two experiments gives the lower. The common polynomial envelope and summed quadratic budgets allow different thresholds and different comparisons in each bin. Taking again \(m=\lfloor c_A\log\log p\rfloor\) proves the claimed range.

## Which diffuse Fourier tests can detect sparse prices?

Assume \(d=o(p)\). Consider any predetermined real two-endpoint test \(P=\sum v_{hk}h(x)k(y)\) with \(\|v\|_1\le1\) and \(\|v\|_2=o(1)\). There exist two **disjoint ordinary** deletion sets, each of size \(o(p)\), whose signed price difference on every interval and adaptive union of third cosets equals
\[
 {\alpha\beta n\over8\sqrt p}\left(\sum_k v_{0k}r^e_{-k}-\sum_hv_{h0}q^e_{-h}\right)
       +O_{L^1}(\sqrt d E_A+d/\sqrt p).                               \tag{3}
\]
Indeed assign a site to the first set with fractional weight \(P_+\), to the second with \((-P)_+\), and otherwise to neither. The full match count weighted by \(|P|^2\), divided by \(p\), is \(O(\|v\|_2^2+p^{-1/2})\) by the deterministic raw-count character estimate with all third cosets and the full interval; \(|P|^2\) has Fourier norm at most one. The first two weights thus have combined counting mass \(o(p)\). In each third bin process sites in additive order. Preserve the count and signed price of each of the first two outcomes while freezing integral assignments. If more than four sites are fractional, the free dimensions after imposing the per-site probability sums exceed the four linear constraints. Move along a kernel direction to eliminate a positive coordinate and continue. Round the final at most four sites within their supports. Any earlier prefix had at most four fractional sites, so its counts and raw signed sums change by \(O(1)\). Interval differences and summation across bins cost \(O(d)\) counts and \(O(d/\sqrt p)\) price. This gives (3) by the two-axis theorem, and keeps the deletion sizes \(o(p)\) when \(d=o(p)\). [Prefix rounding on actual matches](astra-affine-90.md#a-single-sharp-partition-for-all-intervals)

For bounded \(d\), the following criterion is exact for the test class in (3). Along a prime sequence all its responses are \(o(1)\) if and only if for every \(e>0\) the counts of spectral coordinates with \(\sqrt p\,q^e_j\ge e\) and with \(\sqrt p\,r^e_j\ge e\) stay bounded. For sufficiency, bound the small-coordinate contribution by \(2e\); on the bounded sets apply Cauchy–Schwarz using the point caps and \(\|v\|_2=o(1)\). Conversely, on an unbounded-count subsequence take the average of \(K\to\infty\) distinct exterior characters of that row, closed under inversion, all above \(e\). It is real with norms \(1,1/\sqrt K\), and the other axis has no contribution. By (3) at least one of the two ordinary deletions carries a signed price bounded away from zero in modulus on a macroscopic window on most draws. The criterion concerns the signed difference of their prices; orientation of each is a separate question.

A nonnegative test gives the orientation and prices both sides. Orient the score as the **larger-mark endpoint minus the smaller**, write \(h>l\) for the marks and \(w^e\) for the larger row. Choose a probability \(c_j\) on characters with \(\delta=\sum c_j^2=o(1)\), and on that endpoint use \(P=|\sum c_j j|^2\). Define
\[
 W=\sum_{j,k}c_jc_k w^e_{j-k}-l\delta,\qquad \Delta=h-l .
\]
The deletion with weight \(P\) now has oriented price \(\alpha\beta n W/(8\sqrt p)+o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) under \(\sqrt d E_A+d/\sqrt p=o(1)\). Its size is at most \(n\delta+O(d)\) by endpoint injectivity. The complementary price equals \(\alpha\beta n(\Delta-W)/(8\sqrt p)+o_{\mathbb P}(1)\). Attenuation by \(\min(1,\Delta/W)\) when \(W>0\), and zero weight otherwise, gives a near-full ordinary complement of price \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) if \(\sqrt p(\Delta-W)_+\to0\). Both sets refer to the same assignment simultaneously on all the windows. The argument uses the two-axis main term for a one-variable test and prefix rounding. \(P\ge0\) and its Fourier norm equals one. The pointwise inequality \(P\le1\) makes the original assignment admissible.

This directed criterion can equivalently be tested on finite difference tables of growing **sets** \(J\subset\widehat Q\), with \(c\) uniform on \(J\). To see sufficiency of the restriction, sample \(K\to\infty\) indices independently from an arbitrary witnessing \(c\), slowly enough that \(K^2\delta\to0\). All are distinct with probability tending to one. The average off-diagonal kernel \(w^e_{j-k}/h\in[0,1]\) differs from its product mean by \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\): the variance is \(O(1/K)\) since disjoint pairs are independent. The diagonal fraction and both subtracted \(l\delta/h\) terms tend to zero. Thus some set attains the original normalized price within \(o(1)\). In the other direction a uniform set already defines an admissible \(c\). This reduces the test to the density and weights of the differences, beyond a count of large atoms.

## One-sided product tails control every adaptive deletion on every draw

Let \(U=\sqrt p X, V=\sqrt p Y, L=V-U\) under the independent sector experiment and \(\mu=\mathbb E L=O(1)\) by the exact strict means. For **every** affine probe pair, every subset \(S\) of the strict matches of size \(k\), and \(s=\pm1\), one has
\[
 {1\over\sqrt p}\sum_{z\in S}sD_z
 \ \le\ {k\over p}(2T-s\mu)
       +{|D_+|+|D_-|\over p}\,\mathbb E(sL-T)_+,\qquad T\ge0 .         \tag{4}
\]
In fact \(s(V-U)=s(V-\mathbb E U)+s(\mathbb E V-U)-s\mu\). The positive excess above \(T\) of each of the first two terms is at most the corresponding conditional expectation of \((sL-T)_+\) by Jensen. Sum on the deletion using injectivity in each endpoint and extend each nonnegative sum to its entire sector. This proves (4) with the original sector sizes.

In particular, uniform integrability of the **positive part in the gap direction alone** prevents a vanishing-density deletion from reducing the gap by a fixed amount on the true short probes. Two-sided uniform integrability gives vanishing absolute signed price of every such deletion by applying (4) twice. Conversely any sequence of actual \(o(p)\) deletions carrying oriented price at least \(c>0\) forces \(\liminf\mathbb E(sL-T)_+\ge2c\) for every fixed \(T\), since the coefficient in (4) tends to \(1/2\). A growing difference table reaching a fixed fraction of the larger mark in (3)'s directed version therefore forces failure of uniform integrability in the specified direction. Unbounded counts of cap-scale exterior atoms suffice for two-sided failure by the two-set version. Uniform integrability of both scaled strict endpoints is equivalent to that of \(L\): conditional Jensen yields the centered endpoint estimates with uniformly bounded shifts, and the converse follows by addition. No global uniform integrability or its failure on all prime optimal faces follows.

## A prime-power control at the resolved deletion scale

At order \(N=s^2\) for odd rational primes \(s\to\infty\), use the [genuine subfield optimizers](astra-affine-90.md#subfield-obstruction-and-finite-checks). The strict plus row equals one at the \(s-2\) nonidentity subfield points and zero elsewhere. The strict minus row equals \(2/(s+1)\). For nonzero subfield probes all strict affine scores are \(D_z=2/(s+1)>0\), since \(x\) in the subfield would force \(y\) there also. Thus the maximum deleted **negative** price is zero. In the independent product, \((-D)_+\) equals \(1-2/(s+1)\) with probability \((s-2)/((N-5)/4)\) and is otherwise zero. For \(d=1\), the full interval and \(k=\lfloor s/16\rfloor\), (1) predicts \(k(1-2/(s+1))/s\to1/16\), because the product mass of the positive tail is asymptotic to \(s/8\) after multiplication by \(M_0=N/32\). This gives a genuine obstruction already at deletion budget of order \(\sqrt N\) when the probes are confined to a subfield.
