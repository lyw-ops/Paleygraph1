## Findings compared with the local routes

**Common-neighbor weights.** In the first response, ChatGPT identified the Legendre-family trace in the common-neighbor count on adjacent pairs. This identity also appears in [Jig #7](https://jig.so/p/7). In our notation, for $a\in D_+$,

$$|N_H(1)\cap N_H(a)|=\frac{p-15+t(a)}8.$$

Expanding the three square indicators over all $y\in\mathbb F_p$ gives $p-3+t(a)$ before dividing by eight. Excluding $y=0,1,a$ subtracts four at each point. The analogous count for $a\in D_-$ in $\overline H$ is $(p-7+t(a))/8$. The unweighted, quadratic and cubic terms give $p+1+t(a)$, and the three deleted contributions are $0,4,4$. On either allowed support the common-neighbor kernel is therefore affine in $(s*s)(a)=(t(a)-2)/2$. This identifies the constant-plus-triangle-weighted primal family with the `chebyshev_2` row in [the restricted LP experiment](../data/witnesses.csv), after rescaling and changing basis. Its nearly flat performance at the displayed large primes motivates an asymptotic obstruction theorem specific to this family.

**Follow-up proof draft for the triangle weights.** The same Mellin argument as in [the third-update notes](mellin.md) gives joint convergence of $X=\lambda/\sqrt p$ and $Y=KT_2(X)$ to independent variables. The first is the real part of a uniform unit complex number. The second is the average of two independent $SU(2)$ standard traces. Here is the additional group calculation used to identify the supports.

The two feedback objects are $N_2$ and $N_2\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi(a)}$. Each has convolution dimension two, is arithmetically self-dual and is geometrically Lie-irreducible by the previous norm-one and singularity arguments. Thus both separately have geometric and arithmetic group $SL(2)$ by [Katz, Theorem 14.1](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf). In their joint geometric group, both projections are surjective. If its identity component is smaller than the product, the Lie-algebra version of Goursat's lemma says it is a diagonal graph up to conjugacy. The normalizer of a diagonal $SL(2)$ in $SL(2)\times SL(2)$ allows only a central sign in the second factor. The two objects would then differ by a rank-one character of order dividing two in the convolution category. Such a character is punctual, since it is trivial on the identity component (Katz Corollary 6.7). A nonidentity punctual twist translates the unique singular point away from $1$. An identity twist would give isomorphic objects even though their inertia eigenvalues at $0$ differ by a sign. Thus the joint geometric group is the product. Its arithmetic group is contained in the same product by the two symplectic pairings.

Adjoining the Jacobi rank-one object gives the direct product with $\mathbb G_m$ by the central torus argument from the third-update notes. There is no arithmetic enlargement because the joint arithmetic group already embeds in $\mathbb G_m\times SL(2)\times SL(2)$. For a fixed polynomial in the traces, the moment calculation in those notes gives the Haar moment from the multiplicity of $\delta_1$ plus $O(p^{-1/2})$ for the other summands. The bounded range then gives joint weak convergence. In particular for all large primes, one can find a nontrivial frequency arbitrarily close to each of the corner pairs $(X,Y)=(\pm1,\pm2)$.

Write the triangle family as $f=\delta_1+(a/\sqrt p)1_{D_\sigma}+(4b/p)1_{D_\sigma}(s*s)$. At a nontrivial frequency,

$$\widehat f(k)=1+\frac a2(\sigma X_k-p^{-1/2})+b\big(T_2(X_k)+\sigma Y_k+3/p\big).$$

This is the Fourier transform of the $D_\sigma$ mask, using $(s*s)(1)=n-1$. Choose the corner $X=-\sigma\operatorname{sgn}(a)$ and $Y=-2\sigma\operatorname{sgn}(b)$, with either sign for zero coefficients. At the exact corner, the leading nonconstant terms total at most $-|a|/2-|b|$. At an $\epsilon$-nearby frequency the upper bound changes by $O((\epsilon+p^{-1/2})(|a|+|b|))$, uniformly in the coefficients. Feasibility thus bounds the whole parameter set and gives $\limsup(|a|/2+|b|)\le1$. At the trivial frequency the triangle term is $O(1)$ for bounded $b$, using boundedness of $Y_0$ and $X_0=-1/\sqrt p$. Hence the normalized objective is $a/(2\sqrt2)+o(1)$, and the best value tends to $1/\sqrt2$ by the constant witness lower bound. This proof awaits review along with the underlying Mellin calculation.

**Exact $p=17$ benchmark.** ChatGPT proposed the exact values $\theta_+=8-4\sqrt2$, $\theta_-=2+\sqrt2$. We can certify them by Fourier rows. With generator $3^2$ for $Q$, the two offset sets in $\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$ are $D_+=\{\pm1,4\}$ and $D_-=\{\pm2,\pm3\}$. Give the plus row value $2-\sqrt2$ at $\pm1$, value $3-2\sqrt2$ at $4$ and diagonal $1$. Give the minus row value $1/2$ at $\pm2$, value $1/\sqrt2$ at $\pm3$ and diagonal $1$. The Fourier values are

$$1+2(2-\sqrt2)\cos(\pi k/4)+(3-2\sqrt2)(-1)^k,$$
$$1+\cos(\pi k/2)+\sqrt2\cos(3\pi k/4),$$

respectively. Each is nonnegative for $k=0,\ldots,7$. Their trivial entries multiply to $8$, so the complementary identity certifies optimality. The constant-only plus witness gives $1+3/(1+\sqrt2)=3\sqrt2-2<8-4\sqrt2$. Both exact values agree with [the independent LP run](../data/theta-certified.csv).

**Transfer from $G(n,1/2)$.** ChatGPT flagged that the multiplicatively averaged graph matrices are circulant and commute. ## Independent audit of the fourth update

In the follow-up, ChatGPT checked the norm-one-torus sign calculation and pointed out that the even-character restriction also introduces a norm-minus-one sum. For instance, the quadratic Kummer traces of $1+u^2$ and $1-u^4$ have opposite full Frobenius–Schur indicators. On the restricted characters the two cosets contribute at the same scale. The audit gives restricted limits $0$ and $2$ respectively. This rules out a general transfer of the individual sign under the square projection. The [local inertia argument](iteration-parity.md) uses a different input. It excludes odd invariant tensors before projecting, by tracking eigenvalues of local inertia and both Kummer lifts. This later argument was developed separately from the audit and still merits an independent check. [Research chat](https://chatgpt.com/c/6afb49a1-9c94-83ee-9ddf-86c68a7bd124)

The analytic contribution of the audit is an exact hyperbolic dissipation formula for the bulk-model increments. It extends to the full iteration with the marked weight included. For $v^{t+1}=K\Phi(v^t)$ and $\gamma_i(v)=\operatorname{arsinh}(v_i/(2\sqrt{w_i}))$, it reads

$$\|v^{t+1}-v^t\|_2^2-\|v^{t+2}-v^{t+1}\|_2^2
=16\sum_i w_i\sinh^2\frac{\gamma_i(v^{t+1})-\gamma_i(v^t)}2
+\frac1n\left(\sum_i[\Phi_i(v^{t+1})-\Phi_i(v^t)]\right)^2.$$

To check it, use $\|Kz\|_2^2=\|z\|_2^2-\|Jz\|_2^2$ and write $v_i=2\sqrt{w_i}\sinh\gamma_i$, $\Phi_i(v)=2\sqrt{w_i}\cosh\gamma_i$. The same calculation with $v^*$ as the second iterate gives $\|v^t-v^*\|_2^2-\|v^{t+1}-v^*\|_2^2=4D(v^t)+\|J(\Phi(v^t)-\Phi(v^*))\|_2^2$, where $D(v)=2\sum_i w_i(\cosh(\gamma_i(v)-\gamma_i(v^*))-1)$ is the relative discrepancy. If $\|v^*\|_2\le C$, this gives some $t<T$ at which the marked log difference is at most $C/(2\sqrt T)$. The calculation also yields a bound on the sum of the increment dissipations independent of $p$, since $\|v^1\|_2^2\le4W$. Increment dissipation controls movement in the hyperbolic variables. The associated uniform fourth-moment hypothesis in the audit concerns the bulk model and is still open; a residual decay statement for that model needs a separate comparison with the true theta certificate at growing depth.

## Independent check of critical subgroup defects

The follow-up asked whether a self-inverse positional coset $C=cB$ at $|B|=\sqrt p+O(1)$ can have just a bounded number of exceptions to a constant quadratic sign. ChatGPT derived the exact all-lag autocorrelation of the full coset-sum vector and constructed an integer vector satisfying it with fixed deficit $3$. The integer construction survives even when the formal field order is a prime, so the absolute values of all Jacobi coefficients alone cannot settle the question.

ChatGPT also used the product identity $\prod_{a\in cB}(1-a)=1-c^b$ for $cB\ne B$ and a reduction of Jacobi sums modulo $p$ to exclude deficit $3$ at the positive identity block when $p=b^2+1$, $b=2\ell$, and $\ell\ge5$ is odd. The parity relation in that family then gives $F(B)\le b-7$. At $p=37$, $b=6$, direct enumeration gives the exceptional value $F(B)=3$.

The [local proof](coset-defects.md#excluding-every-bounded-deficit) uses the same modular half-interval of zeros and an additional step. Bounded deficit at the square-root bound gives a bounded coset decomposition of the residual even-index sums. Modulo $p$, the nonsquare coset sums are then values of a rational function. Its uncanceled pole contradicts the many repeated integer values forced by the second moment. This gives a growing gap to $\sqrt p$ uniformly over the subgroups and cosets and resolves the arithmetic subproblem posed in the follow-up.
