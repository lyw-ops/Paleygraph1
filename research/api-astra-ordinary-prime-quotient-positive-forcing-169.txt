## Result

**Status: inconclusive for the actual ordinary-prime target, but the proposed positive-cone inference is not valid by itself.**

The exact nonnegative dual from Round 168 is correct for the genuine data:

- the ordinary-prime family and its true least-energy minimizer \(F\);
- the physical mark \(q\) at \(e_0\);
- the true zero set \(U\);
- the minimum-support complementary optimizer \(R\);
- its positive support \(A\subset U\);
- the genuine mark-zero projector
  \[
  P_0=P_\epsilon-\frac{P_\epsilon e_0e_0^*P_\epsilon}{d};
  \]
- and
  \[
  \mathcal W_A=\operatorname{im}P_0\cap\{v:v_A=0\}.
  \]

With the actual affine representative \(F_A=F_*-\sqrt n\,h_A\), one has
\[
F\in F_A+\mathcal W_A,\qquad F_A\perp\mathcal W_A,
\]
and
\[
\|F-F_A\|_2^2=nC_{\rm q}(A).
\]

Moreover, for the genuine active set \(U\setminus A\), the correction
\[
d_A^{\rm phys}=F-F_A
\]
belongs to the dual cone generated by the actual projected columns
\[
v_i=P_{\mathcal W_A}e_i,\qquad i\in U\setminus A.
\]
Thus there is \(\mu_i\ge 0\) with
\[
d_A^{\rm phys}=\sum_{i\in U\setminus A}\mu_i v_i,
\]
and
\[
nC_{\rm q}(A)
 =
\sup_{\lambda\ge0}
\left(
-2\sum_i\lambda_i(F_A)_i
-\left\|\sum_i\lambda_i v_i\right\|_2^2
\right).
\]

For any normalized positive dual \(\pi\),
\[
\pi\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\pi_i=1,\qquad
s=-\langle F_A,\pi\rangle>0,
\]
the exact quotient identity is
\[
C_{\rm q}(A)
 =\frac{s^2}{n\|P_{\mathcal W_A}\pi\|_2^2}.
\]

This is a genuine one-sided formula. It does **not**, however, provide a prime-uniform lower bound for
\[
\|P_{\mathcal W_A}\pi\|_2
\]
in terms of the forcing \(s\).

## Precise failure of the positive-cone inference

Positivity only says that the relevant dual lies in
\[
\operatorname{cone}\{v_i:i\in U\setminus A\}.
\]
It gives no coercivity for the projection of that cone onto \(\mathcal W_A\). In particular, the following finite-dimensional model satisfies the same formal cone geometry.

Take
\[
\mathcal W_A=\mathbb R^2,\qquad v_1=(1,0),
\]
and let
\[
d=L v_1,\qquad (F_A)_1=-L,
\]
with \(L>0\) arbitrary. Then the positive dual \(\pi=\delta_1\) gives
\[
s=-\langle F_A,\pi\rangle=L,\qquad
\|P_{\mathcal W_A}\pi\|_2=\|v_1\|_2=1,
\]
and hence
\[
C_{\rm q}(A)=\frac{L^2}{n}.
\]
The positive-cone hypothesis remains true for arbitrarily large \(L\). Therefore positivity alone cannot imply \(C_{\rm q}(A)=o(T)\), nor can it imply a forcing-to-projected-mass estimate independent of the actual spectral geometry.

The obstruction is not that the displayed finite-dimensional model is an ordinary-prime Jacobi model; it is that **the proposed inference uses no Jacobi-specific input capable of excluding this behavior**. The relaxed positive-interpolant construction in Round 165 gives the same scope-accurate warning: a nonnegative complementary vector, the correct mark scaling, and the correct Gram-level structure do not prevent arbitrarily large centered inverse cost.

## What remains to be proved for the actual prime family

For a fixed-gap ordinary-prime sequence \(p_j\to\infty\), with the actual physical mark \(q_j\), actual \(F_j\), \(R_j\), \(A_j\), and \(P_{0,j}\), one needs a prime-uniform estimate of the form
\[
\|P_{\mathcal W_{A_j}}\pi_j\|_2^2
\gtrsim
\frac{s_j^2}{n_j\,o(T_j)}
\]
for the actual positive duals \(\pi_j\), together with a separate estimate for the positive-support cost
\[
C_+(A_j)
 =
c_{0,A_j}^*G_{0,A_jA_j}^{\dagger}c_{0,A_j}.
\]

Equivalently, one needs both
\[
C_+(A_j)=o(T_j)
\]
and
\[
C_{\rm q}(A_j)=o(T_j)
\]
for an admissible growing window \(T_j\to\infty\), evaluated along the fixed-gap prime family \(p_j\to\infty\). The existing low-rank and Schur estimates only control spectral portions above a cutoff. At cutoff
\[
s_j=T_j^{-1/2},
\]
the established zero-block estimate gives, schematically,
\[
\text{high-spectrum inverse debit}
\ll \frac{D_{B_j}}{s_j}
= D_{B_j}\sqrt{T_j},
\]
where \(D_{B_j}=O_\eta(|B_j|/n_j)\) under the already-assumed small-block condition. This is useful if
\[
D_{B_j}=o(T_j^{-1/2}),
\]
but no fixed-gap argument currently supplies that condition, and the low-spectrum component may still carry the source.

Thus the exact positive dual identifies the remaining obstruction; it does not remove it.

## Separate positive-support cost

The elimination identity is
\[
c_0^*G_0^\dagger c_0
=
\underbrace{c_{0,A}^*G_{0,AA}^\dagger c_{0,A}}_{C_+(A)}
+
\underbrace{
(c'_B)^*
\left(
G_B-G_{0,BA}G_{0,AA}^\dagger G_{0,AB}
\right)^{-1}
c'_B
}_{\text{quotient/zero-block cost}}.
\]

The first term is invariant under changing the complementary basis while \(A\) is fixed. It is not controlled by positivity of the dual on \(U\setminus A\). Hence even a successful one-sided estimate for the quotient correction would leave a separate obligation:
\[
C_+(A)=o(T).
\]

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Actual positive-dual alignment test — highest priority

For the genuine ordinary-prime data along a fixed-gap sequence \(p_j\to\infty\):

1. construct \(F_j\), \(R_j\), \(A_j\), \(F_{A_j}\), and \(P_{\mathcal W_{A_j}}\);
2. compute a minimum-support representation
   \[
   d_{A_j}^{\rm phys}=\sum_i\mu_{j,i}v_{j,i},\qquad \mu_{j,i}\ge0;
   \]
3. normalize \(\pi_j=\mu_j/\|\mu_j\|_1\);
4. measure
   \[
   \rho_j=
   \frac{\bigl(-\langle F_{A_j},\pi_j\rangle\bigr)^2}
        {\|P_{\mathcal W_{A_j}}\pi_j\|_2^2};
   \]
5. test whether
   \[
   \rho_j=o(n_jT_j)
   \]
   for an admissible \(T_j\to\infty\), while independently checking
   \[
   C_+(A_j)=o(T_j).
   \]

This directly tests the missing physical spectral gain and preserves the actual mark, actual support, and actual projected sector.

### 2. Jacobi low-spectrum source-alignment test

Use the actual \(B_j=U'_j\setminus A_j\), its genuine centered Gram block \(G_{B_j}\), and the source \(c_{0,B_j}\). At
\[
s_j=T_j^{-1/2},
\]
decompose
\[
c_{0,B_j}
=
P_{s_j}c_{0,B_j}
+
(I-P_{s_j})c_{0,B_j}.
\]

Test whether the actual source satisfies a prime-uniform low-spectrum bound such as
\[
\langle c_{0,B_j},P_{s_j}G_{B_j}^{\dagger}P_{s_j}c_{0,B_j}\rangle
=o(T_j),
\]
or, after Schur transfer, whether the corresponding low-spectrum contribution to \(d_{A_j}\) is \(o(T_j)\). The existing rank estimate controls the dimension of \(P_{s_j}\), but not the alignment of the genuine source with that subspace. This is precisely the remaining Jacobi-specific issue.

No fixed-gap exclusion or one-match/zero-match spectral gain follows until one of these tests succeeds.
