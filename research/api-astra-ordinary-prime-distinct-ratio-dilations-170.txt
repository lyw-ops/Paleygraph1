## Verdict

**Inconclusive.** The distinct-\(z\) target
\[
\mathcal E_{\neq}
:=\sum_{s\neq 1}\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
w(t)\sum_{\substack{z,z'\in Q\\z\neq z'}}
M_z(s)M_{z'}(s)\,R_z(s,t)R_{z'}(s,t)
\stackrel{?}{=}O(p^{5/2})
\]
is not obtained from the available row-norm bounds. Here \(p\) runs through the same ordinary-prime family, \(v\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) is the actual physical mark, \(Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2\), \(g=|f|^2\) is the actual physical weight extended by zero, \(w=g(1-g)\), and
\[
M_z(s)=1_{\{\lambda_z(s)\in Q\}},
\qquad
\lambda_z(s)=\frac{1-z}{1-sz}.
\]
All sums are counting sums for each fixed \(p\), with the constants required uniformly as \(p\to\infty\).

The exact same-\(z\) diagonal is at the target scale \(O(p^{5/2})\). The remaining distinct-\(z\) term has a valid general upper bound \(O(p^3)\), leaving precisely one factor \(p^{1/2}\) to recover.

## 1. Exact physical row

For the \(\alpha\)-slot, retain the actual marked coordinates
\[
A=1-vx,\quad C=1-vux,\quad
Y=1-vy,\quad D=1-vuy,
\qquad
s=\frac{DA}{CY}.
\]
Set
\[
z=\frac CD=\frac{A}{sY}.
\]
After paying the already identified \(x=0\), \(y=0\), and \(szY=1\) boundary terms, the row at fixed \(z\) is a nonnegative reciprocal-dilation correlation:
\[
R_z(s,t)
=
\sum_Y
g(Y)g(tzY)\,g(D)g(zD),
\]
where \(D\) is determined by
\[
\frac{1-D}{D}
=
\lambda_z(s)\frac{1-Y}{Y}.
\]
The actual square condition is exactly
\[
u\in Q
\iff
\lambda_z(s)\in Q.
\]
Thus the physical kernel is, up to the priced boundary terms,
\[
H_\alpha(s,t)=\sum_{z\in Q}M_z(s)R_z(s,t).
\]
The reciprocal slot and the second slot in the same sign are obtained by the stated physical involutions; they only introduce an absolute constant factor. The two signs are squared separately in the positive criterion.

Define
\[
G=\sum_x g(x)\ll p^{1/2},
\qquad
L_a=\sum_D g(D)g(aD).
\]
Then
\[
0\le L_a\le G,\qquad \sum_a L_a=G^2.
\]

## 2. The same-\(z\) diagonal is proved

For fixed \(t,z\), multiplicative Young gives
\[
\sum_s R_z(s,t)^2
\ll L_{tz}^{\,2}L_z.
\]
The square mask only decreases this. Therefore
\[
\sum_z\sum_s M_z(s)R_z(s,t)^2
\ll
\sum_z L_{tz}^{\,2}L_z
\le
G^2\sum_zL_z
=
G^4.
\]
After the genuine outer weight,
\[
\sum_t w(t)\sum_z\sum_s M_z(s)R_z(s,t)^2
\ll
\Bigl(\sum_t w(t)\Bigr)G^4
\ll G^5
\ll p^{5/2}.
\]
This confirms the diagonal estimate in the twisted physical criterion.

## 3. What the distinct-\(z\) norm bound actually gives

For the off-diagonal piece, Cauchy in \(s\) and then in \(z\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_s\sum_{z\neq z'}M_z(s)M_{z'}(s)R_z(s,t)R_{z'}(s,t)
&\le
\left(\sum_z\Bigl(\sum_sR_z(s,t)^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\right)^2\\
&\ll
\left(\sum_z L_{tz}\sqrt{L_z}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using \(L_{tz}\le G\), \(\sum_zL_{tz}=G^2\), and \(\sum_zL_z=G^2\),
\[
\left(\sum_z L_{tz}\sqrt{L_z}\right)^2
\le
\left(\sum_z L_{tz}\right)
\left(\sum_z L_{tz}L_z\right)
\le
G^2\cdot G^3
=
G^5.
\]
Consequently the currently available bound is
\[
\mathcal E_{\neq}
\ll
\sum_t w(t)\,G^5
\ll
G^6
\ll p^3.
\]

This is the exact quantitative gap: the target is \(G^5\asymp p^{5/2}\) in the ordinary scale, while the unconditional row-norm argument costs \(G^6\asymp p^3\).

The actual \(w(t)=g(t)(1-g(t))\), rather than merely \(|w(t)|\le1\), has not yet been converted into the missing \(G^{-1}\) gain.

## 4. The two square masks do not by themselves give a power saving

For \(z\neq z'\), put
\[
q=\frac1{\lambda_z(s)}.
\]
The common-slope relation is
\[
q'
=
\frac1{\lambda_{z'}(s)}
=
1+c_{z,z'}(q-1),
\qquad
c_{z,z'}
=
\frac{z'(1-z)}{z(1-z')}.
\]
For the genuine interior rows, \(c_{z,z'}\neq0,1\). The two square masks become
\[
1_Q(q)\,1_Q(q').
\]

Let \(\eta\) be the quadratic character. On nonzero arguments,
\[
1_Q(x)=\frac{1+\eta(x)}2.
\]
The relevant quadratic-character correlation is
\[
\sum_q \eta(q)\eta(1+c(q-1))=O(1)
\qquad(c\neq0,1),
\]
a direct Jacobi/quadratic-polynomial calculation. Hence, after omitting only \(O(1)\) exceptional \(q\)-values,
\[
\#\{q:q\in Q,\ 1+c(q-1)\in Q\}
=
\frac p4+O(1).
\]

Thus the two masks have a strong **centered** correlation estimate, but their positive product retains a \(p/4\) main term. The relation \(c_{z,z'}\neq1\) therefore does **not** by itself produce a power saving for the positive collision. A true weighted estimate for the row profiles is required.

## Remaining gap

The missing statement can be isolated in either of the following forms.

### Unmasked sufficient estimate

It would suffice to prove, for the actual \(g,w\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_t w(t)
\left(\sum_z L_{tz}\sqrt{L_z}\right)^2
\ll p^{5/2}.
}
\tag{A}
\]
This estimate discards both square masks, so it is stronger than needed but is a precise smaller arithmetic target. The presently available information only gives \(O(p^3)\).

### Centered-mask estimate

Alternatively, write
\[
1_Q(q)=\frac12+\rho(q),
\qquad
\rho(q)=1_Q(q)-\frac12.
\]
The \(1/4\) term is controlled by an unmasked estimate such as (A). The remaining terms require a weighted affine-Jacobi estimate for the actual row profiles
\[
K_{z,t}(q):=R_z(s(q),t).
\]
For \(q'=1+c_{z,z'}(q-1)\), one needs bounds at total scale \(O(p^{5/2})\) for the three quantities
\[
\sum_{t}w(t)\sum_{z\neq z'}
\left|
\sum_q \rho(q)K_{z,t}(q)K_{z',t}(q')
\right|,
\]
\[
\sum_{t}w(t)\sum_{z\neq z'}
\left|
\sum_q \rho(q')K_{z,t}(q)K_{z',t}(q')
\right|,
\]
and
\[
\sum_{t}w(t)\sum_{z\neq z'}
\left|
\sum_q \rho(q)\rho(q')K_{z,t}(q)K_{z',t}(q')
\right|.
\]
The unweighted Jacobi bound alone does not imply these weighted estimates.

The zero-match \(4+4\) class remains separate and is not addressed here.

## Ranked next tests

1. **First priority: test the weighted \(L_a\)-correlation estimate (A).**  
   For the actual ordinary-prime family and actual \(g_p,w_p\), evaluate or prove
   \[
   \sum_t w_p(t)
   \left(\sum_z L_{p,tz}\sqrt{L_{p,z}}\right)^2
   \ll p^{5/2}.
   \]
   This is the shortest route to the target because it would bound the distinct-\(z\) term even after deleting both square masks. If it fails at scale \(p^3\), the proof must use genuine mask cancellation.

2. **Second priority: prove a weighted centered affine-Jacobi estimate.**  
   Keep
   \[
   q' =1+c_{z,z'}(q-1)
   \]
   and test whether the actual reciprocal-dilation profiles \(K_{z,t}\) have enough multiplicative Fourier dispersion for the centered expressions above to be \(O(p^{5/2})\). The finite-field mask identity gives \(O(1)\) only for constant weights; the decisive issue is whether the actual \(g(Y)g(tzY)g(D)g(zD)\) profiles are sufficiently nonconcentrated.

No finite computation, nor failure of (A) for an artificial \(g\), would settle the original asymptotic question unless \(g\) is the actual \(g_p=|f_p|^2\) from the specified physical family.
