# Compression gaps for sets of bounded doubling through density-one primes

Let $p\equiv1\pmod4$ be prime, $Q$ the nonzero squares, $n=|Q|$, and $m_0=n/\sqrt p$. Use the Paley multiplier $K$ on $\widehat Q$. There is a relative-density-one set of these primes on which the following holds. For every fixed $0<c<C$ and $D$, the compressions $K_{SS}$ have a uniform norm gap below one for sufficiently large $p$ whenever

$$c m_0\le |S|\le C m_0,\qquad |S+S|\le D|S|.$$

The same statement holds with $S-S$ in place of $S+S$, by the Ruzsa sum–difference inequality. This excludes bounded-doubling support shapes for a critical slow mode on these primes. The exact smoothing solution still needs a support theorem to apply it.

## The only bounded-doubling exception at this scale

In an arbitrary sequence of eligible field orders $q$, a sequence of compressions of norm tending to one at this scale, with bounded doubling, must have a cover by a bounded number of cosets of a frequency subgroup of order comparable to $m_0$. Here both the bound on the number of cosets and the size-comparison constants are uniform along the sequence.

If no uniform cover bound existed, one could first take a subsequence on which the $j$th term has no cover by at most $j$ cosets of any group of order in $[m_0/j,jm_0]$. The proof below would find a further subsequence on which a cover has fixed constants, a contradiction.

By the proper form of the Green–Ruzsa theorem, a bounded-doubling set lies in a translate of a proper coset progression $I=H+P$ with $|I|\ll_{c,C,D} m_0$ and bounded rank. The quotient progression has an injective box parameterization $\sum_{j=1}^d k_j d_j$ modulo $H$, with $0\le k_j<M_j$. Write $M=\prod_jM_j$. Use a subsequence of fixed rank on which each side length is fixed or grows. Call the growing coordinate set $U$ and its complement $D_0$. Put $M_U=\prod_{j\in U}M_j$ and let $\ell_U$ be the order of the subgroup generated by the growing steps in $\widehat Q/H$. If $\ell_U/M_U$ stays bounded, $H$ together with those steps has order $|H|\ell_U\asymp m_0$. The bounded side lengths give only a bounded number of its cosets in $I$. It suffices to show that $\ell_U/M_U\to\infty$ precludes the near-unit compression. The proof uses the box argument with both bounded and growing sides. [Tao, Theorem A.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.3535) · [Earlier progression argument](progression-boxes.md)

Translations by $H$ preserve $I$ and commute with the compression, so diagonalize them first. In each block, corresponding to a positional coset $C_0$ of $B=H^\perp$, the quadratic form for a unit box vector is

$$\frac1b\sum_{x\in C_0}s(x)|Y(\Theta(x))|^2,
\qquad b=|B|=n/|H|,
\qquad Y(\theta)=\sum_{\mathbf k}y_{\mathbf k}e^{2\pi i\mathbf k\cdot\theta},$$

where $e^{2\pi i\Theta_j(x)}=d_j(x)$ for chosen lifts of the steps. This follows by Fourier transforming separately in the $H$ variable on each full coset of the box. Each block entry is a twisted coset sum divided by $b$. Averaging over the characters in $H$ and using the full Jacobi bound gives

$$\left|\frac1b\sum_{x\in C_0}s(x)\eta(x)\right|\le\frac{\sqrt q}{b}$$

for every character $\eta$ of $Q$. Also $M\asymp b/\sqrt q$ by the size of $I$.

Suppose some block has Rayleigh quotient tending to $\sigma=\pm1$. The box autocorrelation $r_y$ is taken in $\widehat Q/H$. If the positional coset is translated from $B$, incorporate the corresponding phase factors into the box coefficients when forming the autocorrelation. Its support has at most $2^d M$ entries. The entry bound implies $\sum|r_y|\gtrsim M$, hence $\sum|r_y|^2\gtrsim M$. Orthogonality in the distinct quotient frequencies gives the second and fourth norms of $Y$ on $C_0$ as $1$ and $\sum|r_y|^2$, respectively, with normalized counting measure and the appropriate powers. There is therefore an $x_0\in C_0$ with $|Y(\Theta(x_0))|\gtrsim\sqrt M$. As in the [box proof](progression-boxes.md#a-peak-and-its-rescaled-relation-lattice), a subsequence of the entire functions

$$M^{-1/2}Y(\Theta(x_0)+(z_j/M_j)_j)$$

converges locally uniformly to an entire function $F$ nonzero at zero.

The scaled relation lattices

$$\Lambda_q=\{(k_j/M_j)_j:\sum k_j d_j\in H\}$$

are separated in sup norm by at least one, by properness. After a further locally convergent subsequence their limit is a discrete subgroup $\Lambda$. Set $W=\operatorname{span}\Lambda$ and $V=W^\perp$. The intersection of $\Lambda_q$ with the growing-coordinate subspace has covolume $\ell_U/M_U$. Its limit cannot contain a full basis in that subspace when this covolume tends to infinity. More explicitly, the coordinates in $D_0$ of relation vectors lie in fixed discrete lattices. Every limiting vector of $\Lambda$ with zeros in $D_0$ therefore comes from vectors with zeros there eventually. Projection of $\Lambda$ onto $D_0$ has discrete image, so the span of its kernel is $W\cap\mathbb R^U$. If $W$ contained all of $\mathbb R^U$, one could approximate a full basis of that kernel by bounded lattice vectors in $\Lambda_q\cap\mathbb R^U$, contradicting their divergent covolume. Thus some unit vector $v\in V$ has $v_U\ne0$.

## Sampling in a growing direction

Form the scaled positional measure $\mu_q$ by giving mass $M/b$ to each rescaled point of $C_0$ and including the period translates. Subtracting $\Theta(x_0)$ with $x_0\in C_0$ turns the sampling into averaging over $B$. For a compact smooth test $g$, Poisson summation gives the limit $\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\widehat g(\lambda)$ of $\int g\,d\mu_q$. Equivalently the limiting measure is a positive constant times the sum of Lebesgue measures on $V+w$ for $w$ in the dual of $\Lambda$ inside $W$. This also covers $W=\{0\}$. Separation gives dominated convergence in the Poisson sums.

Let $\omega_q$ weight the points by $s(x)$. The Rayleigh deficit implies that every local compact integral of $|F|^2$ against $\mu_q-\sigma\omega_q$ tends to zero, using the locally uniform entire convergence. There are only a bounded number of period lifts of each point in a fixed compact set because all periods are at least one. For a smooth test in a tube supported close enough to the central plane $V$ to miss the other planes, it follows that $\sigma\int g\,d\omega_q\to\int g\,d\mu$. Indeed $F$ is real analytic and nonzero at zero on $V$, so its zeros have measure zero there. A continuous amplitude cutoff makes the transfer valid before removing the cutoff.

Take the tube along the chosen $v\in V$, with test $g_T(z)=h_T(v\cdot z)\psi(\pi_{v^\perp}z)$. Here $h_T$ is the indicator of $[-T,T]$ smoothed by a fixed smooth positive probability, and $\psi$ is a fixed nonnegative transverse bump equal to one near zero, chosen small enough to miss the other planes. The limiting $\mu$ integral is a positive constant times $T$. On the other hand the twisted coset bound gives

$$\left|\int g_T\,d\omega_q\right|\le\frac{\sqrt q}{b}\sum_{\mathbf k}|\widehat g_T((k_j/M_j)_j)|.$$

After division by $M$, with $T$ fixed, the sum converges to the mixed integral over continuous $U$ coordinates and the fixed lattice of $D_0$ coordinates, times the constant $1/\prod_{j\in D_0}M_j$. This is a Riemann-sum limit on the growing coordinates with a summable Schwartz bound on the bounded ones. The mixed integral is $O(1+\log T)$. To prove it, at each fixed lattice value $\xi_{D_0}$ change variables in $\xi_U$ to $t=v\cdot\xi$ and the coordinates $\eta$ in $\mathbb R^U\cap v^\perp$, with constant Jacobian since $v_U\ne0$. The transverse projection $\xi-tv$ has $D_0$ component $\xi_{D_0}-t v_{D_0}$ and $\eta$ component $\eta$. Thus the integral and lattice sum of $|\widehat\psi(\xi-tv)|$ at fixed $t$ are bounded uniformly in $t$ by Schwartz decay. Finally $\int |\widehat h_T(t)|\,dt=O(1+\log T)$. Since $M\sqrt q/b$ is bounded, the Fourier estimate contradicts the limit proportional to $T$. This proves the structural exception, including boxes with bounded sides.

## Removing the exception on density-one primes

For each fixed $L>1$, the primes with a divisor of $p-1$ in $[L^{-1}\sqrt p,L\sqrt p]$ have relative density zero. On $X/2<p\le X$, take the smaller of the divisor and its complement in $p-1$. It belongs to $[c_L\sqrt X,\sqrt X]$, covered by $O_L(1)$ dyadic intervals below $\sqrt X$. Ford's shifted-prime upper bound, Theorem 6 with shift $-1$, and the dyadic divisor estimate in Theorem 1 give at most

$$O_L\left(\frac{X}{\log X\,(\log X)^\delta(\log\log X)^{3/2}}\right),\qquad
\delta=1-\frac{1+\log\log 2}{\log 2}>0$$

such primes. Summing dyadically and using the prime number theorem in the progression $1\pmod4$ gives the relative-density-zero assertion there as well. A diagonal choice of increasing cutoffs gives a single relative-density-one set of primes avoiding every fixed comparison range eventually. For example, avoid the range with $L=j$ above a cutoff $X_j$ chosen so its exceptional density bound up to every $x\ge X_j$ is at most $1/j$. For $X_j\le x<X_{j+1}$ every excluded prime up to $x$ is counted by the range with $L=j$. [Ford, Theorems 1 and 6](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v168-n2-p01.pdf)

The critical subgroup in the structural exception would give a divisor of $n$ of order $\sqrt p$, impossible along this prime set. By contradiction this proves the asserted uniform gap. In particular, a critical-scale near-null direction on these primes can have no cover of comparable size with bounded doubling. If its high set satisfies the hypotheses of the [signed stationary comparison](signed-coset-bands.md#transfer-from-one-sign-of-the-diagonal), the gap applies to the corresponding one-sided band. The argument does not turn a doubling bound on a positive-density subset into a cover of the whole support. It does not rule out supports with growing doubling or resolve the theta limit.

The subgroup block identity was independently checked by dense Fourier matrices at four small group–subgroup pairs, with discrepancies below $6\times10^{-15}$. The spectral-gap limit uses the Poisson and divisor arguments above.

## Mass outside the structured set for an actual slow mode

The compression gap can also be applied to a direction of the full stationary linearization without assuming that the whole high set is structured. This form allows both signs of the diagonal on the candidate support.

Let $P=I-J$, $P_\sigma=(P+\sigma K)/2$ and $H=K-PDP$ on the mean-zero space, where $D$ is any real diagonal with $\|D\|\le1$. For a mean-zero unit vector $y$ with $\|Hy\|\le t\le1$, put $y_\sigma=(I+\sigma D)y/2$. Since $PDy=Ky-Hy$ and $K$ is an isometry on this space,

$$\|PDy\|\ge1-t,\qquad \|(K-D)y\|^2=\|Hy\|^2+\|JDy\|^2\le 2t.$$

In particular $\|y_\sigma-P_\sigma y\|\le\sqrt{t/2}$. For any frequency set $S_\sigma$ with $\lambda_{\max}(\sigma K_{S_\sigma S_\sigma})\le1-\delta_\sigma$, projecting onto its coordinates gives

$$\|\mathbf1_{S_\sigma}P_\sigma\|^2
=\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{I_{S_\sigma}-J_{S_\sigma S_\sigma}+\sigma K_{S_\sigma S_\sigma}}{2}\right)
\le 1-\delta_\sigma/2.$$

Hence the following estimate holds separately for each sign, with distinct sets allowed:

$$\|\mathbf1_{S_\sigma^c}y_\sigma\|\ge
\sqrt{\delta_\sigma/2}\,\|P_\sigma y\|-\sqrt{t/2}.$$

If $S_+=S_-=S$ and both gaps are at least $\delta$, the combined norm of the two exterior components is at least $(\sqrt{\delta/2}-\sqrt t)_+$ by the triangle inequality in the direct sum. On the exterior the sum of their squared norms is $\tfrac12\sum_{i\notin S}(1+D_i^2)|y_i|^2\le\|\mathbf1_{S^c}y\|^2$. Thus any critical-scale bounded-doubling $S$ on the density-one prime set leaves a positive fraction of the squared norm of every sufficiently slow direction outside $S$, uniformly in the stationary weight. The estimate applies to linear combinations in the entire spectral window $[-t,t]$.

It is stable under adding $o(m_0)$ frequencies to $S$. The difference between the extended compression and the compression on $S$ padded by zeros has Frobenius norm at most $\sqrt{2|S||E|+|E|^2}/m_0$ for added set $E$, from the entrywise Jacobi bound. The transfer excludes near-total concentration in a structured cover. It places no bound on the exterior contribution to the marked inverse when that contribution is spread across a larger set.

A dense numerical check used stationary diagonals at three weights for each of four prime and three square orders up to $401$. The signed inequality held in all 738 checks on the smallest absolute eigenvalues, with positive lower bounds in 273 checks using intervals, random sets, or the largest signed diagonal coordinates.
