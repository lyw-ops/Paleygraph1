## Finding: fixed-depth arithmetic does not decide the zero-floor defect at \(M=10\)

Let \(U_{p,M}\) be a genuine zero-floor capped fixed point and
\[
\alpha_{p,M}
=\|h_{p,M}\|_{2,n}^{2}+2M\mathbb E_n|h_{p,M}|,
\qquad h_{p,M}=U_{p,M}-C_M(U_{p,M}).
\]
At zero floor, the exact norm identity gives, up to the vanishing marked-floor correction,
\[
\alpha_{p,M}=4-\mu_{p,M}^{\,2}+o_p(1),
\qquad
\mu_{p,M}=\mathbb E_n\Phi_0(C_M(U_{p,M})).
\tag{A}
\]
Hence \(\alpha_{p,M}\to0\) is equivalent to the bulk activation mean tending to \(2\). The fixed-depth parity argument only controls the marked coordinate; it does not imply this mean convergence.

### A checked fixed-depth calculation

For the genuine averaged iteration
\[
W_{j+1}=\frac12\bigl(W_j+K\Phi_0(C_M(W_j))\bigr),\qquad W_0=0,
\]
the first iterate is
\[
W_1=\frac12K a_0
     =\sqrt n\,K e_0+o_{L^2}(1)
     =:x+o_{L^2}(1),
\]
because the zero-floor activation at \(W_0\) is supported at the mark and equals \(2\sqrt n+O_M(n^{-1/2})\) there.

Quadratic Jacobi equidistribution gives the prime-uniform limiting law
\[
x_i\Longrightarrow \sqrt2\cos\theta,\qquad \theta\sim {\rm Unif}[0,2\pi].
\]
Therefore, for every fixed \(M>0\),
\[
\lim_{p\to\infty}\mu_{1,M}(p)
=
I_1(M)
:=
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\min(\sqrt2|\cos\theta|,M)\,d\theta.
\tag{B}
\]
In particular, for \(M\ge\sqrt2\),
\[
I_1(M)=\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi}\approx0.900316,
\tag{C}
\]
far below the target value \(2\). At \(M=10\), clipping is absent at this first bulk step, but the mean is still \(2\sqrt2/\pi\), not \(2\).

This is consistent with the averaged norm identity: the constant \(4\) comes from the squared marked activation, whereas the normalized activation mean \(\mu_j\) receives only \(O(n^{-1/2})\) directly from the mark. Thus fixed-depth marked cancellation does **not** supply the missing \(\mu_j\to2\) estimate.

### Why the available averaged comparison stops here

For the fixed point \(U_{p,M}\), the exact midpoint comparison yields
\[
\frac1J\sum_{j<J}D(W_j,U_{p,M})
\le \frac{2(M^2+4)}{J},
\tag{D}
\]
where \(D\) controls the bulk \(L^2\)-distance of \(h(W_j)\) from \(h(U_{p,M})\), as well as the mean and residual terms.

Consequently, after a routine Cauchy–Schwarz estimate, one obtains
\[
\left|
\alpha_{p,M}
-\frac1J\sum_{j<J}\alpha(W_j)
\right|
\le C_M J^{-1/2}+o_p(1)
\tag{E}
\]
for an explicit \(C_M=O(M^2)\). Combining (A), (E), and the averaged norm identity gives the useful reduction
\[
\limsup_{p\to\infty}\alpha_{p,M}
\le
4-\liminf_{J\to\infty}\liminf_{p\to\infty}
\frac1J\sum_{j<J}\mu_j(p)^2.
\tag{F}
\]
Thus the unweighted zero-floor defect would vanish if one could prove the prime-first, growing-depth estimate
\[
\lim_{J\to\infty}\liminf_{p\to\infty}
\frac1J\sum_{j<J}\mu_j(p)^2=4.
\tag{G}
\]
The fixed-depth trace theorem gives no such conclusion: (B) already shows that the first fixed-depth mean is \(0.9003\) at \(M=10\), and its constants are not uniform in \(j\). Passing from fixed \(j\) to \(J\to\infty\) requires a genuine depth-uniform estimate on the clipped iterates.

### No obstruction at \(M=10\) from the current seed bound

The seed-correlation lower bound (4) only obstructs \(2\le M\le9/4\). At \(M=10\), its first term is too large to force a positive \(\liminf\alpha_{p,M}\). Likewise, cap monotonicity gives
\[
\|h_{p,M}\|_{1,n}\le \|h_{p,N}\|_{1,n}\qquad(M>N),
\]
but supplies no upper bound tending to zero at any finite large cap. Therefore the present material neither proves nor refutes
\[
\alpha_{p,10}\longrightarrow0.
\]

The precise remaining gap is (G), or a substitute estimate controlling the growth of the averaged zero-floor orbit uniformly enough in depth before taking the prime limit. This is genuinely separate from the optimizer-weighted tail estimate.

## Ranked next tests

1. **Depth-uniform mean test (highest priority).** Prove, for \(M=10\),
   \[
   \lim_{J\to\infty}\limsup_{p\to\infty}
   \left(2-\frac1J\sum_{j<J}\mu_j(p)\right)=0,
   \]
   together with a uniform bound on \(\frac1J\sum_{j<J}\mu_j(p)^2-\bigl(\frac1J\sum\mu_j(p)\bigr)^2\). This would imply (G) and hence unweighted defect vanishing via (F).

2. **Rigorous fixed-depth progression to \(j=2,3,\ldots\).** Compute the limiting clipped-field laws and \(\mu_j(M)\) explicitly or with certified interval bounds. This will not settle the asymptotic target by itself, but it can identify whether the means exhibit a monotone drift toward \(2\), or whether a persistent finite-depth deficit/correlation is emerging.
