## Result: no new prime estimate; the first missing step is support-sensitive

I worked with the actual prime family
\[
p\equiv1\pmod 4,\qquad n=(p-1)/2,
\]
the normalized inner product
\[
\langle f,g\rangle_n=\frac1n\sum_i f_i g_i,
\]
and the physical marked coordinate \(i=0\). The bulk is \(i\neq0\). Fix the zero-floor ordinary complementary pair at a fixed marked cap \(M\):
\[
Y=Kb,\qquad b_i=|Y_i|\quad(i\neq0),
\]
with \(b_0=\sqrt{4n+C_M(Y_0)^2}\), and
\[
\nu=\mathbb E_n b,\qquad
F_\pm=\frac{b\pm Y}{\nu}.
\]
Thus \(F_\pm\ge0\), \(\mathbb E_nF_\pm=1\), and the two factors have complementary bulk support.

Let
\[
S_{\rm Jac}=2\sqrt n\,Ke_0.
\]
The kernel bound gives
\[
\|S_{\rm Jac}\|_\infty\le 2\sqrt{p/n}=2\sqrt2+O(p^{-1}).
\]

### 1. The exact signed seed identity

Since \(Y=Kb\) and \(K=K^*\),
\[
\langle b,S_{\rm Jac}\rangle_n
 =2\sqrt n\,\langle b,Ke_0\rangle_n
 =\frac{2\sqrt n}{n}Y_0.
\]
Writing \(Y_0=\eta b_0\), with
\[
b_0=2\sqrt n+O_M(n^{-1/2}),
\]
gives
\[
\langle b,S_{\rm Jac}\rangle_n
=4\eta+O_M(n^{-1}).
\]
Removing the marked summand yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_n\!\left[1_{\{i\ne0\}}b_i(S_{\rm Jac})_i\right]
=4\eta+O_M(n^{-1}).
}
\tag{1}
\]
This is an exact reduction of the ordinary theta question to the actual signed bulk pairing. It uses the genuine ordinary optimizer \(b=|Y|\), not a surrogate.

For a fixed cutoff \(L\), define
\[
b_i^{[L]}=\min(b_i,L),\qquad
T_{p,L}
=\mathbb E_n\!\left[1_{\{i\ne0\}}(b_i-L)_+(S_{\rm Jac})_i\right].
\]
Then
\[
4\eta+O_M(n^{-1})
=
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}b_i^{[L]}(S_{\rm Jac})_i]
+T_{p,L}.
\tag{2}
\]

### 2. What prime trace estimates actually control

For every fixed bounded even polynomial \(Q_p\) in a fixed-complexity collection of negative Mellin fields, the existing prime trace estimate gives
\[
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}Q_p(i)(S_{\rm Jac})_i]
=O_Q(p^{-1/2}).
\tag{3}
\]
The parity is correct: \(Q_pS_{\rm Jac}\) has odd total arity, including exceptional frequencies, and omitting the mark costs \(O_Q(n^{-1})\).

However, \(b^{[L]}=\min(|Y|,L)\) is the clip of the **actual ordinary row**. No fixed-complexity negative-field expression approximating \(b^{[L]}\) in bulk \(L^1\) or \(L^2\) is presently available. Therefore (3) cannot be applied to the first term on the right of (2).

This is the first missing support step.

### 3. The unconditional tail bound is nonvanishing

The actual solution satisfies
\[
0\le b_i\le 2\sqrt p
\]
on the bulk, because the bulk cap is inactive and
\[
|Y_i|=|(Kb)_i|\le \sqrt p\,\nu\le2\sqrt p.
\]
Consequently,
\[
|T_{p,L}|
\le \|S_{\rm Jac}\|_\infty\,
   \mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}(b_i-L)_+]
\le 2\sqrt{p/n}\,\nu
\le 4\sqrt{p/n}.
\tag{4}
\]
Hence
\[
\limsup_{p\to\infty}|T_{p,L}|\le4\sqrt2
\]
uniformly in fixed \(L\). This does not tend to zero as \(L\to\infty\) in the required prime-first order
\[
\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{p\to\infty}.
\]

The quadratic identity gives the sharper formal statement
\[
H_{p,L}
=\frac2\nu\,
 \mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}b_i(b_i-L)_+],
\]
but no uniform-integrability estimate for the actual \(b_i^2\) is known. Thus neither the absolute tail nor the signed tail is controlled by the available arithmetic input.

The cancellation in \(T_{p,L}\) could in principle make the signed tail small even when \(H_{p,L}\) is large, but no prime trace argument currently detects that cancellation because the sign pattern of \(S_{\rm Jac}\) is being tested against the nonlinear support profile \((b-L)_+\).

### 4. Consequence for the bounded-window parity route

A bounded-window argument would need, for fixed \(L\), a prime-uniform approximation such as
\[
\inf_{Q\in\mathcal P_{\rm even}^{\rm fixed}}
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}|b_i^{[L]}-Q_i|]
\longrightarrow0
\]
or an analogous normalized \(L^2\) estimate, with the polynomial complexity fixed before \(p\to\infty\). Then (3) would imply that the clipped pairing is \(o(1)\), and one would only need the signed tail \(T_{p,L}\).

Neither approximation is presently proved for the actual ordinary optimizer. Fixed-depth approximations to capped iterates do not imply such an approximation for the actual fixed point \(Y=Kb\). In particular, the argument cannot replace \(b\) by a bounded negative-field polynomial without an additional support theorem.

## Status

**Inconclusive, with a precise failure.** The actual complementary ordinary factors give the exact identity (1), but the existing prime trace estimates stop at fixed-complexity polynomial expressions. The first unavailable implication is
\[
\text{fixed-expression trace control}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{trace control of }\min(|Y|,L)
\]
for the actual optimizer.

No genuine signed-tail estimate, ordinary bounded-window parity theorem, or square-tail theorem has been established here. The result does not refute the asymptotic theta target.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Actual weighted signed-tail test — highest priority

For fixed \(M,L\), attempt to prove directly
\[
\limsup_{p\to\infty}
\left|
\mathbb E_n\!\left[
1_{\{i\ne0\}}(b_i-L)_+(S_{\rm Jac})_i
\right]
\right|
\le \varepsilon(L),
\qquad
\varepsilon(L)\to0.
\tag{5}
\]
This is the weakest arithmetic statement that closes the signed route through (2), and it allows cancellation unavailable to absolute square-tail estimates. The order must be
\[
p\to\infty\quad\text{for fixed }M,L,
\qquad\text{then }L\to\infty.
\]
A useful first subtest is a weighted pairing estimate with the actual sector factors:
\[
\mathbb E_n\!\left[
1_{\{i\ne0\}}(b_i-L)_+
(F_{+,i}-F_{-,i})
\right],
\]
since \(S_{\rm Jac}\) is the marked Jacobi seed corresponding to the same antisymmetric sector.

### 2. Fixed-window support approximation — second priority

For every fixed \(M,L\), seek an even bounded negative-field polynomial \(Q_{p,L}\), with complexity chosen before the prime limit, satisfying
\[
\limsup_{p\to\infty}
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}|b_i^{[L]}-Q_{p,L}(i)|]
\le \rho(L),
\qquad \rho(L)\to0.
\tag{6}
\]
Then the odd-arithmetic trace estimate gives
\[
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}Q_{p,L}S_{\rm Jac}]
=O_{L}(p^{-1/2}),
\]
while (6) transfers this to the clipped actual row. The remaining input would again be (5), or an independently proved signed tail bound.

This test directly targets the missing support-sensitive approximation and does not assume optimizer concentration or an unproved \(L^2\) estimate.
