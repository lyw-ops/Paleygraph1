## Checked conclusion

For the selected ordinary-prime family, with \(m=p-1\), the actual full-lift law \(\omega\), both square lifts \(i=0,1\), the regular indicators \(R_i\), and the physical marking
\[
\{\alpha,\delta\}\quad\text{versus}\quad\{\bar\beta,\bar\gamma\},
\]
the positive one-match form is indeed
\[
\mathcal P_1
 =\frac1m\sum_{\sigma,r,t}
 g(t)(1-g(t))\,|\widetilde U_{\sigma,r}(t)|^2,
\]
where \(g(t)=|f(t)|^2\), \(r\) ranges over the full character group, and
\[
\widetilde U_{\sigma,r}(t)=\sum_a U_{\sigma,r}(a)a(t).
\]

The existing hypotheses imply the checked bound
\[
\boxed{\mathcal P_1\ll m\kappa\ll p^{1/2}},
\qquad
\kappa=\|\omega\|_\infty\ll p^{-1/2},
\]
but they do **not** imply the required
\[
\boxed{\mathcal P_1\ll p^{-1/2}}.
\]

Thus the target remains open. The precise missing input is a physical spectral-localization estimate for the co-moving rows, not another pointwise Gauss bound.

---

## 1. What follows from the actual probability law

For either sign \(\sigma\), each marked slot has marginal law \(\omega\), or its inverse-reflection, which has the same cap. Since \(|D_{i,k}|=1\) on the regular set and \(0\le R_i\le1\),
\[
|U_{\sigma,r}(a)|\le 2\omega(a)\le 2\kappa,
\]
and
\[
\sum_a |U_{\sigma,r}(a)|
 \le \frac12\sum_{i=0}^1\sum_{k\in\sigma}
       \mathbb E_W R_i(W,a_k/r)
 \le 2.
\]

Consequently, with counting norms and unnormalized Mellin transforms,
\[
\frac1m\sum_t|\widetilde U_{\sigma,r}(t)|^2
 =\sum_a |U_{\sigma,r}(a)|^2
 \le 4\kappa.
\]
Summing over both signs and all \(m\) values of \(r\),
\[
\frac1m\sum_{\sigma,r,t}
 |\widetilde U_{\sigma,r}(t)|^2
 \ll m\kappa
 \ll p^{1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]

The actual lifted autocorrelation satisfies
\[
\ell=\omega-\omega*\omega,
\qquad
\|\ell\|_\infty
 \le \|\omega\|_\infty+\|\omega*\omega\|_\infty
 \le 2\kappa.
\]
Using the exact physical-to-character identity,
\[
\mathcal P_1
 =\sum_{\sigma,r}\sum_{a,a'}
   \ell(a'/a)\,
   U_{\sigma,r}(a)\overline{U_{\sigma,r}(a')},
\]
up to the harmless inversion convention fixed by the symmetry of \(\ell\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal P_1
 \le 2\kappa
 \sum_{\sigma,r}
 \left(\sum_a|U_{\sigma,r}(a)|\right)^2
 \ll m\kappa
 \ll p^{1/2}.
\tag{2}
\]

This uses the actual \(\omega\), its actual cap, both lifts, and the regular indicators. It is a valid bound, but it is three powers of \(p^{1/2}\) away from the desired normalized estimate.

The prior paid errors remain valid: in the original normalization,
\[
\text{one-match contribution}
 =\frac{p^3}{m}\mathcal P_1+O(p^{3/2}).
\]
Hence \(\mathcal P_1=O(p^{-1/2})\) would give the desired \(O(p^{3/2})\) contribution.

## 2. The exact physical localization required

Let
\[
D_{s_0,s_1}
 =\{t\in\mathbb F_p^\times:
    s_0\le g(t)\le s_1\},
\qquad
0<s_0\le s_1<1.
\]
On this fixed moderate physical band,
\[
g(t)(1-g(t))\ge c_{s_0,s_1}:=s_0(1-s_1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal P_1
 \ge \frac{c_{s_0,s_1}}m
   \sum_{\sigma,r}\sum_{t\in D_{s_0,s_1}}
       |\widetilde U_{\sigma,r}(t)|^2.
\]
Thus the target would require
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\sigma,r}\sum_{t\in D_{s_0,s_1}}
       |\widetilde U_{\sigma,r}(t)|^2
 \ll_{s_0,s_1} p^{1/2}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This is a genuine localization statement. By (1), the unrestricted spectral mass is only known to be \(O(p^{3/2})\), whereas (3) permits only \(O(p^{1/2})\) on the moderate physical range. Cap bounds and individual residual Gauss estimates do not supply this factor-\(p\) localization.

The moderate band itself has size \(O(\sqrt p)\), since
\[
\sum_t g(t)
 =m\sum_a\omega(a)^2
 \le m\kappa
 \ll\sqrt p.
\]
But its small cardinality is not enough: the rows could still place essentially all of their spectral mass there.

## 3. Exact \(r\)-averaged reduction

The co-moving parametrization gives a useful exact reduction. Fix marked labels \(a,a'\), write
\[
h=a'/a,
\qquad
\psi=a/r.
\]
Then \(a'/r=h\psi\), and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{r}
 R_i(W,a/r)D_{i,k}(W,a/r)\,
 \overline{
 R_{i'}(W',a'/r)D_{i',k'}(W',a'/r)}
\\
&\qquad =
\sum_{\psi}
 R_i(W,\psi)D_{i,k}(W,\psi)\,
 \overline{
 R_{i'}(W',h\psi)D_{i',k'}(W',h\psi)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

All fixed Gauss factors remain inside \(D\). In particular, for each lift,
\[
D_{i,k}(W,\psi)
 =
C_i(W)\times
\text{the other three moving normalized Gauss factors},
\]
with
\[
C_i=(\beta\gamma)(-1)\,
 \overline{g_{\chi^i\alpha\gamma}}\,
 g_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\,
 \overline{g_{\chi^i\beta\delta}\,
 g_{\gamma\delta}\,g_{\alpha\beta}}.
\]
No \(C_i\), lift average, regular cut, or physical mark is removed in (4).

Expanding the moderate-band mass gives the exact reduced convolution
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\sigma,r}\sum_{t\in D_{s_0,s_1}}
 |\widetilde U_{\sigma,r}(t)|^2
\\
&=
\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{a,a'}
 \left(\sum_{t\in D_{s_0,s_1}}(a/a')(t)\right)
 \sum_r U_{\sigma,r}(a)\overline{U_{\sigma,r}(a')},
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
and the last \(r\)-sum is given by (4), followed by the actual four-character expectations.

Equation (5) identifies the remaining problem precisely: one needs cancellation in the \(h\)-shifted \(3+3\) Gauss correlation after averaging with the actual marked-label probabilities. The generic complete Katz bound gives only the previously known \(O(\sqrt p)\)-scale residual, which is sufficient for the already-paid two-match terms but not for (3).

## 4. Why autocorrelation alone is insufficient

The actual autocorrelation identity gives positivity and the exact multiplier \(g(1-g)\), but it does not by itself force the rows away from the moderate band.

For example, in the formal phase-free model
\[
R_i\equiv1,\qquad D_{i,k}\equiv1,
\]
one gets
\[
U_{\sigma,r}(a)=2\omega(a),
\]
for every \(\sigma,r\). Then
\[
\mathcal P_1
 =8\sum_t g(t)^2(1-g(t)).
\tag{6}
\]
The cap and Parseval estimates only give
\[
\sum_t g(t)^2(1-g(t))
 \le \sum_t g(t)
 \ll\sqrt p.
\]
They do not give \(O(p^{-1/2})\).

This is not a counterexample using the genuine Gauss phases; it is a precise obstruction to deriving the target from the autocorrelation law and cap alone. The genuine \(C_i\) and moving Gauss factors must produce additional cancellation or physical localization.

---

## 5. Zero-match class

The zero-match class is separate and remains open even if the one-match estimate is proved.

After all exact matches are removed, its residual regular correlation is a genuine \(4+4\) Gauss sum. The frequency-deletion errors are already controlled: at a deleted frequency there is at least one \(g_1=-p^{-1/2}\), giving an \(O(p^{3/2})\) cost in the original normalization.

The surviving complete zero-match term has no matching probability factor \(\kappa\). Thus the generic complete \(4+4\) estimate cannot be paid for by the cap in the way available for two or more matches. A separate weighted estimate is needed, at the same normalized target scale:
\[
\text{zero-match normalized mass}\ll p^{-1/2}
\]
if the original contribution is to be \(O(p^{3/2})\).

In particular, the one-match positive form does not absorb the zero-match term, and no conclusion about zero matches follows from (2)–(5).

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Analytic test: prove the \(r\)-averaged shifted-correlation bound

Target the exact quantity in (3), using the reduction (4). Split the \(h=a'/a\) sum into:

1. \(h=1\), repeated-label, and product-balance pieces, already controlled at \(O(p^{3/2})\) in the original normalization;
2. nontrivial \(h\) with all regular factors retained;
3. the \(g\approx0\) and \(g\approx1\) tails outside fixed moderate bands.

The decisive estimate is
\[
\sum_{\substack{\sigma,a,a'\\a'/a\ne1}}
\left|\sum_{t\in D_{s_0,s_1}}(a/a')(t)\right|
\left|
\sum_{\psi}
 R_i(W,\psi)D_{i,k}(W,\psi)
 \overline{R_{i'}(W',h\psi)D_{i',k'}(W',h\psi)}
\right|
\ll p^{1/2},
\]
after the actual \(\omega^{\otimes4}\) averaging and the two-lift average.

This is the highest-value test because it directly addresses the missing factor \(p^{-1}\) relative to the unrestricted Parseval bound and preserves the actual prime family, physical marking, and normalized Gauss parameters.

### 2. Diagnostic test: compute the exact decomposition along the selected prime family

For many primes in the already-selected ordinary family, compute without replacing any weights:

- the actual \(\omega\) and \(\ell=\omega-\omega*\omega\);
- both lifts \(i=0,1\);
- every regular indicator \(R_i\);
- the fixed factors \(C_i\);
- the exact rows \(U_{\sigma,r}\);
- \(\mathcal P_1\), \(p^{1/2}\mathcal P_1\), and the moderate-band mass in (3);
- the contributions grouped by \(h=a'/a\).

This cannot settle the asymptotic statement, but it will distinguish the two relevant possibilities:

- genuine physical localization, with the moderate-band mass \(O(\sqrt p)\);
- a persistent \(h\)-sector obstruction, suggesting that the target requires a new cancellation mechanism rather than a refinement of the current cap argument.

All limits here are in the order: fix the physical thresholds and regular conventions, evaluate the exact finite-\(p\) quantity on the selected ordinary primes, then study \(p\to\infty\).
