# The least corrected root price and the physical price of a sharp shortfall

The least corrected coefficient over all sharp opposite laws equals a root-versus-cubic secant on the full primary polytope. The secant can be large even for a genuine delayed pin. A different estimate uses the physical pin to replace that coefficient by a bounded one, charging the excess of the physical deficit over the normalized cubic. On a spreading prime quotient, almost unit efficiency forces an actual translate of the quartic optimizer to pay this excess at an almost maximal root.

## The exact correction, for a fixed witness and across all sharp witnesses

Write \(\mathcal F_+\) for the primary probability polytope, with mark \(a\), and \(\mathcal Q_a\) for its maximizing face. Fix an exterior mask \(P\) with positive quartic minimum and any minimizing row \(q\). Write \(u=q/a\), \(\mu_i=1_P(i)u_i^3/\sum_Pu^3\), and \(\eta=q(\mu)/a=\sum_Pu^4/\sum_Pu^3\). Here \(x(z)=\sum_i x_i z_i\). Strict convexity fixes the masked row, hence also \(\mu,\eta\). The first variation gives \(x(\mu)\ge a\eta\) for every \(x\in\mathcal Q_a\). Each sharp witness has normalized exterior shortfall \(s/S=\mu\) and efficiency \(D/S=\eta\). [Sharp test and cubic contact](astra-quartic-89.md)

For a given sharp law and floor \((w,c)\), put \(e_i=(w_i-c)_+\) on the exterior and \(\beta=1/(na)\). Let \(\mathcal V\) be the opposite optimizer polytope. The maximum root removal in the complementary optimizer cone is
\[
 d_w=\max_{y\in\operatorname{cone}(\mathcal V),\ y_i\le e_i\ (i\ne0)}y_0
     =\min_{l_i\ge0}\left\{\sum_{i\ne0}e_i l_i:
          \sum_{i\ne0}l_i v_i\ge\beta\quad(v\in\mathcal V)\right\}.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The cone is generated by the finitely many vertices. Writing its element as a nonnegative combination of those vertices proves (1) by LP duality. Each generator has exterior mass \(1-\beta>0\), giving a bounded primal. Positive mass on a shortfall site is impossible by complementary support. The least signed coefficient for this law is \((w_0-c-d_w)/S\).

Over all sharp laws the least signed coefficient and its nonnegative version are
\[
 \boxed{\quad b_*=\max_{x\in\mathrm{vert}(\mathcal F_+),\ x_0<a}
                   \frac{a\eta-x(\mu)}{a-x_0},
             \qquad B_*=(b_*)_+.\quad}                                 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed any corrected normalized field \(W=(c-w+y)/S\) belongs to the opposite sector including constants, with \(W_i\le\mu_i\) on the exterior and equality on the positive exterior of \(q\). Consequently \(\overline W=q(W)=a(\eta+W_0)\). Testing all primary laws against \(W\) shows that \(-W_0\) bounds all secants in (2).

Conversely choose the displayed vertex maximum \(b\). Every vertex satisfies \(x(\mu)-b x_0\ge a(\eta-b)\), using first variation on the top face. The inequality therefore holds on the full polytope, with equality at \(q\). The dual of minimizing \(x(\mu-b1_{\{0\}})\) over the primary probabilities chooses an opposite field \(W\le\mu-b1_{\{0\}}\) of maximal mean. To see the dual space, write the primary constraints as total mass one and vanishing nonconstant opposite moments. Their real linear span is exactly the opposite sector with constants. Complementary slackness at \(q\) gives \(W_0=-b\), \(W_i=\mu_i\) at positive exterior sites of \(q\), and \(\overline W=a(\eta-b)\). Thus \((W_i)_+=\mu_i\) on the exterior. For \(C>\max(0,\max_i W_i)\), set \(N=\sum_i(C-W_i)\), \(w=(C-W)/N\), \(c=C/N\). This actual law has shortfalls \(\mu/N\), gain \(\eta/N\), and coefficient \(b\) before subtraction. Since every corrected field satisfies the lower bound, any further subtraction has zero root. This also proves attainment for the full signed optimization. For \(na>1\), testing the uniform primary law gives \(b_*\ge(na\eta-1)/(na-1)\).

There is a bounded-coefficient test even when (2) is large. For \(B\ge0\) the smallest uniform additive loss for the primary laws is the finite LP value
\[
 \Phi(B)=\max_{x\in\mathcal F_+}
     [\eta-x(\mu)/a-B(1-x_0/a)]\ge0.                                 \tag{3}
\]
Its zero set on \([0,\infty)\) is \([B_*,\infty)\). Let \(S_j\) be the sum of \(j\) independent increments of law \(\mu\), let \(x^j_i=\mathbb E q_{i+S_j}\), and put \(f_j=1-x^j_0/a\). Translation invariance gives \(x^j\in\mathcal F_+\) and \(x^j(\mu)=x^{j+1}_0\). Thus \(f_0=0, f_1=1-\eta\), and \(f_{j+1}\le1-\eta+Bf_j+\Phi(B)\). A small positive value of (3) can suffice for a fixed number of spreading steps even when the exact tangent in (2) is steep.

## A bounded coefficient at the physical pin

Take the actual [growing delayed pin](astra-persistent-88.md), a fixed gap \(\gamma=\sqrt n\,a\ge1+\delta\), allowance and cutoff \(v=\delta/8\), and \(m=R_0/\sqrt n=1+o(1)\), so \(\overline R=(1+v)m\). Set \(r=(R)_-1_P\), with exterior mask \(P=\{i\ne0:(R_i)_->v\}\), and let \(h\) be its actual positive terminal companion on the exterior. Put
\[
 A=m(\gamma-1-v)-v,\quad H=m\gamma,\quad
 B_h=\eta H/A,\qquad E_i=(r_i-(A/\eta)\mu_i/a)_+.
\]
Uniformly at this pin \(A\gg_\delta1\) eventually and \(B_h=O_\delta(1)\). For every feasible primary probability \(x\), opposite orthogonality gives \(x(r)\ge R_0 x_0-\overline R-v=A-H(1-x_0/a)\), since the exterior negative part below the cutoff costs at most \(v\). It follows that
\[
 x(E)\ge (A/\eta)\,[\eta-x(\mu)/a-B_h(1-x_0/a)].                      \tag{4}
\]
In particular (4) bounds \(\Phi(B_h)\) by the capacity \((\eta/A)\max_{x\in\mathcal F_+}x(E)\). The walk allows a tighter estimate on the actual rows. For a prime divisor \(\ell\mid n\), push \(\mu\) to \(\rho\) on the quotient of order \(\ell\) and put \(M=1/\|\rho\|_\infty\). For \(k\ge2\), set \(J_k(B)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}B^j\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac A\eta\left[1-\frac{\|\rho^{*k}\|_\infty}{a}-
                    (1-\eta)J_k(B_h)\right]_+
 \le\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}B_h^{k-1-j}\,x^j(E).\quad}                        \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(x^k_0\le\|\rho^{*k}\|_\infty\), by summing the primary mass in each quotient fiber. Iterate (4) from the exact first step. For an upper on a right-hand term one can maximize \(x(E)\) with total mass one, point cap \(a\), and mass at most \(\|\rho^{*j}\|_\infty\) on each quotient fiber. The last cap follows by convolving the projected primary law with the reflected quotient walk. The polytope \(\mathcal F_+\) can also be imposed. These capacities use the positive excess of the actual pin over the optimizing cubic on this very mask.

Suppose along primes \(a\ell\to\infty\) and \(aM\ge\zeta>0\). The prime quotient estimate gives
\[
 \|\rho^{*k}\|_\infty\le\ell^{-1}+M^{-1}
    [8/\sqrt{k+1}+(15/16)^{k-2}],\qquad M\ge4.
\]
Thus for some fixed \(k=k(\zeta)\), \(f_k\ge1/2\) eventually. If \(\eta\to1\), (5) forces a positive excess payment \(x^j(E)\gg_{\delta,\zeta}1\) at some \(1\le j<k\). This conclusion holds for any chosen actual quartic minimizer and requires no upper on (2). In particular small capacities for these \(k-1\) walks exclude almost unit efficiency. [Prime spreading proof](astra-dual-90.md) · [Cauchy–Davenport in Tao](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308286)

There is also a finite bound at a single displaced mark. Fix any relative tolerance \(0<\tau\le1\), put \(B=\max(B_h,1/\tau)\), and set
\[
 c_\tau=\frac{1-\|\rho^{*k}\|_\infty/a-(1-\eta)J_k(B)}{J_{k-1}(B)}.
\]
If \(c_\tau>0\), some \(t\) in the support of \(\mu^{*j}\), \(1\le j<k\), satisfies
\[
  0<1-q_t/a\le\tau,\qquad q^t(\mu)/a\le\eta-c_\tau,\qquad
                    q^t(E)\ge Ac_\tau/\eta,\quad q^t_i=q_{i+t}.          \tag{6}
\]
In fact the walk expectation of \(g_B(t)=\eta-q^t(\mu)/a-B(1-q_t/a)\) at time \(j\) is \(f_{j+1}-(1-\eta)-B f_j\). Summing with weights \(B^{k-1-j}\) from \(j=1\) to \(k-1\) leaves \(f_k-(1-\eta)J_k(B)\). Thus some word attains \(g_B(t)\ge c_\tau\). The point cap and \(B\ge1/\tau\) give the upper root loss, while equality at the mark would make \(q^t\) an optimizer with cubic price at least \(a\eta\). Apply (4) since \(B\ge B_h\). For any fixed tolerance, the spreading assumptions and \(\eta\to1\) make the lower price in (6) bounded away from zero.

One can locate the same obstruction at a strictly submaximal site of the minimizing row approaching its cap. On a subsequence let all \(f_i\) for \(0\le i\le k\) converge, and take the first index \(j+1\) with positive limit. Then \(j\ge1\), \(f_j\to0\), and \(f_{j+1}\to c>0\). For \(t\sim\mu^{*j}\) let \(q^t_i=q_{i+t}\) and
\[
 g(t)=\eta-q^t(\mu)/a-B_h(1-q_t/a).
\]
This quantity is at most one and has expectation \(f_{j+1}-(1-\eta)-B_h f_j\to c\). A positive fraction of these words have \(g(t)\ge c/3\) eventually. Markov's inequality for \(1-q_t/a\), whose mean tends to zero, permits choosing one such word with \(q_t/a\to1\). Moreover \(q_t<a\), since at equality the translate belongs to \(\mathcal Q_a\) and first variation would give \(q^t(\mu)\ge a\eta\). On this *single translate*,
\[
 q^t(\mu)/a\le\eta-c/3,\qquad q^t(E)\ge Ac/(3\eta).
\]
Here the positive constant for the almost-maximal translate may depend on the subsequence. The translate is feasible and maximizes at the displaced mark \(-t\).

At the length with the round-88 counting saving, this translate pays order-\(d\) height on \(\{r>(A/\eta)\mu/a\}\), even after deleting any \(O(d^2L^2)\) sites and keeping only \(n q^t_i>c' d\sqrt{\log D}\) for some \(c'>0\) depending on the surplus. In fact \(E\le r\le h/(2d-1)\) on \(P\), and the discarded low densities cost at most \(c'd\sqrt{\log D}\,\mathbb E r=O_\delta(c')\). For a deletion \(F\) of the stated size, use any admissible early approximation scale \(L\ge2\) with \(\log_2^*L\le(12/13-\xi)\log_2^*p\) and fixed \(0<\xi<12/13\). The bounded approximation gives \(\|h1_F\|_{2,n}=o(1/L)\) because \(|F|=n^{o(1)}\). The point cap then gives \(q^t(h1_F)\le a\sqrt{n|F|}\|h1_F\|_{2,n}=o(d)\). The prime quotient conditions and almost unit efficiency apply to the selected physical mask. Intermediate efficiencies and shortfalls too small on the quotient are still open.
