## Result

Let \(Q\) be the actual eligible-prime family, with the physical marking and square masks of the cited rounds, and let  
\[
g=|f|^2,\qquad G=\sum_{x\in Q}g(x)=O(\sqrt p).
\]
For the genuine right action
\[
(a,c):(r,b)\mapsto (ar,b+cr),
\]
write \(\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}(a,c)\) for the correlation restricted to
\[
a,c\neq 0,\qquad a\neq 1,
\]
with both physical charts retained. Put
\[
\varepsilon_*=\frac1{1280},\qquad
\varepsilon_1=\frac1{840},\qquad
\eta=\varepsilon_*+\varepsilon_1=\frac{53}{26880}.
\]

The established pointwise estimate gives, uniformly on the actual physical generic branch,
\[
0\leq \Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}(a,c)
\ll p^{1-\eta}.
\tag{1}
\]

The total first mass is
\[
\sum_{a,c}\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}(a,c)\leq G^4\ll p^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, for
\[
N_{\mathrm{gen}}(t)
=\#\{(a,c):\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}(a,c)>t\},
\]
one has the checked tail bound
\[
N_{\mathrm{gen}}(t)
\ll \min\!\left(p^2,\frac{p^2}{t}\right),
\qquad
0<t\leq Cp^{1-\eta},
\tag{3}
\]
and \(N_{\mathrm{gen}}(t)=0\) above the range in (1).

There is also a row-wise bound. For fixed \(a\), the physical line-pair identity gives
\[
\sum_c\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}(a,c)
\leq \sum_r m_r m_{ar}
\ll G^2\,p^{1/2-\varepsilon_*}
\ll p^{3/2-\varepsilon_*},
\]
and the analogous fixed-\(c\) estimate follows from the \(b'\)-row bound. Hence
\[
N_a(t),\,N^c(t)\ll \frac{p^{3/2-\varepsilon_*}}t.
\tag{4}
\]
The fixed-row squared estimates from Round 176 give, after summing over rows,
\[
\sum_{a,c}\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}(a,c)^2
\ll p^{7/2-2\varepsilon_*-\varepsilon_1},
\tag{5}
\]
but (5) is weaker than the existing all-level bound
\[
E_w(\mu_0)\ll p^{11/4-\delta_*/2},
\qquad \delta_*=2\varepsilon_*.
\]

Thus the nonmonomial projective overlap does yield a genuine rich-range statement: at the largest possible level,
\[
N_{\mathrm{gen}}\!\left(\asymp p^{1-\eta}\right)
\ll p^{1+\eta},
\]
rather than the trivial \(O(p^2)\). However, this does not produce a useful energy saving by itself.

Indeed, integrating (3),
\[
\sum_{\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}>T}\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}^2
\leq
2\int_T^{Cp^{1-\eta}}uN_{\mathrm{gen}}(u)\,du
\ll p^{3-\eta}
\]
for \(T\geq 1\). This is still far above the desired \(O(p^2)\) weighted-energy scale, and it gives no \(O(p^{3/2})\) matching-number bound.

## Why the projective-overlap input does not close the gap

The two-torus argument applies to a single common heavy level set
\[
X\subseteq \{x:g(x)\geq \tau,\ g(\phi(x))\geq\tau\}.
\]
It gives a cardinality obstruction for that common set. The weighted correlation, however, has a mixed dyadic decomposition in the four physical labels
\[
(Y,D,Y',D').
\]
A generic term may have four different levels. The argument currently provides no uniform hereditary quotient control for these mixed-level sets with losses small enough to sum over all level quadruples.

In particular, the following configuration is not excluded by the proved estimates:

- about \(p^{1+\eta}\) generic right differences;
- each having correlation of size \(p^{1-\eta}\);
- total first mass \(O(p^2)\);
- fixed-\(a\) and fixed-\(c\) row bounds still respected after distributing these differences over the \(p\) rows.

Such a configuration would contribute
\[
p^{1+\eta}\cdot p^{2-2\eta}=p^{3-\eta}
\]
to the squared energy. This is not an actual counterexample in the ordinary-prime family; it is a precise obstruction to deducing the target from the currently proved maximum, first-mass, and row estimates.

The fixed projective-label cuts of Round 175 do not resolve this: they remove finitely many exact shared-label relations at \(O(G^4)=O(p^2)\) cost, but the remaining all-distinct generic contribution still has no mixed-level overlap estimate.

## Hypotheses and order of limits

All statements above retain:

1. the original eligible prime family \(Q\);
2. the physical marking and both square masks;
3. the endpoint exclusions \(r,b,r',b'\notin\{0,1\}\);
4. the genuine right action \((r,b)\mapsto(ar,b+cr)\);
5. the actual weight \(g=|f|^2\), not a uniform surrogate.

The asymptotic interpretation is \(p\to\infty\) through the eligible primes, with \(\varepsilon_*,\varepsilon_1\) fixed. No mixed-level reduction or replacement of the physical masks by an unmarked field model has been used.

## Conclusion

The mission is **inconclusive but yields a quantitative rich-tail improvement**:

\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{gen}}(t)\ll \min(p^2,p^2/t),
\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{gen}}\ll p^{1-53/26880}.
}
\]

This is a valid nonempty high-correlation improvement, but it does not reach either the sufficient \(O(p^{3/2})\) full weighted bound or the \(O(p^2)\) generic squared-energy target.

### Ranked next tests

1. **Mixed-level two-torus test — highest priority.**  
   For dyadic levels \(\lambda_Y,\lambda_D,\lambda_{Y'},\lambda_{D'}\), form the actual physically marked set of \(Y\)-values supporting the corresponding generic correlations. Prove a hereditary bound for the simultaneous pair of projective maps
   \[
   Y\mapsto \frac{aY}{1-cY},
   \qquad
   D\mapsto D'(Y,D),
   \]
   with losses tracked in all four level parameters. The needed outcome is a summable tail estimate stronger than
   \[
   N_{\mathrm{gen}}(t)\ll p^2/t,
   \]
   ideally \(N_{\mathrm{gen}}(t)\ll p^{2-\kappa}/t\) throughout the rich range.

2. **One-match/zero-match decomposition test — second priority.**  
   Expand the generic energy according to zero, one, or two literal physical-label coincidences. Apply the fixed projective-label cuts only after the decomposition, and test whether the remaining one-match term admits a second independent pin. A successful estimate would need to exploit both physical inputs before Cauchy–Schwarz; using only the existing one-match incidence bound reproduces the \(p^{29/16-o(1)}\) obstruction and cannot reach \(O(p^{3/2})\).
