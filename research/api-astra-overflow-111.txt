### Result: a universal \(o(p^{-1/2})\) distinct-triple bound is false on the prescribed square-field control

Let \(q=r^{2}\), \(r\equiv1\pmod 2\), and use the static square-field spectrum from the supplied references:
\[
S=B^\perp\subset G=\widehat Q,\qquad |S|=s=\frac{r+1}{2},\qquad
n=|Q|=\frac{r^{2}-1}{2}.
\]
Thus
\[
\rho:=\frac{s}{n}=\frac1{r-1}.
\]
Let \(u=1_S\), and let \(k=\lambda/\sqrt q=\lambda/r\). The exact subfield formula gives
\[
Ku=u-\rho\mathbf1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_n(Ku)^3
=\rho(1-\rho)^3-(1-\rho)\rho^3
=\rho(1-\rho)(1-2\rho).
\tag{A}
\]

For \(\mu\) uniform on \(S\), the identity
\[
\mathbb E_n(Ku)^3
=(r\rho)^3\sum_{a,b,c\in S}\mu_a\mu_b\mu_c\,T(a,b,c)
\]
therefore yields the exact total weighted correlation
\[
\sum_{a,b,c\in S}\mu_a\mu_b\mu_c\,T(a,b,c)
=\frac{(r-2)(r-3)}{r^3}
=\frac1r-\frac5{r^2}+O(r^{-3}).
\tag{B}
\]

The repeated-index contribution is \(O(r^{-2})\): the collision mass is \(O(s^{-1})=O(r^{-1})\), while the translated two-point Jacobi estimate gives \(T(a,a,c)=O(r^{-1})\) after averaging over the free shifts; the all-equal part is \(O(r^{-2})\) directly. Since the probability of three distinct indices is
\[
1-\frac{3}{s}+\frac{2}{s^{2}}=1+O(r^{-1}),
\]
(B) implies
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{s(s-1)(s-2)}
\sum_{\substack{a,b,c\in S\\a,b,c\ {\rm distinct}}}
T(a,b,c)
=
\frac1r+O(r^{-2}).
}
\tag{C}
\]

In particular, the distinct-triple average has a nonzero positive square-root main term. With the matching sign \(\epsilon=+1\), it is of exactly the size required by the necessary inequality (1). Hence no statement of the form
\[
\sum_{\substack{a,b,c\in C\\\text{distinct}}}
\pi_a\pi_b\pi_c\,T(a,b,c)
=o(p^{-1/2})
\]
can hold uniformly for all positive near-saturating weights, even after collisions are removed.

This is an exact static obstruction on square fields, not an additive-field surrogate. It also confirms that the leading term is not generated by repeated configurations: the distinct sector itself carries \(r^{-1}=p^{-1/2}\).

### Scope and remaining gap

The example is prescribed and lives on \(q=r^{2}\), whereas the repair problem fixes \(t\) first and then takes a prime subsequence. Thus it does **not** produce a prime trajectory or an actual repair weight. It does, however, refute any proposed arithmetic lemma based only on:

1. positivity of the weight;
2. effective width \(\asymp\sqrt p\);
3. individual Katz bounds; and
4. removal of collisions.

Any prime-only improvement must use additional structure of the actual repair history, such as marked pairing, translation-difference restrictions, or simultaneous information from the exterior \(r=1_{C^c}\bar h\). The square-field witness shows that positivity and critical width alone are insufficient.

A further warning is that the exact identity \(Ku=u-\rho\mathbf1\) creates a coherent cubic contribution despite the pointwise normalized Jacobi correlations being individually square-root-sized. Therefore a proof attempting to sum Katz bounds with sign cancellation, without excluding approximate subfield-annihilator geometry, cannot succeed.

### Ranked next tests

1. **Prime analogue of the square witness.** Search for primes and positive weights \(\pi\) with a large set of translation differences \(h\) for which \(\lambda_h/\sqrt p\) is coherently positive, and test whether the distinct cubic average has a stable \(p^{-1/2}\) coefficient. This is the decisive arithmetic experiment.

2. **Use repair-specific anti-coset information.** Prove that the actual marked core \(C\) cannot have the translation concentration exhibited by \(S=B^\perp\), jointly with the forced centered exterior defect. A generic weighted Jacobi estimate is impossible by (C); the missing hypothesis must exclude this coherent geometry rather than merely sharpen Katz entrywise.
