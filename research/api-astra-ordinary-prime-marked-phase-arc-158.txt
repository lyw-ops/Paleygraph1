## Result: the available ordinary-prime incidence input does not yet give an \(O(p)\) bound

I kept the actual family and marking as follows. Let \(p\to\infty\) through the stipulated ordinary primes \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\), let
\[
Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,\qquad n=|Q|=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
and let \(b\) be the actual retargeted short-block iterate, with \(b_0=0\), \(r=\mathbb E b\), and
\[
\nu_a=\frac{b_a}{nr},\qquad \sum_a\nu_a=1.
\]
All norms below are counting \(\ell^2\)-norms on \(Q\) or \(\mathbb F_p\), while the norms on the index vector \(b\) are the normalized norms from the repair argument.

Write
\[
f(x)=\sum_{a\in\widehat Q}\nu_a\,a(x)\quad (x\in Q),
\]
extended by zero off \(Q\). Let \(\rho=-\sigma\), choose \(v\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) with \(\chi(v)=\rho\), and use the actual physical affine reflection
\[
x\longmapsto 1-vx.
\]
Put
\[
F_0(x)=f(1-vx),
\qquad
g_a(x)=F_0(x)\,\overline{a(1-vx)}
\]
on the physical contributing set \(x\in Q\), with zero elsewhere. Thus
\[
C_a(u)=\sum_{x\in Q}g_a(ux)\overline{g_a(x)},\qquad
M_a=\sum_{u\in Q\setminus\{1\}}|C_a(u)|^2.
\]

The two target quantities are
\[
W_{\rm arc}=\sum_{\rho k(a)\ge1/2}\nu_aM_a
\]
and
\[
S_\rho^{\rm add}
 =\frac12\sum_a\nu_a(1+\rho k(a))M_a+O(p).
\]

Along the hypothetical critical branch in the actual short block, the supplied argument gives
\[
W_{\rm arc},\,S_\rho^{\rm add}
 \gg \frac{pE^{-1/2}}{\log(2/E)},
\qquad
E=e+p^{-1/2}\to0.
\]
The question is whether ordinary-prime structure gives an \(O(p)\) upper on the same actual quantities.

### 1. Exact physical incidence identity

For the arc, define
\[
f_{\rm arc}(t)=\sum_{\rho k(a)\ge1/2}\nu_a\,a(t).
\]
Since
\[
\sum_{\rho k(a)\ge1/2}\nu_a\,\overline{a(r_u(x))}a(r_u(y))
 =f_{\rm arc}\!\left(\frac{r_u(y)}{r_u(x)}\right),
\]
where
\[
r_u(x)=\frac{1-vux}{1-vx},
\]
we obtain the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{\rm arc}
={}&\sum_{\substack{u\in Q\setminus\{1\}\\x,y\in Q}}
F_0(ux)\overline{F_0(x)}
\overline{F_0(uy)}F_0(y)\,
f_{\rm arc}\!\left(\frac{r_u(y)}{r_u(x)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The diagonal \(x=y\) is
\[
\nu(I_{\rm arc})
\left(
N^2-\sum_{x\in Q}|F_0(x)|^4
\right),
\qquad N=\sum_x|F_0(x)|^2.
\]
Thus any upper must control the genuinely off-diagonal, physically reflected ratio incidence with the actual weight \(f_{\rm arc}\). Replacing the actual \(f_{\rm arc}\) by an unsigned support count loses the prime-marking information.

### 2. What the current prime incidence theorem actually proves

Let \(X\subset Q\), and let \(h\) be supported on \(X\), \(|h|\le1\). Define
\[
A_X(u)=|X\cap uX|.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{u\ne1}
\left|\sum_x h(ux)\overline{h(x)}\right|^2
\le
\sum_{u\ne1}A_X(u)^2.
\]

For the shifted-coset sets appearing after the physical reflection, the ordinary-prime incidence estimate gives
\[
\#\{u\ne1:A_X(u)\ge t\}\ll_h \frac p{t^2}
\]
when the original frequency set is a union of \(h=O(1)\) multiplicative cosets of subgroups of size comparable with \(\sqrt p\).

Summing the tail gives only
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{u\ne1}A_X(u)^2
&\le \sum_{t\le |X|}(2t-1)\#\{u:A_X(u)\ge t\}\\
&\ll_h p\sum_{t\le |X|}\frac1t\\
&\ll_h p\log(2p).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore the presently available prime incidence input yields, even in this favorable compact-support model,
\[
M_a\ll_h p\log(2p),
\]
not \(O_h(p)\).

The logarithm is not a bookkeeping artifact in this deduction: the exponent \(t^{-2}\) is exactly the borderline tail for the second moment. To obtain \(O(p)\) from this route one needs either

\[
\#\{u:A_X(u)\ge t\}\ll_h \frac{p}{t^{2+\epsilon}}
\]
for some \(\epsilon>0\), or a direct weighted estimate
\[
\sum_{u\ne1}A_X(u)^2\ll_h p.
\]

The actual \(F_0\) is not compactly supported in general. The pin only supplies a constant wrong-sector component of size \(O(p^{-1/2})\) plus an \(L^2\)-small residual; it does not supply the pointwise \(O(p^{-1/2})\) residual needed to apply the preceding estimate directly. Thus even the \(O(p\log p)\) conclusion is conditional on a pointwise or level-set truncation of the actual physical row.

A useful checked conditional variant is the following. If
\[
F_0=h+r,\qquad \operatorname{supp}(h)\subset X,\quad |h|\le1,
\quad\|r\|_\infty\ll p^{-1/2},
\]
then every correlation changes by \(O(1)\):
\[
\left|\langle F_0(u\cdot),F_0\rangle
-\langle h(u\cdot),h\rangle\right|\ll1.
\]
Consequently
\[
\sum_{u\ne1}|\langle F_0(u\cdot),F_0\rangle|^2
\ll \sum_{u\ne1}A_X(u)^2+p
\ll_h p\log(2p).
\]
The actual pin does not currently justify this hypothesis uniformly on the arc or on the matched additive class.

### 3. Why a generic prime inverse theorem is insufficient at the present threshold

There is a prime-field obstruction to the proposed inverse route. Since \(Q\) is cyclic of order \(n\), choose a generator \(z\) and let
\[
X=\{1,z,z^2,\dots,z^{m-1}\},
\qquad m\asymp\sqrt p,\quad 2m<n.
\]
For \(u=z^d\), with \(|d|<m\),
\[
|X\cap uX|=m-|d|.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{u\ne1}|X\cap uX|^2
&=2\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j^2\\
&=\frac{(m-1)m(2m-1)}3\\
&\asymp m^3
\asymp p^{3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This construction exists over every such prime field and uses no proper subfield. It is a multiplicative progression, not a subfield-like cluster.

Thus an inverse theorem of the form

> \(M_a\gg pL\), \(L\to\infty\), forces concentration on \(O(1)\) multiplicative cosets of size \(\asymp\sqrt p\)

cannot follow from multiplicative energy alone. The progression above has \(M\asymp p^{3/2}\) while having no bounded-coset description.

The actual physical row is more constrained: its support or high-level set must simultaneously interact with
\[
x\in Q,\qquad 1-vx\in Q,
\]
and the coefficients arise from the positive measure \(\nu\) with \(f(1)=1\), \(b_0=0\), and the actual phase constraint. I have not produced a physical \(b\) realizing the progression example. It is therefore not a counterexample to the actual theorem. It does prove a precise limitation: an ordinary-prime multiplicative-energy inverse theorem, without using the affine reflection and the positive-definite marking, is too weak.

### Conclusion

No \(O(p)\) upper has been proved for the actual
\[
\sum_{\rho k(a)\ge1/2}\nu_aM_a
\]
or for
\[
S_\rho^{\rm add}
=\frac12\sum_a\nu_a(1+\rho k(a))M_a+O(p).
\]

The current prime-only information gives:

1. a physical incidence identity with the exact actual weight \(f_{\rm arc}\);
2. an \(O(p\log p)\) bound for a compactly supported shifted-coset model, assuming a pointwise-small residual;
3. no inverse theorem at the relevant \(M_a/p\to\infty\) threshold, because multiplicative progressions over prime fields already have energy \(\asymp p^{3/2}\).

Hence the ordinary-prime order is essential for the available shifted-incidence estimate, but it has not yet been coupled strongly enough to the actual marking, the affine reflection, and the phase arc to produce the required \(O(p)\) bound.
