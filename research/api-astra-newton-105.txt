### Checked obstruction for the bare adjacent block

Let \(n=|Q|\), let \(K\) be the actual Paley convolution operator with multiplier \(\chi(1-a)\) on the square subgroup, and let
\[
D_j=\operatorname{diag}(d_{j,x}),\qquad
\Delta_j=D_{j+1}-D_j.
\]
Here the indices are the physical nine-center coordinates, with the marked coordinate \(t_0=n\) and unmarked coordinates \(t_i=t\). No vertex-sign-matrix model is used.

The actual multiplier satisfies
\[
K^2=I-P_1,\qquad P_1=\frac1n{\bf1}{\bf1}^{\!*}.
\]

Define the bare block
\[
B_j=K\Delta_jK.
\]

#### Proposition: unavoidable trivial Mellin component

Put
\[
\bar\delta_j=\frac1n\operatorname{tr}\Delta_j
 =\frac1n\sum_x\bigl(d_{j+1,x}-d_{j,x}\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{tr}B_j
 =\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_jK^2)
 =(n-1)\bar\delta_j.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max_x |(B_j)_{xx}|
 \ge \frac{|\operatorname{tr}B_j|}{n}
 =\left(1-\frac1n\right)|\bar\delta_j|. \tag{A}
\]

This is an exact finite-field statement, uniform in the depth \(j\), and does not use an unsigned resolvent estimate.

Therefore any proposed signed bound of the form
\[
\max_{x,y}|(K\Delta_jK)_{xy}|
 \le C n^{-\beta}q^j,\qquad \beta>0, \tag{B}
\]
requires the necessary cancellation
\[
|\bar\delta_j|
 \le \frac{C}{1-1/n}\,n^{-\beta}q^j. \tag{C}
\]

In particular, normalized physical control
\[
\|\Delta_j\|_{2,n}\le Lq^j
\]
only gives \(|\bar\delta_j|\le Lq^j\); it gives no factor \(n^{-\beta}\). Recall that the corresponding counting norm is
\[
\|\Delta_j\|_2=\sqrt n\,\|\Delta_j\|_{2,n}.
\]
Thus normalized adjacent contraction alone cannot imply (B).

### Why the Mellin-parity multiplication fails

Decompose
\[
\Delta_j=\bar\delta_j I+F_j,\qquad \operatorname{tr}F_j=0.
\]
Then
\[
K\Delta_jK
 =\bar\delta_j(I-P_1)+KF_jK. \tag{D}
\]

The scalar term \(\bar\delta_jI\) is the trivial Mellin character. Multiplication by the actual Paley kernel does not turn it into a negative-parity term; instead it gives
\[
KIK=I-P_1.
\]
Hence the zero Mellin mode survives on the diagonal with size \(\bar\delta_j\). The ordinary \(\chi(1-a)\) twist does not remove it. Any trace argument that tracks only nontrivial boundary fibers therefore misses precisely this obstruction.

For the actual nonlinear diagonal,
\[
d_{j,x}=\Phi_t'(V_{j,x}),
\]
one has, writing \(h_{j,x}=V_{j+1,x}-V_{j,x}\),
\[
\bar\delta_j
 =\frac1n\sum_x \Phi_t''(V_{j,x})h_{j,x}
 +O\!\left(\|h_j\|_{\infty}^2\,
       \sup|\Phi_t'''|\right). \tag{E}
\]
Neither the nine-center symmetry nor \(\sum_x h_{j,x}=0\) forces the first term in (E) to vanish, because the weight \(\Phi_t''(V_{j,x})\) is not constant. Thus a genuine field-uniform signed estimate requires an additional zero-mode cancellation, not presently supplied.

A weaker estimate for the traceless part is available only under a stronger counted-\(\ell^1\) hypothesis. Since the actual kernel entries obey
\[
|K_{xy}|\le \frac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt n},
\]
\[
|(KF_jK)_{xy}|
 \le \frac3n\sum_i |(F_j)_{ii}|.
\]
Thus
\[
\|\operatorname{diag}F_j\|_{\ell^1}\le C\sqrt n\,q^j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\max_{x,y}|(KF_jK)_{xy}|
 \le 3C\,n^{-1/2}q^j. \tag{F}
\]
But normalized second-norm contraction only gives
\[
\|\operatorname{diag}F_j\|_{\ell^1}
 \le n\|F_j\|_{2,n}=O(nq^j),
\]
which yields no \(n^{-1/2}\) gain. Equation (F) identifies the missing strengthening, rather than proving it.

### Comparison with the resolvent-side derivative correction

Let
\[
Z_j=(I-KD_j)^{-1}K.
\]
The exact adjacent term is
\[
T_j=Z_{j+1}\Delta_j Z_j.
\]
Writing \(R_j=Z_j-K\),
\[
T_j
 =K\Delta_jK
 +R_{j+1}\Delta_jK
 +K\Delta_jR_j
 +R_{j+1}\Delta_jR_j. \tag{G}
\]

The existing shifted-column estimate \(Z_j(x,y)=O(n^{-\beta})\) does not by itself make the correction terms in (G) \(O(n^{-\beta}q^j)\). For example, using only normalized second-norm control,
\[
\|\Delta_j\|_2\le \sqrt n\,L_Dq^j,
\]
and an \(O(n^{-\beta})\) entry bound on one resolvent side gives at best
\[
|(R_{j+1}\Delta_jK)_{xy}|
 \lesssim n^{1/2-\beta}q^j,
\]
not \(n^{-\beta}q^j\). This is exactly where confusing normalized and counting \(E_2\) would produce a false gain.

For a purely scalar increment \(\Delta_j=c_jI\), the full term is
\[
T_j=c_j Z_{j+1}Z_j.
\]
If the resolvents are quantitatively close to \(K\), the same trivial-mode obstruction persists. More precisely, with
\[
e_j=\|Z_j-K\|_{2\to2},\qquad z_j=\|Z_j\|_{2\to2},
\]
\[
\left|
\frac1n\operatorname{tr}(Z_{j+1}Z_j)
-\left(1-\frac1n\right)
\right|
\le e_{j+1}z_j+e_j. \tag{H}
\]
Thus a small-resolvent-correction regime preserves a scalar increment at trace level. Current contraction data do not establish a sufficiently small right side in (H) uniformly at the relevant depths, so this does not yet refute cancellation in the complete \(T_j\); it does show that such cancellation must be proved, not inferred from Mellin parity.

### Conclusion and remaining gap

The bare block has a precise obstruction: unless
\[
\frac1n\sum_x
\left[\Phi_t'(V_{j+1,x})-\Phi_t'(V_{j,x})\right]
=O(n^{-\beta}q^j),
\]
no signed \(n^{-\beta}q^j\) entry estimate can hold, even though the increment is geometrically small in normalized \(E_2\). This obstruction is field-uniform and valid at increasing depth.

It remains open whether the actual nine-center stationary rows satisfy this zero-mode cancellation, and, if they do, whether the traceless component has the Mellin weighted \(\ell^1\) or parity estimate needed for (F).

**Ranked next tests**

1. Compute and certify the scalar mode
   \[
   c_j=\frac1n\sum_x
   [\Phi_t'(V_{j+1,x})-\Phi_t'(V_{j,x})]
   \]
   on the actual nine-center rows, reporting \(c_j/q^j\) uniformly in the eligible field. A nonzero field-uniform lower bound is a genuine obstruction.

2. If \(c_j\) vanishes to the required order, project to
   \[
   F_j=\Delta_j-c_jI
   \]
   and test the diagonal Mellin trace \(KF_jK\), especially its zero and translated-source fibers, before attempting the full resolvent correction in (G).
