## Checked result

Let \(p\to\infty\) through odd primes, \(n=(p-1)/2\), with the original physical mark \(q\) and the genuine Jacobi-sector projector \(P_\epsilon\). Use counting norms. Let \(A=\{i:w_i>0\}\) be the actual positive complementary support and \(B=U'\setminus A\) an actual independent extension, as in the cited construction.

Write
\[
G_B=G_{0,BB},\qquad c_B=(c_0)_B,
\]
where
\[
c_0=\frac1{\sqrt n}\left(\mathbf 1_{U'}+\frac{q-1}{d}b_0\right),
\qquad d=(P_\epsilon)_{00}.
\]
The physical entry estimates give, uniformly over actual unmarked columns,
\[
(G_B)_{ii}=\frac12+O(n^{-1}),\qquad
|(G_B)_{ij}|\le \frac{\sqrt p}{2n}+O(n^{-1}),
\qquad |(c_B)_i|=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]

Assume the actual selected extension satisfies
\[
b:=|B|\le (1-\eta)\frac{n}{\sqrt p},
\qquad 0<\eta<1.
\]
Then Gershgorin gives, for sufficiently large primes,
\[
G_B\ge \frac{\eta}{4}I_B.
\]
Consequently the genuine B-first inverse debit
\[
D_B:=\langle c_B,G_B^{-1}c_B\rangle
\]
obeys
\[
D_B\le \frac4\eta \|c_B\|_2^2
       =O_\eta\!\left(\frac bn\right)
       =O_\eta(p^{-1/2}).
\]

Thus, for every admissible growing scale
\[
T\to\infty,\qquad T=o(\sqrt n),
\]
and cutoff \(s=T^{-1/2}\), the B-first total—including its low spectral part—is already
\[
\langle c_B,\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}(G_B)G_B^{-1}c_B\rangle
\le D_B
=O_\eta(p^{-1/2})
=o(T).
\]

The complementary high-spectrum estimate is also explicit:
\[
\langle c_B,\mathbf 1_{[s,\infty)}(G_B)G_B^{-1}c_B\rangle
\le \frac{\|c_B\|_2^2}{s}
=O_\eta\!\left(\frac{b\sqrt T}{n}\right)
=O_\eta\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{T}{p}}\right).
\]
This is \(o(1)\), hence \(o(T)\), under the displayed admissible \(T\)-regime.

## A-first residual

After eliminating \(B\), the actual residual source on \(A\) is
\[
\rho_A:=G_{0,AB}G_B^{-1}c_B,
\]
and the quotient matrix is
\[
Q_A=G_{0,AA}-G_{0,AB}G_B^{-1}G_{0,BA}.
\]
The block positivity estimate gives
\[
\|\rho_A\|_2^2
\le D_B-\|c_B\|_2^2
\le D_B.
\]
Therefore, for the positive spectral part of \(Q_A\),
\[
\langle \rho_A,\mathbf 1_{[s,\infty)}(Q_A)
 Q_A^{-1}\rho_A\rangle
\le \frac{D_B}{s}
=O_\eta\!\left(\frac{b\sqrt T}{n}\right)
=O_\eta\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{T}{p}}\right).
\]

This is a valid actual projected-mass estimate for the transferred B-source. It does **not** control
\[
\langle \rho_A,\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}(Q_A)
 Q_A^{-1}\rho_A\rangle,
\]
because the factor \(Q_A^{-1}\) can be arbitrarily large on eigenvalues below \(s\). The rank estimate
\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}(G_B)
\le
\frac{b^2/(4n)+O(b/\sqrt n)}{(1/2-s)^2}
\]
likewise gives no source overlap estimate and therefore does not close this gap.

Most importantly, \(C_{\rm q}(A)\) is the A-first physical positive-dual cost:
\[
nC_{\rm q}(A)=\|d_A^{\rm phys}\|_2^2,
\]
equivalently the actual positive-cone dual value. The B-first bound \(D_B=O_\eta(b/n)\) does not imply a bound on the low-spectrum part of this A-first residual. The two debits are order-dependent and must not be identified.

## Precise failure

The requested prime-uniform actual inverse-tail bound is **not established**.

What is proved is:

1. Under the actual small-\(B\) hypothesis,
   \[
   D_B=O_\eta(b/n)=O_\eta(p^{-1/2}),
   \]
   so the entire B-first debit is \(o(T)\).

2. At cutoff \(s=T^{-1/2}\), the transferred source has high-spectrum cost
   \[
   O_\eta(b\sqrt T/n)=O_\eta(\sqrt{T/p}).
   \]

3. No prime-uniform estimate is available for the A-first residual in the interval
   \[
   0<\lambda\le T^{-1/2}.
   \]

4. The condition
   \[
   |B|\le (1-\eta)n/\sqrt p
   \]
   has not been proved for every actual fixed-gap sequence. Nor has an analogous bound been proved for the combined eliminated set consisting of zero and small positive complementary weights.

The abstract scaled-cone obstruction does not refute the ordinary-prime target: it has the correct positivity and normalization features but lacks the actual Jacobi Fourier geometry, physical marking, and adaptive prime support. Hence it only shows that positivity alone is insufficient.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Prove an actual low-weight elimination bound

For the genuine complementary coefficients \(w\), define
\[
C_H:=B\cup\{i\in A:0<w_i<H\}.
\]
The first test should establish, along every fixed-gap admissible prime sequence, a bound of the form
\[
|C_H|\le (1-\eta_H)\frac{n}{\sqrt p}
\]
for some fixed or slowly growing \(H\). Then Gershgorin and Schur elimination would control the entire \(C_H\)-debit. The remaining high-weight block could be treated by the signed Jacobi difference form. The critical missing estimate is a prime-uniform bound on the number of actual zero/low-weight coordinates.

### 2. Obtain a source-weighted Jacobi low-tail estimate

For the actual \(Q_A\), test directly whether one can prove, with \(s=T^{-1/2}\),
\[
\left\langle d_A,\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}(Q_A)
 Q_A^\dagger d_A\right\rangle=o(T),
\]
or the stronger projected-mass estimate
\[
\left\|\mathbf 1_{(0,s]}(Q_A)d_A\right\|_2^2
=o(sT).
\]
A viable route is a weighted Fourier-minor or Jacobi-kernel estimate that uses the actual source
\[
c_0=\frac1{\sqrt n}
\left(\mathbf1+\frac{q-1}{d}b_0\right)
\]
rather than only the low-eigenvalue rank. Finite-prime computations can identify the relevant eigenvalue/source alignment, but they cannot by themselves establish the required prime-uniform asymptotic bound.
