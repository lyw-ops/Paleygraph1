## Result: the proposed two-level gain is not available from the present hypotheses

Work on the same selected family of ordinary eligible primes \(p\to\infty\) as in the cited rounds, with the physical set \(Q\), both square masks, and the endpoint exclusions retained. Put
\[
\mu_0(r,b)=g(Y)g(D),\qquad G=\sum_{x\in Q}g(x)=O(\sqrt p),
\]
and
\[
\Delta(h)=\sum_{j:\,j,jh\text{ physical}}\mu_0(j)\mu_0(jh),
\qquad
E=\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb F_p)}\Delta(h)^2.
\]
All sums here are counting-norm sums; the relevant limit is \(p\to\infty\) along the selected ordinary-prime family.

### 1. The unavoidable identity contribution is already of target size

At \(h=e=(1,0)\),
\[
\Delta(e)=\sum_{(Y,D)\text{ physical}}g(Y)^2g(D)^2
 \le \left(\sum_Y g(Y)^2\right)^2
 \le G^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Delta(e)^2\le G^4=O(p^2).
\]

This bound is sharp under the available hypotheses \(0\le g\le1\) and \(G=O(\sqrt p)\): if \(g\) is approximately an indicator of a set of size \(\asymp\sqrt p\), then \(\sum g^2\asymp\sqrt p\), and the identity term is \(\asymp p^2\), subject only to the physical masks. Hence no uniform argument based solely on the cap, \(G=O(\sqrt p)\), and hereditary heavy-set information can produce \(o(p^2)\) for the full energy.

This does not obstruct the desired \(O(p^2)\) bound, but it shows that any successful two-level argument must isolate the identity and prove
\[
\sum_{h\ne e}\Delta(h)^2=O(p^2)
\]
rather than seek a global power saving.

### 2. Fixed projective-label cuts do not yield a further saving

For
\[
Y'=\frac{aY}{1-cY},\qquad
D'=\frac{(a-Y')D+Y'-aY}{(a-1)D+1-aY},
\]
the preceding fixed-map argument gives, for every fixed \(T\in\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\),
\[
\sum_h \Delta_T(h)^2=O(G^4)=O(p^2),
\]
where \(\Delta_T\) denotes the portion satisfying one prescribed projective relation among \(Y,D,Y',D'\).

The proof is already essentially optimal at the level of the supplied information:

- for fixed \(h,Y\), the \(D\)-map is injective;
- for \(Y'=T(Y)\), there are at most two \(Y\)'s unless the maps coincide identically;
- for \(D'=T(D)\), the same holds except for at most two exceptional \(Y\)'s;
- the exceptional identity difference contributes \(O(G^4)\);
- the total first mass over all right differences is \(O(G^3)\), and the pointwise bound is \(O(G)\).

Thus the estimate is \(O(G^3)\cdot O(G)=O(G^4)\). A heavy threshold \(g\ge\tau\), with \(\tau=p^{-\varepsilon_*}\), does not improve this calculation: it changes cardinalities but not the available weighted marginal bounds, because the weights remain bounded above by \(1\) and the total mass is still only controlled by \(G\).

The nonmonomial nature of the \(D\)-map does not by itself supply a second independent saving. Its pole is
\[
D=\frac{aY-1}{a-1}\qquad(a\ne1),
\]
which is affine in \(Y\). The fixed-map argument controls coincidences after \(Y\) is fixed, but gives no new global incidence estimate when \(h=(a,c)\) is also summed. This is precisely the missing two-level input.

### 3. Quantified gap to the target

The existing heavy-level estimate gives
\[
E=O\!\left(p^{11/4-\delta_*/2}\right),
\qquad
\delta_*=\frac1{640},
\]
so
\[
E^{1/4}=O\!\left(p^{11/16-\delta_*/8}\right).
\]
The target \(E=O(p^2)\) would require
\[
E^{1/4}=O(p^{1/2}),
\]
hence an additional fourth-root saving of
\[
p^{-3/16-\delta_*/8}.
\]
Equivalently, after the identity term is separated, the nonidentity contribution must improve by roughly
\[
p^{-3/4-\delta_*/2}
\]
relative to the current energy estimate.

The current heavy vertical-fiber result
\[
m_{\mathcal L}=O(p^{1/2-\delta_*})
\]
only contributes the factor \(m_{\mathcal L}^{1/8}\), namely \(p^{-\delta_*/8}\). It is therefore quantitatively far too small to close the all-weight energy target. The same conclusion applies to the current one-match gain: it improves the exponent but does not imply the required \(O(p^{3/2})\) matching estimate or the zero-match estimate.

## Precise failure

No new two-level overlap estimate has been established. More specifically, the available hypotheses—ordinary-prime cap, \(G=O(\sqrt p)\), both physical square masks, and hereditary energy bounds for heavy \(g\)-sets—prove only:

1. the identity contribution is \(O(p^2)\) and can be saturated;
2. every fixed projective-label cut is already \(O(p^2)\) in squared energy;
3. the nonidentity all-distinct contribution still lacks a global incidence estimate over \(h\).

Therefore the proposed gain is **inconclusive**, not refuted for the actual Fourier weights \(g=|f|^2\). The obstruction is genuine for the weaker abstract class \(0\le g\le1\), \(G=O(\sqrt p)\), but it does not prove that the actual \(g\)'s arising from the selected ordinary primes realize the obstruction.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: a nonidentity heavy-projective incidence estimate

For dyadic levels \(g\asymp u\), \(g\asymp v\), define the corresponding physical line set \(\mathcal L_{u,v}\). Prove, uniformly along the same ordinary-prime family and with both square masks retained,
\[
\sum_{h\ne e}
\left(
\sum_{\substack{j,jh\in\mathcal L_{u,v}\\
\text{all four labels distinct}}}
g(Y)g(D)g(Y')g(D')
\right)^2
\ll p^{2+o(1)}
\]
after summing the dyadic levels.

A sufficient stronger form would be a power-saving bound for heavy levels,
\[
\sum_{h\ne e}\Delta_{u,v}(h)^2
\ll p^{2-\eta}(uv)^{-\!O(1)}
\]
for some fixed \(\eta>0\). This is the direct test of whether the reciprocal-affine \(D\)-map supplies a genuine second incidence constraint beyond the one-label cuts.

### 2. Second priority: test arithmetic realizability of the obstruction

Construct or rule out, for the actual selected ordinary primes, Fourier weights \(g=|f|^2\) having simultaneously:

- \(G\asymp\sqrt p\);
- a heavy support of size \(\asymp\sqrt p\);
- large overlap with its reciprocal-affine images;
- physical points surviving both square masks.

If such weights can be realized, the identity-size obstruction is not merely abstract and any improvement must target only the nonidentity part. If they cannot be realized, the missing ingredient should be formulated as a hereditary Fourier restriction on heavy sets, stronger than the current multiplicative-energy and shifted-set bounds. Finite-prime experiments can guide this test, but the conclusion must ultimately be uniform as \(p\to\infty\) along the ordinary-prime family.
