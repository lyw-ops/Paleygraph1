## Checked result: the proposed \(O(p)\) bound is incompatible with the actual near-critical branch

Let \(p\to\infty\) through ordinary primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), with

\[
Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,\qquad n=|Q|=(p-1)/2,\qquad G=\widehat Q.
\]

Keep the actual R150 retargeted deficit \(b\), with physical marked coordinate \(0\), so \(b_0=0\), and define the actual anchor law

\[
\nu_j=\frac{b_j}{\sum_{\ell\in G}b_\ell}.
\]

No uniform replacement for \(\nu\) is made below.

Assume the near-critical R150/R151 branch:

\[
\frac{m_{\mathrm{eff}}}{m_0}\to1,\qquad
\frac{\|L_\sigma b\|_{2,n}}{\mathbb E b}\to0,\qquad
m_0=\frac n{\sqrt p}.
\]

The conclusion is conditional on this branch existing along an ordinary-prime sequence.

---

### 1. Exact five-factor expansion

Put \(\rho=-\sigma\), choose the actual \(v\) with \(\chi(v)=\rho\), and write

\[
f(t)=\sum_{\alpha\in G}\nu_\alpha\,\alpha(t),\qquad t\in Q,
\]

with \(f=0\) off \(Q\). Define

\[
F_0(x)=f(1-vx),\qquad x\in Q,
\]

where \(f(0)=0\). Thus \(x=0\), the physical marked coordinate, is never included in the \(Q\)-sums, and the root \(1-vx=0\) is also excluded by the support of \(f\).

For \(a\in G\),

\[
g_a(x)=F_0(x)\overline{a(1-vx)}
\]

and, for \(u\in Q\setminus\{1\}\),

\[
C_a(u)=\sum_{x\in Q}g_a(ux)\overline{g_a(x)}.
\]

Since

\[
r_u(x)=\frac{1-vux}{1-vx},
\]

averaging over the actual anchor law gives

\[
\mathbb E_\nu |C_a(u)|^2
=
\sum_{x,y}
F_0(ux)\overline{F_0(x)F_0(uy)}F_0(y)
f\!\left(\frac{r_u(y)}{r_u(x)}\right).
\]

The off-diagonal portion is the sum over \(x\neq y\).

Now set

\[
z=1-vx,\qquad T_u z=1-u+uz.
\]

Because \(x\in Q\) and \(\chi(v)=\rho\),

\[
x\in Q \Longleftrightarrow \chi(1-z)=\rho.
\]

Define the shifted Jacobi sum

\[
J_u(A,B)
=
\sum_{\substack{z\in Q\\ z\neq1,\ T_u z\in Q\\ \chi(1-z)=\rho}}
A(T_u z)B(z),
\]

for \(A,B\in G\). The conditions \(z\in Q\), \(T_u z\in Q\), and \(\chi(1-z)=\rho\) retain all quadratic lift conditions and all physical roots.

Expanding the five copies of \(f\), the exact off-diagonal term is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O_p
={}&
\sum_{u\in Q\setminus\{1\}}
\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\varepsilon\in G}
\nu_\alpha\nu_\beta\nu_\gamma\nu_\delta\nu_\varepsilon\\
&\quad\cdot
\Big[
J_u(\alpha\bar\varepsilon,\bar\beta\varepsilon)
J_u(\bar\gamma\varepsilon,\delta\bar\varepsilon)
-
J_u(\alpha\bar\gamma,\bar\beta\delta)
\Big].
\end{aligned}}
\]

The second term is the \(x=y\) subtraction. Summing it exactly gives

\[
\mathcal D_p
=
N^2-\sum_{x\in Q}|F_0(x)|^4,
\qquad
N=\|F_0\|_2^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbb E_\nu\sum_{u\neq1}|C_a(u)|^2
=
\mathcal D_p+\mathcal O_p.
\]

In the near-critical branch, \(N\asymp\sqrt p\), so

\[
\mathcal D_p=O(p).
\]

### 2. Ordinary-prime Jacobi estimate and resonance classification

Lift \(A,B\in G\) to multiplicative characters \(\widetilde A,\widetilde B\) of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\). Expanding the three quadratic conditions gives

\[
J_u(A,B)
=
\frac18
\sum_{e_0,e_1,e_2\in\{0,1\}}
\rho^{e_1}
\sum_z
\widetilde B(z)\chi^{e_0}(z)
\chi^{e_1}(1-z)
\widetilde A(T_u z)\chi^{e_2}(T_u z),
\]

up to the explicitly omitted roots \(z=0,1,T_u z=0\).

For \(u\in Q\setminus\{1\}\), the three linear forms

\[
z,\qquad 1-z,\qquad T_u z=1-u+uz
\]

have distinct zeros. Therefore the ordinary-prime Weil bound gives

\[
J_u(A,B)=O(\sqrt p)
\]

unless the corresponding Kummer sheaf is geometrically constant. The only constant contribution occurs when

\[
A=B=1_G.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
J_u(1,1)=\frac p8+O(\sqrt p),\qquad
J_u(A,B)=O(\sqrt p)\quad\text{if }(A,B)\neq(1,1).
}
\]

The constants are uniform in \(u\in Q\setminus\{1\}\).

For the first shifted sum in the five-factor expression, the exceptional condition is

\[
\alpha\bar\varepsilon=1,\qquad
\bar\beta\varepsilon=1,
\]

equivalently

\[
\alpha=\beta=\varepsilon.
\]

For the second, it is

\[
\gamma=\delta=\varepsilon.
\]

Thus the doubly exceptional resonance is exactly

\[
\boxed{\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\delta=\varepsilon.}
\]

Its leading contribution is

\[
\sum_{u\neq1}\sum_{\varepsilon\in G}
\nu_\varepsilon^5
\left(J_u(1,1)^2-J_u(1,1)\right)
=
\frac{p^3}{128}\sum_{\varepsilon}\nu_\varepsilon^5
+O\!\left(p^{5/2}\sum_\varepsilon\nu_\varepsilon^5\right).
\]

For an exactly flat law on \(m\asymp\sqrt p\) anchors,

\[
\sum_\varepsilon\nu_\varepsilon^5=m^{-4}\asymp p^{-2},
\]

so this fully resonant component is only \(O(p)\). It does not by itself obstruct an \(O(p)\) theorem.

The remaining Weil decomposition gives the crude bound

\[
|\text{five-factor product contribution}|
\ll
p^2
+
p^{5/2}\sum_\varepsilon\nu_\varepsilon^3
+
p^3\sum_\varepsilon\nu_\varepsilon^5.
\]

For an exactly flat \(m\asymp\sqrt p\) law this is

\[
O(p^2)+O(p^{3/2})+O(p).
\]

Thus ordinary-prime Weil estimates alone do **not** prove the desired \(O(p)\) bound. The unresolved \(O(p^2)\) term is the diffuse nonresonant contribution and would require cancellation after summing over \(u\), not merely pointwise Jacobi bounds.

### 3. Exact obstruction from the actual critical distribution

The stronger conclusion comes from the actual near-critical geometry, without a coset template.

The R150/R151 flattening statement gives a set \(S\subset G\), \(|S|\sim m_0\), such that:

1. the actual anchor law \(\nu\) is asymptotically concentrated and flat on \(S\);
2. the squared law \(b_j^2/\sum_i b_i^2\) is also asymptotically uniform on \(S\);
3. the within-\(S\) phase deficit tends to zero.

Therefore, with

\[
d_a=
\frac{\sum_j b_j^2(1-\rho k(j-a))}
{\sum_j b_j^2},
\]

one has

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu}d_a=o(1).
\]

For the actual reflected function \(g_a\), the affine-reflection estimate gives

\[
\eta_{g_a}
\ll d_a+\frac{L}{\sqrt p},
\qquad
\|g_a\|_2^2\gg \frac{\sqrt p}{L},
\]

where \(L=\max(1,m_{\mathrm{eff}}/m_0)\to1\).

The ordinary-prime cap estimate then implies

\[
L^2(1+D_{g_a})\log(2L(1+D_{g_a}))
\gg
\min\{\eta_{g_a}^{-1/2},p^{1/4}\}.
\]

Hence

\[
D_{g_a}\longrightarrow\infty
\quad\text{in }\nu\text{-probability}.
\]

By definition,

\[
pD_{g_a}
\le
\sum_{u\neq1}|C_a(u)|^2.
\]

Consequently, taking the \(\nu\)-expectation and using the order of limits \(p\to\infty\) first along the actual ordinary-prime sequence,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1p\,
\mathbb E_{\nu}
\sum_{u\neq1}|C_a(u)|^2
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\]

Since \(\mathcal D_p=O(p)\), the exact off-diagonal expression itself satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathcal O_p}{p}\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\]

Thus the proposed deficit-weighted \(O(p)\) estimate is false on any actual near-critical branch satisfying the stated R150/R151 hypotheses. This is not a finite computation and does not use uniform anchors. It is a conditional ordinary-prime obstruction: if the branch exists, the weighted moment is necessarily \(\omega(p)\), and the off-diagonal five-factor sum supplies the excess.

## Precise remaining gap

The five-factor Jacobi expansion identifies the arithmetic bottleneck:

- the fully exceptional resonance is only \(O(p)\) for a flat \(m_0\)-scale law;
- pointwise ordinary-prime Weil bounds leave a diffuse \(O(p^2)\) envelope;
- the actual critical distribution has no permitted coset, periodicity, or additive-energy hypothesis that would currently control this diffuse term;
- nevertheless, the cap/correlation argument proves that the complete weighted sum is \(\omega(p)\) whenever the actual critical branch survives.

Therefore a complete \(O(p)\) upper bound cannot be the correct theorem for the actual near-critical branch. A successful arithmetic analysis should instead identify the positive main contribution or prove that the near-critical branch cannot occur.
