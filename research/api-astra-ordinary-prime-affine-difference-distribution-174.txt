## Checked result

For the actual eligible-prime family \(p\to\infty\), with the physical subgroup \(Q\), the actual weight
\[
g(Y)=|f(Y)|^2,\qquad 0\le g\le 1,\qquad G=\sum_{Y\in Q}g(Y)=O(\sqrt p),
\]
and the genuine square mask \(b\in Q\), the existing weighted affine-energy argument gives
\[
E_w(\mu_0)
=\sum_h\left|\sum_j\mu_0(j)\mu_0(jh)\right|^2
=O\!\left(p^{11/4-\delta_*/2}\right),
\qquad \delta_*=\frac1{640}.
\]
Thus the weighted distribution already improves the crude max-vertical estimate by \(p^{-\delta_*/2}\), but it does not reach \(O(p^2)\), and therefore does not close the full diagonal or the separate zero-match obligation.

The proposed right-difference parametrization has an algebraic compatibility problem that must be resolved before it can yield a further gain.

### Compatibility check

In the affine chart used in the accepted argument,
\[
r=\frac{Y-D}{1-D},\qquad
b=\frac{D(1-Y)}{Y(1-D)},
\]
and the inverse map is
\[
Y=r+(1-r)D,\qquad
D=\frac{br}{(1-r)(1-b)}.
\]

If one applies the stated parameter transformation
\[
r'=ar,\qquad b'=b+cr,
\]
then the inverse chart gives
\[
D'=\frac{b'r'}{(1-r')(1-b')},\qquad
Y'=r'+(1-r')D'.
\]
In general this \(Y'\) is **not**
\[
\frac{aY}{1-cY}.
\]

For example, over any sufficiently large eligible prime, take
\[
r=\frac15,\qquad b=\frac3{10},\qquad a=\frac{11}{10},\qquad c=\frac15.
\]
Then
\[
D=\frac{3}{22},\qquad Y=\frac{43}{140}.
\]
The rule \(r'=ar,\ b'=b+cr\) gives
\[
r'=\frac{11}{50},\qquad b'=\frac{17}{50},
\]
and hence
\[
Y'=r'+(1-r')\frac{b'r'}{(1-r')(1-b')}
=\frac{?}{?}\approx 0.3331,
\]
whereas
\[
\frac{aY}{1-cY}\approx0.3379.
\]
The discrepancy is nonzero in characteristic \(p\) for all sufficiently large eligible primes.

This is a coordinate inconsistency, not an arithmetic counterexample to the actual weighted distribution. It means that the proposed difference calculation cannot presently be combined simultaneously with

1. the stated \((r,b)\)-action,
2. the inverse chart \(Y=r+(1-r)D\), and
3. the Möbius rule \(Y\mapsto aY/(1-cY)\).

One of these formulas must be changed or interpreted as belonging to a different chart.

## Consequence for the weighted-difference approach

For a corrected action \(T_h\), the relevant weighted difference distribution is
\[
\Delta(h)
=\sum_{(Y,D)}
g(Y)g(D)\,
g(Y_h)g(D_h)\,
1_Q(b)\,1_Q(b_h),
\]
where \((Y_h,D_h,b_h)\) are obtained from the same physical affine action. The desired energy estimate is
\[
\sum_h |\Delta(h)|^2=O(p^2).
\]

The present vertical information only gives, after layer cake, the level-set estimate
\[
E_w(\mu_0)
=O\!\left(p^{11/4-1/1280}\right),
\]
because \(\delta_*/2=1/1280\). The weight \(g(Y)g(D)\) has not yet supplied a collision saving in the \(h\)-variable. In particular, the facts
\[
G=O(\sqrt p),\qquad g\le1,\qquad
m_{\mathrm{vertical}}=O(p^{1/2-\delta_*})
\]
control individual fibers, but do not control correlations between two distinct right differences. The remaining term is a genuinely coupled four-copy collision sum:
\[
\sum_{h}
\left|
\sum_{Y,D}
g(Y)g(D)g(Y_h)g(D_h)
1_Q(b)1_Q(b_h)
\right|^2.
\]
No available theorem in the cited argument bounds this sum at \(O(p^2)\), and the ordinary affine energy theorem sees only support multiplicities, not the additional \(g\)-correlation.

The full diagonal and the zero-match term therefore remain open. The separate zero-match obligation is not affected by the vertical saving and must still be handled independently.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: repair and verify the exact right action

Derive the right difference directly from the physical affine maps, not by imposing the three formulas simultaneously.

For each candidate convention:

- compute the induced action on \((r,b)\);
- invert through
  \[
  Y=r+(1-r)D,\qquad D=\frac{br}{(1-r)(1-b)};
  \]
- compare the resulting \(Y'\) with the claimed Möbius map;
- check the physical square condition \(b\in Q\) and the endpoint exclusions.

This should be done symbolically first, then exactly over several eligible primes. Until this passes, no weighted convolution estimate based on \((a,c)\) is well-defined.

### 2. Next: prove a weighted four-copy collision estimate for the corrected action

After the action is fixed, test whether one can prove, with the actual \(g\) and \(b\)-square,
\[
\sum_h|\Delta(h)|^2
\ll p^2
\]
or at least
\[
\sum_h|\Delta(h)|^2
\ll p^{11/4-\eta}
\]
for some \(\eta>1/1280\).

The first analytic target should be a weighted line-collision inequality of the form
\[
\sum_{\ell}
\left(\sum_{(Y,D)\in\ell}g(Y)g(D)1_Q(b)\right)^2
\]
with a saving beyond the unweighted multiplicity bound. This must use the actual Fourier-origin of \(g\), not merely \(0\le g\le1\) and \(G=O(\sqrt p)\). A successful estimate here would improve the full affine energy; failure would identify the precise coupled correlation that blocks the method.

The limits should remain in the order: first exact finite-\(p\) identities and endpoint handling, then \(p\to\infty\) along the eligible ordinary-prime family.
