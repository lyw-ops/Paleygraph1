## Result

For the actual prime family \(p\to\infty\) through odd primes, with

- \(Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2\),
- the actual optimizer \(\nu=\nu_p\), its full-lift law \(\omega=\omega_p\),
- \(g=|f|^2\), extended by \(0\) off \(Q\),
- \(w(s)=g(s)(1-g(s))\),
- the physical marking \(v\neq0\),
- the genuine \(b\)-square condition \(b\in Q\),
- and the completed positive majorant, including its named endpoint repairs,

the available affine-energy argument does **not** prove
\[
\sum_s w(s)\,|H(s,s)|^2=O(p^{3/2}).
\]

It gives only the inherited bound
\[
\sum_s w(s)\,|H(s,s)|^2
\le
\sum_{s,t}w(t)\,|H(s,t)|^2
=O(p^{23/8}),
\]
after the heavy/light decomposition with \(\tau=p^{-1/8}\). Thus the diagonal remains open, with a missing saving of \(p^{7/8}\) in the squared weighted norm.

This is a failure of the proposed sufficient mechanism, not a refutation of the diagonal target.

---

## Exact diagonal under attack

After the affine change of variables from the genuine physical coordinates, the two unconstrained arguments are
\[
s=b+r e,\qquad e=\frac1E,
\]
with
\[
r=\frac{Y-D}{1-D},\qquad
b=\frac{D(1-Y)}{Y(1-D)},
\]
and \(b\in Q\) is the actual physical square restriction. The map \((Y,D)\mapsto(r,b)\) is injective on the retained nondegenerate region.

On the diagonal \(t=s\), the completed positive contribution of a pair \((Y,D)\) is
\[
g(Y)g(D)\,
g\!\left(\frac{rD}{Y(s-b)}\right)
g\!\left(\frac{rs}{s-b}\right),
\]
with \(b\in Q\), \(s\neq b\), and the zero/pole conventions of the completion. Hence
\[
H(s,s)
=
\sum_{\substack{(Y,D)\ {\rm retained}\\ b(Y,D)\in Q}}
g(Y)g(D)\,
g\!\left(\frac{r(Y,D)D}{Y(s-b(Y,D))}\right)
g\!\left(\frac{r(Y,D)s}{s-b(Y,D)}\right)
+\text{endpoint repairs}.
\]

The two restored factors have the exact character expansion
\[
\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
\omega(\alpha)\omega(\beta)
(\alpha\beta)(r)\,
\beta(D/Y)\,
\alpha(s)\,
\overline{\alpha\beta}(s-b).
\]
The pole \(s=b\) is zero for every character, including the trivial character. The blocks with \(\alpha=1\), \(\beta=1\), or \(\alpha\beta\) in any fixed bounded exceptional family are already controlled using
\[
\|\omega\|_\infty=\kappa=O(p^{-1/2}),
\]
and contribute \(O(\kappa G^2)=O(\sqrt p)\) pointwise, hence \(O(p^{3/2})\) after the \(w(s)\)-weighted square.

The unresolved part is therefore the regular two-character average over the genuine \((r,b)\)-family, with \(b\in Q\) retained.

---

## What the affine-energy proof actually supplies

For the heavy region
\[
g(Y),g(D)>\tau,\qquad \tau=p^{-1/8},
\]
the affine-energy estimate gives, uniformly in the auxiliary parameter \(t\),
\[
\|M_{\mu_{\alpha,t}}\|_{\mathbf1^\perp\to\mathbf1^\perp}
=O(p^{15/16}),
\]
while the input vector has norm \(O(\sqrt G)=O(p^{1/4})\), where
\[
G=\sum_x g(x)=O(\sqrt p).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\|H_{\rm heavy}(\cdot,t)\|_2=O(p^{19/16})
\]
uniformly in \(t\), and therefore
\[
\sum_{s,t}w(t)|H_{\rm heavy}(s,t)|^2
=O(p^{23/8}).
\]

For the diagonal, however, the input itself depends on \(s\):
\[
h_s(e)=g(s/e),
\]
and the coefficient measure also depends on the same \(s\) after the diagonal substitution. Thus the estimate is being applied to
\[
M_{\mu_s}h_s,
\]
not to one fixed operator acting on one fixed vector.

The two-dimensional estimate controls
\[
\sum_{s,t}w(t)\,|M_{\mu_t}h_t(s)|^2,
\]
but the desired quantity is
\[
\sum_s w(s)\,|M_{\mu_s}h_s(s)|^2.
\]
There is no valid diagonal-restriction principle turning the former into an \(O(p^{3/2})\) estimate. The affine energy controls the operator norm after averaging over group differences; it does not control the covariance created when the same parameter \(s\) selects both the operator and the evaluation point.

The light part is also insufficient with the current pointwise estimate. Indeed,
\[
H_{\rm light}(s,s)\ll \tau G^2,
\]
so
\[
\sum_s w(s)|H_{\rm light}(s,s)|^2
\ll \tau^2G^4\sum_s w(s)
\ll \tau^2 p^{5/2}.
\]
With \(\tau=p^{-1/8}\), this is \(O(p^{9/4})\), still larger than \(O(p^{3/2})\). Thus even improving the heavy diagonal argument would not by itself close the target unless the light pairs are treated more sharply or the cutoff is pushed much lower.

---

## Conclusion

The proposed diagonal route is **inconclusive but quantitatively obstructed at the current level**:

1. The exact full positive diagonal has been isolated with the physical mark and \(b\in Q\) retained.
2. Trivial and bounded-character blocks are paid at the target scale.
3. The known affine-energy bound yields only \(O(p^{23/8})\) for the diagonal by restriction from the two-dimensional estimate.
4. The diagonal dependence \(t=s\) introduces a genuine operator/input covariance not seen by the two-dimensional norm.
5. The current light-pair estimate contributes \(O(p^{9/4})\) at \(\tau=p^{-1/8}\).

Therefore there is no checked proof of
\[
\sum_s w(s)|H(s,s)|^2=O(p^{3/2})
\]
from the present machinery.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Expand the diagonal square and solve the coupled incidence problem

This is the highest-value test. Expand
\[
\sum_s w(s)|H(s,s)|^2
\]
using two physical pairs \((Y,D)\) and \((Y',D')\), retaining both square conditions \(b,b'\in Q\). The resulting equations involve the two fractional-linear arguments
\[
\frac{rD}{Y(s-b)},\qquad \frac{rs}{s-b}
\]
and their primed analogues.

The target is a direct bound for the coupled covariance, separating:

- \(b=b'\),
- \(r=r'\),
- equality of one restored argument,
- equality of both restored arguments,
- and the genuinely off-diagonal \(b\neq b'\), \(r\neq r'\) region.

The already-known injectivity and quadratic-root bounds should control the collision strata. A successful estimate must exploit \(w(s)\), not merely count all \(s\), and must retain \(b,b'\in Q\). This is the most plausible route to a genuine \(p^{7/8}\) saving over the inherited \(p^{23/8}\) bound.

### 2. Regular-character diagonal covariance with the square mask

Use the exact phase
\[
(\alpha\beta)(r)\,\beta(D/Y)\,
\alpha(s)\,\overline{\alpha\beta}(s-b)
\]
and expand the \(s\)-weighted square. For regular \(\alpha,\beta,\alpha',\beta'\), study the resulting sum over \(s,b,b'\) with
\[
1_Q(b)1_Q(b')\,w(s).
\]

The first subtest should fix the character quadruple and determine whether the \(s\)-sum reduces to a Jacobi/Kloosterman-type correlation with a power saving from the genuine \(w(s)=g(s)(1-g(s))\) weight. The second should average those bounds against the actual \(\omega(\alpha)\omega(\beta)\omega(\alpha')\omega(\beta')\).

A bound of \(O(p^{3/2})\) must use cancellation in this regular covariance; absolute values or the existing two-dimensional affine energy will not suffice.
