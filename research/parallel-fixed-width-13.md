# Fixed positive width: local-inertia audit and zero-floor proof

**Result.** For either sign and every fixed $r>0$, the reciprocal regularized value satisfies $M_{q,\sigma}(r)\to1$ as $q\to\infty$ through field orders $q\equiv1\pmod4$. The convergence is uniform on compact subsets of $(0,\infty)$. The derivative of $-\tfrac12\log M_{q,+}(r)$ with respect to $\log r$ also tends to zero locally uniformly. This is the fixed-width input to [ETH Problem 26](https://randomstrasse101.math.ethz.ch/posts/PaleyGraph/); the moving width near $q^{-1/2}$ is a separate question. In particular the result includes square orders and is consistent with their endpoint obstruction.

This audit uses the local sign idea in [iteration-parity.md](iteration-parity.md) but applies it directly to zero floor. No compact character group calculation from [mellin.md](mellin.md), Banach-algebra estimate from [uniform-fixed-depth.md](uniform-fixed-depth.md), or limit of stationary points as the floor vanishes is needed. The stronger rank-weighted algebra is in fact not closed under the absolute value of its formal real class functions; see the last section.

## 1. Local sign rules, with the convolution representative specified

Fix an odd $q$, an auxiliary $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q$ and a complex realization of the $\ell$-adic traces. Use pure weight-zero, arithmetically semisimple objects in Katz's middle-convolution category on $\mathbb G_m$. All local claims are after passage to the geometric field. Write $\star$ for middle convolution; its unit is $\delta_1$ and its dual is Verdier dual composed with multiplicative inversion. Use only objects that are tame everywhere and have no interior singularities or punctual constituents away from $1$. Geometric Kummer constituents are excluded. Purity and semisimplicity are preserved by the relevant middle convolutions (Katz, Chapters 2 and 4).

- A **negative** object has no punctual summands and the semisimplified geometric inertia of its generic sheaf at $1$ is quadratic (all eigenvalues of a tame generator are $-1$). Its stalk at $1$ vanishes. Its convolution dual is again negative.
- A **positive** object has unipotent inertia on its generic sheaf at $1$ and may have punctual summands at $1$. Twisting its nonpunctual part on $\mathbb G_m\setminus\{1\}$ by $\mathcal L_{\chi(1-a)}$ and taking middle extension gives a negative object $T(M)$. This introduces no geometric Kummer constituent, since the new nonpunctual constituents have nontrivial inertia at $1$. Purity follows by middle extension of the pure local systems. The trace is $s(a)t_M(a)$ everywhere, including at $1$ where the twist kills the full punctual contribution and its new nonpunctual stalk is zero. Here $s(a)=\chi(1-a)$ and $t_M$ is the alternating perverse stalk trace.

**Two negative factors, or two positive factors, have positive product.** In [Rojas-León, §2, Theorem 5.1 and Corollaries 5.3, 5.7, 5.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1398), the multiplication product is computed in the quotient that kills Kummer complexes. This is the same quotient as the image representative of Katz's middle convolution (see [Katz, Chapter 2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf)). The interior vanishing-cycle representation and the wild parts at $0,\infty$ are insensitive to the discarded Kummer terms. In particular the formula is applicable to the semisimple middle-convolution representative; it is unnecessary to assume that arbitrary compactly supported convolutions are semisimple or that they equal their images.

For a semisimple perverse representative $C$, write $V$ for its generic sheaf and $C(a)_{\mathrm{vc}}$ for the representation in Rojas-León's §2. That section gives

$$0\longrightarrow V/V^{I_a}\longrightarrow C(a)_{\mathrm{vc}}\longrightarrow \mathcal H^0(C)_a\longrightarrow0.$$

The rightmost term has trivial inertia. The wild boundary terms in Theorem 5.1 vanish for the tame inputs, and at an interior point the only possible contribution is from $(1,1)$ at $a=1$. For two nonpunctual factors of the same type the two input vanishing-cycle representations have the same semisimplified inertia sign whenever nonzero. By Corollary 5.7 the term is their tensor product, with semisimplified inertia trivial. The exact sequence then shows that the output generic inertia is unipotent, since the invariant stalk subspace itself has trivial inertia. At $a\ne1$ the vanishing-cycle representation is zero, so there is neither an interior singularity nor a punctual term. The output is tame by Corollary 5.9. Punctual summands of positive objects are geometric copies of the unit, and their convolutions preserve positive type. All these statements persist under direct sums. This proves the closure rule. In particular one need not identify vanishing cycles with the *whole* generic inertia representation or treat Corollary 5.7 as preserving a Jordan-block decomposition.

**Odd words have no geometric unit summand.** Pair all factors except the first. The even subproduct is positive. If the full word had a geometric $\delta_1$ summand, rigidity would give a nonzero morphism from the negative dual of the first factor into the positive subproduct. A morphism on nonpunctual middle extensions is determined on their common lisse open and respects the opposite tame inertia eigenvalues, so is zero. A morphism from such a semisimple negative object to a punctual constituent is also zero. These are morphisms of the actual perverse representatives because the Katz category is full. The case of one factor is immediate. No sign claim about the **entire inertia** of an odd product is needed.

**Uniform complexity.** Put $D(M)=\chi_c(\mathbb G_m,M)$ (convolution dimension) and let $R(M)$ be generic rank. For a tame nonpunctual object with at most the one interior singularity, $D(M)$ is the drop of rank at $1$ by Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich; add the punctual dimension for a general object. Tensor-category dimensions multiply. On a generic multiplication fiber $a\ne1$, two nonpunctual factors of ranks $R_1,R_2$ give the middle extension of the tensor of their lisse restrictions with possible singularities at the two distinct points $1,a$. Tame Euler characteristic gives minus the sum of the two drops, bounded in absolute value by $R_1D_2+R_2D_1$. The compactly supported degree-zero term is zero. The degree-two term is zero as well: a common irreducible constituent of one dual local system and the translated second local system would have to be lisse at both interior points. It would then be everywhere tame and lisse on $\mathbb G_m$, hence geometric Kummer, excluded here. The image in $H^1$ from $H^1_c$ bounds the generic rank of middle convolution (Katz, Chapters 2 and 19; see also the generic-fiber description in the proof of Theorem 22.2). Punctual factors contribute their dimension times the other's rank. Thus

$$R(M\star N)\le R(M)D(N)+D(M)R(N).$$

For mere boundedness a possible $R_1R_2$ correction for $H^2_c$ would also suffice. Kummer twisting by $\mathcal L_{\chi(a)}$ preserves both types and the ranks and dimensions. The operation $T$ keeps generic rank, with new convolution dimension equal to that rank. Hence all fixed words and fixed expressions below have bounded dimensions, ranks and numbers of terms as $q$ varies. No bound as the expression grows is used.

## 2. Mellin traces and the two needed estimates

Let $Q=(\mathbb F_q^\times)^2$, $n=(q-1)/2$, and $Ff(k)=\sum_{a\in Q}k(a)f(a)$ for $k\in\widehat Q$. Write $E(M;\psi)=\sum_{a\ne0}\psi(a)t_M(a)$ on the full character group. If $k$ lifts to $\psi$, put

$$A(M)(k)=\tfrac12\big(E(M;\psi)+E(M;\psi\chi)\big)=F(t_M|_Q)(k).$$

The last equality holds at **every** $k$. In particular $K=F\operatorname{diag}(s)F^{-1}$ satisfies $K A(M)=A(T(M))$ for a positive $M$. For the negative base object $L=\mathcal L_{\chi(1-a)}(1/2)[1]$ with middle extension understood, $t_L=-q^{-1/2}s$; hence $x:=\sqrt n K e_0=Fs/\sqrt n=-\sqrt{q/n}\,A(L)$. These identities also fix the normalization of the column at the mark. The symmetry $s(a^{-1})=s(a)$ gives real symmetric $K$, and $K^2=I-J$ with $J=\mathbf1\mathbf1^*/n$. In particular $\|K\|_{2\to2}\le1$ and $\|x\|_{2,n}\le1$, where $\|y\|_{2,n}:=n^{-1/2}\|y\|_2$.

Use [Katz, Overview, Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 4.2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf). At all characters $E(M;\psi)$ is the trace on compactly supported cohomology in degree zero, of dimension $D(M)$ and weights at most zero. Thus $|E(M;\psi)|\le D(M)$ **including the bad characters**. For a character good for a direct sum of inputs, these traces multiply as the trace of the middle convolution. There are at most twice the sum of the input generic ranks many bad full characters: only a twist that cancels a boundary tame eigencharacter can fail. Good characters also give $E(M^\vee;\psi)=\overline{E(M;\psi)}$, by purity and the tensor dual. Consequently conjugates on $\widehat Q$ may be replaced by duals with a bounded error supported on $O(1)$ frequencies. In expanding products of lift averages, choose one lift throughout each product and expand both options in each factor by using the Kummer $\chi(a)$ twist. This turns each expanded product into an ordinary Mellin product, which may then be averaged over the two lifts. Per term, replacing it by its middle-convolution trace costs a bounded error at $O(1)$ frequencies. These bounds refer to a fixed expression with uniformly bounded coefficients, and so the error is $O(n^{-1/2})$ in $\|\cdot\|_{2,n}$ and $O(n^{-1})$ in its mean.

Call a field a **real negative expression** if it is the real part of a finite linear combination of $A(M)$ for negative objects, with the number of terms, coefficients, dimensions and ranks uniformly bounded in $q$. Each such expression is uniformly bounded pointwise. Expand its real parts by the preceding dual rule. Two consequences, also valid for finitely many joint inputs of this kind, are:

1. Every fixed odd polynomial in the expressions has mean $O(q^{-1/2})$. For each monomial the mean of the convolution field equals $t_{M_1\star\cdots\star M_j}(1)$ by character orthogonality. The geometric punctual term at $1$ is absent by the odd rule. The remaining $\mathcal H^{-1}$-stalk has weights at most $-1$, so its trace is bounded by the generic rank times $q^{-1/2}$. The exceptions cost $O(q^{-1})$. These are estimates over the base field; no equidistribution in a growing extension of any *fixed* base field is substituted for the prime-aspect limit.
2. Applying $K$ to an even polynomial in the expressions gives a real negative expression up to $O(q^{-1/2})$ in $\|\cdot\|_{2,n}$. Replace each monomial by its positive convolution field (including $\delta_1$ for a constant), use $K A(M)=A(T(M))$, and pass the exceptional error through the contraction $K$. Taking real parts is legitimate since $K$ and the actual polynomial input are real. The same estimates permit the coefficients of the finite combinations to vary within fixed bounds.

These arguments use the even-product parity for the whole nonlinear recursion. In contrast [mellin.md](mellin.md) deals with the original Chebyshev fields from the first feedback and uses an explicit symmetry group to establish their mixed odd moments; neither its Frobenius–Schur sign computation nor independence of those fields is required here.

## 3. Direct passage through absolute value, with the marked input separated

For the zero-floor activation let $\Phi(z)_0=\sqrt{z_0^2+4}$ and $\Phi(z)_i=|z_i|$ for $i\ne0$. Write $d=(1-r)/(1+r)$. For $0<r\le1$, iterate $v_{t+1}=dK\Phi(v_t)$ from $v_0=0$; subscripts $t$ below denote time, and $(v_t)_0$ the marked coordinate. Let $Y_t=\sqrt n v_t$, $a_t=(v_t)_0$. The exact identities are

$$Y_{t+1}=d\{b_t x+K|Y_t|\},\qquad b_t=\sqrt{a_t^2+4}-|a_t|\in[0,2],$$
$$a_{t+1}=d\{b_t K_{00}+n^{-1}\textstyle\sum_k x_k|Y_t(k)|\}.\tag{1}$$

Indeed the exact scaled activation at the mark is $|Y_t(0)|+\sqrt n b_t$, and $K_{0k}=x_k/\sqrt n$. The diagonal is $K_{00}=-1/n$ (the two Jacobi sums at the trivial lift and its quadratic twist both equal $-1$); just $K_{00}=o(1)$ from the column bound would suffice. In particular the formula has not replaced the marked input with a bulk absolute value.

At fixed $t$, $Y_t$ can be approximated to any prescribed $\|\cdot\|_{2,n}$ accuracy in field limsup by a real negative expression. The induction starts at zero. At step $t$, first choose such an approximant $B$ of the desired accuracy. On $[-\sup|B|,\sup|B|]$, approximate $|y|$ uniformly by a real even polynomial $P$. Uniform approximation of a continuous function on an interval, followed by even symmetrization, suffices. Then

$$\big\|K|Y_t|-KP(B)\big\|_{2,n}\le \|Y_t-B\|_{2,n}+\sup_{|y|\le \sup|B|}|\,|y|-P(y)|.$$

Apply estimate 2 to $KP(B)$ and add $d b_t x$; $d b_t$ is bounded. No uniform pointwise bound on the *true* $Y_t$ is used, so polynomial approximation never has to be made on its possibly larger range. This proof works uniformly even if $d=d_q$ varies arbitrarily in $[0,1]$ at each field. More generally this induction applies at fixed depth to an even, globally Lipschitz real activation with that same kind of bounded marked forcing. What is closed here is approximability in normalized $\ell_2$, not the rank-weighted representation ring.

To test the marked sum in (1), use the same approximant $B$ and even $P$. Cauchy–Schwarz, $\|x\|_{2,n}\le1$, and the Lipschitz bound for $|\cdot|$ show that the error upon replacing $|Y_t|$ by $P(B)$ is at most the two chosen approximation errors. The normalized average of $xP(B)$ is $O(q^{-1/2})$ by estimate 1, since $x$ is itself negative and $P$ even. Fix the accuracies and depth before taking $q\to\infty$, then send the accuracies to zero. Equation (1) gives $a_t\to0$ at every fixed depth. This proves the zero-floor claim directly. The alternative [positive-to-zero floor comparison](negative-pairing.md#exactly-zero-bulk-floor) is also valid: at a common input, the activation errors in unscaled coordinates have ordinary $\ell_2$ norm at most $2\sqrt\tau$, giving $\|v_t^{(\tau)}-v_t^{(0)}\|_2\le 2\sqrt\tau\sum_{j=1}^t d^j$. At fixed $d<1$ this even gives a vanishing-floor comparison uniformly in time, but its constant blows up as $d\uparrow1$.

## 4. Fixed depth to fixed width, and a derivative consequence

The map $dK\Phi$ is a contraction for $0\le d<1$ (including $r=1,d=0$). Let $z(d)$ be its unique fixed point. The identity $\|\Phi(z)\|_2^2=\|z\|_2^2+4$ gives

$$\|z(d)\|_2\le\frac{2d}{\sqrt{1-d^2}},\qquad |z(d)_0-a_t|\le d^t\frac{2d}{\sqrt{1-d^2}}.$$

Thus for every fixed $D<1$, first taking $q\to\infty$ at fixed $t$ in the uniform fixed-depth result, then $t\to\infty$, gives $\sup_{0\le d\le D}|z(d)_0|\to0$. Alternatively, uniformity follows by taking any sequence $d_q\in[0,D]$ in (1).

For completeness, the QPs use $Q_+(r)=J+rP_++r^{-1}P_-$ with $P_\sigma=(I-J+\sigma K)/2$, and $Q_-=Q_+^{-1}$. Set $A_\pm=(\Phi(z)\pm z)/2$. The fixed-point identity gives $A_-=Q_+A_+$ by resolving separately on the two sectors and on the mean. The bulk products vanish and the marked product is one. Hence $A_+/A_{+,0}$ is the unique anchored plus minimizer by the KKT conditions and $M_+(r)=A_{+,0}^{-2}$. In particular

$$m_q(r):=-\tfrac12\log M_{q,+}(r)=\operatorname{arsinh}(z(d)_0/2),\qquad M_{q,-}(r)=1/M_{q,+}(r).$$

For $r>1$ the two signs are exchanged upon replacing $r$ by $1/r$. This completes the fixed positive width proof.

The optimizer of the strictly convex QP is continuous for $r>0$ by local uniform coercivity and uniqueness. The value therefore has a continuous derivative given by differentiating $Q_+(r)$ at the optimizer, using upper and lower value comparisons with the two minimizers. With $h(d)=K\Phi(z(d))$, this gives on $r\le1$

$$\frac{dm_q}{d\log r}=-\frac{r}{2(1+r)^2}\langle h(d),K h(d)\rangle.$$

Indeed for $r\ne1$, $X=z/(1-r)=h/(1+r)$ and $P_+X=P_+A_+$, $P_-X=-P_-A_-$, so the derivative is $-r\langle X,KX\rangle/2$. It extends to $r=1$ by continuity, where $h(0)=2Ke_0$ gives the exact value $dm_q/d\log r=1/(2n)$. On $|d|\le D<1$ the contraction argument also gives, for $d,e$ in this interval,

$$\|z(d)-z(e)\|_2\le \frac{|d-e|}{1-D}\,\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-D^2}}.$$

In this formula the fixed point is well-defined for negative $d$ too, with $z(-d)=-z(d)$; the sector computation extends to $r>1$. The bound follows by subtracting the two fixed point equations and using the $1$-Lipschitz activation and the uniform bound for $\|\Phi(z(e))\|_2$. Hence $h$ is uniformly bounded and Lipschitz on those compact intervals, and the displayed derivatives are uniformly Lipschitz in $\log r$ there. On any compact interval in $\log r$, compare a derivative to a difference quotient over a small increment contained in a slightly larger compact interval. The uniform error is at most the common Lipschitz bound times the increment, while the quotient tends uniformly to zero at a fixed increment by the already proved value convergence. This proves the asserted locally uniform derivative limit, including through support changes. Its Lipschitz and contraction constants need not stay bounded down to $r=0$.

## 5. A counterexample to absolute value in the stronger algebra

The algebra in [uniform-fixed-depth.md](uniform-fixed-depth.md) uses irreducible coefficient weights $(D(M)+R(M))^2$. Its spectral-radius step for a self-adjoint $X$ at positive floor is consistent with §1: the rank bound supports the polynomial coefficient estimate, and the formal and actual squared evaluations lie in $[0,R_0]$ for $R_0$ large enough. The radius bound for $(R_0-X^2)/(R_0+4\tau)$ is then strictly less than one. Substituting $\tau=0$ loses the strict inequality, and the formal norm really can diverge. This is independent of the exceptional-character coordinate.

Take the same negative object $L$ of §2. Its convolution dimension is one, and its geometric convolution group is $\mathbb G_m$. Its $j$-fold powers $L^{\star j}$ for $j\ge1$ have generic rank $j$. To see this, $L$ is geometrically the disjoint-parameter hypergeometric of type $(1,1)$ with parameters $1;\chi$: the defining single sum at $a\ne1$ is a constant Gauss factor times $\chi(a-1)$, or one can read this off from the rank-one local monodromies. Katz's repetition rule gives type $(j,j)$ for the $j$th convolution power, of generic rank $j$. The dual powers have the same rank. See [Katz, *Convolution and equidistribution*, Corollary 6.3 and its proof](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf) and [Katz, *G₂ and hypergeometric sheaves*, §2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/g2hyper62finalcorr.pdf). In particular no nonzero power can be the unit, so the one-dimensional geometric convolution group is the whole torus.

The self-adjoint formal expression $L+L^\vee$ corresponds on its compact torus to $2\cos\theta$. For $q\equiv1\pmod4$, the Kummer $\chi(a)$ twist exchanges these two objects: inversion of $\mathcal L_{\chi(1-a)}$ introduces the factor $\chi(a)$ since $\chi(-1)=1$, and the weight normalization is preserved under convolution duality. Thus the example also belongs to the twist-invariant algebra used to represent the actual Paley column. The $2k$th complex Fourier coefficient of $2|\cos\theta|$ has magnitude $4/[\pi(4k^2-1)]$ for $k\ge1$. Its corresponding weight in the formal norm is $(1+2k)^2$, so the sum of weighted absolute coefficients diverges. If even polynomials converged in that algebra to an element representing the formal absolute value, the coefficient at each $L^{\star 2k}$ would have to equal the corresponding Fourier coefficient: norm convergence gives uniform convergence of formal class functions and their coefficients by character orthogonality. Enlarging the generating family does not change these coefficients, since the one-dimensional characters pull back along the quotient onto the torus. No exceptional array can change the formal coordinate. Thus this norm cannot justify a zero-floor functional calculus, even at the first input. It does not obstruct the weaker approximation in §3, nor does it give any counterexample to the fixed-width QP limit.
