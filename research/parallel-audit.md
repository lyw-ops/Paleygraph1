# Independent audit: positive-density Poisson smoothing

Target: [ETH Randomstrasse101, Conjecture 26](https://randomstrasse101.math.ethz.ch/posts/PaleyGraph/) asks whether the **ordinary** Lovász number of the complement of the subgraph on the nonzero squares of $\mathbb F_p$ is asymptotic to $\sqrt{p/2}$ through all primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$. The [ledger](ledger.md) uses $\sqrt n$, $n=(p-1)/2$, an asymptotically equivalent normalization. The corresponding target in the [rank expansion](random-weight-ranks.md) is $a_1=2\log(\theta_+/\sqrt n)\to0$.

**Verdict.** The fixed positive density elimination in [poisson-smoothing.md](poisson-smoothing.md) survives the audit. The deterministic traffic theorem applies to the **full real frequency space**, and Fan's rotational invariance hypothesis is used on the Haar reference only. Below are explicit checks of the polynomial transfer, normalization and concentration. In contrast, the actual log spectra have no intensity-uniform sub-Gaussian bound, and a one-mark prime-square control gives both a nonzero sparse Poisson objective and, with a sufficiently small floor, failure of a second moment estimate proportional to the intensity. These are obstructions to a field-independent sparse extension, not counterexamples to Conjecture 26.

## Verified: complementary entropy and the floor (lines 5–15, 80)

For positive weights $w^\epsilon=z+\epsilon\mathbf1$, write $W=\sum_i z_i$, and let $P,Q$ be the two mean-one optimizers for $w^\epsilon$. The exact unnormalized products are $A_i B_i=w_i^\epsilon$ with $A=\sqrt{(W+n\epsilon)/n}\,P$, $B=\sqrt{(W+n\epsilon)/n}\,Q$. In particular $P_iQ_i=n(z_i+\epsilon)/(W+n\epsilon)$. For $W>0$ the sum of the two nonnegative gaps $F_+(z)-\sum z_i\log P_i$ and $F_-(z)-\sum z_i\log Q_i$ is

$$W\log(1+n\epsilon/W)+\sum_{i:z_i>0}z_i\log\frac{z_i}{z_i+\epsilon}\le n\epsilon.$$

This is $W$ times the relative entropy of $z/W$ with respect to $(z+\epsilon\mathbf1)/(W+n\epsilon)$. Hence the absolute error of the *difference* of the two objectives is at most $n\epsilon$ (both vanish at $W=0$). For $W=1$ the interval width in line 13 is twice this sum. At Poisson intensity $c=t/n$, division by $t$ costs at most $\epsilon/c$. Thus this estimate permits a fixed floor at fixed $c$ with a subsequent $\epsilon\downarrow0$, and at a sparse scale a floor of size $\delta c$ gives error at most $\delta$.

## Verified with the cited inputs: delocalization and fixed depth (lines 17–41)

**Matrix and theorem.** On the full real Fourier space, $K=F\operatorname{diag}(s)F^{-1}$ with $s(x)=\chi(1-x)$ on the nonzero squares. Inversion invariance of $s$ makes $K$ real symmetric; $s(1)=0$ and $s(x)^2=1$ for $x\ne1$ give $K\mathbf1=0$ and $K^2=\Pi=I-J$. In either field family the two extensions of a frequency give the Jacobi bound $|K_{ij}|\le\sqrt q/n$, including the trivial coefficient $-1/n$. Consequently $H_0=K+J$ has $H_0^2=I$, $\Pi H_0\Pi=K$, and $\|H_0\|_{\max}\le(\sqrt q+1)/n$. The same checks apply to $-K+J$. These are all the matrix hypotheses of [Gorini–Jones–Kunisky–Pesenti, Theorems 1.6/5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11729v1). Applying the theorem to an even-coordinate compression, to the unpunctured $H_0$ directly, or invoking their strong-cactus GFOM result on $K$ would require different hypotheses. The comparison here uses none of those steps.

**Independent marks and moments.** A monomial in one coordinate of a polynomial network has a rooted connected graph, by induction on matrix multiplication and coordinatewise products. Taking a uniform-coordinate expectation gives $1/n$ times a sum over all index labels. For each equality partition $\pi$ of the vertices, write $d_B$ for the total exponent of the independent marks on the block $B$. Integrating the marks leaves the constant $\prod_{B\in\pi}\mathbb E Z^{d_B}$ multiplying the injective traffic polynomial of the connected quotient. This covers products of rooted outputs and a power of the mark at the root. Möbius inversion reduces all terms to the cited traffic limits and bounds all even empirical moments. No independence of the entries of $K$ is used. In particular expected odd polynomial tests vanish by comparison with the exactly sign-symmetric r-ROM. Its Haar conjugate can be used with the iid signs from Definition 2.5; a balanced diagonal gives the same unpunctured traffic limits by Theorem 4.2, since both converge in tracial $L^2$ to the Rademacher law. Expanding both projections couples only connected traffic components via factors $1/n$; their normalized products have the same limits by the traffic concentration for the Haar references (Lemma B.7). Using the iid signs directly avoids this balanced-to-iid step.

**State evolution on the actual reference.** For bounded nonconstant $Z$ choose polynomials $f_t(x,z)$ even in $x$, with nonconstant $f_0(0,z)$. Center by the deterministic state mean $m_t=\mathbb E f_t(s_t g,Z)$ and feed $\alpha(f_t-m_t)$ into the unpunctured Haar matrix $H$. [Fan, Assumption 4.2 and Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.11892v5#S4.SS2) apply: the initial polynomial and side data are independent of the Haar basis, their empirical moments converge, the bounded sign spectrum has variance one, and the nonlinearities and derivatives have polynomial growth. Ensure nondegeneracy by adding an arbitrarily small even power of degree larger than the last-variable degree at each step. Conditional on the earlier Gaussian coordinates and $Z$, the newest Gaussian has a nonzero innovation since the preceding covariance is nonsingular. No linear combination of the earlier inputs and Gaussian coordinates has degree two or more in that innovation. This verifies the induction in Fan's condition (e). For constant input the actual punctured iteration is identically zero.

Every derivative expectation in Fan's $\Phi_t$ is zero, since the only nonzero candidate is odd in the last centered Gaussian, independent of $Z$. The limiting first cumulant is zero, and the variance is $\alpha^2\operatorname{Var}(f_t(s_tg,Z))$ because only the second cumulant, equal to one, survives in the covariance formula. Fan explicitly allows replacing the Onsager coefficients by their deterministic limits immediately after (4.7), so the centered Haar polynomial iteration with all corrections zero has this state evolution.

**Puncturing and unbounded activations.** For any stage input vector $b$, the difference between $\Pi H\Pi b$ and $H(b-m_t\mathbf1)$ is

$$-\overline{H(b-m_t\mathbf1)}\,\mathbf1+(m_t-\bar b)\Pi H\mathbf1.$$

At the unpunctured reference inputs both scalars tend to zero in probability: the first is the preceding formula's centered AMP output mean (up to $\alpha$), and the second follows from the state evolution for the inputs. Higher empirical moments of all fixed Haar polynomial networks, including $H\mathbf1$, are bounded by the unpunctured traffic theorem and the marked graph argument. In a fixed-depth polynomial expansion of successive differences, every term contains at least one of those small scalars; all other scalar factors and normalized vector $\ell_p$ norms are tight by the moment bounds and Hölder. This proves convergence to zero in probability in every fixed normalized $\ell_p$. Punctured network moments have higher-moment bounds by r-ROM traffic, so uniform integrability gives moment convergence in expectation. The deterministic transfer now gives the same expected polynomial moments, determining the joint annealed law of a uniform-coordinate output and the bounded mark (Gaussian marginal and bounded marginal give joint moment determinacy).

Polynomial approximation can be sequential, in $L^2$ under the Gaussian/mark law of the previous **polynomial** stage. The squared activation approximation error has polynomial growth and its empirical expectation converges by the higher moments. The $1$-Lipschitz property in $x$ and $\|K\|\le1$ propagate these errors in $\ell_{2,n}=n^{-1/2}\ell_2$. Minkowski's inequality for the distance to constants controls the change in the state standard deviation. Truncating an unbounded nonnegative $Z$ costs at most $2\|\sqrt{Z+\epsilon}-\sqrt{(Z\wedge L)+\epsilon}\|_{L^2}$ per stage, so finite first moment suffices for this truncation step. This proves the stated variance recursion and fixed-depth weighted log cancellation. For the latter the derivative of $2Z\operatorname{arsinh}(x/(2\sqrt{Z+\epsilon}))$ in $x$ has modulus at most $\sqrt Z$. The part on $Z>L$ is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz using $\mathbb E[Z\mathbf1_{Z>L}]$ and the bounded second moments of both runs.

## Verified: removing damping and taking the dense tail (lines 43–88)

At fixed $\alpha<1$, Banach contraction gives $\|v_\alpha-v_t\|_{2,n}\le 2\alpha^{t+1}\|\sqrt w\|_{2,n}/(1-\alpha)$ with $w=Z+\epsilon\mathbf1$. The variance bound $s_{t+1}^2\le(1-2/\pi)s_t^2+4\mathbb E w_i$ comes from $\mathbb E\sqrt{s_t^2g^2+4w_i}\ge s_t\mathbb E|g|$. It yields $\limsup_n\mathbb E\|v_\alpha\|_{2,n}^2\le2\pi\mathbb E w_i$ at each fixed $\alpha$, uniformly in that parameter *after* taking the field limit. The order matters. With $u_a=\sqrt{v_a^2+4w}$, $\gamma_a=\operatorname{arsinh}(v_a/(2\sqrt w))$, and star denoting $\alpha=1$, the exact cross identity is

$$4\sum_i w_i\cosh(\gamma_{\alpha,i}-\gamma_{*,i})=n\bar u_\alpha\bar u_*+(1-\alpha)\langle\Pi u_\alpha,\Pi u_*\rangle.$$

Here $n\bar u_*^2=4\sum w_i$ and $\bar u_\alpha\le\bar u_*$ by the diagonal version of the norm identity. This gives line 54 with its stated sign and factor. Cauchy–Schwarz, $Z_i\le w_i$ and $\cosh x-1\ge x^2/2$ turn the pairing bound into convergence of the signed test.

**Simplification.** The exact identity in line 49 already gives $\mathbb E\|\Pi u_\alpha\|_{2,n}^2\le4(c+\epsilon)/(1-\alpha^2)$ at every $n$. This weaker bound than the state evolution bound still suffices for damping removal: writing $M_n=c^{-1}\mathbb E\|u_*\|_{2,n}^2$, the comparison just proved gives

$$\frac{2}{nc}\mathbb E\left|\sum_i Z_i(\gamma_{\alpha,i}-\gamma_{*,i})\right|
\le 2\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right)^{1/4}\left((1+\epsilon/c)M_n\right)^{1/4}.$$

Thus the uniform-in-$\alpha$ state evolution norm estimate is optional for this step. **Conditional sparse criterion:** on a sequence $c_n\downarrow0$, for each fixed small $\delta>0$ it would suffice to bound $M_n$ at $\epsilon=\delta c_n$ and prove normalized damped cancellation, $\mathbb E[2\sum Z_i\gamma_{\alpha,i}]/(nc_n)\to0$ at each fixed $\alpha<1$. Neither claim follows from the fixed-law traffic limit. The criterion separates the two missing estimates and needs no additional bound for the damped norm.

**Gaussian transport.** The stated compound Poisson density is a mixture of gamma densities. Write $f_c=e^{-c-z}h$; the weights proportional to the terms of $h$ satisfy $\mathbb E j(j+1)=cz$. Thus $-1\le(\log f_c)'\le-1/2$ for $z\ge4c$, and $1\le\overline F_c/f_c\le2$. Above the Gaussian atom threshold, differentiation gives

$$\frac{d\sqrt{z+\epsilon}}{dg}=\frac{\phi(g)}{2\sqrt{z+\epsilon}\,f_c(z)}.$$

At the atom this has a finite right-hand limit for fixed $c,\epsilon>0$, since $f_c(0+)=ce^{-c}$; below the atom it is zero. In the tail $\phi(g)/\overline\Phi(g)=O(1+g)$ and $ce^{-c-z}\le\overline F_c(z)=\overline\Phi(g)\le e^{-g^2/2}$, giving a bounded derivative. This is a global Lipschitz quantile map for fixed $c,\epsilon$.

**Log gradient and centering.** In the [weighted projection identity](marked-response.md#weighted-complementary-projections), the projections onto $\operatorname{diag}(\nu/P)V_+$ and $\operatorname{diag}(\nu/Q)V_-$ are orthogonal with sum the projection onto $\nu^\perp$. Their difference $R$ has operator norm at most one. Since $u_{*,i}=2\nu_i\cosh\gamma_{*,i}$,

$$\nabla_\nu\log u_{*,i}=\frac{e_i+\tanh(\gamma_{*,i})R e_i}{\nu_i}.$$

The positive-floor domain is convex. The gradient bound $2/\sqrt\epsilon$ and coordinatewise quantile Lipschitz bound compose in Euclidean norm without any factor of $n$. [Gaussian concentration (Ledoux, Herbst argument)](https://www.numdam.org/item/SPS_1999__33__120_0.pdf) at parameter $2$ gives $\mathbb E u_{*,i}^2\le e^{2B^2}(\mathbb E u_{*,i})^2$ for $B=2L_{c,\epsilon}/\sqrt\epsilon$, by Jensen for the first moment. Uniqueness, the circulant $K$, and iid inputs give the same mean at every frequency. The mean identity is $\mathbb E u_{*,i}=2\mathbb E\sqrt{(\sum w_i)/n}\le2\sqrt{c+\epsilon}$. The bound needed here is annealed and uniform in $n$; no concentration of the mean over the frequencies needs to be assumed.

This supplies line 59 by Cauchy–Schwarz, then $G_n(cn)\to0$ by sending first $n\to\infty$, then $\alpha\uparrow1$, then $\epsilon\downarrow0$ at fixed $c>0$. The Poisson size bias and harmonic increment bound then give $a_{\lfloor cn\rfloor}\to0$. A finite grid in any compact range of $k/n$ applies since $|a_k-a_m|\le\sum_{j=m+1}^k1/j$ for $k\ge m$. The remaining range uses $|a_k|\le(n-1)/k$ by summing the rank series. The entire tail telescopes to $a_{\lceil cn\rceil-1}$, so this is a signed tail estimate. With the separate localized-dimension estimate in [rank-partitions.md](rank-partitions.md#the-densely-sampled-regime), the $o(n)$ terms and the constant $-1$ also disappear upon summation for $c\ge\log2$, yielding line 86. This last conclusion uses that dimension estimate in addition to the log proof.

## Counterexamples: what cannot pass to sparse intensity

**The log sub-Gaussian constant actually diverges.** This does not need square fields. Take any sequence $c\downarrow0$, $0<\epsilon\le Cc$, with the same independent compound Poisson inputs and a transitive frequency problem. Put $Y_i=\log u_{*,i}$ and $s=\tfrac12\log(1/(c+\epsilon))$. Jensen and the first-moment identity give $\mathbb E Y_i\le\log(2\sqrt{c+\epsilon})$. An event consisting of one jump of size at least one at $i$ has probability $ce^{-c-1}$ and forces $u_{*,i}\ge2$. In particular

$$\Pr(Y_i-\mathbb E Y_i\ge s)\ge ce^{-c-1}.$$

A sub-Gaussian bound $\Pr(Y_i-\mathbb E Y_i\ge s)\le e^{-s^2/(2B^2)}$ therefore requires $B^2\ge s^2/[2(\log(1/c)+c+1)]=(1+o(1))\log(1/c)/8$ as a lower bound. Also the actual Lipschitz norm of the *quantile-composed* log function is at least $\phi(g_c)/(2\epsilon c e^{-c})$, where $\Phi(g_c)=e^{-c}$. For this take all Gaussian coordinates below the atom except the one crossing it; at the all-floor vector $v_*=0$ and $\nabla_\nu \log u_{*,i}=e_i/\sqrt\epsilon$. The Mills ratio makes the lower bound asymptotic to $\sqrt{2\log(1/c)}/(2\epsilon)$. The first obstruction rules out even a different transport achieving a uniform centered sub-Gaussian estimate for this variable. It does not alone rule out a direct second moment bound for $u_*$.

**One mark, and an iid Poisson example with total intensity one.** Over $q=r^2$ with $r$ an odd prime, the subfield multiplicative group is a clique of size $b=r-1$ in the localization. The negative-side row $\delta_1+2\mathbf1_{s=-1}/(r+1)$ has coefficient $m=(r+1)/2$ at zero and $(r-\lambda_i)/(r+1)\ge0$ elsewhere. This follows from $2\mathbf1_{s=-1}=1-s-\delta_1$ and $|\lambda_i|\le r$. With the product relation and $bm=n$ this gives exact $\theta_+=b$, $\theta_-=m$. Then $a_1=\log(2(r-1)/(r+1))\to\log2$. Even $\liminf a_2\ge\log2-1/2$ by the harmonic increment bound. In the iid Poisson model, the same bound or the derivative identity gives $|G_n(t)-a_1|\le t/2$: the latter follows from $|d_{N+2}|\le N+1$ and $\mathbb E[1/(N+2)]\le1/2$. Thus

$$\liminf_{r\to\infty}G_n(1)\ge\log2-\tfrac12>0.$$

The law here is exactly the independent law of the dense proof with $c=1/n$. For any fixed $0<\delta<\log2-1/2$, the signed log test of the exact **positive-floor** solution at $\epsilon=\delta/n$, divided by total intensity $t=1$, still has mean bounded below by $\log2-1/2-\delta$ in the limit inferior. This follows from the verified floor error, without using a statement about fixed-depth parity for sparse inputs.

Finite recurrence check: build $\mathbb F_{r^2}=\mathbb F_r[X]/(X^2-d)$ with a nonsquare $d$, enumerate the powers of a generator of the squares, and set $s_j=0$ at the identity and $s_j=\pm1$ by membership of $1-x_j$ in the squares. A direct DFT for $r=3,5,7,11,17,31$ checked inversion symmetry, $\sum_j s_j=-1$, subfield inclusion and feasibility of the stated minus row (float tolerance $10^{-9}$ on its smallest coefficient). For $r=11,17,31$ the explicit lower bound $a_1-1/2$ is already positive, respectively $0.010826$, $0.075364$ and $0.128609$. The asymptotic result above is exact and does not use the float check.

**Actual second moment blow-up relative to $c$ for a sequence of sparse floors.** This uses the exact support-size argument from [rank-partitions.md](rank-partitions.md#an-extensive-defect-at-a-single-mark-over-square-fields). Let $S=\{0\}\cup\{j:\lambda_j=r\}$ be the possible positive support of a plus optimizer for one mark at zero, and let $Y$ be the preceding minus witness in frequency, so $Y_0=m$ and $\eta_r=\min_{j\notin S}Y_j>0$. We have $|S|/n=O(r^{-1/2})$: for nontrivial frequencies $\lambda_j/r$ is the real part of a unit Gauss quotient (up to the unit quadratic Gauss phase). The two lifts give conjugate phases. [Katz, Theorem 9.6](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/Katz-GKM.pdf) bounds their averaged nonzero $h$-th moments by $O(|h|/r+1/q)$; the positive test $M^{-2}|1+z+\cdots+z^{M-1}|^2$ majorizes the indicator of phase one and has average $O(1/M+M/r)$. Take $M=\lfloor\sqrt r\rfloor$. Equidistribution alone suffices for $|S|=o(n)$.

Take $t=1$, $c=1/n$ and $\epsilon_r=n^{-1}\min(1/n,\eta_r/2)$. The event of exactly one jump overall with size $X\in[1,2]$ has probability $e^{-1}(e^{-1}-e^{-2})$, independent of $r$. Translate its label to zero. The floor-gap bound applied to the plus optimizer at $Xe_0+\epsilon_r\mathbf1$ gives $X\log(b/P_0)\le n\epsilon_r$. Orthogonality with $Y$ therefore gives

$$\frac1n\sum_{j\notin S}P_j\le\frac{1-P_0/b}{\eta_r}\le\frac{n\epsilon_r}{\eta_r}\le\frac12.$$

By Cauchy–Schwarz $\sum_{j\in S}P_j^2\ge n^2/(4|S|)$, and $u_*=\sqrt{(X+n\epsilon_r)/n}(P+Q)$ with $P,Q\ge0$. On this event $\|u_*\|_{2,n}^2\ge1/(4|S|)$. Consequently $c^{-1}\mathbb E\|u_*\|_{2,n}^2\gtrsim n/|S|\gtrsim\sqrt r$. The moment bound proportional to $c$ is false with a constant uniform over these vanishing floors, even in the iid model. This argument does not give moment divergence for a fixed *relative* floor $\epsilon=\delta c$.

**Scope still open.** These sparse examples give no failure at prime orders of the signed objective and no iid example with $k\to\infty$, $k/n\to0$. The fixed-density theorem and harmonic modulus do not control that window or the first finitely many $a_k$ as $p\to\infty$. Any extension using only transitivity, the delocalized symmetric involution, and iid input must avoid implying $G_n(1)\to0$ for the square controls.
