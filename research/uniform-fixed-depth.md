# Uniform bounds at each fixed iteration depth

This is a proof draft for a strengthening of the [fixed-depth inertia argument](iteration-parity.md). For every fixed $\tau>0$ and depth $t$, the exact scaled Picard field $\sqrt n\,v^{(t)}$ and the exact scaled averaged field $\sqrt n\,z_t$ are uniformly bounded over field orders $q\equiv1\pmod4$ with $n=(q-1)/2\ge\tau$. For each fixed accuracy, they can also be approximated in sup norm by negative Mellin fields of bounded complexity, apart from a uniformly bounded absolute sum of frequency errors. The argument uses compact-group character estimates to extend the large-weight power-series method to a fixed number of iterations at any positive weight.

The constants here can depend on depth and weight. The [signed majorant criterion](iterate-majorants.md) needs an estimate as depth goes to infinity through the field-first limit. The square-order comparison already forces linear growth in that order of limits for the positive-side repair cost at small weights.

## Generic ranks in a fixed tensor category

Use the pure weight-zero, arithmetically semisimple middle-convolution category from [Katz, Chapters 4–6](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf). Take the full tensor subcategory of everywhere tame objects that are lisse away from $1$, allowing punctual summands at $1$. The tame multiplicative-convolution formulas preserve this class, including mixed products. For generic rank $r(M)$ and convolution dimension $d(M)$ the [generic fiber bound](large-smoothing.md#an-exact-bound-for-even-products) gives

$$r(M*N)\le r(M)d(N)+d(M)r(N),\qquad d(M*N)=d(M)d(N).$$

The rank bound also applies to the mixed products here. At a generic fiber, two nonpunctual constituents have singularities at two distinct points. Their tensor product has no geometric constant summand: such a summand would identify constituents lisse at both interior points and tame at the boundary, which would be Kummer. The tame Euler characteristic on the generic fiber gives the stated bound, with punctual constituents contributing their convolution dimension times the other rank. [Katz, Chapter 19](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf) · [Rojas-León, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1398)

Suppose $V$ is a generator of bounded dimension $d$ and bounded rank, and $R$ is a simple constituent of a tensor word of length at most $m$ in $V,V^*$. Then $r(R)$ is bounded by a polynomial in $m$, with constants depending only on the generator bounds. This estimate is for each constituent, even though the rank of the full tensor product can grow exponentially.

To prove it, realize the arithmetic tensor group as a compact subgroup $G\subseteq U(d)$ with faithful representation $V$. Put $U=(1\oplus V)\otimes(1\oplus V)^*$, $D=(d+1)^2$, and

$$a(g)=D^{-1}\operatorname{tr}U(g)=\frac{|1+\operatorname{tr}g|^2}{(d+1)^2}.$$

Then $0\le a\le1$ and $1-a(g)$ is bounded above and below by constants depending only on $d$ times $\|g-I\|_{\rm F}^2$. This follows by expanding the numerator of $1-a$ and using $0\le d^2-|\operatorname{tr}g|^2\le d\|g-I\|_{\rm F}^2$. Every ball of radius $s\le1$ about the identity in $G$ has Haar mass at least $c_d s^{2d^2}$: a maximal separated set covers $G$ and has size $O_d(s^{-2d^2})$ by packing in the ambient space.

If $\|g-I\|_{\rm F}\le1/(2m)$, the norm of $g-I$ on a tensor word of length at most $m$ is at most $1/2$, by telescoping unitary tensor factors. In particular $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}R(g)\ge d(R)/2$. Let $N$ be an integer of order $C_d m^2\log(m+2)$ with $C_d$ sufficiently large. In the character inner product of $a^N$ with $R$, the ball of radius $c_d/\sqrt N$ contributes at least a constant times $d(R)N^{-d^2}$. The real part of the rest of the ball of radius $1/(2m)$ is nonnegative. The absolute contribution outside it is at most $d(R)e^{-c_d N/m^2}$, which is smaller than half the first bound by choice of $C_d$. The multiplicity of $R$ in $U^{\otimes N}$ is therefore at least $c_d D^N d(R)N^{-d^2}$. The rank of that whole object is at most $N r(U)D^{N-1}$ by the generic fiber bound. Additivity of rank now gives the polynomial estimate on $r(R)$.

The numbers and dimensions of the possible $R$ also grow polynomially in $m$. Their matrix coefficient spaces are mutually orthogonal and contained in the span of polynomials of degree at most $m$ in the matrix entries of $V,V^*$. The dimension of the latter space is at most $\binom{m+2d^2}{2d^2}$.

## An algebra with the exceptional characters included

For each $q$, use the complexified representation ring with basis the simple objects and coefficient norm

$$\left\|f\right\|_{\rm rep}=\sum_M |f_M|(d(M)+r(M))^2.$$

The bound on $r(M*N)$ makes this norm submultiplicative. For example, for two simples sum $d+r$ over the summands with multiplicity, square that sum, and use $d(M*N)+r(M*N)\le (d(M)+r(M))(d(N)+r(N))$. Denote the full Mellin evaluation by $E_q(f)$, a function on the characters of $\mathbb F_q^\times$. On good characters it agrees with evaluating the compact-group class function of $f$ on a Frobenius conjugacy class. A character good for the direct sum of a family remains good for its whole generated tensor category. The complement has size at most twice the rank of that direct sum. At all characters the absolute trace of an object is bounded by its convolution dimension. [Katz, Overview, Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 4.2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf)

Represent a frequency field by a pair $(f,e)$ with actual evaluation $E_q(f)+e$, where $e$ is an arbitrary complex frequency vector. Define multiplication by requiring that the formal representation coordinate and the actual evaluation each multiply separately. Thus the second coordinate of the product of $(f,e)$ and $(g,h)$ is

$$E_q(f)h+E_q(g)e+eh+E_q(f)E_q(g)-E_q(fg).$$

The last term is continuous into $\ell_1$ for the representation norm. For simples $M,N$ it is supported on at most $2(r(M)+r(N))$ exceptions and is bounded there by $2d(M)d(N)$. The other products are continuous for $\ell_1$ on the second coordinate. Likewise the involution on the first coordinate given by the complex conjugate of coefficients and taking convolution dual extends by conjugating the actual evaluation. Its discrepancy with conjugation of the bare Mellin evaluation is supported at the bad characters and bounded in $\ell_1$ by the same dimension–rank estimate.

Take the uniform completion over $q$ of these pairs with a uniform bound on the number, ranks and dimensions of the simple objects in $f$, bounded coefficients, and $\sup_q\|e_q\|_1<\infty$. The norm is $\sup_q(\|f_q\|_{\rm rep}+\|e_q\|_1)$ up to a universal constant to ensure submultiplicativity. The operations above make this a unital commutative Banach algebra. Both the formal compact-group class functions and the actual evaluations are bounded by this norm. The sup norm of a formal class function can be taken on any compact group generated by its constituents, with no change if the generating family is enlarged. For infinite sums it is obtained by uniform approximation, or equivalently on the compact inverse limit of the finite-generator groups.

For an element of the dense finite-generator families let $B$ be the larger of the two sup norms, of its formal class functions and of the actual evaluations, both uniformly over fields. The spectral radius of the algebra element is at most $B$. In fact the first coordinate of its $m$th power has norm bounded by $\mathrm{poly}(m) B^m$. To see this, take the direct sum of the formal constituents as $V$ in the polynomial rank bound. The character of the power is the pointwise power of the formal class function. Each irreducible coefficient is bounded in absolute value by its $L^2(G)$ norm, at most $B^m$, by character orthogonality. The polynomial rank and dimension bounds control the norm of the sum. For the second coordinate of the power, a character good for $V$ has $E_q(f^m)=E_q(f)^m$ and both $|E_q(f)|$ and $|E_q(f)+e|$ are at most $B$. The $\ell_1$ difference there is at most $mB^{m-1}\|e\|_1$ by telescoping powers. At the uniformly bounded number of bad characters, bound $E_q(f^m)$ by the representation norm and the actual evaluation to the power $m$ by $B^m$. These estimates prove the spectral radius upper bound. Every continuous algebra character thus has absolute value at most $B$ on the dense subalgebra, and the estimate extends by norm continuity to the completion. The commutative Banach algebra spectrum gives the same spectral radius bound on the completion.

For a self-adjoint pair $X$, both its formal class functions and its actual evaluations are real. Choose $R_0$ larger than the respective sup squares. The element $(R_0-X^2)/(R_0+4\tau)$ then has spectral radius at most $R_0/(R_0+4\tau)<1$. The binomial series gives a norm-convergent series of even polynomials for $\sqrt{X^2+4\tau}$. This step uses the spectral radius, so it does not require an upper bound smaller than $2\sqrt\tau$ on the norm of $X$.

## Applying the algebra to smoothing

Work in the invariant algebra for the order-two twist by $\chi(a)$ on the formal coordinate, and shift by $\chi$ on the full character arrays. The twist is monoidal for middle convolution and commutes with the involution. Its invariant pairs evaluate to functions on the dual of the square subgroup. For formal objects of positive type, applying $K$ on evaluation is represented exactly by the twist by $\chi(1-a)$ on the nonpunctual part. This uses Fourier inversion and the vanishing stalk at $1$ of the resulting negative type. In the representation norm this step costs at most a factor $4$, since its convolution dimension equals the generic rank of the input. A punctual summand is killed by the multiplier at $1$.

For the exceptional vector $e$ the field $Ke$ is represented by a sum of twisted rank-one negative objects using the columns $K\delta_h$. The cost is $O(\|e\|_1/\sqrt n)$ in representation norm by the [rank-one column identity](large-smoothing.md#an-exact-bound-for-even-products). An unbounded number of such columns causes no difficulty for membership in the completion: restricting this part to fields with $n$ bounded gives a finite-generator family, and the norm of the omitted part goes to zero uniformly as the cutoff grows. Hence the step $K$ is bounded from pairs with positive formal type to pairs with negative formal type. For real evaluated fields, averaging the output pair with its involution preserves the actual field and gives a self-adjoint pair of negative type.

Start from $X_0=0$. If the scaled iterate is represented by a self-adjoint pair with negative formal type, the norm-convergent even polynomials for $\sqrt{X_t^2+4\tau}$ have positive formal type by the [even-product rule](iteration-parity.md#types-of-local-inertia). Apply the bounded step $K$ and add the marked forcing $b_t x$, with $x=Fs/\sqrt n$ and $0\le b_t\le2$ for $n\ge\tau$. This is the exact recurrence for the scaled Picard iterate. The averaged update uses half this step plus half the previous field, and the same marked forcing rule with the current input. Both therefore have a lift in the completed algebra at each fixed depth. The uniform sup bound follows from bounded evaluation. Taking finite-generator approximants in the negative subspace proves the approximation assertion, since the actual evaluation error is bounded by the algebra norm and the approximants have uniformly bounded $\ell_1$ exceptional vectors.

The remaining question is a bound on the growth with $t$ or an estimate on the upper part of the scaled fields that uses the prime hypothesis. The algebra proof is field-general. In particular its constants cannot be treated as a sublinear growth bound for the signed cost.

## The two largest-coordinate repair costs at fixed depth

The stronger approximation also gives a limitation of the [explicit repair](iterate-majorants.md). For each fixed $T,\tau$, its two normalized costs $\sqrt{nW}\,\mathcal M_\sigma$ differ by $o(1)$ as $q\to\infty$, over prime or other eligible field orders. This concerns the explicit majorants. The exact signed LPs can give unequal values.

Put $Y_j=\sqrt n\,z_j$, $\phi(y)=\sqrt{y^2+4\tau}$, and keep the bounded marked coefficient $b_j$ in the averaged update. The exact recursion for $KY_j$ is

$$KY_{j+1}=\tfrac12 KY_j+\tfrac12 b_j(\sqrt n e_0-n^{-1/2}\mathbf1)+\tfrac12(I-J)\phi(Y_j).$$

Off the mark, the scaled cost $\sqrt n C_\sigma=(\sigma Y_T+KY_T)/(2T)$ is consequently a linear combination of $\sigma Y_T$, the finitely many even activations $\phi(Y_j)$ and bounded constants. At any prescribed accuracy approximate the $Y_j$ by the negative objects above with bounded complexity. The $\ell_1$ error bounds let us omit only boundedly many characters to achieve this accuracy pointwise. Replace the even activations on the bounded range by fixed even polynomials in those approximants. Outside the bounded exceptions, each scaled cost is now uniformly close to a real class function $H_{\sigma,q}$ of the compact group generated by the negative approximants. Its Lipschitz constant in the ambient unitary matrices is bounded independently of $q$, since the degrees, coefficient sizes and generator dimensions are bounded.

All good-character Frobenius classes are in the same arithmetic coset of the compact geometric subgroup. This follows from Katz's description of the multiplicative-type quotient, using characters of the base field. The two functions have equal maxima on this coset: the simultaneous scalar $-I$ is in the geometric group by the inertia argument, so multiplying by it reverses the odd input term and preserves the even terms and scalars. [Katz, Lemma 7.1](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf) · [Inertia parity](iteration-parity.md#types-of-local-inertia)

For each fixed tolerance $\epsilon>0$, uniformly in $q$, a positive Haar fraction of the coset has $H_{\sigma,q}$ within $\epsilon$ of its maximum. Indeed use a ball of fixed radius about a maximizing element, the uniform Lipschitz bound and the compact subgroup packing bound. The same holds for a positive fraction of the frequency rows for all large $q$. To see this, test with a fixed continuous cutoff of the difference between the function and its maximum, then approximate that cutoff uniformly by a polynomial on the bounded range. The polynomial coefficients after this shift are bounded uniformly. For each resulting tensor expression the good-character mean is its trace at $1$ up to $O(q^{-1})$ from the bad characters. The punctual trace at $1$ equals the trace of the arithmetic coset representative on geometric invariants, which is the Haar coset average of the character. The remaining middle-extension stalk gives $O(q^{-1/2})$ at the fixed complexity. This proves the required approximation to the cutoff mean, with constants permitted to depend on the tolerance and depth. Deleting the other bounded frequency exceptions has vanishing effect on the mean.

The top-$h$ average of the bulk scaled costs, for $h\asymp\sqrt q$, is now within the chosen accuracy of the coset maximum. The lower bound uses the positive fraction near that maximum and $h=o(n)$. For the upper bound, the omitted coordinates are bounded at fixed depth by the uniform algebra estimate and the recursion for $KY_T$ off the mark. They contribute $O(1/h)$ to this average. Hence the difference between the two bulk top-$h$ averages tends to zero when the approximation accuracy is sent to zero. The two repair sizes $h$ differ by only $O(q^{-1/2})$, which has vanishing effect. Finally the contribution of including the mark in the top sum of the original costs is $(C_{\sigma,0})_++O(n^{-1/2})$ at fixed depth: it replaces at most one bulk value, of size $O(n^{-1/2})$. The two marked costs differ by $z_{T,0}/T=O(n^{-1/2})$. Multiplying by the bounded factor $\sqrt{nW}/h$ proves the claim.

In particular, for $0<\tau<1$ the square-order obstruction to the positive repair carries over to the negative repair in the field-first limit. This implication needs the uniform algebra and trace arguments above. It concerns one sufficient LP certificate and leaves open the behavior of the two LP optima through primes.
