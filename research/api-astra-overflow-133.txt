### New signed upper

Let \(U=Ka\) be the actual zero-floor capped fixed point at fixed cap \(M\), let \(Y=Kb\) be the actual ordinary optimizer row with the same marked coordinate, and retain the notation
\[
C=C_M(U),\qquad h=U-C,\qquad
\mu=\mathbb E_n a,\qquad \nu=\mathbb E_n b,\qquad
\eta=Y_0/b_0.
\]
On the bulk,
\[
b_i=|Y_i|,\qquad
G_i=\frac{b_i-\eta Y_i}{\nu}
     =\frac{(1-\eta\tau_i)|Y_i|}{\nu},
\quad \tau_i=\operatorname{sgn}Y_i.
\]

Define the exact signed endpoint statistic
\[
S_p=
\mathbb E_n\!\left[
1_{\{i\ne0\}}G_i
\bigl(
1_{\{\tau_iU_i<0\}}(2|C_i|+|h_i|)
-
1_{\{\tau_iU_i\ge0\}}|h_i|
\bigr)\right].
\]
By the fixed-point form of the signed identity, \(S_p=\mu-(a_0/b_0)\nu\).

#### Proposition: weighted signed upper

Assume the ordinary pair satisfies the exact zero-floor equations above and the quadratic comparison
\[
(\mu-\nu)^2+\cdots+
4\mathbb E_n[(CY)_-]
\le 2\mathbb E_n(hY). \tag{A}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
S_p\le
2(1+|\eta|)\frac{\|Y\|_{2,n}}{\nu}\,\|h\|_{2,n}.
}
\tag{B}
\]

Indeed, discard the negative aligned-spill term and use
\[
G_i\le \frac{1+|\eta|}{\nu}|Y_i|.
\]
The mismatch contribution is supported where \(CY<0\), hence
\[
S_p\le \frac{1+|\eta|}{\nu}
\left(2\mathbb E_n[(CY)_-]+\mathbb E_n(|Y||h|)\right).
\]
From (A) and Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[
4\mathbb E_n[(CY)_-]
\le 2\mathbb E_n(hY)
\le2\|h\|_{2,n}\|Y\|_{2,n},
\]
while \(\mathbb E_n(|Y||h|)\le\|Y\|_{2,n}\|h\|_{2,n}\). This proves (B). Notice that the aligned and mismatched sets remain in the same signed test; no separate positive-overlap estimate was substituted.

Since the cap deficit is
\[
\alpha_M(p)=4-\mu^2
=\text{(bulk and marked spill energy)},
\]
we have \(\|h\|_{2,n}^2\le\alpha_M(p)\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
S_p\le
2(1+|\eta|)
\frac{\|Y\|_{2,n}}{\nu}
\sqrt{\alpha_M(p)}.
}
\tag{C}
\]

This is a genuine prime-family inequality for the actual optimizer and actual capped fixed point. It is not a reflection or finite-dimensional endpoint comparison.

### Explicit unconditional version

The ordinary entry bound gives
\[
|Y_i|\le \sqrt p\,\nu,
\]
hence
\[
\frac{\|Y\|_{2,n}}{\nu}\le \sqrt p.
\]
Also \(b_0^2=4n+C_M(Y_0)^2\ge4n\), \(\nu\le2\), and
\[
|Y_0|\le\sqrt p\,\nu,
\]
so
\[
|\eta|=\frac{|Y_0|}{b_0}\le\sqrt{\frac pn}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
S_p\le
2\left(1+\sqrt{\frac pn}\right)\sqrt{p\,\alpha_M(p)}.
}
\tag{D}
\]
For \(n=(p-1)/2\), the prefactor is bounded by \(2(1+\sqrt2+o(1))\).

Thus the proposed signed upper follows unconditionally under the quantified rate
\[
\alpha_M(p)=o(p^{-1}).
\]
This rate is not presently available from the fixed-cap mean comparison. The existing energy identities only give \(\alpha_M(p)\to0\) if one first establishes the relevant large-cap unweighted convergence, without the \(p^{-1}\) rate.

### What would close the asymptotic target

A stronger and natural ordinary-support hypothesis is
\[
\sup_{\substack{p\equiv1(4)\\p\ge p_0}}
\frac{\|Y_p\|_{2,n}}{\nu_p}<\infty,
\qquad
\inf_{p\ge p_0}\nu_p>0. \tag{E}
\]
Under (E), (C) gives
\[
\limsup_{p\to\infty}S_p
\le C_M\limsup_{p\to\infty}\sqrt{\alpha_M(p)}.
\]
Therefore the following precise input would suffice:
\[
\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{p\to\infty}\alpha_M(p)=0
\quad\text{together with (E).} \tag{F}
\]
The signed endpoint statistic then has vanishing large-cap upper bound, and the exact identity
\[
\mu-\frac{a_0}{b_0}\nu=S_p,
\qquad \frac{a_0}{b_0}=1+O_M(n^{-1}),
\]
transfers this to the ordinary endpoint comparison. The fixed-horizon averaging errors remain \(O_M(J^{-1})+O_M(J^{-1/2})\), so one takes the prime limit first, then \(J\to\infty\), and finally \(M\to\infty\).

### Remaining gap

The new estimate reduces the arithmetic problem to one of two concrete alternatives:

1. prove the ordinary optimizer support ratio in (E), or a weaker bound
   \[
   \frac{\|Y_p\|_{2,n}}{\nu_p}=o(\alpha_M(p)^{-1/2});
   \]
2. prove the stronger prime-family deficit rate \(\alpha_M(p)=o(p^{-1})\), which would make (D) sufficient without any ordinary-support improvement.

No claim is made here that either input is known. The finite-depth parity results do not provide (E), because the ordinary optimizer is a prime-dependent infinite-depth object.

### Ranked next tests

1. **Highest priority:** numerically and analytically test
   \[
   R_p=\|Y_p\|_{2,n}/\nu_p
   \]
   for the actual ordinary optimizer across primes and fixed \(M\). A uniform \(O(1)\) bound would convert mere large-cap unweighted convergence into the desired signed upper.

2. **Second:** derive a prime-uniform estimate for \(\alpha_M(p)\) from averaged iterates, specifically whether \(\alpha_M(p)\ll_M p^{-1-\delta}\) or even \(o(p^{-1})\) is plausible. Failure at the \(p^{-1}\) threshold would identify the exact limitation of the unconditional bound (D).
