## Result

I do not obtain the required actual-prime bound
\[
\frac{d_U^2}{n}=\omega\!\left(\frac1{T(p)}\right)
\]
for the adaptive zero set. The Fourier-minor reduction is exact, but the available orbit information does not control the determinant weights. A finite prime calculation gives a concrete obstruction to any proof based only on eligible orbit counts.

The precise unresolved quantity is the physical-row leverage
\[
\Lambda_p:=\frac1n\mathbf 1_{U'}^*H'^{-1}\mathbf 1_{U'},
\qquad
\frac{d_U^2}{n}=1-\Lambda_p,
\]
where \(U'\) is the actual maximal independent subset of the actual prime zero columns. The target is
\[
1-\Lambda_p=\omega(1/T(p))
\]
along every hypothetical fixed-gap sequence, for one admissible \(T(p)\to\infty\), with \(p\to\infty\) through primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), and with the sign \(\epsilon\) and floor fixed.

### Exact prime setup

Let
\[
Q=\mathbb F_p^{\times 2},\qquad n=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
and retain the physical mark \(0\). For fixed \(\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), the actual Jacobi sector is
\[
\Omega_\epsilon=\{1\}\cup
 \{a\in Q:\chi(1-a)=\epsilon\}.
\]
The row \(1\) is the physical constant Fourier row; it must not be replaced by an arbitrary row.

The sector sizes are
\[
|\Omega_+|=\frac{p-1}{4}=\frac n2,\qquad
|\Omega_-|=\frac{p+3}{4}=\frac{n+2}{2}.
\]
These follow from
\[
\#\{a\in Q:\chi(1-a)=\epsilon\}
=\frac{p-3-2\epsilon}{4}.
\]

Let \(F\) be the true least-bulk-energy optimizer for this sign, let
\[
S=\{i\ne0:F_i>0\},\qquad U=(S\cup\{0\})^c,
\]
and let \(U'\subseteq U\) be a maximal independent subset of the actual columns \(JE_U\). Put \(k=|U'|\) and
\[
H'=E_{U'}^*J E_{U'}.
\]
All norms below are counting norms.

With normalized Fourier matrix \(M\),
\[
J=M^*1_{\Omega_\epsilon}M.
\]
Since the row indexed by \(1\) is \(n^{-1/2}\mathbf 1_{U'}^*\),
\[
H'-\frac1n\mathbf1\mathbf1^*
 =M_{\Omega_\epsilon\setminus\{1\},U'}^*
  M_{\Omega_\epsilon\setminus\{1\},U'}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\;
\frac{d_U^2}{n}
=
\frac{\det(H'-\mathbf1\mathbf1^*/n)}{\det H'}
=
1-\frac1n\mathbf1^*H'^{-1}\mathbf1
\;}
\]
when \(k>0\), with the empty-set convention giving ratio \(1\) for \(k=0\). If \(k\ge|\Omega_\epsilon|\), the numerator vanishes.

By Cauchy–Binet,
\[
\det H'
 =\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq\Omega_\epsilon\\|I|=k}}
 |\det M_{I,U'}|^2,
\]
and
\[
\det(H'-\mathbf1\mathbf1^*/n)
 =\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq\Omega_\epsilon\setminus\{1\}\\|I|=k}}
 |\det M_{I,U'}|^2.
\]
Thus the ratio is exactly the weighted probability that the selected eligible row minor omits the physical constant row.

## Exact translation-orbit formula

For a \(k\)-set \(I\subseteq Q\), define
\[
E_\epsilon(I)=\{t\in Q:tI\subseteq\Omega_\epsilon\}.
\]
Multiplication of the row indices by \(t\) changes Fourier-minor columns only by unit-modulus factors. Hence \(|\det M_{tI,U'}|\) is constant on the translation orbit of \(I\).

Consequently the determinant ratio is a weighted average of
\[
\alpha_\epsilon(I)
=
\frac{\#\{t\in E_\epsilon(I):1\notin tI\}}
     {\#E_\epsilon(I)}.
\]
The weights are the actual squared minor weights multiplied by the number of eligible translates. Equivalently,
\[
\frac{d_U^2}{n}
=
\frac{\sum_{\mathcal O}
 w_{\mathcal O}\,\#E_\epsilon^{\,\mathrm{good}}(\mathcal O)}
      {\sum_{\mathcal O}
 w_{\mathcal O}\,\#E_\epsilon(\mathcal O)},
\]
with \(w_{\mathcal O}=|\det M_{I,U'}|^2\) for any representative \(I\) of the orbit, after the harmless stabilizer normalization.

The only general orbit estimate is
\[
1\in tI\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\in I^{-1},
\]
so at most \(k\) translates are bad:
\[
\alpha_\epsilon(I)\ge 1-\frac{k}{|E_\epsilon(I)|}.
\]
This is useless when \(|E_\epsilon(I)|\) is small, which is exactly what can happen at high rank.

## Concrete obstruction to an orbit-count proof

At the actual prime \(p=13\), take \(\epsilon=+1\). Then
\[
Q=\{1,3,4,9,10,12\},
\qquad
\Omega_+=\{1,4,10\}.
\]
For \(k=2=|\Omega_+|-1\), consider
\[
I_1=\{1,4\}.
\]
Its eligible translates are
\[
E_+(I_1)=\{1,10\},
\]
and both translated row sets contain \(1\):
\[
1I_1=\{1,4\},\qquad 10I_1=\{1,10\}.
\]
Thus this orbit has
\[
\alpha_+(I_1)=0.
\]

On the other hand,
\[
I_2=\{4,10\}
\]
has
\[
E_+(I_2)=\{1\},
\]
and its sole eligible row set omits \(1\), so
\[
\alpha_+(I_2)=1.
\]

Therefore even at rank \(k=|\Omega_+|-1\), the sector admits orbit classes with fractions \(0\) and \(1\). The actual ratio depends entirely on the squared Fourier-minor weights of the adaptive \(U'\). Counting eligible minors, or knowing only that a nonzero minor exists, cannot give a lower bound.

This finite example does **not** settle the asymptotic actual-prime problem: it does not identify the least-energy optimizer or its \(U'\). It does establish a precise failure of the proposed unweighted orbit shortcut.

## A checked sufficient condition, not presently proved

Let
\[
r_\epsilon=|\Omega_\epsilon|,\qquad \Delta_p=r_\epsilon-k.
\]
If the actual adaptive Gram matrix satisfies
\[
\left\|H'-\frac{r_\epsilon}{n}I\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\eta_p
\]
with \(r_\epsilon/n-\eta_p>0\), then
\[
\mathbf1^*H'^{-1}\mathbf1
\le \frac{k}{r_\epsilon/n-\eta_p},
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{\;
\frac{d_U^2}{n}
\ge
\frac{\Delta_p-n\eta_p}{r_\epsilon-n\eta_p}.
\;}
\]
Thus a sufficient actual-prime criterion is
\[
\Delta_p-n\eta_p
=
\omega\!\left(\frac n{T(p)}\right).
\]
No estimate of this type is currently available for the adaptive \(U'\). In particular, a Weil estimate for fixed small row sets does not address the regime \(k\) near \(r_\epsilon\), nor the determinant weighting.

## Comparison with signed leakage

The exact signed transfer remains a potentially more flexible route. For any \(y\in\operatorname{Ran}J\), with
\[
\ell=E_U^*y,\qquad
H=E_U^*JE_U,\qquad u=\mathbf1_U,
\]
one has
\[
d_U\|y\|_2
\ge
\left|
\langle\mathbf1,y\rangle
-\langle u,H^\dagger\ell\rangle
\right|.
\]
A prime-uniform candidate satisfying
\[
\langle\mathbf1,y\rangle\ge c n,\qquad
\|y\|_2^2=o(nT),
\]
and retaining a fixed fraction of its signed mean after the correction
\[
\langle u,H^\dagger\ell\rangle
\]
would exclude the fixed gap. No such candidate with controlled leakage on the actual \(U\) is established.

The projection-only model from the previous round shows that exact projection algebra, complementarity, and optimization do not imply the required decay: it has a fixed gap while its marked return tail is of order \(m\) for \(T=o(m)\). Hence the missing ingredient must use the actual cyclic Jacobi structure or a genuinely signed cancellation estimate.

## Remaining gap

The unresolved statement is one of the following equivalent actual-prime estimates, for both fixed signs \(\epsilon\):

1. **Weighted Fourier-minor form**
   \[
   \frac{
   \sum_{I\subseteq\Omega_\epsilon\setminus\{1\}}
   |\det M_{I,U'}|^2
   }{
   \sum_{I\subseteq\Omega_\epsilon}
   |\det M_{I,U'}|^2
   }
   =
   \omega(1/T(p)).
   \]

2. **Leverage form**
   \[
   \frac1n\mathbf1^*H'^{-1}\mathbf1
   \le 1-\omega(1/T(p)).
   \]

3. **Signed leakage form**
   Find a genuine \(y\in\operatorname{Ran}J\) supported or concentrated on the actual marked sector for which the signed correction above is \(o(n)\) relative to its mean.

No actual-prime asymptotic estimate of any of these forms is proved here.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: exact adaptive orbit-weight diagnostics

For increasing primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), separately for \(\epsilon=\pm1\):

- compute the true least-energy optimizer \(F\);
- form the actual \(S,U\), then the actual independent \(U'\);
- record \(k\), \(\Delta_p=r_\epsilon-k\), \(\Lambda_p=n^{-1}\mathbf1^*H'^{-1}\mathbf1\);
- group the Cauchy–Binet terms by translation orbit;
- measure what fraction of the denominator weight lies on orbits with
  \[
  \alpha_\epsilon(I)\ll 1/T(p).
  \]

This directly tests whether the obstruction is caused by high-rank orbit concentration. It is diagnostic only unless accompanied by a uniform theorem, but it identifies the exact orbit families that an analytic argument must control.

### 2. Second priority: prove a source-specific adaptive Gram/leakage estimate

Attempt one of the following prime-uniform estimates on the actual \(U'\):

\[
\left\|H'-\frac{r_\epsilon}{n}I\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=o\!\left(\frac{\Delta_p}{n}\right),
\qquad
\Delta_p=\omega(n/T(p)),
\]
or directly
\[
\mathbf1^*H'^{-1}\mathbf1
\le n\left(1-\omega(1/T(p))\right).
\]

If adaptive Gram flatness is too strong, use the signed criterion instead: construct a low-frequency or marked spectral candidate \(y\) from the actual Jacobi operator and estimate the signed correction
\[
\langle u,H^\dagger E_U^*y\rangle
\]
rather than its unsigned norm. This is the most plausible way to exploit the physical diagonal \(K_{00}=-1/n\), which is invisible to the raw orbit-count argument.
