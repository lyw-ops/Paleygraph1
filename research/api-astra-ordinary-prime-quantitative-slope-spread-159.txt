## Checked outcome

Let \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\) run through the ordinary primes in the hypothetical genuine near-critical branch. Put
\[
Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,\qquad n=|Q|=(p-1)/2,
\]
and retain the actual retargeted block with \(b_0=0\), probability law
\[
\nu_a=\frac{b_a}{\sum_j b_j},\qquad
f(x)=\sum_{a\in\widehat Q}\nu_a\,a(x),
\]
the physical favorable sign \(\rho=-\sigma\), and the pin
\[
\sum_{\chi(1-x)=-\rho}|f(x)|^2=O(1).
\]
All norms below are counting norms on \(Q\) or \(\mathbb F_p\). The limit is \(p\to\infty\) through this ordinary-prime family; the marked gap and the actual short block are retained before taking this limit.

Define
\[
W_\epsilon=\{x\in Q:|f(x)|\ge1-\epsilon\},\qquad
\lambda=\min\left(1,\frac{|W_\epsilon|}{\sqrt p}\right).
\]

The proposed modulus
\[
\frac{\lambda^{14}}{\log^2(2/\lambda)}
\ll e+\epsilon+p^{-1/2}
\tag{1}
\]
is not presently proved by the available Mit'kin input. The obstruction is quantitative and identifiable: two separate pairwise Mit'kin bounds lose too much when they are summed over the \(O(\lambda^{-2})\) good \(H\)-cosets.

What can be checked is the following.

---

## 1. Near-unit set \(\Rightarrow\) bounded-diameter subgroup

Assume
\[
\epsilon\le c\lambda^2
\tag{2}
\]
with \(c\) sufficiently small.

Since \(|f(x)|=|f(x^{-1})|\), \(W_\epsilon\) is inversion-symmetric and contains \(1\). For
\[
d(x)=\sqrt{2(1-|f(x)|)},
\]
the multiplicative triangle inequality gives
\[
d(xy)\le d(x)+d(y).
\]
Hence every word of length \(r\) in \(W_\epsilon\) satisfies
\[
|f(x)|\ge1-O(r^2\epsilon).
\tag{3}
\]

Parseval and the critical bound \(\|f\|_2^2\asymp\sqrt p\) imply that every fixed-length product set on which the right-hand side of (3) remains bounded away from zero has size \(O(\sqrt p)\).

If the Cayley graph generated by \(W_\epsilon\) had a geodesic of length \(3m\), then the \(m+1\) one-step neighborhoods of vertices at distances \(0,3,\dots,3m\) would be disjoint. Each has size \(|W_\epsilon|=\lambda\sqrt p\). Taking \(m\asymp\lambda^{-1}\) contradicts the \(O(\sqrt p)\) Parseval bound. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{diam}_{\langle W_\epsilon\rangle}(1)\ll\lambda^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]

Write
\[
H=\langle W_\epsilon\rangle,\qquad h=|H|.
\]
Then
\[
h\ge\lambda\sqrt p,\qquad h\ll\sqrt p.
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, (3) and (4) give
\[
1-\frac1h\sum_{x\in H}|f(x)|^2
\ll \frac{\epsilon}{\lambda^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

The factor \(\lambda^{-2}\) in (6) is important. Thus the proposed \(\epsilon\) in (1) must either mean the propagated defect \(\epsilon/\lambda^2\), or (1) needs \(\epsilon\) replaced by \(\epsilon/\lambda^2\).

---

## 2. Coset concentration and the number of good cosets

Let \(A=H^\perp\subset\widehat Q\). Orthogonality gives
\[
\frac1h\sum_{x\in H}|f(x)|^2
=\sum_C\nu(C)^2,
\]
where \(C\) runs over \(A\)-cosets. From (6), one coset \(C_0\) has
\[
\nu(C_0)=1-O\!\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda^2}\right).
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(f_{C_0}\) be the Fourier component supported on \(C_0\). Using the cap
\[
\max_a\nu_a\ll p^{-1/2},
\]
we obtain
\[
\|f-f_{C_0}\|_2^2
\ll \sqrt p\,\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda^2}.
\tag{8}
\]

For an \(H\)-coset \(\Omega\subset Q\), put
\[
S_\Omega=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\chi(1-x).
\]
The ordinary-prime Jacobi estimate gives
\[
\sum_\Omega S_\Omega^2=O(p).
\tag{9}
\]
If \(\Omega\) has at most \(h/4\) points of the wrong sign, then
\[
|S_\Omega|\ge h/2-O(1).
\]
Consequently, the number of good \(H\)-cosets is
\[
K\ll \frac{p}{h^2}\ll\lambda^{-2}.
\tag{10}
\]

The pin and (8) imply that all but relative mass
\[
t_B\ll \frac{\epsilon}{\lambda^2}+p^{-1/2}
\tag{11}
\]
of \(|f|^2\) lies on a union \(B\) of at most \(K\ll\lambda^{-2}\) good \(H\)-cosets.

This part is uniform over the actual prime family and retains the original physical sign \(\chi(1-x)=\rho\).

---

## 3. What two separate Mit'kin bounds actually give

For one ordered pair of \(H\)-cosets, the two Mit'kin applications would need to give the uniform tail
\[
\#\{u\ne0,1:R_{A,B}(u)\ge t\}
\ll \frac{p^{3/2}}{h\,t^2},
\tag{12}
\]
where \(R_{A,B}(u)\) is the corresponding shifted-intersection count.

The exact useful cutoff is
\[
t\ge t_0:=C\left(\frac{\sqrt p}{h}\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Below \(t_0\), the trivial bound \(\#\{u\}\le p\) already gives the same weak-\(\ell^2\) size. For a single pair one also has \(t\le h\); for \(t>h\) the pair contribution vanishes. The nontrivial interval \(t_0\le t\le h\) requires
\[
h^3\gg \sqrt p,
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
\lambda^3p\gg1.
\tag{14}
\]

Equation (12) gives the pairwise weak-\(\ell^2\) norm
\[
\|R_{A,B}\|_{2,\infty}
\ll \frac{p^{3/4}}{\sqrt h}
=\sqrt p\left(\frac{\sqrt p}{h}\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]

However, the physical correlation of a function supported on a union of \(K\) cosets is a sum of \(K^2\) such pair contributions. Weak-\(\ell^2\) subadditivity therefore gives
\[
\|R_B\|_{2,\infty}
\ll K^2\frac{p^{3/4}}{\sqrt h},
\]
and hence
\[
D_g
:=\sup_{s\ge1}\frac{s^2}{p}
  \#\{u\ne1:|C_g(u)|\ge s\}
\ll K^4\frac{\sqrt p}{h}.
\tag{16}
\]
Using \(K\ll\lambda^{-2}\) and \(h\ge\lambda\sqrt p\),
\[
D_g\ll\lambda^{-9}.
\tag{17}
\]

This is the quantitative loss missing from the proposed \(\lambda^{14}\) modulus.

---

## 4. Consequence for the actual reflected functions

For an anchor \(a\), retain the actual modulation, affine reflection \(x\mapsto1-vx\) with \(\chi(v)=\rho\), and restriction to \(Q\). Let \(g_a\) be the resulting reflected function.

The actual squared-weight phase defect is
\[
d_a=\sum_j\frac{b_j^2}{\sum_i b_i^2}
      \bigl(1-\rho k(j-a)\bigr).
\]
The genuine marked block gives
\[
\mathbb E_{a\sim\nu}d_a\ll e.
\tag{18}
\]
Since the marked arc
\[
I_{\rm arc}=\{a:\rho k(a)\ge1/2\}
\]
has \(\nu\)-mass bounded below by a fixed constant from the retained marked gap, there are anchors in \(I_{\rm arc}\) with
\[
d_a\ll e.
\tag{19}
\]

For those anchors, (11) and the reflection estimates give
\[
\eta_{g_a}
\ll e+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda^2}+p^{-1/2}.
\tag{20}
\]
The cap inequality from the affine-correlation argument is
\[
\mathcal L^2(1+D_g)\log\!\bigl(2\mathcal L(1+D_g)\bigr)
\gg
\min\bigl(\eta_{g_a}^{-1/2},p^{1/4}\bigr).
\tag{21}
\]
Here \(\mathcal L=\max(1,m_{\rm eff}/m_0)=O(1)\) on the genuine critical branch. If one uses the safer uniform choice \(\mathcal L\ll\lambda^{-1}\), then (17) gives
\[
\mathcal L^2(1+D_g)\log(2\mathcal L(1+D_g))
\ll
\lambda^{-11}\log(2/\lambda).
\]
Thus the presently justified pairwise bookkeeping yields the conditional modulus
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\frac{\lambda^{22}}{\log^2(2/\lambda)}
\ll
e+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda^2}+p^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

If one uses the actual critical fact \(\mathcal L=O(1)\), the same calculation improves the exponent \(22\) to \(18\). I have stated (22) because it is uniform with the conservative \(\mathcal L\ll\lambda^{-1}\) cutoff.

The cap branch is valid whenever the left side of (21) is \(o(p^{1/4})\); for (22), this is ensured by
\[
\lambda^{-11}\log(2/\lambda)=o(p^{1/4}).
\tag{23}
\]
If (23) fails, the \(p^{1/4}\) branch itself gives the \(p^{-1/2}\) term in (22).

## 5. What would prove the proposed \(\lambda^{14}\) bound?

The candidate
\[
\frac{\lambda^{14}}{\log^2(2/\lambda)}
\ll e+\epsilon+p^{-1/2}
\tag{24}
\]
would follow from the same argument if one had the genuinely aggregate estimate
\[
D_g\ll\lambda^{-5}.
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed, with the safe \(\mathcal L\ll\lambda^{-1}\), (21) would then have left side
\[
\ll\lambda^{-7}\log(2/\lambda),
\]
which yields (24).

A sufficient arithmetic statement is the aggregate weak-\(\ell^2\) estimate
\[
\sup_s \frac{s^2}{p}
\#\{u:|Y\cap uY|\ge s\}
\ll K^2\frac{\sqrt p}{h},
\qquad K\ll\lambda^{-2},
\tag{26}
\]
rather than the \(K^4\)-loss obtained by summing \(K^2\) pairwise weak-\(\ell^2\) bounds.

The two separate Mit'kin counts in (12) do **not** imply (26): after summing \(K^2\) nonnegative pair contributions, weak-\(\ell^2\) subadditivity gives \(K^4\), not \(K^2\). Therefore (24) is currently a precise unproved strengthening, not a checked consequence of R151–R158.

There is also a notation issue: the subgroup propagation necessarily replaces the raw amplitude threshold by the effective defect \(\epsilon/\lambda^2\). Thus (24) is correctly stated with \(\epsilon/\lambda^2\) unless the near-unit threshold is chosen initially at size \(O(\epsilon\lambda^2)\).

---

## 6. Smoothing rate that is genuinely obtained

The amplitude estimate does give an explicit artificial-convolution smoothing law. For every \(k\ge1\),
\[
n\|\nu^{*k}\|_2^2
=\sum_{x\in Q}|f(x)|^{2k}
\le |W_\epsilon|+(1-\epsilon)^{2k}\|f\|_2^2.
\]
Hence
\[
n\|\nu^{*k}\|_2^2
\ll \left(\lambda+e^{-2k\epsilon}\right)\sqrt p.
\tag{27}
\]
In particular, to make the right side \(O(\lambda\sqrt p)\), it is enough to take
\[
k\gg \epsilon^{-1}\log(1/\lambda).
\tag{28}
\]

For the symmetrized law \((\nu*\check\nu)^{*k}\), its Jacobi mean is bounded by
\[
p^{-1/2}\sum_x|f(x)|^{2k}
\ll \lambda+e^{-2k\epsilon}.
\tag{29}
\]

This is a real quantitative smoothing statement. It does **not** control the original marked moment
\[
S_\rho^{\rm add}
=\frac12\sum_a\nu_a(1+\rho k(a))M_a+O(p).
\tag{30}
\]
The law in (27) changes the anchor distribution and destroys the original coupling between \(\nu_a\), the physical slope \(f(t)\), and the five factors entering \(M_a\). Therefore no conclusion of the form
\[
S_\rho^{\rm add}=O(p)
\]
or even
\[
\sum_{a\in I_{\rm arc}}\nu_aM_a=O(p)
\]
follows from (27)–(29).

The only direct mass consequence is
\[
\nu(W_\epsilon)
\le \|\nu\|_\infty |W_\epsilon|
\ll\lambda.
\tag{31}
\]
That is a nontrivial statement about the physical near-unit slope set, but it is not a weighted upper bound for the marked anchor moment.

---

## Remaining gap

The mission is therefore **inconclusive for the actual moment**.

What is established, conditional on the stated uniform same-\(H\) pair incidence estimate, is:

1. bounded-diameter formation of \(H\) at scale \(\lambda^{-1}\);
2. concentration on \(O(\lambda^{-2})\) good \(H\)-cosets;
3. an explicit pairwise-incidence modulus, conservatively
   \[
   \lambda^{22}/\log^2(2/\lambda)
   \ll e+\epsilon/\lambda^2+p^{-1/2};
   \]
4. the artificial-convolution rate (27)–(29).

What is not established is either:

- the aggregate incidence estimate needed to reach the proposed \(\lambda^{14}\) modulus; or
- a comparison transferring this smoothing or amplitude exclusion to the original coupled quantity \(\sum_{a\in I_{\rm arc}}\nu_aM_a\).

---

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Prove or disprove the aggregate same-\(H\) incidence bound

For ordinary primes, \(H\le Q\), \(h=|H|\ge\lambda\sqrt p\), and \(B\) a union of \(K\ll\lambda^{-2}\) good \(H\)-cosets, test the target
\[
\sup_s\frac{s^2}{p}
\#\{u:|Y\cap uY|\ge s\}
\ll K^2\frac{\sqrt p}{h}.
\]
The exact nontrivial range is
\[
C(\sqrt p/h)^{1/2}\le s\le Kh,
\]
with the low-\(s\) range handled trivially. Success would yield the proposed \(\lambda^{14}\) modulus, after replacing the raw threshold by the propagated defect \(\epsilon/\lambda^2\). Failure would identify the sharp \(K^4\) obstruction behind (22).

### 2. Establish a coupled weighted-band inequality for the original marked kernel

Retain the actual physical marking \(\rho=-\sigma\), the original reflected rows, and the five-weight quantity \(M_a\). Seek an estimate of the form
\[
\sum_{a\in I_{\rm arc}}\nu_aM_a
\le Cp+
\text{controlled error involving }
\sum_x|f(x)|^{2k},
\]
or a dyadic version that retains the actual \(\nu_aM_a\) coupling. This must not replace the original law by \(\nu^{*k}\). A successful inequality would convert (27) or (24) into a genuine \(O(p)\) upper on a nontrivial weighted slice of (30).
