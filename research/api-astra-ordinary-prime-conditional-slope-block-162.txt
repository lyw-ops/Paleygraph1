## Checked result: the proposed block does not yet yield an \(O(p)\) estimate

Work on the genuine selected ordinary-prime family \(p\to\infty\), with
\[
Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,\qquad n=|Q|=(p-1)/2,
\]
the actual marked law \(\nu\), its lift \(w\), and the physical map
\[
r_u(x)=\frac{1-vux}{1-vx}.
\]
All norms below are counting norms on \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) or \(Q\), and the limit is first \(p\to\infty\) along the selected prime family. Any fixed amplitude thresholds are held fixed before taking this limit.

### 1. A concrete moderate slice

Fix constants
\[
a,b\in(0,1),\qquad a+b>1,
\]
and \(\delta>0\). At the four physical vertices impose
\[
|f(1-vx)|^2,\ |f(1-vy)|^2\ge a,
\]
\[
|f(1-vux)|^2,\ |f(1-vuy)|^2\ge b.
\]
For the slope impose a dyadic amplitude layer
\[
p^{-\sigma-\Delta}\le |f(t)|^2<p^{-\sigma},
\qquad |f(t)|^2\le 1-\delta,
\]
where \(\sigma\ge0\) and fixed \(\Delta>0\). Denote this slope set by \(D_{\sigma,\delta}\subset Q\setminus\{1\}\).

The exact five-character graph has Fourier tuple
\[
(a,c/a,b^{-1},d,b/(cd)),
\]
with full column triple
\[
(b^{-1},d,b/(cd)),
\]
and graph sign
\[
\chi(1-d).
\]
The corresponding physical five weights are retained; no replacement by a uniform conditional ratio law is made.

The two-threshold result applies on this slice. Since \(a+b>1\), every nonzero cross-difference between the two physical levels has the favorable sign. In particular, after the already-proved \(O(p)\) exclusions,
\[
\chi(1-d)=\rho
\]
throughout the supported off-diagonal slice, up to the removed exceptional terms. Thus the signed multiplier is not expected to create cancellation here: on the main support it is essentially a fixed sign.

### 2. Exact graph estimate with all five actual weights

Let \(h_j\) be the five physical weights, each equal to \(f\) restricted to its specified physical layer. For fixed \(d\neq0,1\), let
\[
T_d=\sum_{(a,b,c)\in V_d}
 \chi(1-d)\prod_{j=1}^5 h_j(\text{corresponding graph coordinate}).
\]
The exact row-disjointness of the graph gives
\[
|T_d|
 \le \sqrt{p-4}\prod_{j=1}^5\|h_j\|_2.
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is the genuine graph with the column triple \((b^{-1},d,b/(cd))\); it is not the unweighted singular-value estimate applied after replacing the physical weights by uniform densities.

Summing over the slope layer gives the only unconditional consequence currently available from the exact flattening:
\[
\left|\sum_{d\in D_{\sigma,\delta}}T_d\right|
 \le |D_{\sigma,\delta}|\,\sqrt p
       \prod_{j=1}^5\|h_j\|_2.
\tag{2.2}
\]
If
\[
N_j=\#\operatorname{supp}(h_j),\qquad
|h_j|\le p^{-\alpha_j/2}
\quad\text{on its layer},
\]
then
\[
\|h_j\|_2\le p^{(\gamma_j-\alpha_j)/2},
\qquad N_j\le p^{\gamma_j},
\]
and (2.2) becomes
\[
\left|\sum_{d\in D_{\sigma,\delta}}T_d\right|
 \ll
 p^{\,\gamma_5+(1+\sum_{j=1}^5\gamma_j-\sum_{j=1}^5\alpha_j)/2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Here \(\gamma_5\) records the number of slopes in the chosen layer. If instead one first uses Cauchy in \(d\), the same graph information gives
\[
\left|\sum_{d\in D_{\sigma,\delta}}T_d\right|
 \le |D_{\sigma,\delta}|^{1/2}
      \left(\sum_{d\in D_{\sigma,\delta}}|T_d|^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
but no improved bound for the conditional second moment follows from the already-established orthogonality alone. A generic conditional Gram estimate merely reproduces the same obstruction.

### 3. Why this does not cover the selected moderate block

For the fixed high-pair thresholds \(a,b\), the four physical weights are \(O(1)\) on their supports. The two-threshold argument supplies sign information and geometric restrictions, but not a sufficiently small counting support. The slope layer is also not known to have a small enough true weighted mass.

Consequently, (2.3) is \(O(p)\) only under the explicit exponent condition
\[
\gamma_5+\frac{1+\sum_{j=1}^5\gamma_j-\sum_{j=1}^5\alpha_j}{2}
\le 1,
\]
equivalently
\[
\sum_{j=1}^5\alpha_j
\ge 2\gamma_5+\sum_{j=1}^5\gamma_j-1.
\tag{2.4}
\]
The moderate slice has no presently proved bound implying (2.4). In particular, if the four physical layers have effective size \(p^{1/2+o(1)}\), as suggested by the actual near-flat law, and the slope layer has comparable size, then the right side of (2.4) is approximately \(2\), while the fixed moderate amplitudes contribute no positive \(\alpha_j\). The graph estimate is then far above \(O(p)\).

The favorable sign does not repair this: on the \(a+b>1\) block it is essentially constant rather than oscillatory. Therefore the proposed “signed conditional slope” route has produced a valid conditional inequality, but not the required saving.

### Evidence and precise remaining gap

What is proved:

1. The exact ordinary-prime family, actual \(w\)-weights, physical marking, and graph sign \(\chi(1-d)\) are retained.
2. The five-weight graph estimate (2.1) is valid with the exact column triple \((b^{-1},d,b/(cd))\).
3. The two-threshold condition \(a+b>1\) forces the favorable sign on the genuine physical support, after the known \(O(p)\) exclusions.
4. The explicit exponent criterion (2.4) identifies when the graph estimate would imply \(O(p)\).

What remains open:

\[
\boxed{\text{A genuine conditional second-moment/incidence estimate across the slope }d}
\]
that exploits the actual five weights and improves on (2.2). It would need to show, for the fixed slice,
\[
\sum_{d\in D_{\sigma,\delta}} |T_d|^2
\]
is smaller than the generic conditional Gram bound by a power of \(p\), or equivalently establish a weighted incidence estimate for the physical cross-ratio graph. The existing rank-two orthogonality and the ordinary-prime exclusions do not provide this.

This is a scoped failure of the proposed sufficient bound, not a refutation of the asymptotic \(O(p)\) target and not evidence against the existence of the ordinary-prime family.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Weighted slope-incidence second moment — highest priority

For the same fixed \(a,b,\delta,\sigma,\Delta\), test whether
\[
\sum_{d\in D_{\sigma,\delta}}
\left|
 \sum_{(a,b,c)\in V_d}
 \chi(1-d)\prod_{j=1}^5h_j
\right|^2
\]
admits a bound of the form
\[
\ll p^{1+\varepsilon}
\left(\prod_{j=1}^5\|h_j\|_2^2\right)
\]
or, more realistically, a saving by \(p^\kappa\) over the generic conditional Gram estimate. This is the direct test of whether the actual slope marking creates usable cross-fiber cancellation.

### 2. Dyadic physical cross-ratio incidence with the \(a+b>1\) sign constraints

Keep the four physical thresholds and count configurations
\[
(x,y,u):
\quad
|f(1-vx)|^2,|f(1-vy)|^2\ge a,\quad
|f(1-vux)|^2,|f(1-vuy)|^2\ge b,
\]
with \(t=r_u(y)/r_u(x)\in D_{\sigma,\delta}\). Test a bound retaining the actual slope weight,
\[
\sum_{x,y,u}
 |f(t)|^2
 \prod_{\text{four vertices}}1_{\{|f|^2\ge a\text{ or }b\}}
 \ll p^{1+\varepsilon}.
\]
The sign constraints should be incorporated before Cauchy, not after absolute values are taken. A successful estimate here would give the needed \(O(p)\) control without pretending that the conditional ratio law is uniform.
