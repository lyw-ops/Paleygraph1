## Result

Work along the actual ordinary-prime sequence \(p\to\infty\), with the selected near-critical short block \(b\), the physical marking \(b_0=0\), and
\[
\nu_a=\frac{b_a}{nr},\qquad r=\mathbb E b,\qquad a\in Q.
\]
Here \(Q\subset \mathbb F_p^\times\) is the actual multiplicative set used in the construction, \(k(a)\) is the global completed-root coefficient, and \(k(a-b)\) denotes the distinct within-set coefficient appearing after translating by the marked coordinate. They must not be identified.

For the two nonzero additive-frequency signs, the already established formula is
\[
\mathcal F_\pm
=
\frac12\sum_{a\in Q}\nu_a\bigl(1\pm k(a)\bigr)M_a+O(p),
\]
where \(M_a\ge 0\) is the true anchor-dependent squared-correlation moment. The \(O(p)\) term includes the full-field extension, zero-frequency removal, wrong physical sign, \(t=1\), and the fixed Jacobi/Gauss degeneracies.

### 1. The completed-root average

With the normalization in which the completed root is \(q(1+O(\delta))\), the marking identity
\[
B=-\frac{\ell^{\mathsf T}b}{\ell_0}
\]
gives, after dividing by \(nr\),
\[
\rho\sqrt p\sum_{a\in Q}\nu_a k(a)
=
q\bigl(1+O(\delta)\bigr)+O(1).
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{\;
\rho\sum_{a\in Q}\nu_a k(a)
=
\frac{q}{\sqrt p}
+O\!\left(\delta+p^{-1/2}\right).
\;}
\]
The \(O(p^{-1/2})\) term is the normalization/completion error after division by \(\sqrt p\). This is the claimed relation, with the actual \(\nu\), not a uniform or squared-weight replacement.

The same calculation does **not** produce a relation with \(k(a-b)\). The latter occurs only after translating the physical correlation by the actual anchor and remains a separate within-set quantity.

### 2. What this implies for the two signs

Set
\[
T=\sum_a\nu_aM_a,\qquad
K_M=\sum_a\nu_a k(a)M_a.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal F_\pm=\frac12(T\pm K_M)+O(p).
\]
The completed-root identity controls only
\[
\bar k_\nu:=\sum_a\nu_a k(a)
=
\rho\frac{q}{\sqrt p}
+O(\delta+p^{-1/2}),
\]
not \(K_M\). Precisely,
\[
K_M
=
\bar k_\nu\,T
+
\sum_a\nu_a\bigl(k(a)-\bar k_\nu\bigr)M_a.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal F_\pm
=
\frac12(1\pm\bar k_\nu)T
\pm\frac12
\sum_a\nu_a\bigl(k(a)-\bar k_\nu\bigr)M_a
+O(p).
\]

The final covariance term is the unresolved coupled-law quantity. No independence assumption is valid: \(M_a\) is formed from the same physical anchor \(a\) whose law is \(\nu_a\).

The available marginal estimates give only
\[
\sum_aM_a=O(np),\qquad
M_a\ge0,\qquad
\max_aM_a=O(p^{3/2}),
\]
and, using \(\max_a\nu_a=O(p^{-1/2})\),
\[
T\le \max_a\nu_a\sum_aM_a=O(n\sqrt p).
\]
This is not an \(O(p)\) estimate uniformly in the actual range of \(n\). More importantly, the scalar constraint on \(\sum\nu_a k(a)\) does not control the covariance: \(M_a\) could in principle concentrate on anchors where \(k(a)\) has one preferred sign while preserving the same small \(\nu\)-average of \(k\).

Thus the proposed sign coupling is **not sufficient by itself** to bound either favorable form by \(O(p)\).

## Checked conclusion

The new valid joint-law statement is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_a\nu_a k(a)M_a
=
\left(
\rho\frac q{\sqrt p}+O(\delta+p^{-1/2})
\right)\sum_a\nu_aM_a
+
\sum_a\nu_a
\left(k(a)-\rho\frac q{\sqrt p}\right)M_a
+O\!\left((\delta+p^{-1/2})T\right).
}
\]
Hence an \(O(p)\) upper for at least one physical sign would follow from either

\[
\sum_a\nu_aM_a=O(p),
\]
or the genuinely coupled estimate
\[
\sum_a\nu_a
\left(k(a)-\rho\frac q{\sqrt p}\right)M_a=O(p)
\]
together with a suitable bound on \(T\). Neither is presently established.

The actual prime family remains the ordinary primes on the selected near-critical branch; the physical signs are the two additive-frequency signs \(+\) and \(-\); and all limits are taken first along the actual selected block for each ordinary \(p\), then \(p\to\infty\). No replacement of \(\nu_aM_a\) by independent marginals has been used.

## Remaining gap

The missing estimate is a many-slope ordinary-prime bound for the covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(k,M)
=
\sum_a\nu_a
\left(k(a)-\sum_b\nu_bk(b)\right)M_a.
\]
The complete-kernel expansions show where it enters, but the available Jacobi and additive-convolution bounds control \(M_a\) pointwise or after uniform-anchor averaging, not under the correlated law \(\nu_aM_a\).

## Ranked next tests

1. **Highest priority: coupled covariance estimate.**  
   Insert \(k(a)-\rho q/\sqrt p\) directly into the completed three-root kernel and test whether the resulting many-slope sum has an \(O(p)\) bound. The target is
   \[
   \sum_a\nu_a
   \left(k(a)-\rho\frac q{\sqrt p}\right)M_a=O(p).
   \]
   This is the shortest route to controlling one of \(\mathcal F_\pm\), and it preserves the actual anchor law and both physical signs.

2. **Localization stress test on the actual law.**  
   Partition anchors by the phase of \(k(a)\), for example
   \[
   E_\pm=\{a:\ \pm\Re k(a)\ge c\},
   \]
   and compare
   \[
   \sum_{a\in E_\pm}\nu_aM_a
   \quad\text{with}\quad
   \sum_{a\in E_\pm}\nu_a.
   \]
   A valid \(O(p)\) result requires a nontrivial restriction estimate for \(M_a\) on these phase sets. If only the marginal mass of \(E_\pm\) is controlled, the test fails: it does not exclude concentration of \(M_a\) on the favorable phase set.
