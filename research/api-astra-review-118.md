# Review of the common-fiber cold derivative test

The [returned draft](api-astra-overflow-118.txt) prices a common exceptional set for the full truncated signed sum by \(B_F\sqrt{|F|}\,\|C\|_\infty r/(1-q_*)\), where \(B_F\) bounds the scaled truncated outer row on that set. This follows from the counting first norm of the actual inner row restricted to the common set. The price is for the actual sum and does not require a packet model of the inner row at the excluded positions. Its stated exponent condition and interior angular implication are valid under their explicit hypotheses. The [receipt](api-astra-118-receipt.json) records the completion.

The [local exact-inverse estimate](cold-exact-exception-budget-118.md) handles exceptions before truncating. At the same residual scale it allows \(O(n^\beta)\) coordinates using the exact inverse entry bound already required by the starter. The physical summand at the mark is \(O(n^{-1})\) directly. The exact variant and the scaled truncated-entry variant are separate sufficient budgets. Neither analytic error estimate provides the arithmetic complement mean by itself, and additional good-coordinate deletions have to be charged in such a mean.

The finite-word parity calculation uses the common bulk sheet and the established initial and one-step packet rules. It accounts for curvature and both responses: the bulk curvature is even, and the outer column, marked susceptibility, and inner response are negative terms. In this representation the whole product has no geometric punctual summand and uses the fixed base and translated lift pairs. The argument is structural at each fixed finite word, including a growing chosen length at each finite field. It does not bound the actual coefficients or ranks uniformly. The missing input is an actual moving starter and a finite-word mass bound with uniform angular margin on a fixed inner square. The common cold domain remains open.
