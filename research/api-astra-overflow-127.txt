## Result: the sparse-preimage mechanism does not by itself yield a prime-uniform gain

Let
\[
E_{m,j}:=-n\left\langle \delta U_m,\,
D_m(I-\epsilon K)W_{m,j}\right\rangle_{2,n},
\qquad W_{m,j}=Pr_{m,j},
\]
with the exact \(D_m\), \(M_i\), \(S_i\), and \(r_{m,j}\) from Round 125. The adjoint identity gives
\[
n[T(q_{m+1,j})-T(q_{m,j})]=E_{m,j}.
\tag{A}
\]

The sparse-preimage conclusion is stronger than an absolute \(L^2\) estimate, but it still does not imply a one-sided estimate. Precisely, at the cutoff \(q_M^m=O(n^{-1/4})\), let
\[
S_{m,j}=\operatorname{supp}r_{m,j}.
\]
Then
\[
|S_{m,j}|=O_J(\sqrt n),\qquad
\|r_{m,j}\|_\infty=O_J(n^{-1/2}),\qquad
\|r_{m,j}\|_{1,n}=O_J(n^{-1}).
\tag{B}
\]
Since \(K\) has entries \(O(n^{-1/2})\), one also has
\[
\|Kr_{m,j}\|_\infty=O_J(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{C}
\]
Consequently the two pieces of the adjoint test,
\[
E_{m,j}
=-n\langle\delta U_m,D_mr_{m,j}\rangle_{2,n}
+\epsilon n\langle\delta U_m,D_mKr_{m,j}\rangle_{2,n},
\tag{D}
\]
are both potentially of order one under the currently available hypotheses.

### Quantified obstruction

The obstruction is not merely that Cauchy–Schwarz is crude. The scale \(O(1)\) is compatible with all the sparse bounds.

Indeed, suppose for illustration that \(S\) has \(c\sqrt n\) sites, \(r_x\asymp n^{-1/2}\) on \(S\), and \(|D_x|\asymp1\) there. A vector \(\delta U\) with
\[
\delta U_x\asymp 1\quad (x\in S),\qquad \delta U_x=0\quad (x\notin S)
\]
has normalized norm
\[
\|\delta U\|_{2,n}\asymp
\sqrt{\frac{|S|}{n}}
=O(n^{-1/4}),
\]
which is exactly the critical increment scale at the truncation height. But then
\[
n\left|\langle \delta U,Dr\rangle_{2,n}\right|
\asymp
n\cdot\frac{|S|}{n}\cdot n^{-1/2}
=O(1).
\tag{E}
\]
The same calculation applies to \(DKr\), because (C) gives the same pointwise scale. Thus sparsity plus the critical \(L^2\) size does not force \(E_{m,j}=o(1)\), nor does it force a sign.

This is an algebraic obstruction to the proposed mechanism, not a counterexample produced by an actual Paley trajectory: the actual trajectories may have additional arithmetic structure, but that structure has not been extracted in the current estimates.

### Why positivity is unavailable

There is no positivity principle for (D). The cap secant is
\[
D_m(x)=
\frac{\Phi_t(C_M(U_{m+1,x}))-\Phi_t(C_M(U_{m,x}))}
     {U_{m+1,x}-U_{m,x}}
\]
when the denominator is nonzero. In an unsaturated interval its sign is the sign of the intermediate \(U\)-value; across a sign change it can have either sign. Hence \(D_m\) is not a positive diagonal multiplier.

Moreover, the transport term has no fixed sign because \(K\) is a signed Jacobi kernel. The two terms in (D) can therefore reinforce rather than cancel. The fact that the support of \(r\) is the union of the two genuine active sets does not alter this: the active sets are adaptive functions of the same prime-dependent trajectory, and no independent equidistribution statement for them is presently available.

The output deletion and both stationary factors are already retained in \(\mathcal A\), hence in \(r_{m,j}\). They do not supply an omitted positive remainder that could repair this sign problem.

## Exact missing arithmetic statement

A sufficient prime-uniform result would have to control an adaptive weighted correlation of the form
\[
\sup_{\substack{S\subset\mathbb F_p\\ |S|\le C\sqrt n}}
\ \sup_{\substack{|d_x|\le1\\
\|v\|_{2,n}\le Cn^{-1/4}}}
\left|
n\left\langle v,\,
d\,\bigl(r-\epsilon Kr\bigr)
\right\rangle_{2,n}
\right|
=o(1),
\tag{F}
\]
for the special \(r=r_{m,j}\) generated by the actual backward recursion, or at least a one-sided version with upper bound \(o(1)\). The already proved bounds (B)–(C) are insufficient for (F), as shown by (E).

A less ambitious but still adequate statement would be a signed cancellation estimate directly along the actual trajectory:
\[
\sum_{m=m_0}^{L-1}E_{m,j}=o(1)
\quad\text{uniformly in eligible primes},
\tag{G}
\]
or a one-sided bound
\[
\sum_{m=m_0}^{L-1}E_{m,j}
\le o(1).
\]
Without (F), (G), or an independently controlled upper at one endpoint, the telescoping argument cannot contradict the positive fixed-depth lower. The tail estimate after \(L\) only removes the later increments; it does not control the finite signed prefix.

### What has been established

1. The exact adjacent-cap identity (A) remains valid with both true stationary factors, the exact output mask, both adjacent repair trajectories, and the true cap secant.
2. The sparse preimage gives a localized representation (D) with support \(O_J(\sqrt n)\), first norm \(O_J(n^{-1})\), and maximum \(O_J(n^{-1/2})\).
3. These bounds reduce the missing estimate to a genuinely weighted, signed, adaptive prime correlation.
4. They do **not** imply a prime-uniform gain, because the critical increment can correlate with the sparse support at order one and neither \(D_m\) nor \(K\) has a usable positivity property.

Thus the proposed arithmetic estimate is currently **inconclusive but structurally narrowed**: the missing ingredient is not another norm estimate, but cancellation for the trajectory-dependent sparse correlation in (F) or (G).

## Ranked next tests

1. **Highest priority: exact finite-prime correlation test.** For eligible primes, compute separately
   \[
   A_{m,j}= -n\langle\delta U_m,D_mr_{m,j}\rangle_{2,n},
   \qquad
   B_{m,j}= \epsilon n\langle\delta U_m,D_mKr_{m,j}\rangle_{2,n},
   \]
   retaining signs and the two active sets. Test whether \(A_{m,j}+B_{m,j}\) exhibits cancellation, a stable sign, or order-one spikes near genuine cap crossings. This directly distinguishes a viable signed-prefix mechanism from the quantified obstruction.

2. **Second priority: prove a restricted weighted Jacobi bound.** Instead of the impossible-looking supremum in (F), exploit the recursion defining \(r_{m,j}\) and seek
   \[
   \left|n\langle\delta U_m,D_m(r_{m,j}-\epsilon Kr_{m,j})\rangle\right|
   \le C_J n^{-\eta}
   \]
   for some \(\eta>0\), after decomposing \(r_{m,j}\) into its \(M_j\mathcal A\) source and the finitely many transported rows. A bound for arbitrary adaptive sparse supports is likely too strong; a bound tied to these specific stationary-factor coefficients is the more realistic target.
