## Result

For ordinary primes
\[
p\equiv1\pmod 4,\qquad p\to\infty
\]
along the hypothetical genuine near-critical branch, the regular rank-two transform is geometrically controlled but does **not** presently yield the required weighted cancellation under the actual five-draw law.

The checked conclusion is:

> After removing the six finite/infinite trivial-root slices and the three dense-edge boundary resonances, the rank-two kernel has two degree-two eigenvalues of absolute value \(p\), hence
> \[
> |\mathscr K|\le 2p.
> \]
> However, with the true independent weights
> \[
> w_Tw_Uw_Vw_{U'}w_{V'},
> \]
> the remaining outer Jacobi factor contributes size \(\sqrt p\), so pointwise trace geometry gives only an \(O(p^{3/2})\) bound. The needed \(O(p)\) estimate requires a weighted phase saving of \(p^{-1/2}\), which is not supplied by the prime slope-spread or convolution-smoothing inputs.

Thus the proposed transform is not refuted geometrically, but the sufficient bound remains unproved.

---

## 1. Actual law, marking, signs, and lifts

Write
\[
Q=\mathbb F_p^{\times 2},\qquad n=|Q|=(p-1)/2.
\]

The physical marking is the zero Fourier coordinate
\[
b_0=0.
\]
The actual probability law on \(\widehat Q\) is
\[
\nu_a=\frac{b_a}{nr},
\]
and the inverse transform is
\[
f(x)=\sum_{a\in\widehat Q}\nu_a\,a(x),\qquad x\in Q.
\]

The physical sign is the selected favorable sign
\[
\rho=-\sigma.
\]

Every \(a\in\widehat Q\) has two full-field lifts. If \(\eta\) is one such lift, its true weight is
\[
w_\eta=\frac12\nu_{\eta|_Q}.
\]
The five independent physical draws are therefore
\[
T,\ U,\ V,\ U',\ V'
\]
with product law
\[
w_Tw_Uw_Vw_{U'}w_{V'}.
\]

The relevant norm information remains
\[
\max_a\nu_a=O(p^{-1/2}),\qquad
\|f\|_{\ell^2(Q)}^2\asymp \sqrt p,
\]
where the latter is the unnormalized counting norm. The marked condition \(b_0=0\) is essential: it excludes the trivial physical anchor but does not flatten the coupled five-variable law.

## 2. Regular rank-two kernel

For the \(s=1\) branch, use the notation
\[
\beta=\frac VU,\qquad
C=\frac{U'}{V'},\qquad
\alpha=\frac U{U'},\qquad
\delta=\frac T{U'},
\]
and, for the slope lift \(q\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
D=\delta\chi^q,\qquad
A=\alpha\bar\delta\chi^{q+1}.
\]
Then
\[
AD\chi=\alpha.
\]

The rank-two kernel is
\[
\mathscr K(\beta,A,C,D)
=
\sum_{z,y}
\beta(z)\,(\alpha\bar\delta\chi^{q+1})(z+1)\,
C(y)\,(\delta\chi^q)(y+1)\,
\chi(z-y).
\]

On the clean locus
\[
U_0=
(\mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,-1,\infty\})^2\setminus\Delta
\]
with the three blown-up diagonal boundary points removed, all boundary inertias are nontrivial. The tame rank-one sheaf is clean on the resulting normal-crossing compactification. Compactly supported and ordinary cohomology coincide, and affine vanishing plus duality place the cohomology in degree two with dimension \(2\). Therefore Deligne’s estimate gives
\[
|\mathscr K|\le 2p,
\]
and duality shows that both degree-two eigenvalues have modulus exactly \(p\).

This is a genuine rank-two pure trace statement, not merely a square-root upper bound.

The outer factor is
\[
J\!\left((V/V')\chi^i,L\right),
\qquad
L=\frac{U/V}{U'/V'},
\qquad i\in\{0,1\},
\]
and is bounded pointwise by \(O(\sqrt p)\) away from its principal degeneracies.

Consequently the normalized regular summand
\[
\Phi_{q,i}(T,U,V,U',V')
=
\frac{
J((V/V')\chi^i,L)\,
\mathscr K(\beta,A,C,D)
}{p^{3/2}}
\]
satisfies only
\[
|\Phi_{q,i}|\ll 1.
\]

The target is instead
\[
\left|
\mathbb E_{w_Tw_Uw_Vw_{U'}w_{V'}}
\Phi_{q,i}
\right|
\ll p^{-1/2}.
\]
Equivalently, the unnormalized regular contribution must be \(O(p)\), not merely \(O(p^{3/2})\).

## 3. Boundary and resonant contributions

The nine removable contributions are controlled absolutely.

### Six trivial-root slices

The slices where one of
\[
\beta,\ A,\ C,\ D
\]
or one of their two infinity transforms is trivial have total contribution \(O(p)\) for each \(q,i\). The argument uses:

- \(\kappa=\max_\eta w_\eta=O(p^{-1/2})\);
- three independent character constraints whenever a \(p\)-sized Jacobi factor occurs;
- the worst kernel sizes \(O(p^{3/2})\) together with weight \(O(\kappa^3)\);
- outer principal events imposing the same number of independent constraints.

Thus the true five-weight law is retained in these estimates; no uniform character average is substituted.

### Three dense-edge resonances

The remaining boundary resonances are
\[
\beta C\chi=1,\qquad
AD\chi=\alpha=1,\qquad
(\beta ACD)^{-1}\chi=1.
\]
They correspond respectively to the three blown-up diagonal points. Their generic contributions are \(O(p)\), and the principal-product subevents also cost \(O(p)\), using the same three-constraint counting.

Therefore, after the nine exclusions,
\[
S_{\mathrm{total}}
=
S_{\mathrm{reg}}+O(p),
\]
where \(S_{\mathrm{reg}}\) is the five-weighted regular rank-two sum.

This part of the proposed strategy is supported.

## 4. What the ordinary-prime smoothing input actually proves

The ordinary-prime near-unit-slope result gives, for every \(\epsilon_p\downarrow0\),
\[
\#\{x\in Q:|f(x)|\ge1-\epsilon_p\}=o(\sqrt p).
\]

It also gives convolution smoothing: for every integer sequence \(k_p\to\infty\),
\[
n\|\nu^{*k_p}\|_2^2
=
\sum_{x\in Q}|f(x)|^{2k_p}
=
o(\sqrt p).
\]

These are genuine statements for the actual \(\nu\). They do not, however, control the original five-draw regular phase. The reason is structural:

- the rank-two phase depends on coupled ratios
  \[
  \beta,\ C,\ \alpha,\ \delta,\ L;
  \]
- replacing \(\nu\) by a convolution law changes these ratios and destroys the original coupling;
- the actual pair law \(\nu*\check\nu\) retains Jacobi alignment, while sufficiently deep convolutions lose it.

Indeed, for the smoothed law one has
\[
\left|\mathbb E_{\nu^{*k_p}*\check\nu^{*k_p}}
\,\rho k(a)\right|=o(1),
\]
whereas the original near-critical law has mean Jacobi phase tending to \(1\). Therefore convolution smoothing cannot presently be transferred back to the actual regular sum without an additional stability theorem.

There is also a direct obstruction to the most tempting convolution shortcut. For the favorable slope function
\[
h_\rho(t)=1_{\{\chi(1-t)=\rho\}}f(t),
\]
the unnormalized multiplicative Fourier norm satisfies
\[
\max_a|\widehat h_\rho(a)|
\ge (1-o(1))\sqrt p.
\]
Hence a per-\(u\) \(O(1)\) convolution norm is false on the genuine critical scale.

## 5. Why prime slope exclusion is insufficient

The prime-specific spread theorem excludes a family of \(\asymp\sqrt p\) slopes with \(|f|\to1\). It does not exclude an order-\(\sqrt p\) family with amplitude bounded below by a fixed constant \(<1\). Such a family could still contribute coherently to
\[
\sum_{a\in I_{\rm arc}}\nu_a M_a,
\qquad
M_a=\sum_{u\in Q\setminus\{1\}}|C_a(u)|^2.
\]

On the hypothetical critical branch, the marked arc satisfies the lower bound
\[
\sum_{a\in I_{\rm arc}}\nu_a M_a
\gg
\frac{pE^{-1/2}}{\log(2/E)},
\qquad
E=e+p^{-1/2}\to0.
\]
Thus the existing inputs produce a superlinear lower bound on the relevant positive moment, but no opposing upper bound.

The square-field model also shows that the pin, phase alignment, and critical effective support alone do not force an \(O(p)\) upper: there the matching additive class has size \(s^3+O(s^2)\), corresponding to \(p^{3/2}+O(p)\). This is not an ordinary-prime counterexample, but it demonstrates that the current local hypotheses do not imply the desired estimate by themselves.

---

## Precise failure

The proposed rank-two transform succeeds at the geometric stage:

1. both eigenvalues are known to have modulus \(p\);
2. all nine boundary/resonant pieces cost \(O(p)\);
3. the exact five-independent-draw law and the physical sign \(\rho=-\sigma\) are preserved.

It fails at the only remaining decisive step:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No checked relation currently converts ordinary-prime slope spread or convolution smoothing}
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\text{into an }O(p^{-1/2})\text{ phase average for }
\Phi_{q,i}
\text{ under }
w_Tw_Uw_Vw_{U'}w_{V'}.
}
\]

Pointwise rank-two purity therefore yields \(O(p^{3/2})\), while the required bound is \(O(p)\). The mission is inconclusive, not supported.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: determinant-stratified five-draw correlation test

For each ordinary prime \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), compute the exact regular quantity
\[
R_{q,i}(p)
=
\sum_{T,U,V,U',V'}
w_Tw_Uw_Vw_{U'}w_{V'}
\Phi_{q,i}(T,U,V,U',V')
\]
after the nine exclusions.

Then stratify the sum by the exponent matrix of the ratio map
\[
(T,U,V,U',V')
\longmapsto
(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta,L).
\]

The decisive finite test is whether, outside finitely many determinant-zero strata,
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\Phi_{q,i}\mid \text{three ratio coordinates}
\right]
\ll p^{-1/2}
\]
uniformly in the remaining two coordinates, or whether a positive-density determinant stratum has conditional mean of order \(1\).

- A uniform \(p^{-1/2}\) conditional estimate would provide the missing transfer.
- A persistent order-one conditional mean would name a genuine resonant regime that defeats the rank-two strategy.

This test keeps all five actual weights and both slope/outer lifts.

### 2. Second priority: test a bounded-depth transfer from smoothed laws

For fixed \(k\), compare the original regular average with the \(k\)-fold smoothed law:
\[
R_{q,i}(\nu)-R_{q,i}(\nu^{*k})
\]
using the exact ratio parametrization, not a total-variation bound.

The required statement would be a stability estimate of the form
\[
|R_{q,i}(\nu)-R_{q,i}(\nu^{*k})|
\le O(p^{-1/2})+o_k(1)
\]
after normalization by \(p^{3/2}\), uniformly along ordinary primes and the physical marked branch.

This is the less plausible route because the original Jacobi alignment is known to disappear as \(k\to\infty\). A finite computation should therefore first test \(k=2,3\), conditioned on the outer ratio \(L\), and determine whether the discrepancy is already order one. If so, convolution smoothing cannot be used as a transfer mechanism; if not, the resulting conditional identity would be a concrete new theorem target.
