## Result

For the actual ordinary-prime family \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\), with
\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,
\]
fixed sign \(\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), physical mark \(0\), and Fourier constant row indexed by \(1\), the proposed aggregate signed cofactor mass is exactly a reformulation of the existing determinant ratio. It gives no new lower bound without an additional estimate.

Thus the cofactor simplification is **refuted as a sufficient new argument**, while the asymptotic deficit itself remains **inconclusive**.

All norms below are counting \(\ell^2\)-norms on the physical coordinates. The Fourier matrix \(M\) is normalized unitary.

---

## 1. Actual setup

Let \(U\) be the genuine zero-column set of the least-bulk-energy optimizer, excluding the physical mark \(0\). Let
\[
U'\subseteq U,\qquad k=|U'|,
\]
be a maximal linearly independent subset of the actual columns \(JE_U\). Put
\[
\Omega_\epsilon=\{1\}\cup\{a\in Q:\chi(1-a)=\epsilon\}.
\]

Define
\[
H=M_{\Omega_\epsilon,U'}^*M_{\Omega_\epsilon,U'},
\]
and let
\[
c=M_{\{1\},U'}=\frac1{\sqrt n}(1,\ldots,1)^T.
\]
The row \(1\) is the Fourier constant row; it is not the physical mark \(0\).

Removing that row gives
\[
G=H-cc^*.
\]
Write
\[
\mathsf D=\det H,\qquad
\mathsf N=\det G,\qquad
\mathsf B=\mathsf D-\mathsf N.
\]

By Cauchy–Binet:

- \(\mathsf N\) is the total squared-minor mass omitting the constant row;
- \(\mathsf B\) is the total squared-minor mass containing the constant row.

The desired deficit is
\[
\frac{\mathsf N}{\mathsf N+\mathsf B}
=\frac{\det G}{\det H}
=\frac{d_U^2}{n},
\]
where \(d_U\) is the actual distance from the all-ones physical vector to \(\operatorname{Ran}(JE_U)\).

For a fixed-gap subfamily of ordinary primes, with the admissible spectral parameter \(T=T(p)\to\infty\) chosen along that subfamily, the target replacement estimate is
\[
\mathsf N\ge \frac{L_p}{T_p}\mathsf B,
\qquad L_p\to\infty,
\]
equivalently
\[
k\mathsf N\ge \frac{kL_p}{T_p}\mathsf B.
\]

---

## 2. Exact aggregate cofactor identity

For every \((k-1)\)-set
\[
I\subseteq\Omega_\epsilon\setminus\{1\},
\]
let \(v_I\) be the actual cofactor vector for adjoining one row, and let
\[
g_I=M E_{U'}v_I.
\]
Then \(w(I,a)=|g_I(a)|^2\).

Cauchy–Binet gives the exact double counts
\[
\sum_I |g_I(1)|^2=\mathsf B,
\]
and
\[
\sum_I\sum_{a\in\Omega_\epsilon\setminus\{1\}}|g_I(a)|^2
=k\mathsf N.
\]
The factor \(k\) occurs because each \(k\)-row minor omitting \(1\) has \(k\) possible \((k-1)\)-row cofactors.

For each individual \(I\), the actual signed Jacobi-sector identity is
\[
\sum_{a\in\Omega_\epsilon\setminus(I\cup\{1\})}|g_I(a)|^2
=
\frac12\left(
\|v_I\|_2^2
+\epsilon\langle E_{U'}v_I,K E_{U'}v_I\rangle
-|g_I(1)|^2
\right).
\]
Therefore, summing over \(I\),
\[
\mathcal L
=
\frac12\left(
\sum_I\|v_I\|_2^2
+\epsilon\sum_I\langle E_{U'}v_I,K E_{U'}v_I\rangle
-\mathsf B
\right)
=k\mathsf N.
\]

This is the desired “aggregate replacement mass,” but it is exactly \(k\det G\).

## 3. Why the adjugate step is circular

The cofactor covariance is
\[
\sum_I v_Iv_I^*=\operatorname{adj}(G).
\]
Let
\[
A=\operatorname{adj}(G),\qquad
S=\operatorname{tr}A,\qquad
C=\operatorname{tr}(E_{U'}^*KE_{U'}A).
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal L=\frac12(S+\epsilon C-\mathsf B),
\qquad
\mathsf B=c^*Ac.
\]

The sector decomposition itself gives
\[
G=\frac12\left(I_k+\epsilon E_{U'}^*KE_{U'}-cc^*\right),
\]
hence
\[
\epsilon E_{U'}^*KE_{U'}=2G-I_k+cc^*.
\]
Taking the trace against \(A=\operatorname{adj}(G)\), and using
\[
GA=(\det G)I_k=\mathsf N I_k,
\]
gives
\[
\epsilon C
=
2k\mathsf N-S+\mathsf B.
\]
Substitution yields
\[
\mathcal L
=
\frac12\bigl(S+2k\mathsf N-S+\mathsf B-\mathsf B\bigr)
=k\mathsf N.
\]

So the signed Jacobi term is not an independent source of positivity. It is algebraically forced to cancel the cofactor norm and constant-row terms.

### Consequence

Any argument using only

1. \(\sum_Iv_Iv_I^*=\operatorname{adj}(G)\),
2. the signed Jacobi decomposition,
3. \(\|K\|_{2\to2}\le1\), and
4. determinant or trace identities,

recovers the original determinant ratio and cannot prove
\[
\mathsf N/\mathsf B\gg 1/T
\]
without a genuinely new estimate on the actual adaptive matrix \(G\), its inverse, or the actual cofactor family.

This is a limitation of the **cofactor simplification**, not a counterexample to the desired ordinary-prime deficit.

## 4. Norm information alone is insufficient

Even at the relaxed matrix level, positivity and contraction do not force a useful determinant ratio.

Take \(a=k/n\le 1/2+O(n^{-1})\), choose a unit vector \(e\), and let
\[
c=\sqrt a\,e,\qquad
G_\delta=\delta\, ee^*+(I-ee^*),
\]
with \(0<\delta\ll1\). Then
\[
H_\delta=G_\delta+cc^*
\]
remains positive and contractive for \(\delta+a<1\), while
\[
\frac{\det G_\delta}{\det H_\delta}
=\frac{\delta}{\delta+a}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The corresponding signed cofactor terms cancel exactly as above.

This relaxed example does not describe an actual Fourier prime, but it proves that a lower bound cannot follow from the abstract Gram, norm, and contraction constraints alone. The missing input must use actual prime arithmetic or actual adaptive leakage.

## 5. Evidence from the supplied results

The existing results support the following conclusions.

- Whole-row translation isolation gives no lower bound for actual minor weights at the relevant high ranks.
- The macroscopic Gram is not asymptotically scalar in the required regime.
- The actual entrywise-square mixing estimate for \(K\circ K\) does not control the adaptive matrix \(\operatorname{adj}(G)\).
- The exact marked leakage identity already gives
  \[
  \frac{\mathsf N}{\mathsf D}=\frac{d_U^2}{n}.
  \]
- The fixed-gap hypothesis forces a marked low-spectrum window, but no prime-uniform estimate on the actual adaptive zero set has yet been proved.

There is therefore no checked prime-uniform cofactor estimate above \(1/T\).

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Direct actual leakage/capacity estimate — highest priority

For the actual zero set \(U\), with the physical mark \(0\) retained separately, prove along the fixed-gap ordinary-prime family
\[
n-\langle u,H^\dagger u\rangle
\ge \frac{L_p n}{T_p},
\qquad
u=\mathbf1_U,
\qquad L_p\to\infty.
\]
For the independent reduction \(U'\), this is
\[
n-\langle u,H^{-1}u\rangle
\ge \frac{L_p n}{T_p}.
\]

Since
\[
\frac{d_U^2}{n}
=
\frac{\mathsf N}{\mathsf D}
=
1-\frac{\langle u,H^\dagger u\rangle}{n},
\]
this directly yields the required deficit, with the correct physical mark, mean scale, norm scale, and order of limits
\[
p\to\infty\ \text{through the fixed-gap ordinary primes},\qquad T_p\to\infty.
\]

This is the cleanest non-circular route.

### 2. Actual cofactor-weighted signed cancellation, with the true critical block

Measure
\[
C_p=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(E_{U'_p}^*K E_{U'_p}\operatorname{adj}(G_p)\right)
\]
with the actual \(U'_p\), rather than replacing \(\operatorname{adj}(G_p)\) by a scalar or using only its trace.

A first diagnostic is whether
\[
\epsilon C_p
\ge -\eta\,\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{adj}(G_p)
\]
holds with some \(\eta<1/2\) uniformly on the ordinary-prime family. Because
\[
\mathsf B_p
=c_p^*\operatorname{adj}(G_p)c_p
\le \frac{k_p}{n}\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{adj}(G_p)
\le \left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{adj}(G_p),
\]
such a bound would force a nontrivial cofactor deficit at least at the \(1/k_p\) scale. It beats the desired \(1/T_p\) scale whenever \(T_p/k_p\to\infty\); otherwise the estimate must be refined at the true \(k_p/T_p\) scale.

In parallel, the remaining moderate critical block must be tested with the actual slope-weighted source measure
\[
\mu_p(B)=\frac1n\|\mathbf1_B(A_p)g_{S,p}\|_2^2,
\]
not with an unweighted spectral trace. The relevant test is a prime-uniform bound on
\[
\int_{0<\lambda\le A_0/T_p}\lambda^{-2}\,d\mu_p(\lambda)
\]
or an equivalent direct signed-tail estimate.

---

## Final status

The actual signed cofactor formula is correct, but its aggregate use is exactly
\[
\text{replacement mass}=k\det G.
\]
The adjugate identity and the signed Jacobi decomposition therefore do not supply the missing lower bound; they merely rewrite the original Fourier-minor deficit.

The ordinary-prime asymptotic target remains open. The best next route is the actual marked leakage estimate; the cofactor route requires a genuinely new prime-uniform estimate for the adaptive cofactor covariance or for the true slope-weighted critical block.
