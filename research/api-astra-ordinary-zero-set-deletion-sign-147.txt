## Result

For the actual ordinary prime family
\[
p\to\infty,\qquad p\ \text{odd prime},\qquad n=(p-1)/2,
\]
fix the floor \(0<\tau<1\) first. Use the true zero-start marked orbit, with \(T=T(p)\to\infty\) chosen inside the simultaneous marked window containing \(T+1\), and use normalized averaging
\[
\mathbb E f=\frac1n\sum_{i\in G}f_i,\qquad G=\widehat{(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2}.
\]
Only after the prime limit is taken may one send \(\tau\downarrow0\). Let \(L=L(p)\ge0\) satisfy \(L=o(T)\).

Let \(F\) be a true nonnegative mean-one optimizer on sign \(s\), with root \(q_s=F_0\), and let \(R\) be a true nonnegative mean-one optimizer on the opposite sign \(-s\), with root \(q_{-s}\). Thus
\[
q_s q_{-s}=n.
\]

Define
\[
D_i=1_{\{i\ne0\}}(F_i-L)_+.
\]

Then the exact opposite-sector orthogonality gives
\[
\mathbb E(FR)=1=\frac{F_0R_0}{n}.
\]
Since \(F,R\ge0\), it follows that
\[
F_iR_i=0\qquad(i\ne0).
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{supp}D\subseteq\{i\ne0:R_i=0\}. \tag{A}
\]

This is exact and is stronger than support in an ambient forbidden arc. The deleted tail really lies on the genuine opposite zero set.

For the marked score, with \(\sigma=-s\),
\[
\Xi
 =Z-\mathbf1-\frac{P_\sigma g_T}{2TM}
 =U_\sigma-\mathbf1-\frac{g_T-\mathbb Eg_T}{2TM}
 \in V_\sigma,
\]
where \(g_T\) is the actual terminal increment and \(G_T=\sqrt n\,g_T\). The exact clipping identity is
\[
\mathcal R_{F'}
=
-\mathbb E(D\Xi)-\frac{d}{n-1}\Xi_0,
\qquad d=\mathbb ED. \tag{B}
\]
The root-restoration term is \(O_\tau(n^{-1/2})\), uniformly in \(F\) and \(L\), because \(\Xi_0=O_\tau(\sqrt n)\).

Thus the desired one-sided deleted-tail estimate is precisely
\[
\mathbb E(D\Xi)\ge-o(1) \tag{C}
\]
for at least one actual optimizer \(F\), one admissible \(T(p)\), and some \(L=o(T)\).

## What the exact zero set does and does not force

The exact zero-set fact (A) does **not** force (C). Indeed \(R\in U_{-s}\) and \(\Xi\in V_{-s}\), so \(R\) and \(\Xi\) lie in the same opposite sector. Orthogonality gives no cancellation of
\[
\mathbb E(1_{\{R=0\}}\,\Xi).
\]
The pointwise constraints \(F\ge0\) and \(R\ge0\) only locate \(D\); they do not determine the sign of \(\Xi\) on that set.

The only unconditional norm estimate currently available is
\[
|\mathbb E(D\Xi)|
 \le \|D\|_{2,n}\|\Xi\|_{2,n}
 \le C_\tau\sqrt T\,\|D\|_{2,n}. \tag{D}
\]
This is not \(o(1)\) under the permitted hypotheses \(L=o(T)\). In particular, exact support on \(R=0\) supplies no quantitative decay unless one proves an additional prime-sensitive estimate for either the weighted signed measure \(D\Xi\) or the actual zero-set geometry.

The layer representation makes the missing input explicit:
\[
\mathbb E(D\Xi)
 =
\int_L^\infty
 \mathbb E\!\left[1_{\{i\ne0,\ F_i>u\}}\Xi_i\right]\,du. \tag{E}
\]
Every layer is supported on the genuine zero set of \(R\), but none is known to have nonnegative signed pairing with \(\Xi\).

The existing prime estimates on opposite autocorrelations and difference multiplicities control unsigned quantities such as
\[
\sum_h C_F(h)C_R(h),
\]
or localized positive hinge masses. They do not control the \(\Xi\)-weighted sign in (E). Replacing the genuine set \(\{R=0\}\) by an unrestricted support mask would therefore be a substantive strengthening, not a valid consequence of the current estimates.

## Square control

For square orders \(q=r^2\), the same exact support identity and clipping algebra hold, with the physical marking in the \(\sigma=-\) window testing the \(s=+\) ordinary row and root \(q_s=r-1\). The known square terminal-source lower bound is a control showing that the terminal statistic can remain macroscopically positive on the square family.

It does **not** establish the missing prime estimate: no prime/square transfer of the signed quantity \(\mathbb E(D\Xi)\) is known. Thus the square result is a control against silently assuming universal favorable terminal behavior, not an obstruction uniform over ordinary primes.

## Status

**Inconclusive, with a precise obstruction.**

What is proved:

1. The deleted tail of every true optimizer is supported exactly on the true opposite zero set.
2. The clipping residual reduces to the signed pairing (C), up to \(O_\tau(n^{-1/2})\).
3. The available \(L^2\) estimate only gives (D), which is too weak.
4. Opposite-sector orthogonality does not sign the pairing because \(R\) and \(\Xi\) occupy the same sector.

What remains open is a genuinely prime-sensitive bound of one of the forms
\[
\mathbb E(D\Xi)\ge-o(1),
\]
for one selected true optimizer, or
\[
\int_L^\infty
 \mathbb E\!\left[1_{\{F>u\}}\Xi\right]\,du\ge-o(1),
\]
with the bulk/root exclusions retained exactly.

### Ranked next tests

1. **Weighted active-row test on the true zero set.**  
   For a selected true optimizer \(F\), opposite optimizer \(R\), and \(T(p)\), expand \(\Xi\) in its real Fourier rows and test whether the actual mask
   \[
   I_R=\{i\ne0:R_i=0\}
   \]
   satisfies a prime-uniform estimate
   \[
   \left|
   \mathbb E\!\left[
     1_{I_R}(F-L)_+\Xi
   \right]\right|
   =o(1)
   \]
   or, preferably, a one-sided lower bound. The test must retain the physical weights of \(\Xi\), not merely the support or difference multiplicities. This is the most direct route to (C).

2. **Uniform obstruction test over the true optimizer face.**  
   Define
   \[
   M_{p,T,L}
   =
   \sup_{F\in\mathcal F_s^{\rm opt}}
   \mathbb E\!\left[1_{\{i\ne0\}}(F_i-L)_+\Xi_i\right].
   \]
   Search for an ordinary-prime subsequence, with fixed \(\tau\), \(T(p)\) in the marked window, and \(L=o(T)\), on which
   \[
   M_{p,T,L}\le-c_{\tau}<0.
   \]
   Such a bound would genuinely refute the favorable deleted-tail route. Any analogous negative result on squares should be recorded separately as a square control and not treated as a prime obstruction.
