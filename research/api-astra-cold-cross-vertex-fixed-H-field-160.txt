## Result

For the stipulated family—eligible primes \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\), \(n=(p-1)/2\), the original complex square, both lifts, physical marking \(t_0=n\) and \(t_i=t\) off zero, and the translated \(P_{5000}\) norm—the available checked arithmetic gives a genuine common constituent at the two actual heights \(h=601,602\), but not a substantially signed cross-vertex contraction.

The common constituents are the fixed rank-four and rank-next fields
\[
F_4,\qquad G,
\]
appearing in both decompositions
\[
H_h=J_h+\gamma F_4+\delta G,\qquad h=601,602.
\]

Here \(J_h\) contains the other fixed centers and the genuine height-\(h\) Picard residual. The coefficients \(\gamma,\delta\) are the same prescribed coefficients at both heights; the height dependence is entirely in \(J_h\).

### Common arithmetic label before transport

At a fixed full rank-three, Jacobi, and Legendre label \(\lambda\), let
\[
x=T_{1,\lambda},\qquad y=T_{3,\lambda}.
\]
The exact scalar contributions
\[
xJ_h,\qquad 3y\gamma^2F_4^2J_h,\qquad 3y\delta^2G^2J_h
\]
have the same arithmetic label. The factors \(3\) count the possible position of \(J_h\).

The genuine normalized ordered-pair units are

\[
F_4^2:\quad \frac18,\qquad
G^2:\quad \frac1{16},
\]
with trivial Jacobi exponent on both lifts. Hence the common scalar coefficient is exactly
\[
x+3\left(\frac{\gamma^2}{8}+\frac{\delta^2}{16}\right)y.
\]

This is an actual arithmetic merger, not a formal comparison of independent \(H_{601}\) and \(H_{602}\).

### Effect of the two physical \(K\)-transports

The physical transport is the same real symmetric operator \(K\) at both vertices, with
\[
K^2=I-P_1.
\]
Therefore equal incoming arithmetic labels remain equal after the generic part of either \(K\)-transport. The two-lift arithmetic decomposition and the physical mark correction do not destroy the common label: the marked column is supported at the prescribed physical coordinate \(t_0=n\), while the exceptional finite-field corrections remain explicit \(Ke_0\)-type columns and are charged separately.

The relevant exact rank-four transport identity has the form
\[
K(C_1W)
 =
F_4-\frac{n(1+q+h_q)}{2q^2}Ke_0,
\qquad |h_q|\le 2q,
\]
and the corresponding rank-next identity is
\[
K(C_1F_4)=-G+D_qKe_0
\]
with bounded \(D_q\). Thus the generic \(F_4\) and \(G\) output labels are genuine common transported labels; the \(Ke_0\) corrections are not part of that common label and must remain separately priced.

Applying the same label collection at both physical vertices gives the certified local saving reported in the scalar-unit replay:
\[
\text{saving}>2.9\cdot10^{-7}
\]
in at least one retained coefficient, using the actual amplitudes in the separate \(h=601\) and \(h=602\) moduli. The resulting full positive comparison remains
\[
\left\|A_{602}A_{601}-B_3^2\right\|_{P_{5000}}
<0.66348896
\]
eventually along the stated eligible-prime family.

## What this does and does not prove

It proves:

1. \(F_4\) and \(G\) are named true constituents shared by both actual heights.
2. Their squared ordered-pair units have the exact common scalar label above.
3. The common label survives the generic parts of both physical \(K\)-transports.
4. The marked and exceptional transport corrections can be retained separately without invalidating the local saving.
5. The saving is quantitatively real but only of order \(10^{-7}\) in the checked retained coefficient.

It does **not** prove a cross-vertex signed cancellation involving the actual height residuals. The obstruction is structural:

\[
H_{602}-H_{601}=J_{602}-J_{601}.
\]

The shared \(F_4\) and \(G\) pieces cancel in this difference, so they provide common labels but do not relate the two genuine Picard residuals. In the composed difference
\[
A_{602}A_{601}-B_3^2
 =
E_{602}A_{601}+B_3E_{601},
\]
the remaining \(J_{601}\) and \(J_{602}\) factors occur in different vertex positions. No checked identity currently identifies their full transported arithmetic labels, including their physical \(Ke_0\) corrections and the actual marking coefficients. Consequently the common \(F_4/G\) labels yield only the already-recorded local debit, not the required nonlinear bridge to the \(0.51961239\) target.

The positive-comparison obstruction remains: with formal \(H_h\)-degrees separated, exact \(W\)-collection alone cannot reduce the relevant retained comparison to the target. A finite-prefix or failed sufficient bound does not settle the signed asymptotic problem.

## Hypotheses and order of limits

All statements above use:

- primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) in the stipulated eligible-prime family;
- \(n=(p-1)/2\);
- both arithmetic lifts;
- the original complex square;
- physical marking \(t_0=n\), \(t_i=t\) for \(i\ne0\);
- the actual heights \(601\) and \(602\);
- the translated \(P_{5000}\) norm;
- exact physical \(K\)-transport, with \(Ke_0\) and other exceptional columns retained;
- first fixed heights \(601,602\), then \(p\to\infty\) through the eligible primes.

The inequality is therefore an eventual finite-field statement in the prime limit, not a statement obtained by first replacing the actual residuals with independent formal centers.

## Precise remaining gap

A successful next step must produce one of the following:

1. an exact common full output label for a term containing \(J_{601}\) at one vertex and \(J_{602}\) at the other, including both physical transports and all exceptional columns; or
2. a signed identity expressing a nontrivial part of \(J_{602}-J_{601}\) through the common \(F_4/G\) fields or another nonlinear invariant, with a norm estimate strong enough to close the gap from \(0.66348896\) to \(0.51961239\).

Neither is currently established.
