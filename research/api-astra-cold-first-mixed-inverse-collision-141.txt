## Result

For the eligible family \(q=p\) and \(q=p^{2}\) with \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\), let  
\[
G=\mathbb F_q^{\times 2},\qquad n=|G|=(q-1)/2,
\]
retain the physical marked seed
\[
S=2\sqrt n\,K e_0,\qquad t_0=n,
\]
and use the original closed square. The mixed first-seed/\(Y\)-term does **not** become lisse at \(x=-1\) after subtracting the pure comparator and symmetrizing the two mixed orders.

More precisely, its parameter-local vanishing-cycle class contains the rank-two Legendre class pulled back by
\[
\lambda=x^2,
\]
together with its arithmetic quadratic mate. Both mates have the same nontrivial unipotent inertia at \(x=-1\). The exceptional seed correction is lisse and therefore cannot remove this class.

Thus the pure subtraction removes the \(a^{2}\)-pure obstruction, but it does not remove the first genuine mixed obstruction.

This is a local result only. It does **not** yet establish a weighted whole-operator price below the frozen budget.

---

## Local calculation

Write the first seed row, including its marked correction, in the linearized normalization as
\[
d_S(v)=a\,s(v)+b,\qquad
a=\frac1{\sqrt{tn}},\qquad
b=\frac{a-1/n}{n},
\]
away from the closed-square boundary point \(v=1\). Here
\[
s(v)=\chi(1-v),
\]
and the \(b\)-term is exactly the inverse-Fourier image of the exceptional change
\[
S_0=-\frac2{\sqrt n}
\]
relative to the bulk prescription.

The \(Y\)-part of \(KC_2\) is the rank-two Legendre object; the remaining term
\[
-\frac{2\sqrt q}{q}C_1
\]
is lisse at the collision and is irrelevant to the vanishing-cycle class. The inverse-line kernel for two rows is
\[
s(x)\sum_{u\in G}s(x/u)\,d_1(u)d_2(x^2/u).
\]

For the order
\[
(d_1,d_2)=(d_S,d_Y),
\]
the collision is
\[
(x,u)=(-1,1).
\]
At this point
\[
x/u=-1,\qquad x^2/u=1.
\]
Therefore the seed contributes the nonzero scalar
\[
d_S(-1)=a\,\chi(2)+b
\]
in the leading seed normalization, while the \(Y\)-factor is evaluated at its singular parameter \(1\). The local contribution is consequently
\[
\bigl(a\chi(2)+b\bigr)\,
\bigl(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Leg}}\circ[x\mapsto x^2]\bigr)
\]
up to the fixed nonzero normalization of the \(Y\)-summand.

The two quadratic mates arise from the square restriction. They are the Legendre local system and its quadratic arithmetic mate. At \(x=-1\), the square map \(x\mapsto x^2\) is étale, and the mate is unramified at the collision. Hence both have the same nontrivial unipotent inertia. Their coefficients agree at \(u=1\), since the quadratic factor takes the value \(1\) there. They therefore add rather than cancel.

The reverse order is different:
\[
(d_1,d_2)=(d_Y,d_S).
\]
At the same collision it contains
\[
d_S(x^2/u)=d_S(1).
\]
For the original closed square, the physical marked row satisfies
\[
d_S(1)=0.
\]
Consequently the reverse mixed order has no corresponding Legendre vanishing-cycle contribution at this collision. Symmetrization therefore leaves the first-order class rather than cancelling it.

The exceptional correction \(b\), and the \(C_1\)-correction in
\[
KC_2=Y-\frac{2\sqrt q}{q}C_1,
\]
are lisse near \(x=-1\). They alter the scalar coefficient but cannot cancel the unipotent inertia. Unless the accidental scalar identity
\[
a\chi(2)+b=0
\]
holds, the mixed class is nonzero. For the actual seed normalization this is not an identity in the prime family; at most it could occur at isolated finite parameters.

## What pure subtraction does and does not remove

The pure leading term has the local class
\[
-a^2\bigl(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Leg}}\circ[x\mapsto -1/x]\bigr)
\]
from the formula in the supplied pure calculation. The square difference subtracts this pure comparator, so that class is removed.

The mixed term instead has the class
\[
\boxed{
\bigl(a\chi(2)+b\bigr)
\left[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Leg}}\circ[x\mapsto x^2]
\;+\;
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Leg}}^{\mathrm{quad}}\circ[x\mapsto x^2]
\right]
}
\]
up to the common nonzero \(Y\)-normalization and the physical symmetrization factor. This is a different parameter-local object. Pure subtraction does not touch it.

The collision is in the parameter \(x\), not in the global convolution variable \(z\). The usual argument that
\[
H_c^2=0
\]
from nontrivial inertia at \(z=1\) remains valid. It does not imply lissity in the parameter \(x\), nor does it remove the \(x=-1\) nearby-cycle contribution.

## Hypotheses and evidence

### Hypotheses used

1. \(q=p\) or \(p^2\), with \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), and \(G=\mathbb F_q^{\times2}\).
2. The physical mark is retained: \(S=2\sqrt n\,Ke_0\), \(t_0=n\).
3. The \(Y\)-part means the rank-two Legendre component of \(KC_2\), with the displayed \(C_1\)-correction kept separately.
4. The original closed square is used, so the physical seed row vanishes at the boundary position \(1\).
5. The square restriction retains both arithmetic quadratic mates.

### Evidence

- The local convolution coordinate is \(u=x/z\), with the second parameter \(x^2/u\).
- At \((x,u)=(-1,1)\), the forward mixed order sees \(d_S(-1)\) against the Legendre singularity at parameter \(1\).
- The reverse order sees the physical boundary value \(d_S(1)=0\).
- \(x\mapsto x^2\) is étale at \(-1\), so it preserves the nontrivial unipotent inertia.
- Arithmetic quadratic twisting is unramified at the collision and does not change that inertia.
- Exceptional seed and \(C_1\) terms are lisse corrections, not vanishing-cycle terms.

## Remaining gap

The requested **weighted whole-operator price below the frozen budgets** has not been proved. The local calculation gives a surviving mixed rank-two class, but it does not by itself provide a lower bound for the full price or exclude cancellation with other mixed words at the global packet level.

The correct order of limits also remains essential:

1. Fix the mixed word and its physical normalization.
2. Take \(q\to\infty\) through \(q=p,p^2\) in the eligible prime family.
3. Only afterward sum analytic words or increase Picard depth.
4. Any subsequent \(\tau\downarrow0\) limit must come last.

A finite-prime check or the nonvanishing of this one word does not settle the asymptotic full-operator target.

## Ranked next tests

1. **Rank 1 — exact packet-level cancellation test.**  
   Expand all first mixed words containing \(S\) and the \(Y\)-part, including both quadratic mates, the \(C_1\)-correction, the exceptional seed term, and both orderings. Collect equal parameter-local Legendre representations before taking absolute values. The decisive test is whether the coefficient of the displayed \(x^2\)-pulled-back unipotent class remains nonzero after this complete collection.

2. **Rank 2 — weighted budget certification.**  
   Assign the surviving mixed class its actual convolution dimension and arithmetic multiplicity, then insert it into the existing \(4.8\)-type weighted norm estimate for the whole inverse-line operator. A strict bound below the frozen budget would establish compatibility with the packet scheme; failure would refute only that pricing prescription, not the underlying geometric collision.
