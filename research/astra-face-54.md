# Coset capacities on the true prime faces and an exact finite quantile comparison

At an eligible prime let $Q=\mathbb F_p^{\times2}$, $n=(p-1)/2$, $G=\widehat Q$, and $s(x)=\chi(1-x)$. Write $D=\{s=-\sigma\}$ for the forbidden positions of a sign-$\sigma$ endpoint probability. A coset of $B^\perp$ for an **odd** order subgroup $B\le Q$ with $b=|B|\ge(1/2+\eta)\sqrt p$ has mass at most $1/2-c_\eta$ on every such probability, for fixed $\eta>0$ and all sufficiently large eligible primes. This holds for either sign and every coset. On the actual least-norm larger-theta face representative, the coordinates attaining the point cap carry at most half the total mass at every sufficiently large prime, including when the mark approaches one. Exact rooted cold moments and face support alone still admit upper-tail optimizers with $1-o(1)$ mass at the cap. These restrictions give no all-prime estimate yet for unrestricted score level sets.

## A strict coset capacity on both entire faces

Set $k=|D\cap B|$ and $c=\chi(2)$. For odd $b$ one has the finite bound
\[
                 6k\ \ge\ 2(b-1)+c-\sqrt p.                       \tag{1}
\]
Indeed $-1\notin B$, and the convolution matrix $s(y/x)$ on $Q$ has norm at most $\sqrt p$ by the Jacobi bound. Compress it to the normalized indicators of $B,-B$. Its diagonal entries are $a=\sigma(b-1-2k)$ and its off-diagonal entries are $d=\sum_{x\in B}s(-x)$. Away from $1$, $s(-x)=s(x)s(x^2)$, so $d=b-1+c-2h$, where $h$ counts the disagreement with the squared input. Squaring permutes $B\setminus\{1\}$ and $h\le2k$. The compressed norm $|a|+|d|$ is at least $\sigma a+d\ge2(b-1)+c-6k$, proving (1). [Jacobi normalization](fourier.md) · [Related doubled-coset compression](astra-coset-48.md)

Let $\delta=k/b>0$, $\vartheta=\pi\delta/4$ and $\kappa=(1-\cos\vartheta)/2$. For every character $i$ on $B$,
\[
 |D\cap B|^{-1}\sum_{x\in D\cap B}\operatorname{Re}i(x)\ \ge\ -1+\kappa.
\]
Its values are uniform on the $m$-th roots for an odd divisor $m$ of $b$. The arc of angular radius $\vartheta$ about $-1$ is empty if $m\vartheta<\pi$, and otherwise contains at most $m\vartheta/\pi+1\le2m\vartheta/\pi$ roots. It holds at most $\delta b/2$ sites, so at least half the forbidden sites have real part at least $-\cos\vartheta$. Translate any target coset to $H=B^\perp$. Translation preserves the Fourier zeros and for $x\in B$ every $i\in H$ contributes one. Averaging the zero identities therefore gives
\[
          \boxed{\ \mu(a+H)\le \frac{1-\kappa}{2-\kappa}<\tfrac12.\ }       \tag{2}
\]
Equation (1) gives a positive lower bound on $\delta$ in the stated range. For **even** $b$ and sign $\sigma=-1$ one has $k\ge(b-2)/4$. Of the $b-2$ points $x\ne\pm1$, at most $2k$ have $s(x)=+1$ or $s(-x)=+1$. Every remaining $x$ has $s(x^2)=+1$, with at most two preimages per positive site. Thus every such subgroup with $b>2$ meets the forbidden set and every coset of $H$ has mass at most $1/2$ by a single Fourier zero. The strict phase gap in (2) uses odd order.

For the full quantile LP, intersect each oriented upper level set with the actual allowed support. If a resulting set lies in two cosets covered by (2), its mass is at most $1-2c_\eta$; additional sites cost at most the point cap apiece. The bounds (2) can also be imposed directly as linear constraints in each sign's full-mass support-and-increment relaxation. This is an upper bound valid for every feasible row before integrating the larger survival function and subtracting $E_t/4$. No such two-coset cover of arbitrary late score tails is known. The proof of (1)–(2) also works over eligible odd prime powers, whereas the next restriction for even-order plus subgroups uses primality.

## The canonical point cap and a loss in full-mass rooted moment relaxations

Choose the least Euclidean norm optimizer $\mu$ of the larger side, with mark $\alpha=\sqrt n\,\mu_0\ge1$, and set $C=\{i:\mu_i=\mu_0\}$. A translate sending any $i\in C$ to the mark is optimal with the same norm. Strict convexity and uniqueness show it is exactly $\mu$. Conversely each period is a cap site, so $C$ is precisely the period subgroup. With $B=C^\perp$ we have $b\ge\alpha\sqrt n\ge\sqrt n$ by total mass. For odd $b$, (1)–(2) give $\mu(C)\le1/2-c_0$ for an absolute $c_0>0$ and sufficiently large primes. For even $b$ on the minus side the previous bound gives $\mu(C)\le1/2$. On the plus side if $\mu(C)>1/2$, the transform is strictly positive in real part throughout $B$, so $B\setminus\{1\}$ has the plus sign. Then $B\cup\{0\}$ is a Paley clique and Hanson–Petridis gives $b(b+1)\le n$, contradicting $b\ge\sqrt n$. This proves the claimed half bound. It also bounds the total mass at the global point cap on the canonical side even though the set attaining that cap was not specified in advance. [Hanson–Petridis, Corollary 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.09134)

Consider now the relaxation retaining full positive probability, the root value $c_s=\gamma_s/\sqrt n$, the point cap $c_s$, the true allowed support (or even the actual canonical support), and all rooted cold increment moments up to $D$. One may impose any additional linear equalities, with total rank $r_s=o(\sqrt n)$ including normalization. At every vertex of either capped polytope, at most $r_s$ coordinates are strictly between zero and the cap. Otherwise a nonzero kernel direction supported on just these coordinates can be followed both ways. In the even formulation the unconstrained inverse-orbit weights are per-site weights, giving at most $2r_s$ such sites. Hence every vertex places mass $\ge1-2r_sc_s=1-o(1)$ at the cap since both marks are bounded. This conclusion still holds with $o(\sqrt n)$ additional inequalities, counting them all in $r_s$.

At each time the integrated maximum of the two terminal quantiles equals a convex piecewise-linear function of the two laws, by the survival formula. Its **maximum** over this relaxation is attained at a pair of vertices, by expressing either law as a convex combination. The charge $E_t/4$ depends only on the orbit. Thus for every $t\le D$ the upper relaxation has a maximizing witness with almost all mass at the point cap on both signs, despite the rigorous canonical half bound. Including the exact terminal oriented means, or the exact wrong-sign expenditures at all earlier times for the chosen reference rows, only adds $O(D)$ linear equalities. This is a uniform obstruction to inferring canonical cap dispersion from those constraints, **not** a positive lower gap in the value of the integrated quantile relaxation. A noncanonical full face can contain other maximizers. The full Fourier constraints remain in the genuine face minimization.

There is an exact support-aware replacement for all the missing positional equations using only translates of the **first** increment. On a candidate law $w$ of total mass one define, for each permitted site $a$,
\[
 d_a=2\sqrt n(w_a-1/n)-\sum_i w_i\sigma Y_1(i-a)
     =4\sqrt n(P_{-\sigma}w)_a.
\]
Here $Y_1=2\sqrt n K e_0$, and $I-J-\sigma K=2P_{-\sigma}$. Impose just $d_a\le0$ on the allowed support. Then
\[
          0\ge\sum_a w_a d_a=4\sqrt n\,\|P_{-\sigma}w\|_2^2.
\]
Conversely every exact row satisfies all these inequalities as equalities. On the exposed support they therefore recover the full optimal face, including its positivity and all past rooted moments. Even if only $d_a\le\varepsilon$ is certified on the support, the squared forbidden projection is at most $\varepsilon/(4\sqrt n)$. This is a residual estimate in counting norm. Converting it to distance to a nonnegative optimal row requires a separate estimate. It supplies a systematic support-indexed LP tightening between few rooted moments and full completion.

## Certified minimization on a degenerate prime face

At $p=97$ the plus even face has two free anchored amplitudes at $10,21$; the minus even face is a singleton. Evenization preserves both oriented score laws exactly. The [checker](astra-face-54-check.py) eliminates the mass-one-at-the-root systems over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{48})$, certifies the complementary positive rows, the full exposed minus rank, and the two plus null directions, each of zero total mass. The free anchored ratios are in $[0,1]^2$ by translation optimality. This parameterization includes the whole even optimal pair. The reference at the origin is the [previously certified least-norm pair](endpoint-norm.md).

For each time the checker rounds every probability coefficient and true score to rationals within $2\cdot10^{-19}$ using Arb. Rounding scores costs at most this sup error, by the increasing quantiles. Over the parameter box each rounded plus entry differs by at most $6\cdot10^{-19}$, so expanding the linear positivity tests by that amount gives a polygon containing the true feasible set. On a true feasible point the survival integral using the rounded weights differs by at most the score diameter times $48\cdot6\cdot10^{-19}$. Exact rational intersections of the polygon edges and the survival-tie lines give every possible minimum of the rounded piecewise-affine objective, bounding the true minimum below. For the upper bound the script moves the candidate slightly toward the relative interior, checks every actual positivity inequality with intervals, and evaluates its objective with the same error. The [six records](astra-face-54-checks.jsonl) include the terminal energy charge. At depth 32 the full-face minimum is in $[2.561786507,2.561786508]$, strictly below the least-norm value in $[2.565670275,2.565670276]$. The energy-corrected full-face objective per time lies in $[0.047068071,0.047068072]$. Through the five earlier sampled depths the two costs agree to the recorded precision. This fixed-prime separation refutes universal optimality of the least-norm pair for the one-time quantile LP.

1. On each large prime, seek one growing late time and a primal pair on the actual full faces for which $(M_t-E_t/4)/t\le o(1)$. Positive lower certificates of order $\epsilon t$ above the energy at every eligible late time on an unbounded prime subsequence refute that window estimate. The depth-32 certificate requires optimizing beyond the canonical pair.
2. For a tail-capacity upper proof, cover both sign tails on their actual supports by cosets with the proved mass caps plus a controlled complement. Integrate the larger certified tail bound before subtracting the terminal energy. A uniform positive lower bound for the best such integrated cover cost above $E_t/4+\epsilon t$ throughout an unbounded late prime window rejects this cover relaxation. The capped moment vertex obstruction alone does not give that objective lower bound.
