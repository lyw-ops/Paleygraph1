## Quantitative result for one candidate mechanism

Let
\[
r_j=\|R_j\|_{2,n},\qquad R_j=V_{j+1}-V_j,\qquad
\|x\|_{2,\mathrm{count}}=\sqrt n\,\|x\|_{2,n}.
\]
The residual requirement for a starter at height \(a=a(n)\) is
\[
n^{(1-\beta)/2}r_a\longrightarrow0. \tag{A}
\]

I tested the following compatible mechanism on the actual nine-center orbit, retaining the Jacobi kernel and the real positive sheets.

Assume that, from height \(a\) onward,

1. the physical packet norm contracts:
   \[
   \|R_{a+k}\|_\pi\le r_a q^k,\qquad q<1; \tag{B}
   \]
2. the actual derivative secants have a packet representation with coefficient mass at most
   \[
   \|D_b-D_a\|_{2,n}\le C_D\sum_{k=0}^{b-a-1}\|R_{a+k}\|_\pi
   \le \frac{C_Dr_a}{1-q}; \tag{C}
   \]
3. the exceptional diagonal part has mass
   \[
   M_{\rm exc}\le C_{\rm exc}r_a;
   \]
4. the truncated negative-field columns at depth \(h\) have dimension mass
   \(Bn^{-1/2}\rho^h\), with tail \(O(q^h)\);
5. the bad Mellin boundary fibers occurring in a product of depth-\(h\) approximants number at most \(F_h\).

Then the three-field parity calculation gives the following actual-resource inequality:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|Z_a(D_b-D_a)Z_a\|_{\max}
\ll{}&
 B^2W_a\rho^{2h}
 \left(p^{-1/2}+\frac{F_h}{p}\right)\\
&+B^2M_{\rm exc}n^{-1}\rho^{2h}
+\Delta_a(\rho q)^h
+\|D_b-D_a\|_\infty q^{2h}, \tag{D}
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
W_a\le \frac{C_Dr_a}{1-q},\qquad
\Delta_a\le \frac{C_Dr_a}{1-q}.
\]
The units are consistent: \(W_a\) and \(M_{\rm exc}\) are field-dimension masses, while \(r_a\) and \(\Delta_a\) are normalized counting quantities. The factor \(p^{-1/2}\) is the nonpunctual three-field trace gain; \(F_h/p\) is the accumulated boundary loss.

Thus a linearly growing boundary count, \(F_h=O(h)\), is not by itself fatal. For \(h=O(\log n)\),
\[
p^{-1/2}+F_h/p=n^{-1/2}O(1)+O((\log n)n^{-1}),
\]
so the boundary contribution is still below the principal \(n^{-1/2}\) term. What remains genuinely unverified is (B), (C), and the uniform exceptional-row bound at a growing actual height.

### Depth compatibility

Suppose the residual contraction in (B) is uniform with \(q=e^{-x}\), \(x>0\), so that
\[
r_a\ll q^{a-a_0}.
\]
Condition (A) requires
\[
a(n)>\frac{1-\beta}{2x}\log n+o(\log n). \tag{E}
\]

The column balance in the partial Neumann argument uses
\[
h(n)\sim\frac{\log n}{4\log(\rho/q)},\qquad
\beta=\frac{x}{2\log(\rho/q)}. \tag{F}
\]
A significant quantified failure is that taking the starter at the *same* depth as this column cutoff cannot work. Indeed, if \(a=h\), then
\[
q^h=n^{-x/[4\log(\rho/q)]}=n^{-\beta/2}.
\]
The required residual scale is \(o(n^{-(1-\beta)/2})\). Since \(\beta\le 1/2\),
\[
\frac{\beta}{2}\le\frac{1-\beta}{2},
\]
with equality only at \(\beta=1/2\), where strict little-\(o\) still fails. Therefore the starter must be substantially deeper than the single-column cutoff. This is not an asymptotic impossibility: one may take \(a(n)\) according to (E), while retaining \(h(n)\) according to (F). It does show that “the same growing depth” is not compatible with the residual requirement.

For the only presently certified contracting rate near the physical orbit,
\[
q=0.996681,\qquad x=-\log q=0.0033245\ldots,
\]
(E) demands approximately
\[
a(n)-a_0>
\begin{cases}
112.8\log n,&\beta=1/4,\\
75.2\log n,&\beta=1/2.
\end{cases}
\]
These are genuine logarithmic depths, not fixed-height perturbations.

## What the actual packet evidence supports

The physical replay gives rates

\[
q_{250}<0.996681,\qquad q_{300}<0.999421,\qquad
q_{600}<1.046434.
\]

Consequently, the proposed positive packet induction does **not** currently establish any uniform \(q<1\) through the required growing starter heights. The height-600 value is not a lower bound on the true operator norm and therefore does not refute the asymptotic target; it only shows that the available absolute packet estimate has lost contraction before the needed logarithmic regime. Signs and parity can improve (D), but they do not repair this residual-resource failure, because (B) is an unsigned physical contraction requirement.

The second unverified step is more specific: no growing-depth construction currently proves that the actual derivative difference \(D_b-D_a\) has a negative-field representation satisfying (C) with uniform \(C_D\), bounded exceptional mass, and controlled boundary union. The existing signed estimate therefore remains conditional even though the \(F_h=O(h)\) loss itself would be harmless.

### Ranked next tests

1. **Cumulative residual test.** Replay the actual nine-center packet rates and compute
   \[
   \sum_{j=a_0}^{a-1}\log q_j.
   \]
   Test directly whether it beats
   \(-\frac{1-\beta}{2}\log n\) at \(a=A\log n\), rather than seeking a pointwise uniform \(q<1\).

2. **Combined signed-source test.** At those same candidate heights, represent the actual \(D_b-D_a\), including its marked exceptional row, and evaluate (D) with the measured \(F_h\). This separates the potentially recoverable signed middle estimate from the presently failed absolute contraction resource.

**Conclusion:** a compatible mechanism exists algebraically only with a starter deeper than the column cutoff, roughly \(a\asymp ((1-\beta)/(2|\log q|))\log n\). The current actual packet data do not verify the necessary growing residual contraction or the derivative-difference representation; they give a precise sufficient-bound failure, not an arithmetic obstruction.
