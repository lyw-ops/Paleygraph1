## Report: ordinary-near-cap-shift-frontier

### Checked conclusion

For ordinary primes \(p\to\infty\), with
\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad G=\widehat{\mathbb F_p^{\times2}},
\]
using the actual even optimizer \(F\) of sign \(s\), the physical root fixed at \(0\), and an actual even terminal activation row \(G_T\) (denoted \(G\) below), the exact shift quantities are
\[
a_h=\frac{q_s-F_h}{\sqrt n},\qquad
b_h=\frac{\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]}{T},
\]
where \((\tau_hF)_i=F_{i+h}\), \(q_s=F_0\), and all expectations use normalized counting measure \(1/n\).

Both \(a_h,b_h\ge0\), and evenness gives
\[
a_{-h}=a_h,\qquad b_{-h}=b_h.
\]
Therefore a positive symmetric convolution of shifts has loss and terminal cost
\[
\sum_h\mu_h a_h,\qquad \sum_h\mu_h b_h.
\]
Consequently, sublinear loss and sublinear actual terminal cost are available through convolution if and only if there is a single shift \(h\) satisfying
\[
q_s-F_h=o(\sqrt n),
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]=o(T). \tag{A}
\]

The complementary-sector identity does give, for an opposite mean-one row \(R\) of sign \(-s\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)R]
   =\frac{(q_s-F_h)q_{-s}}{n}. \tag{B}
\]
Thus a near-cap shift has small complementary overlap whenever \(q_s-F_h=o(\sqrt n)\) and \(q_{-s}=O(\sqrt n)\).

However, (B) does **not** presently imply the second condition in (A). It controls pairing with \(R\), whereas the required terminal quantity pairs the same shifted row with the actual activation \(G\). No prime-uniform inequality has been established that compares
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]
\quad\text{to}\quad
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)R]
\]
on the near-cap set. In particular, positivity of both rows only gives nonnegativity, not an upper bound of the needed scale.

Hence the mission is **inconclusive**, with a precise remaining gap: prove a prime-uniform joint estimate on the near-cap shifts,
\[
\inf_{\substack{h:\ q_s-F_h=o(\sqrt n)}}
\frac{\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]}{T}=o(1),
\]
for ordinary primes and a selectable growing marked time \(T=T(p)\to\infty\) in the simultaneous marked window.

### Why the complementary overlap is insufficient by itself

The identity (B) is useful because it prices near-cap shifts against every opposite feasible row. But it only supplies one scalar constraint. The terminal activation \(G\) is generated by the actual marked orbit and is not an arbitrary opposite-sector optimizer. The available norm bounds give only estimates of the form
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]
 \le \|F\|_{2,n}\|G\|_{2,n},
\]
which are of order at least \(T\) in the relevant normalization and do not yield \(o(T)\).

Likewise, the prime autocorrelation results provide bounds for sets of many near-cap sites through quantities such as
\[
C_F(h)=\sum_i F_iF_{i+h},
\]
or through Fejér mixtures of opposite rows. They can penalize repeated differences and large structured near-cap sets. They do not control a single near-cap shift, nor do they identify the terminal weight \(G\) on that shift.

The exact translated nonvanishing and cap-averaging arguments therefore establish feasibility and root-loss control but leave the actual joint cost untouched.

### Hypotheses used

1. \(p\) ranges over ordinary primes, not square orders or arbitrary prime powers.
2. \(n=(p-1)/2\) and all shifts lie in the cyclic group \(G=\widehat{\mathbb F_p^{\times2}}\).
3. \(F\) is the actual even ordinary optimizer of sign \(s\), with physical mark at \(0\) and root \(q_s=F_0\).
4. \(R\) is an opposite-sign mean-one feasible row, with root \(q_{-s}\).
5. \(G\) is the actual even terminal activation associated with the marked trajectory, and \(T=T(p)\to\infty\) is chosen inside the proved simultaneous marked window.
6. The order of limits remains:
   \[
   p\to\infty\ \text{over ordinary primes},\qquad
   T(p)\to\infty,\qquad
   \tau>0\ \text{fixed first},
   \]
   with any final \(\tau\downarrow0\) taken only after the prime asymptotics.

### Evidence supporting the obstruction

- The convex-hull reduction shows that mixtures cannot improve both nonnegative coordinates unless one actual shift already has \(a_h+b_h=o(1)\).
- The exact complementary identity (B) confirms that near-cap shifts have small overlap with every opposite row, but this is compatible with large overlap with the terminal activation.
- Prime Fejér/autocorrelation estimates control repeated difference geometry and exclude some large or periodic near-cap configurations. They do not handle a singleton or sparse near-cap set.
- The square-order analysis demonstrates that the terminal cost can remain macroscopically large despite near-cap/root information, so complementary orthogonality alone cannot be promoted to a universal terminal estimate.
- Existing marked-window estimates control the root and orbit cancellation but do not give a weighted estimate involving the true optimizer \(F\).

### Remaining gap

A genuinely new prime-specific estimate is needed in one of the following forms:

\[
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]=o(T)
\quad\text{for some near-cap }h,
\]
or a weighted averaged version over near-cap shifts,
\[
\sum_{h\in H_{\rm cap}}\nu_h\,
\mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]=o(T),
\]
together with enough mass on \(H_{\rm cap}\) to select one shift.

The estimate must involve the actual terminal row \(G\), not merely optimizer support, cap multiplicities, or complementary-sector overlap.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Prime-character expansion of the actual terminal weight

Express the actual \(G\) at the selected growing marked time through its Mellin/convolution expansion and study
\[
h\longmapsto \mathbb E_{\rm bulk}[(\tau_hF)G]
\]
on the near-cap set. The target is a prime-uniform bound for the weighted correlation, using Weil-type estimates for the nonconstant character components and the exact sector identity to remove the constant component.

The decisive test is whether the nonconstant contribution can be bounded by \(o(T)\) uniformly for at least one near-cap shift, while retaining the actual physical marking at \(0\). This is the most direct route to (A).

### 2. Near-cap autocorrelation cover with terminal weights

Let
\[
H_\delta=\{h:q_s-F_h\le\delta\sqrt n\}.
\]
Use the complementary budget (B), mixed over the prime Fejér family, to obtain a weighted constraint on the difference profile of \(H_\delta\). Then test whether the resulting autocorrelation bound forces either:

- sufficiently many near-cap shifts, allowing averaging against \(G\), or
- a contradiction unless one near-cap shift has \(b_h=o(1)\).

This should retain the terminal weights throughout; a bound only on \(|H_\delta|\), or only on repeated differences, would not settle the joint cost.
