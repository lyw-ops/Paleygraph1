## Status

**Inconclusive, but the proposed terminal route is algebraically sound under the stated recurrent hypotheses.** It gives a conditional subcubic terminal upper, not yet an asymptotic construction of the required terminal law.

### 1. Exact terminal splice

Work on a filtered recurrent candidate with

\[
b=|G/L|=n/m,\qquad m\asymp\sqrt n,\qquad
T=J-2,\qquad h=\frac{2}{J(J-1)},\qquad
\rho=Jh=\frac2{J-1}.
\]

All quotient laws below are **real nonnegative probabilities** on quotient blocks. If \(r_R\) is the mass of block \(R\), then the physical lift has value \(r_R/m\) on that block. Thus \(r_0=v=r(L)\) is a block mass, not a per-coordinate density. Fourier sectors may be represented complexly, but conjugate sine/cosine modes are both included and the resulting law is real.

For the genuine states \(Y_j\), write

\[
d_j(q)=\sigma\langle q,1_{L^c}Y_j\rangle,\qquad
e_j(q)=\langle q,1_{L^c}|Y_j|\rangle,
\]

and

\[
P(q)=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^T e_j(q)}{Jw},\qquad
S(q)=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^T d_j(q)+d_T(q)}{Jw},
\]

where \(w=q(L)\). The authentic terminal price for an independent \(r\), \(v=r(L)\), is

\[
I(r)=\frac{d_{T+1}(r)-d_T(r)}{v}.
\]

The one-step recurrence identity is

\[
f_j=(1-\sigma\lambda)(a_j+c_j)
+\frac{e_j(q)-d_{j+1}(q)-2g_j}{w}.
\]

Using \(q\) for \(j<T\), and using \(r\) only at the final step,

\[
f_T+I(r)
=(1-\sigma\lambda)(a_T+c_T)
+\frac{e_T(r)-d_T(r)-2g_T}{v}.
\]

The drop from the last nonzero triangular weight to zero contributes the required second copy of \(d_T(q)\). Hence the exact Abel splice gives, after the permitted erasures,

\[
F+\rho S(q)+hI(r)
\ge c_0J-\frac4{\min(w,v)}-o(1), \tag{A}
\]

and, using \(S(q)\le 2P(q)\),

\[
F+2\rho P(q)+hI(r)
\ge \frac{c_0J}{2}-o(1). \tag{B}
\]

These inequalities retain the full recurrent source and use the genuine final increment; no hot-interior surrogate is involved.

Take

\[
A=\frac8{c_0},\qquad w,v\ge \frac AJ.
\]

Then the right side of (A) is at least \(c_0J/2-o(J)\). Consequently:

- if \(F\le o(J)\) and \(P(q)=o(J^2)\), then every eligible \(r\) satisfies
  \[
  I(r)\ge \left(\frac{c_0}{4}-o(1)\right)J^3;
  \]
- the same conclusion follows from \(F\le o(J)\) and \(S(q)=o(J^2)\).

Thus a cheap genuine predecessor and a subcubic authentic terminal law are incompatible with the recurrent branch.

### 2. Conditional subcubic terminal price

Let \(u_R\) be the measured signed block mean of the true increment

\[
Y_{T+1}-Y_T
\]

on exterior block \(R\), with \(u_0=0\). The nonlinear cold map is 1-Lipschitz coordinatewise, and \(K\) is contractive in normalized counting \(\ell^2\). Under the zero start,

\[
\frac1b\sum_R u_R^2\le 4+o(1),\qquad
\frac1b\sum_R (u_R)_+\le 1+o(1).
\]

The second inequality uses the zero global mean of the signed increment and
\(\|Y_{T+1}-Y_T\|_{2,n}\le2\).

Set

\[
s=\frac{AJ^2}{\log J},\qquad H=\{R:u_R>s\}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{|H|}{b}\le \frac{4+o(1)}{s^2}.
\]

Suppose there is an admissible real law \(r\) with \(v=r_0\ge A/J\) and

\[
r_R\le \frac d b\qquad(R\in H),
\]

where \(d=o(J^2)\). Since \(u_R\le s\) off \(H\),

\[
\sum_R r_Ru_R
\le s(1-v)+\frac d b\sum_{R\in H}(u_R)_+
\le s(1-v)+(1+o(1))d.
\]

Therefore

\[
I(r)\le
\frac{s(1-v)+(1+o(1))d}{v}
=o(J^3). \tag{C}
\]

For example, \(d=J\) already suffices:

\[
I(r)\le O\!\left(\frac{J^3}{\log J}+J^2\right)=o(J^3).
\]

This is the genuine terminal upper sought by the mission, **conditional on existence of the capped law**.

### 3. Exact companion-projector test

Let \(P_c\) be the real orthogonal projector onto the permitted nonidentity companion Fourier sector on \(G/L\). For the terminal head \(H\), form

\[
Q_H=P_c-(P_c)_{:H}\bigl[(P_c)_{HH}\bigr]^\dagger(P_c)_{H:}.
\]

If \(k=Q_He_0\) has \(k_0>0\), then

\[
r=\frac{k+t\mathbf 1}{bt},
\qquad t\ge \|k_-\|_\infty,
\]

is a real nonnegative admissible law, uniform on \(H\), with

\[
r_0=\frac1b+\frac{k_0}{bt}.
\]

For \(d\ge1\), it automatically satisfies \(r_R\le d/b\) on \(H\). Thus the root condition is the explicit inequality

\[
\frac1b+\frac{k_0}{bt}\ge \frac AJ.
\]

This gives a finite exact test for the sufficient terminal construction. The corresponding capped-root LP and its real dual can certify either feasibility or failure. However, no through-primes estimate for \(k_0/t\), nor a uniform LP certificate, is currently supplied.

The forced-set obstruction from the earlier flat-price analysis does **not** transfer automatically: its set \(H\) is determined by the flat projector column, whereas the terminal \(H\) is determined by the authentic increment \(Y_{T+1}-Y_T\). Inclusion of one head in the other remains an unproved arithmetic/trajectory statement.

## Remaining gap

The current argument still needs all of the following on the relevant recurrent candidates:

1. a true predecessor law with
   \[
   w\ge A/J,\qquad P(q)=o(J^2)
   \]
   or the corresponding signed \(S(q)=o(J^2)\);

2. a terminal law satisfying the authentic head cap, or a direct proof that
   \[
   \inf_{r:\,r_0\ge A/J} I(r)=o(J^3);
   \]

3. the independent triangular upper \(F\le o(J)\);

4. uniform control through eligible primes, followed separately by treatment of the escaped branch.

Thus the terminal mechanism is **conditionally sufficient**, but the ordinary Lovász-theta limit remains open.

## Ranked decisive next tests

1. **Authentic terminal cap LP.** For every filtered recurrent candidate, compute the actual \(u_R\), take \(H=\{u_R>AJ^2/\log J\}\), and certify feasibility of
   \[
   r\ge0,\quad r\in\mathcal V_\sigma,\quad \sum_Rr_R=1,\quad
   r_0\ge A/J,\quad r_R\le J/b\ (R\in H).
   \]
   A uniform feasible certificate gives \(I(r)=o(J^3)\). A dual infeasibility certificate only shows that this cap method fails, not that the terminal price itself is \(\Omega(J^3)\).

2. **Direct terminal/prior dual pair.** Where the cap fails, solve the exact real quotient LP for the terminal ratio \(I(r)\) and independently the genuine prior ratio \(P(q)\), retaining the original block and erasures. A dual lower bound \(I(r)\ge cJ^3\) is an actual terminal obstruction; an upper \(I(r)=o(J^3)\), together with \(P(q)=o(J^2)\) and \(F=o(J)\), eliminates the recurrent branch via (B).
