# Review of the direct cap-slack response

The [returned draft](api-astra-overflow-117.txt) gives the valid bound \(c_\epsilon\le \sqrt p\,\alpha/(2A)\) for the true capped row. This is the entry estimate and the existing spill-mass upper. It supplies no new prime gain. The reverse implication from a particular overshoot lower to \(\alpha\) is the same estimate with that site's slack retained. The draft correctly stops short of a prime-uniform cap or slack result. The [receipt](api-astra-117-receipt.json) preserves the returned label.

The proposed sequence of small-slack checks would need to cover all relevant levels below the transport budget. Under the critical cap rate the budget is bounded as \(p\) varies, and such a uniform family of checks would suffice by compactness of a sequence of offending slacks. Without that rate, controlling each fixed slack level alone would leave coordinates whose slack grows along the sequence.

The [independent local deduction](weighted-adjusted-cap-117.md) replaces the maximum by the overshoot integrated against a true favored row. The ordinary optimizer gives the sharp fixed-floor estimate if its weighted overshoot vanishes. The explicit true stationary factor gives a weaker fixed-floor bound that suffices along floors tending to zero. Neither sufficient weighted upper needs the cap rate. At the critical rate a persistent mark instead forces a genuine bounded source and a high favored recipient set, both of order \(\sqrt n\), with signed transport exceeding actual local slack. None of these conditional deductions supplies the requisite prime-uniform weighted upper.
