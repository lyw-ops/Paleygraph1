# Actual Jacobi kernel and the core/exterior test

The [returned Astra draft](api-astra-overflow-110.txt) suggests relating the local balance to a signed bilinear sum. [Completion receipt](api-astra-110-receipt.json). Its matrix \(\chi(x-y)/\sqrt p\) on the \(p\)-element additive field is the additive Paley sign operator. The genuine repair uses the \(n=(p-1)/2\) multiplicative-frequency Jacobi matrix of [the Fourier notes](fourier.md), so the draft's additive-field formulas do not apply. Its uncentered exchange example also cannot annihilate constants as required. Even for a centered exchange the scalar saturation would not establish a componentwise repair balance or an actual trajectory.

With the correct \(K\), testing the [core average](repair-core-average-110.md) against \(1_C\) introduces the exterior error \(\|1_C\|_{2,n}\|r\|_{2,n}\). A lower using only the minimum core threshold times cardinality can be smaller than that error as the threshold tends to zero. The size constant of the core also depends on the threshold. The choice \(u=1_C\bar h\) maintains a lower second norm of the correct scale. It gives the exact [core/exterior defect comparison](repair-core-defect-balance-110.md), with the low-amplitude source retained. A positive gap in a Cauchy–Schwarz defect would only contradict the genuine balance if it exceeds the difference between the squared spectral upper and the squared genuine lower. The raw draft does not establish that margin.

An exact comparison at square order illustrates why the scalar geometry alone is insufficient, including with coherent entries. In the [square subfield control](square-order-control.md), translate \(S=B^\perp\) to a nonzero self-inverse coset \(C\) of \(S\). It exists because \(G/S\) has order \(r-1\), which is even for odd \(r\), when the field size is \(q=r^2\). Then \(0\notin C\), \(1_C\) is even and the same Jacobi convolution has \(K1_C=1_C-\rho\mathbf1\), \(\rho=|C|/n\asymp n^{-1/2}\). With \(\bar h=1_C\) and positive sign, \(P\bar h=\rho\mathbf1\), and \(\|1_C P\bar h\|_{2,n}=\rho^{3/2}\asymp n^{-3/4}\) while the Rayleigh loss is \(\rho\). This is a static square-field example at the same quantitative scales for the scalar tests. It does not arise as an actual averaged negative repair in the argument: in particular a \(b\)-row in the opposite sector with positive mean has positive average on this whole coset by orthogonality, incompatible with negativity everywhere there. The example gives no obstruction or solution through primes.

The returned suggestions were a spectral defect test and an additional structural restriction on the genuine core. Both require a correct Jacobi calculation and the full exterior. The locally verified estimates give a quantitative way to formulate those tests; they do not establish the prime-specific gap.
