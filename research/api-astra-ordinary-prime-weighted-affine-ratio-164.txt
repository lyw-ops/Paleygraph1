## Result: the third high set does not presently yield a new moderate-slope slice

I worked on the actual selected ordinary-prime branch, with

\[
Q=\mathbb F_p^{\times 2},\qquad v\in Q\setminus\{0\},
\]

the original physical square masks, the true characteristic function \(f\) of the marked law \(\nu\), and fixed thresholds \(a,b\in(0,1]\) satisfying \(a+b>1\). The order of limits is: first fix \(a,b\) and the physical mark \(v\), then let \(p\to\infty\) through the selected ordinary-prime family. No threshold is allowed to vary with \(p\).

Put

\[
A_x=1-vx,\qquad C_x=1-vux,\qquad
r_u(x)=\frac{C_x}{A_x},
\]

and retain only the genuine physical domain where all four arguments of \(f\) lie in \(Q\). The actual slope is

\[
t=\frac{r_u(y)}{r_u(x)}.
\]

In the affine chart

\[
X=A_x^{-1},\qquad Y=A_y^{-1},
\]

one has

\[
r_u(x)=u+(1-u)X,
\]

and the slope equation is exactly

\[
Y=tX+\frac{u(t-1)}{1-u}.
\]

Thus the slope is not \(r_u(x)\); it is the ratio of two values of the \(u\)-dependent affine map \(r_u\).

### 1. What the third high set does give

If

\[
|f(A_x)|^2\ge a,\qquad |f(C_x)|^2\ge b,
\]

then the covariance inequality for the actual nonnegative law \(\nu\) gives

\[
|f(r_u(x))|
\ge \sqrt{ab}-\sqrt{(1-a)(1-b)}
=:\delta_{a,b}>0.
\]

This is a genuine pointwise statement on the actual physical variables. Therefore, for every fixed \(u\), the set

\[
R_u=\{r_u(x):x\text{ contributes to the }u\text{-slice}\}
\]

is contained in the fixed characteristic level set

\[
H_\delta=\{z\in Q:|f(z)|\ge \delta_{a,b}\}.
\]

The affine map \(x\mapsto r_u(x)\) is injective for \(u\ne1\), so

\[
|R_u|=h(u),
\]

where \(h(u)=|X\cap u^{-1}Y|\) is the shifted-layer overlap from the previous rounds. In particular,

\[
h(u)\ll_{a,b}\sqrt p.
\]

Moreover, the existing high-layer energy argument applies to the global set \(H_\delta\), giving a prime-power saving of the form

\[
E^\times(H_\delta)\ll_{a,b}p^{3/2-\varepsilon_{a,b}},
\]

with, from the cited shifted-layer argument, one may take \(\varepsilon_{a,b}=2/445\) for sufficiently large \(p\).

This is useful information about the possible \(r_u(x)\), but it does not yet control the actual slopes.

### 2. Precise obstruction at the ratio step

For two contributing points \(x,y\), the slope is

\[
t=\frac{r_u(y)}{r_u(x)}.
\]

Applying the same covariance inequality to the two values \(r_u(x),r_u(y)\) only gives

\[
|f(t)|
\ge |f(r_u(x))|\,|f(r_u(y))|
-\sqrt{(1-|f(r_u(x))|^2)(1-|f(r_u(y))|^2)}
\]

and hence

\[
|f(t)|\ge 2\delta_{a,b}^{\,2}-1.
\]

The assumption \(a+b>1\) guarantees \(\delta_{a,b}>0\), but it does **not** guarantee \(2\delta_{a,b}^2-1>0\). For example,

\[
a=b=0.51
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta_{a,b}=0.02,
\]

so the resulting lower bound is negative and therefore vacuous.

Consequently, the third high set gives no positive characteristic lower bound for the true slope unless the much stronger condition

\[
\delta_{a,b}>\frac1{\sqrt2}
\]

holds. This is not implied by \(a+b>1\). In particular, the ordinary moderate regime \(a,b\) just above the threshold \(1/2\) remains completely uncontrolled by this iteration.

This is an actual obstruction, not merely a missing estimate: the covariance inequality loses information when passing from the two high values \(r_u(x),r_u(y)\) to their ratio. The known lower bound on \(f(r_u(x))\) therefore cannot be composed with itself to produce a useful lower bound on \(f(t)\).

### 3. Why the global energy saving does not automatically repair this

The slope-restricted contribution involves the joint count

\[
\mathcal N(D)
=
\#\{(u,x,y):
r_u(x),r_u(y)\in H_\delta,\ 
r_u(y)/r_u(x)\in D\},
\]

with the original physical masks and the signed condition

\[
\chi(1-t)=\rho.
\]

For each fixed \(u\), the relevant pair count is an energy of the subset \(R_u\subset H_\delta\):

\[
\#\{(x,y):r_u(y)/r_u(x)\in D\}
\le E^\times(R_u)
\]

after discarding the slope restriction. However, the known estimate

\[
E^\times(H_\delta)\ll p^{3/2-\varepsilon_{a,b}}
\]

controls only one global set. It does not imply

\[
\sum_{u\ne1}E^\times(R_u)
\ll p^{1+\sigma/2},
\]

which is the sufficient bound needed for an \(O(p)\) contribution on a band

\[
|f(t)|^2\le p^{-\sigma}.
\]

The obstruction is the missing \(u\)-uniform coupling. The sets \(R_u\) are not independent subsets of \(H_\delta\); they are affine images of the actual shifted intersections, and the same \(u\) also controls the two transported physical factors

\[
f(1-vux),\qquad f(1-vuy).
\]

An incidence estimate applied only to \(R_u\) forgets precisely this shared-\(u\) structure. Conversely, an estimate for \(h(u)\) alone does not see the true ratio weight \(f(r_u(y)/r_u(x))\).

### 4. What remains valid in the stronger endpoint regime

If \(a,b>1/2\), the existing pointwise affine-overlap argument remains valid and gives

\[
h(u)\ll_{a,b}p^{13/27}\qquad(u\ne1).
\]

Thus the off-diagonal block is \(O(p)\) whenever

\[
\sigma\ge \frac{26}{27}.
\]

The third high set does not improve this exponent: its ratio lower bound is only useful when \(\delta_{a,b}>1/\sqrt2\), and even then it gives a high slope set rather than a small-slope estimate. It does not produce the required new moderate-slope cutoff.

## Conclusion

The proposed affine/inverse-incidence route is **inconclusive with a precise failure at the ratio-normalization step**.

- It successfully identifies the actual third high set
  \[
  R_u\subset \{z:|f(z)|\ge\delta_{a,b}\}
  \]
  in the physical affine chart.
- It preserves the genuine slope
  \[
  t=r_u(y)/r_u(x)
  \]
  and the physical square masks.
- It does not yield a positive lower bound for \(|f(t)|\) throughout the \(a+b>1\) region.
- The global multiplicative-energy saving for \(H_\delta\) does not supply the required summed joint estimate over the \(u\)-dependent subsets \(R_u\).

Therefore no new \(O(p)\) moderate-slope region is proved beyond the existing \(\sigma\ge26/27\) high-high slice.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: prove a joint affine-ratio energy estimate

For fixed \(a,b\) and the actual physical mark \(v\), test whether one can prove directly

\[
\sum_{u\in Q\setminus\{1\}}
E^\times(R_u)
\ll_{a,b}p^{3/2-\varepsilon}
\]

for some \(\varepsilon>0\), or more sharply the slope-restricted form

\[
\sum_{u}
\#\{(x,y):
r_u(x),r_u(y)\in H_\delta,\ 
r_u(y)/r_u(x)\in D\}
\ll_{a,b}
p^{1+\sigma/2}
\]

on the desired moderate band. This must retain the shared affine parameter \(u\), the physical masks, and the signed condition \(\chi(1-t)=\rho\). A bound for \(H_\delta\) alone is insufficient.

### 2. Second priority: test a conditional weighted spectrum of the affine fibers

For each \(u\), form the weighted measure

\[
\mu_u(z)
=
\sum_{x:\,r_u(x)=z}
|f(A_x)f(C_x)|
\]

with the original physical restrictions, and study the multiplicative correlation

\[
\sum_{z,w}\mu_u(z)\mu_u(w)\,f(w/z).
\]

The relevant target is a prime-uniform estimate after summing over \(u\), not a pointwise bound in \(u\). Numerically and symbolically test whether the signed condition

\[
\chi(1-w/z)=\rho
\]

forces cancellation in this weighted spectrum beyond the absolute-energy bound. If no such cancellation appears even for fixed moderate \(a,b\), that would give a sharper obstruction to the proposed route.
