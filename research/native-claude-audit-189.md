### 2.2 Proposition 1 and Lemma 2 (re-derived)

1. **Prop. 1(i).** On the chart, \(r=(1-b)Y\) (checked by hand and in my Part A for every pair), and the map \((Y,D)\mapsto(r,b)\) is injective (given \(b\), \(Y=r/(1-b)\) and \(u_D=u_Y/b\)). Two maps with the same intercept have slope ratio \(Y'/Y\), so \(T_0=\sum_b\sum_{Y,Y'}v_b(Y)v_b(Y')g(Y'/Y)\). With \(x=u_Y\), \(Y=1/(1+x)\), (H3) gives \(g(Y)=\psi(x)\) and \(g(D)=\psi(x/b)\); \(\psi(0)=\psi(-1)=0\) handles \(Y\ne1\) and \(Y,D\ne0\). With \(\beta=1/b\in Q\setminus\{1\}\) and \(z=\beta x\), the inner sum is \(c_{\chi(x)}(x'/x)-\psi(x)\psi(x')\) (the excluded term is \(\beta=1\)). Grouping by \(\kappa=x'/x\) and the class of \(x\) gives \(\sum_\kappa\sum_\varepsilon c_\varepsilon(\kappa)A_\varepsilon(\kappa)-Z\). **Correct, and valid for every \(g\)** (only the identification of the \(\kappa=1\) term with \(c\) uses \(g(1)=1\)).
2. **Prop. 1(ii)–(iii).** \(c=\sum_{b\in Q\setminus1}\sum_x\psi(x)^2\psi(x/b)^2=\sum_\varepsilon c_\varepsilon(1)^2-\sum\psi^4\); \(X=\sum_{\beta\in Q\setminus1}c(\beta)^2\) is Lemma A1 with \(m'(b)=c(1/b)=c(b)\). Correct. Also \(\sum_{\lambda}\Delta(\lambda,0)=\sum_bm'(b)^2=X\) (all \(r\ne0\) since \(b\ne1\)), which Prop. 5 uses.
3. **Lemma 2.** For \(\kappa\notin\{0,1\}\) and \(\psi(x)\psi(\kappa x)\ne0\): \(\sigma-1=(1-\kappa)z\) with \(z=-x/(1+x)\), and \(1/\sigma-1=(1-\kappa)x/(1+\kappa x)=(1-1/\kappa)w\) with \(w=-\kappa x/(1+\kappa x)\). Weights by (H3); classes as above; \(x\mapsto z\) and \(x\mapsto w\) are Möbius involutions, hence injective on the support. Since every term is \(\ge0\) and the dropped factor is \(\le1\), \(A_\varepsilon(\kappa)\le\min\{c_\varepsilon(\kappa),c_\varepsilon(1-\kappa),c_{\chi(\kappa)\varepsilon}(1-1/\kappa)\}\). **Correct.**
4. **Cor. 2.2.** For \(g=\mathbf 1_H\): if \(1+z,\,1+(1-\kappa)z\in H\) then \(s=1/(1+z)\), \(\sigma\), \(u=\sigma s\in H\), so the \(z\)-parametrization is a bijection between the two supports and \(A_\varepsilon(\kappa)=c_\varepsilon(\kappa)=c_\varepsilon(1-\kappa)=c_{\chi(\kappa)\varepsilon}(1-1/\kappa)\). **Correct.**

*Replay (my Part A–C):* Prop. 1 (i)–(iii) exact for 18 integer weights at \(p\le53\) from an independent chart; Lemma 2's slope-\((1-\kappa)\) identity, the three-pencil inequality, \(T_0-c\le{\rm CPL}\le{\rm ICR}\), Prop. 5, and the CS identity \(\sum_{\kappa\ne1,\varepsilon}c_\varepsilon(\kappa)^2=X+2c_+(1)c_-(1)\) exact for 15 Fraction weights at \(p\le61\); anharmonic invariance for 5 subgroups at \(p\le113\).

### 2.4 Corollaries 3.1–3.2: what the method cap proves (check "valid method cap")

- **The implication is valid.** If a proof gives \(T_0(g)\le Cp^{5/4-\delta}\) for **every** law satisfying the hypotheses it uses (probability, cap with fixed \(C_{\rm cap}\), hence \(\omega\ge0\), (H2), (H3)), then the interval laws are in its scope and \(X(\mathbf 1_I)\le(\pi^2/4)^5Cp^{5/4-\delta}=O(|I|^{5/2-2\delta})\).
- **Correction (masked to unmasked energy).** \(X(\mathbf 1_I)=\sum_{\kappa\in Q\setminus1}c(\kappa)^2\) is the energy of \(I-1\) **restricted to quotients in \(Q\)**. The unmasked \(E^\times(I-1)\) is not smaller, but it is comparable: \(E^\times(S)^{1/4}\) is an \(L^4\) norm of \(\widehat{\mathbf 1_S}\), so for \(S=P_+\sqcup P_-\) (split by quadratic class) \(E^\times(S)\le16\max E^\times(P_\pm)\le16\,(X(\mathbf 1_I)+|I|^2)\). So the reduction to "energy of shifted geometric progressions below \(|I|^{5/2}\)" holds as the report says, after this step. *Replay:* full/masked ratio 2.7–3.1 at \(p\le40009\).
