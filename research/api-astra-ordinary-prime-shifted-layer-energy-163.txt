## Result

For the selected ordinary-prime critical family
\[
p\to\infty,\qquad Q=\mathbb F_p^{\times 2},\qquad n=|Q|=(p-1)/2,
\]
with the actual Fourier row \(f\), physical marking \(v\in Q\), and
\[
X=\{x\in Q:1-vx\in\mathcal A\},\qquad
Y=\{x\in Q:1-vx\in\mathcal B\},
\]
the available hypotheses imply only
\[
|X|,|Y|\ll \sqrt p.
\]
Here \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\) are the genuine translated high levels of the actual row, and all norms are counting norms on \(\mathbb F_p\).

Let
\[
h(u)=|X\cap u^{-1}Y|,\qquad u\in Q.
\]
The exact off-diagonal multiplicative energy is
\[
E_{\mathrm{off}}(X,Y)
 =\sum_{u\in Q\setminus\{1\}}h(u)(h(u)-1).
\]
Since \(h(u)\le \min(|X|,|Y|)\) and
\[
\sum_{u\in Q}h(u)=|X||Y|,
\]
one obtains the checked universal estimate
\[
E_{\mathrm{off}}(X,Y)
 \le \min(|X|,|Y|)\,|X||Y|
 \ll p^{3/2}.
\]
This is exactly the already-covered \(\sigma=1\) scale. It gives no gain for any fixed \(\sigma<1\).

The multiplicative Fourier identity is
\[
\sum_{u\in Q}h(u)^2
 =\frac1n\sum_{\eta\in\widehat Q}
   |\widehat{1_X}(\eta)|^2|\widehat{1_Y}(\eta)|^2,
\]
where the Fourier transforms are unnormalized:
\[
\widehat{1_X}(\eta)=\sum_{x\in X}\overline{\eta(x)}.
\]
The trivial spectral contribution is
\[
\frac{|X|^2|Y|^2}{n}=O(p).
\]
Therefore the requested estimate
\[
E_{\mathrm{off}}(X,Y)=O\!\left(p^{1+\sigma/2}\right),
\qquad \sigma<1,
\]
would require the genuinely nontrivial bound
\[
\frac1n\sum_{\eta\ne1}
|\widehat{1_X}(\eta)|^2|\widehat{1_Y}(\eta)|^2
\ll p^{1+\sigma/2}.
\]
No such estimate follows from the present level-size, physical-sign, or cross-ratio incidence information.

### Precise obstruction

The obstruction is multiplicative stabilization. If a set \(H\subset Q\) is a multiplicative subgroup and \(X=Y=H\), then
\[
h(u)=
\begin{cases}
|H|,&u\in H,\\
0,&u\notin H,
\end{cases}
\]
so
\[
E_{\mathrm{off}}(H,H)
=(|H|-1)|H|(|H|-1)
\asymp |H|^3.
\]
For \(|H|\asymp p^{1/2}\), this is \(p^{3/2}\), violating every target \(O(p^{1+\sigma/2})\) with \(\sigma<1\).

This is not a counterexample to the actual Fourier-row problem, because it has not been shown that the actual translated layers
\[
1-vX,\quad 1-vY
\]
contain such a subgroup configuration along the selected ordinary-prime family. It is, however, a precise failure of any argument using only:

1. \(|X|,|Y|\ll\sqrt p\);
2. the fact that \(X,Y\subset Q\);
3. the forced physical sign restrictions;
4. the affine-chart injectivity of the slope map.

Those inputs do not exclude a large family of stabilizing dilates. A sign restriction on \(u\), such as \(\chi(u-1)=\rho\), may reduce the subgroup example, but no quantitative bound for the actual stabilizing set
\[
\{u\in Q:\chi(u-1)=\rho,\ |X\cap u^{-1}Y|\ \text{large}\}
\]
is presently available. Likewise, the true translated level constraints have not yet supplied a nontrivial bound on the nonconstant Mellin spectrum.

The same failure applies to a moderate slope band
\[
D_\sigma=\{t\in Q:|f(t)|^2\asymp p^{-\sigma}\},
\qquad \sigma<1.
\]
The physical fiber estimate controls fixed \(t\), but summing over a growing family of slopes still permits the \(p^{3/2}\) stabilization scale. The actual prime-family lower ratio \(\omega(1/T)\) or an equivalent gap exclusion is also not proved here.

## Conclusion

The proposed \(O(p^{1+\sigma/2})\) energy estimate is **inconclusive, with a precise obstruction to the currently available sufficient criterion**. The exact prime family, physical marking, actual high layers, and order of limits have been retained; what is missing is a quantitative exclusion of large stabilizing or nearly stabilizing dilates for the actual sets \(X,Y\).

### Ranked next tests

1. **Actual stabilizer-spectrum test — highest priority.**  
   For the selected ordinary primes \(p\), compute
   \[
   R_\tau=\{u\in Q:h(u)\ge \tau\},
   \]
   separately for all \(u\), for the forced-sign sector \(\chi(u-1)=\rho\), and for the actual favorable cross-sign restrictions. Compare
   \[
   \sum_{u\in R_\tau}h(u)^2
   \]
   with \(p^{1+\sigma/2}\) over dyadic \(\tau\). A bound of the form
   \[
   |R_\tau|\ll p^{1+\sigma/2}\tau^{-2}
   \]
   in the relevant range would directly yield the desired energy estimate.

2. **Nontrivial Mellin fourth-spectrum test — second priority.**  
   For the same actual \(X,Y\), evaluate
   \[
   \mathcal E_{\mathrm{nt}}
   =\frac1n\sum_{\eta\ne1}
   |\widehat{1_X}(\eta)|^2|\widehat{1_Y}(\eta)|^2
   \]
   and its restriction to the physical/sign-admissible slope set. Determine whether the excess is \(O(p^{1+\sigma/2})\), or whether a small collection of characters accounts for a \(p^{3/2-o(1)}\) contribution. The latter would identify the precise near-stabilizer that any future proof must remove.
