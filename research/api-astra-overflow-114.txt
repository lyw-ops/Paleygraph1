### New checked statement: the opposite-mark term is exactly an anchored Jacobi value

Assume the fixed-floor late-source hypotheses from Rounds 112–114, in particular

\[
0\le h\le H,\qquad 0\le g\le G,\qquad 
\mathbb E_n g\asymp n^{-1/2},\qquad 
\mathbb E_n(hg)=O(n^{-1/2}),
\]

with \(H,G\) fixed, and let \(z(y)=\epsilon k(y)=\epsilon\lambda(y)/\sqrt p\). For every fixed polynomial \(P\) satisfying \(P(0)=P'(0)=0\),

\[
\boxed{\;
\mathbb E_n[P(h)gz]
   =\frac{\epsilon}{n}\,K(P(h)g)(0).
\;} \tag{A}
\]

Indeed, by symmetry of the Jacobi kernel,

\[
K(P(h)g)(0)
 =\sum_y \lambda(-y)p^{-1/2}P(h_y)g_y
 =\epsilon n\,\mathbb E_n[P(h)gz].
\]

Since \(K1=0\), the right side is genuinely an off-mark through-primes quantity; the mark \(y=0\) contributes nothing because \(\lambda(0)=0\).

For the actual opposite-factor coefficient

\[
c_-=\frac{\sqrt p}{n}g_-(0)=O(n^{-1/2}),
\]

(A) gives the exact normalization

\[
\boxed{\;
c_-\,\mathbb E_n[P(h)gz]
 =\frac{\epsilon c_-}{n}\,K(P(h)g)(0).
\;} \tag{B}
\]

Thus the unresolved term is not an uncontrolled bulk average: it is a single anchored Jacobi transform of the genuine product \(P(h)g\).

### Prime-uniform size bound at the critical scale

Because \(P(0)=P'(0)=0\), there is \(C_{P,H}\) such that
\(|P(x)|\le C_{P,H}x^2\) on \([0,H]\). Hence

\[
\left|\mathbb E_n[P(h)gz]\right|
 \le C_{P,H}\mathbb E_n(h^2g)
 \le C_{P,H}H\,\mathbb E_n(hg)
 =O_P(n^{-1/2}).
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\;
\left|c_-\,\mathbb E_n[P(h)gz]\right|
 =O_P(n^{-1}).
\;} \tag{C}
\]

This is the sharp scale allowed by the current hypotheses. It proves that the opposite input mark cannot exceed the critical \(n^{-1}\) scale, but it does **not** prove \(o(n^{-1})\), nor does it determine its sign.

For the two relevant tests this specializes to

\[
|c_-\,\mathbb E_n[h^2gz]|=O_P(n^{-1}),
\]

and, with \(P_A(x)=x^2(x-A)^2\),

\[
|c_-\,\mathbb E_n[P_A(h)gz]|=O_P(n^{-1}).
\]

The unmarked expression is therefore exactly

\[
\mathbb E_n[P(h)g(\mu-c_-z)]
 =\mu\,\mathbb E_n[P(h)g]
 -\frac{\epsilon c_-}{n}K(P(h)g)(0), \tag{D}
\]

up to the already established \(O_P(n^{-5/4})\) replacement error.

### What the reciprocal law does—and does not—control

The genuine reciprocal identity gives, pointwise,

\[
g\,g_-=\frac tn+\left(1-\frac tn\right)1_{\{0\}}.
\]

It controls the weighted magnitude and the collision terms, but it does not determine

\[
K(P(h)g)(0)=\sum_y \lambda(-y)p^{-1/2}P(h_y)g_y.
\]

In particular, replacing \(g_-\) off the mark by \(t/(ng)\) does not remove the signed character \(\lambda(-y)\). The reciprocal law is invariant under rearrangements of the \(g\)-mass among sites with different \(\lambda(y)\), whereas (A) changes under precisely those rearrangements. Therefore the reciprocal identity alone cannot supply a sign for the opposite-mark correction.

The exact surviving obstruction is thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{A prime-uniform }o(n^{-1})\text{ result requires }
K(P(h)g)(0)=o(\sqrt n).} \tag{E}
\]

A one-sided \(n^{-1}\) estimate would follow from the stronger anchored criterion

\[
\epsilon\,K(P(h)g)(0)\ge -C_P\sqrt n
\]

with the appropriate sign, while cancellation would require \(o(\sqrt n)\). Neither follows from the reciprocal product or from the existing \(L^1\) and collision estimates.

### Consequence for the two signed moments

For \(P(z)=z^2\), the known unmarked term has size \(n^{-1}\), and (C) shows that the opposite mark can modify its coefficient by another bounded \(n^{-1}\) amount. Hence the sign of the combined coefficient remains unresolved.

For \(P=P_A\), the earlier mark cancellation still removes the explicit \(A\)-atom, but (D) leaves the anchored term (B). Thus the concentration alternative from \(P_A\) is unaffected at the level of scale, but its coefficient cannot yet be made one-sided without controlling \(K(P_A(h)g)(0)\).

### Ranked next tests

1. **Primary:** Evaluate or bound, for the genuine late \(h\) and \(g\),
   \[
   \frac1{\sqrt n}K(h^2g)(0),\qquad
   \frac1{\sqrt n}K\!\left(h^2(h-A)^2g\right)(0)
   \]
   along the prime subsequence. A fixed-sign bound would settle the corresponding signed moment; \(o(\sqrt n)\) would eliminate the opposite-mark contribution at \(o(n^{-1})\).

2. **Secondary:** Use the actual high-\(g\) support from the favored transport estimate and test whether its \(\lambda\)-weighted mass has a prime-uniform sign. This is the smallest arithmetic test that can combine the anchored term with the unmarked distinct sum without replacing the genuine trajectory by a square model.
