## Result

The proposed mission is **not completed**. The current identities reduce the ordinary-prime problem to an exact weighted bilinear correlation, but the available prime estimates do not give a prime-uniform \(o(1)\) bound for any dyadic portion that is guaranteed to contain the first reflected spill.

The relevant prime family is the actual sequence of eligible ordinary primes
\[
p\to\infty,\qquad n=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
restricted to a simultaneous marked window containing a selectable
\[
T=T(p)\to\infty,\qquad T+1\ \text{also marked},
\]
with fixed floor \(\tau>0\), followed only afterward by \(\tau\downarrow0\). All norms below are normalized counting norms
\[
\mathbb E f=\frac1n\sum_i f_i,\qquad
\|f\|_{2,n}^2=\mathbb E|f|^2.
\]

### Exact first-spill reduction

For the actual projected row \(Z=X_{\sigma,T}/M\), put
\[
u=T^{-1/3},\qquad d_0=0,\qquad d_j=(u-Z_j)_+
\]
on the bulk. The first reflected vector is
\[
y^{(0)}=Z+2L_\sigma d,
\]
with the exact bulk formula
\[
y_i^{(0)}
=\max(Z_i,u)+\sigma(Kd)_i-\mathbb Ed
 -2\ell_i\ell^{\mathsf T}d/\ell_0 .
\]

The actual negative set is
\[
E_0=\{i\ne0:y_i^{(0)}<0\},
\]
not a fixed character mask. The first spill is
\[
b_i^{(0)}=1_{E_0}(i)(-y_i^{(0)}).
\]
The established estimates are
\[
\mathbb Ed=O_\tau(T^{-2/3}),\qquad
\|d\|_{2,n}^2=O_\tau(T^{-1}),
\]
and
\[
|E_0|/n=O_\tau(T^{-1/3}),\qquad
\|b^{(0)}\|_{2,n}^2=O_\tau(T^{-1}).
\]

Let \(F\) be a true opposite-sign mean-one optimizer, with physical mark \(q=F_0\), and \(R\) a true optimizer on sign \(\sigma\). Exact complementarity gives
\[
F_iR_i=0
\]
on the bulk. For a selectable cutoff \(H=o(T)\), define
\[
F_H=1_{\rm bulk}(F-H)_+.
\]
The clipping error is \(O_\tau(\sqrt{H/T})\).

If a fixed gap holds, namely
\[
\frac q{\sqrt n}\ge1+\eta,
\qquad
\sqrt{1+\tau}<1+\eta,
\]
then the exact affine constraints imply a positive lower bound
\[
-\frac{\sigma}{n}
  \sum_{\substack{i\in E_0,\ R_i=0\\j\ne0,\ j\ne i}}
      (F_i-H)_+K_{ij}d_j
\ge c_{\tau,\eta}-O_\tau(\sqrt{H/T})-o(1). \tag{A}
\]
The diagonal and both physical roots have already been removed. Thus an \(o(1)\) upper bound for the left side of (A), uniformly along the ordinary-prime family and for one true \(F\), would exclude the gap.

That is the correct target. It is not yet proved.

## Dyadic decomposition

For
\[
D_k=\{j\ne0:2^{-k-1}u<d_j\le2^{-k}u\},
\qquad k\ge0,
\]
the first-spill pairing decomposes as
\[
\mathcal P_H
=
\sum_{k\ge0}\mathcal P_{H,k}
+\mathcal P_{H,\rm tail},
\]
where
\[
\mathcal P_{H,k}
=
-\frac{\sigma}{n}
\sum_{\substack{i\in E_0,\ R_i=0\\j\in D_k}}
(F_i-H)_+K_{ij}d_j.
\]

The known estimates imply only
\[
\sum_k \mathbb E_{D_k} d_j=\mathbb Ed=O_\tau(T^{-2/3}),
\qquad
\sum_k\mathbb E_{D_k}d_j^2=O_\tau(T^{-1}),
\]
and
\[
|E_0|/n=O_\tau(T^{-1/3}).
\]
They do not prevent the \(F_H\)-mass from concentrating on the same sparse, adaptive set \(E_0\) on which \(\sigma Kd\) is negative.

Consequently, even a strict estimate for one fixed level \(D_k\) would not settle (A) unless the complementary levels were also controlled or shown to carry insufficient \(F_H\)-mass.

## Why the currently available prime estimates fail

The available ordinary-prime input is of Weil/intersection type: for fixed translated masks or fixed sets one obtains errors of order \(O(\sqrt p)\), and for filtered intersections
\[
\left||D_A|-n2^{-r}\right|\ll r\sqrt p,
\qquad
\left|\sum_{t\in D_A}\gamma(t)\right|\ll r\sqrt p.
\]

These estimates do not apply with the required strength to (A), for three separate reasons.

1. **The output set is adaptive.**  
   \(E_0\) is defined using the actual sign of
   \[
   \sigma(Kd)_i
   \]
   together with \(\max(Z_i,u)\), the exact mean correction, and the pin correction. It is not a fixed translated intersection.

2. **The weights are optimizer weights.**  
   The coefficient \((F_i-H)_+\) is supported on the common zero set
   \[
   \{i:R_i=0\},
   \]
   but its distribution on that set is uncontrolled. The complementary-zero-set identity removes overlap with \(R\); it does not produce pseudorandomness of \(F\).

3. **Naive absolute-value bounds are too large.**  
   Even granting an entrywise prime bound of the natural \(O(p^{-1/2})\) scale for \(K_{ij}\), absolute summation gives at best a quantity of order
   \[
   \sqrt n\,\mathbb Ed
   =O_\tau(\sqrt n\,T^{-2/3}),
   \]
   which is not \(o(1)\) in the proved marked window. Cauchy–Schwarz similarly leaves
   \[
   \|F_H\|_{2,n}\|d\|_{2,n},
   \]
   and the true optimizer has no sufficiently small normalized \(L^2\)-norm bound.

Thus the fixed-mask Weil estimates do not control the actual signed adaptive pairing. In particular, they cannot justify cancellation merely from the fact that \(E_0\) is a thin set.

## Evidence supporting the reduction

The reduction is supported by the following exact or already established facts.

- The first reflected vector retains the exact adaptive threshold \(\max(Z_i,u)\), the exact mean correction, and the pin correction.
- The true optimizer \(F\) and complementary optimizer \(R\) satisfy exact bulk complementarity.
- The first spill has sparse support and \(O_\tau(T^{-1})\) normalized square mass.
- Under a fixed gap, the optimizer-weighted spill has order-one mass.
- The diagonal contribution is negligible:
  \[
  O_\tau(n^{-1}T^{-2/3}),
  \]
  since \(K_{ii}=-1/n\).
- The cutoff can be chosen with
  \[
  H\to\infty,\qquad H=o(T),
  \]
  so that the clipping error \(O_\tau(\sqrt{H/T})\) vanishes.

The later reflection-flow and averaged-repair arguments do not close this first-spill problem: they convert the gap into a sparse weighted deficit or an approximate annihilator, but still require a prime-sensitive estimate for the true optimizer-weighted correlation.

## Remaining gap

The unresolved statement is:

> For the actual ordinary-prime family, one selectable marked \(T=T(p)\), one true opposite optimizer \(F\), one complementary true optimizer \(R\), and some \(H=o(T)\), prove
> \[
> \sum_k\mathcal P_{H,k}=o(1)
> \]
> after deleting both physical roots and the diagonal.

A weaker result on a genuine dyadic range would be meaningful only if it also identifies the remaining levels and proves that their total optimizer-weighted contribution is negligible. At present the low-\(d\) tail, the adaptive output selection \(E_0\), and the concentration of \(F_H\) on the true complementary zero set remain simultaneously uncontrolled.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: exact weighted dyadic correlation test

For actual eligible ordinary primes, compute or prove an asymptotic bound for
\[
\mathcal P_{H,k}
=
-\frac{\sigma}{n}
\sum_{\substack{i\in E_0,\ R_i=0\\j\in D_k}}
(F_i-H)_+K_{ij}d_j
\]
with the actual \(Z,d,E_0,F,R\), not a fixed-mask surrogate.

The first useful target is a range
\[
k\le k_{\max}(p)
\]
for which
\[
\sup_{\text{eligible }p,T,F,R}
|\mathcal P_{H,k}|=o(1)
\]
and whose union carries a definite fraction of the gap lower bound in (A). This test directly preserves the physical marking, exact threshold, true optimizer, and order of limits.

### 2. Second priority: common-zero-set bilinear estimate

Prove a restricted bilinear estimate for
\[
A\subseteq\{i:R_i=0\},\qquad B\subseteq D_k,
\]
of the form
\[
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in B}
x_iK_{ij}y_j
\right|
\le
o(1)\,\|x\|_{1,n}\|y\|_{1,n}
\]
for the particular prime-generated \(A=E_0\cap\{R=0\}\), \(x=(F-H)_+\), and \(y=d1_{D_k}\).

A viable route would need additional structure—e.g. additive-energy control, a spectral estimate on the true common zero set, or a decomposition of the adaptive spill into finitely many fixed translated masks with an error smaller than the gap scale. The existing filtered-support estimate is insufficient by itself: it gives directional exclusion but not the required weighted cancellation on the actual adaptive set.
