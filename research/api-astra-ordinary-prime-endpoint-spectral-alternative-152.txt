## Result: the endpoint charge alone does not force the required spectral alternative

I obtain a precise partial result, but not the desired contradiction.

Fix a hypothetical ordinary-prime gap and parameters \(\tau,\eta,\omega\), with \(0<\omega<2\). Let \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\) range over ordinary primes, \(n=(p-1)/2\), and choose one admissible growing marked time \(T=T(p)\) with \(T,T+1\) in the simultaneous marked window. Keep the true optimum \(q=q(p)\), the actual mark
\[
z_*=(1+\delta)\frac nq,\qquad \delta=T^{-1/6},
\]
the actual retargeted row \(x\), and the actual negative-part history
\[
b_{j+1}-b_j=-\omega D_jL_\sigma b_j,\qquad b_0=0.
\]
Take the actual step \(j\) selected in the R151 argument with \(m=n^2\), and write
\[
r=\mathbb E b_j,\qquad h=\frac{b_j}{r}.
\]
All norms below are normalized counting norms over the \(n\) divisor coordinates.

For every true complementary optimum \(R\), retain the fixed-floor endpoint cost
\[
C_R
 =
1_{\rm bulk}\Bigl(-(1-\lambda)e_\sigma-c-\delta^{-1/2}R\Bigr)_+,
\]
where \(c=c_{\tau,\eta}>0\) is fixed before \(p\to\infty\). The actual R151 bounds give
\[
\mathbb E(hC_R)\ge c_0-o(1),\qquad
\|C_R\|_{2,n}=O_\tau(T^{-1/2}),\qquad
|\operatorname{supp}C_R|/n=O_{\tau,\eta}(T^{-1}).
\tag{1}
\]

They also give
\[
\|L_\sigma h\|_{2,n}=o(1),
\tag{2}
\]
and, with
\[
m_{\rm eff}=\frac{n}{\mathbb E h^2},
\qquad
m_0=\frac n{\sqrt p},
\qquad
L=\max\left(1,\frac{m_{\rm eff}}{m_0}\right),
\]
the actual pinned-projection estimate implies
\[
m_{\rm eff}\ge (1-o(1))m_0.
\tag{3}
\]

### What the endpoint charge does force

From (1), with \(S_R=\operatorname{supp}C_R\),
\[
c_0-o(1)
 \le \|1_{S_R}h\|_{2,n}\,\|C_R\|_{2,n},
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E\bigl[1_{S_R}h^2\bigr]\gg_{\tau,\eta}T.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently
\[
m_{\rm eff}\ll_{\tau,\eta}\frac nT.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the actual endpoint charge forces concentration of the **coordinate vector \(h=b_j/r\)** on the actual endpoint mask \(S_R\), at the scale \(n/T\). This is a genuine consequence of the signed endpoint lower bound.

Combining (3) and (5),
\[
(1-o(1))m_0\le m_{\rm eff}\ll_{\tau,\eta}\frac nT.
\tag{6}
\]

This is the full support/effective-size conclusion currently available from the actual endpoint charge.

## Prime consequence in the accessible effective-size range

Define, as in the prime restriction,
\[
f(x)=\frac1n\sum_j h_jj(x),\qquad x\in Q.
\]
Then \(h\ge0\), \(\mathbb Eh=1\), \(|f|\le1\), and
\[
\|f\|_{2,Q}^2=\frac n{m_{\rm eff}}.
\]

For a union \(B\subset Q\) of at most \(K\) multiplicative cosets, with each constituent subgroup having size between \(c_1\sqrt p\) and \(c_2\sqrt p\), put
\[
t_B=\frac{\|1_{Q\setminus B}f\|_2^2}{\|f\|_2^2}.
\]
For an anchor \(a\), put
\[
d_a
 =
\frac{\sum_j h_j^2\bigl(1-\rho k(j-a)\bigr)}
     {\sum_j h_j^2},
\qquad \rho=-\sigma.
\]

The ordinary-prime coset-spread estimate applies to the **actual** \(h\), because (2) is stronger than the required fixed \(C\)-bound. Uniformly in \(B\) and \(a\),
\[
\boxed{
t_B+d_a\gg_{K,c_1,c_2,\tau,\eta}
\frac1{L^4\log^2(2L)}
}
\qquad
\left(L\le p^{1/12}\right).
\tag{7}
\]

In particular, if the actual step is critical,
\[
\frac{m_{\rm eff}}{m_0}\to1,
\]
then the flattening argument supplies an anchor \(a\) with
\[
d_a=o(1),
\]
and (7) yields
\[
t_B\gg 1
\tag{8}
\]
for every bounded union \(B\) of the specified square-root cosets. Therefore the critical actual deficit cannot have Fourier mass concentrated in such a \(B\).

This is a genuine excluded prime configuration. It uses the true \(b_j\), the true \(q\), the true mark, and the true \(R151\) history; it is not a replacement by an arbitrary feasible row.

There is also a concrete range condition. Since (6) gives
\[
L\ll \max\left(1,\frac{\sqrt p}{T}\right),
\]
the full prime bound (7) is available whenever
\[
T\gtrsim p^{5/12}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, one needs
\[
m_{\rm eff}\lesssim p^{7/12}.
\]
If the admissible marked window only gives \(T\ll p^{5/12}\), then (7) has not yet been established uniformly over the entire actual effective-size range.

## Precise remaining gap

The endpoint charge does **not** presently imply either

1. \(d_a=o(1)\) for a useful anchor \(a\), except in the separately controlled critical regime; or
2. \(t_B=o(1)\) for a bounded union \(B\) of square-root cosets.

The reason is structural. The endpoint lower bound is only
\[
\mathbb E(hC_R)\ge c_0-o(1),
\]
where \(C_R\) is the actual positive-part mask
\[
\bigl(-(1-\lambda)e_\sigma-c-\delta^{-1/2}R\bigr)_+.
\]
It forces \(h\) to place \(L^2\)-mass on the actual coordinate set \(S_R\), but gives no multiplicative-coset description of \(S_R\), nor any relation between \(C_R\) and the multiplicative Fourier transform \(f\).

The following relaxed construction shows that this missing bridge cannot be supplied from the endpoint inequalities alone. On the same ordinary-prime coordinate set, choose \(S\) with \(|S|=n/T\), and define
\[
h_S=T1_S,\qquad C_S=1_S.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb Eh_S=1,\qquad
\mathbb E(h_SC_S)=1,\qquad
\|C_S\|_{2,n}=T^{-1/2},\qquad
m_{\rm eff}(h_S)=\frac nT.
\]
Thus \(h_S,C_S\) satisfy exactly the endpoint-size and endpoint-charge estimates (1), while
\[
f_S(x)=\frac Tn\sum_{j\in S}j(x)
\]
need not concentrate on any prescribed bounded union \(B\). For a uniformly random \(S\), and any fixed such \(B\),
\[
\mathbb E_S\|1_Bf_S\|_2^2
 \le 1+O\!\left(\frac{T|B|}{n}\right)
 =1+O\!\left(\frac T{\sqrt p}\right),
\]
whereas \(\|f_S\|_2^2=T\). Hence \(t_B\to1\) whenever \(T\to\infty\) and \(T=o(\sqrt p)\).

This example deliberately does **not** satisfy the actual low-motion condition (2). It therefore does not refute an endpoint-to-spectral implication for the actual history. It does rigorously identify the missing ingredient: one must use the exact active-set operators \(L_\sigma D_jL_\sigma\), or an arithmetic property of the actual endpoint mask, beyond the scalar charge and support bounds.

## Verdict

**Inconclusive for the actual asymptotic target.**

What is proved is:

- actual endpoint charge \(\Rightarrow\) coordinate concentration of \(h=b_j/r\) on \(S_R\);
- actual history \(\Rightarrow\) low projected motion;
- in the range \(L\le p^{1/12}\), ordinary-prime arithmetic excludes simultaneous small anchor deficit and bounded-coset Fourier concentration;
- in the critical regime \(m_{\rm eff}\sim m_0\), the anchor deficit is already \(o(1)\), so bounded-coset Fourier concentration is excluded.

What is not proved is that the actual endpoint charge forces the second side of that alternative. In particular, diffuse critical low-energy directions remain compatible with all presently established endpoint inequalities.

## Two ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: exact active-set endpoint estimate

For the actual R151 block, retain the exact matrices \(D_j\) and prove a restricted estimate of the form
\[
\mathbb E(hC_R)
\le o(1)+\Psi\bigl(t_B+d_a\bigr)
\]
for every actual \(R\), with \(\Psi(u)\to0\) as \(u\to0\), or directly prove that
\[
\mathbb E(hC_R)\ge c_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_B+d_a\le \varepsilon_p
\]
for some bounded-coset \(B\) and \(\varepsilon_p\to0\).

This must use the same actual \(b_j\) history and the secant relation
\[
h_{j+1}-h_j=-\omega D_jL_\sigma h_j,
\]
not an arbitrary vector with the same effective support. A successful estimate would combine with (7) and close the prime contradiction.

### 2. Second priority: isolate the effective-size and endpoint-mask ranges

First establish either
\[
T\gtrsim p^{5/12}
\]
at one admissible marked time, or directly prove for the actual step that
\[
m_{\rm eff}\le p^{7/12}.
\]
This activates the prime restriction over the entire relevant actual range.

In parallel, test whether the actual masks
\[
S_R=\left\{-(1-\lambda)e_\sigma>c+\delta^{-1/2}R\right\}
\]
have a uniform affine/multiplicative correlation bound strong enough to transfer coordinate concentration of \(h\) into Fourier concentration of \(f\). Without such an estimate, the endpoint mask remains an arbitrary-looking sparse physical set, and prime coset-spread alone cannot control it.
