## Result

I can derive a uniform all-order analytic tail for the Jacobi series, but the supplied record does **not** determine the requested \(P_{5000}\) price on the **unshifted \(C_1\) source**. The obstruction is specific: the available majorant in *cold-translated-leading-weight-table-142.md* is for a genuinely translated source with initial price vector \((A,b)=(0,1)\), whereas the requested source has a base Jacobi boundary and must be initialized with \((A,b)=(\pi(1),0)=(1,0)\). The corresponding base-column price recursion and the finite marked/lift constants are not supplied.

The all-order tail itself admits the following uniform rational majorant.

---

## 1. Actual parameters and domain

Use the actual family
\[
q=2n+1,\qquad n\ \text{prime or eligible prime-square},
\]
with the original closed square
\[
Q=\left\{t:\left|\Re t-\frac{2031}{128}\right|\le 2^{-17},\
|\Im t|\le2^{-17}\right\},
\]
and the actual marked floor
\[
t_0=n.
\]

The Jacobi scale is
\[
s_n=2\sqrt{\frac pn},\qquad
2.828427\le s_n\le2.828428
\]
for the stated large-\(n\) range. The cold coefficient \(\alpha\) remains explicit; the supplied records only establish
\[
|\alpha|<1.
\]

For every bulk point of \(Q\),
\[
\Re t\ge \frac{2031}{128}-2^{-17}>15.867,
\]
and at the mark \(t_0=n\). Thus, uniformly on the closed square,
\[
|t|^{-1/2}<0.252,\qquad
u:=\frac{s_n}{2\sqrt{|t|}}<0.356,\qquad
z:=u^2<0.127.
\]
The marked point is much smaller, since \(|t_0|^{-1/2}=n^{-1/2}\).

---

## 2. Exact scalar Jacobi expansions

Write
\[
q_k=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k}.
\]
For one Jacobi factor,
\[
d_t(s_n C_1)
 =\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k\,u\,q_k z^k\,C_1^{\,2k+1}.
\]
For one \(\alpha Y\)-factor,
\[
\alpha Yd_t'(s_nC_1)
 =\frac{\alpha}{2\sqrt{|t|}}
 \sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k(2k+1)q_kz^k\,C_1^{\,2k}Y.
\]

The symmetric two-step action is
\[
KD^{\rm J}KD^{\rm L}+KD^{\rm L}KD^{\rm J},
\]
with common physical prefactor
\[
\frac{\alpha s_n}{4t}.
\]

The \(\beta\)-parameter used in the signed pool calculation is
\[
\beta=\frac{s_n^2}{8t}.
\]
The actual marked point uses \(t=t_0=n\), not the bulk value.

---

## 3. Uniform tail after the \(\beta^2\) block

The displayed signed coefficient pools through \(\beta^2\) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{pool}&\text{coefficient}\\ \hline
K(CW)&1-3\beta+\frac{117}{16}\beta^2\\[2mm]
\frac12K(Y^2)&1-\frac52\beta+\frac{183}{32}\beta^2\\[2mm]
\mathcal R_{10}&-\frac34\beta+\frac{93}{32}\beta^2.
\end{array}
\]

For the absolute remainder, retain \(k=0,1,2\) in each one-dimensional Jacobi series and put all terms with total order \(k+\ell\ge3\) into the tail.

Using \(q_k\le1\), the omitted Jacobi mass and first-moment bounds are

\[
\overline m_{\rm J}
 \le u\sum_{k\ge3}z^k
 =\frac{u z^3}{1-z},
\]
\[
\overline a_{\rm J}
 \le u\sum_{k\ge3}(2k+1)z^k
 =\frac{u z^3(7-5z)}{(1-z)^2},
\]
and
\[
\overline m_{\rm L}
 \le \frac{|\alpha|}{\sqrt{|t|}}
       \sum_{k\ge3}(2k+1)z^k
 =\frac{|\alpha|z^3(7-5z)}
        {\sqrt{|t|}(1-z)^2},
\]
\[
\overline a_{\rm L}
 \le \frac{|\alpha|}{\sqrt{|t|}}
       \sum_{k\ge3}(2k+1)^2z^k.
\]

The last sum is explicitly
\[
\sum_{k\ge3}(2k+1)^2z^k
=
\frac{1+6z+z^2}{(1-z)^3}
-\left(1+9z+25z^2\right),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\;
\overline a_{\rm L}
 \le \frac{|\alpha|}{\sqrt{|t|}}
 \left[
 \frac{1+6z+z^2}{(1-z)^3}
 -(1+9z+25z^2)
 \right].
\;}
\]

At the worst bulk endpoint \(u<0.356\), \(z<0.127\), \(|t|^{-1/2}<0.252\), and \(|\alpha|<1\), these give the reproducible bounds
\[
\overline m_{\rm J}<8.3\times10^{-4},
\qquad
\overline a_{\rm J}<7.0\times10^{-3},
\]
\[
\overline m_{\rm L}<4.3\times10^{-3},
\qquad
\overline a_{\rm L}<4.2\times10^{-2}.
\]

At the actual mark \(t_0=n\), the same expressions are smaller by the factor
\[
O(n^{-3})
\]
in the Jacobi order and by the explicit factor \(n^{-1/2}\) in the Legendre-insertion series. Hence the bulk endpoint controls the uniform tail.

For either temporal order, the absolute discarded multiplier is bounded by
\[
\overline\mu
\le
\overline\mu_{\rm J}\mu_{\rm L}
+\mu_{\rm J}\overline\mu_{\rm L}
-\overline\mu_{\rm J}\overline\mu_{\rm L},
\]
where one may use the full majorants from the supplied record,
\[
m_{\rm J}\le \frac{u}{\sqrt{1-z}},
\qquad
a_{\rm J}\le \frac{u}{(1-z)^{3/2}},
\]
\[
m_{\rm L}\le \frac{1}{\sqrt{|t|}(1-z)^{3/2}},
\qquad
a_{\rm L}\le \frac{1+2z}{\sqrt{|t|}(1-z)^{5/2}}.
\]

Consequently, any valid base-column price functional \(B(A,b;\mu)\) satisfying the additive-fiber inequality
\[
A'\le \mu A+\nu b,\qquad b'\le m b
\]
has tail bounded by applying it to the four products
\[
(\overline m_{\rm J},\overline a_{\rm J})
  (\ m_{\rm L},a_{\rm L}),
\qquad
(m_{\rm J},a_{\rm J})
  (\overline m_{\rm L},\overline a_{\rm L}),
\]
in both orders, and multiplying by
\[
\left|\frac{\alpha s_n}{4t}\right|.
\]

This is a uniform ordinary analytic majorant on the original closed square, with the actual \(t_0=n\) included.

## 4. Why the requested \(P_{5000}\) conclusion does not follow

The signed pools are not enough by themselves to price the unshifted source.

The records establish the translated-source estimate
\[
(A,b)=(0,1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{full selected block}<0.237\,\Lambda+O_\Lambda(n^{-1/2}),
\qquad \Lambda=5000.
\]

The requested input instead has
\[
(A,b)=(\pi(1),0)=(1,0).
\]

For that input, the relevant base price after multiplication depends on the actual \(\pi\)-charges of the base fibers generated by

- \(K(CW)\),
- \(K(Y^2)\),
- \(\mathcal R_{10}\),
- and every separated residual family.

The supplied documents give ranks and folded moments for many residual families, but they do **not** give:

1. the actual \(P_{5000}\) price list \(\pi(k)\), rather than only
   \[
   k\le\pi(k)\le2k;
   \]
2. the exact base/off-base decomposition for the unshifted \(C_1\) input;
3. the finite marked and bad-lift constants for this specific unshifted block;
4. a demonstrated inequality converting the three signed pool coefficients into their physical \(P_{5000}\) prices.

Therefore I cannot honestly claim either a strict bound below \(1\) or a refutation above \(1\).

The available \(0.237\Lambda\) estimate is not applicable: it prices a translated source whose initial mass lies in the off-base coordinate \(b\), whereas the present source starts in the base coordinate \(A=1\).
