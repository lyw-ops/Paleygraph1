## Result: inconclusive, with a precise missing estimate

Let \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\) run through the **actual eligible primes**, put
\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad q=2n+1=p,
\]
and use the actual Paley operator \(K\), the actual zero-start Picard orbit, and the actual projected Cesàro row
\[
X_{\sigma,T}=M_T\mathbf 1+\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T P_\sigma A_{\sigma,t},
\qquad z_{\sigma,T}=\frac{X_{\sigma,T}}{M_T}.
\]
The physical marking is coordinate \(0\), with
\[
a=X_{\sigma,T}(0),\qquad z_{\sigma,T}(0)=a/M_T.
\]
All expectations below are normalized counting averages over the \(n\) physical coordinates, with \(d_0=0\).

Take
\[
u=T^{-1/3},\qquad d_i=(u-z_{\sigma,T}(i))_+ \quad (i\ne0).
\]

For a genuine opposite-sector nonnegative mean-one optimizer \(F\), with marked value \(q_*=F_0\), the pinned reflection calculation gives
\[
\boxed{
1-\frac{q_*a}{nM_T}
\;\ge\;
\left(1-\frac{q_*}{n}\right)u
-\mathbb E(Fd)
-O_\tau(T^{-2/3}).
}
\tag{A}
\]
The \(O_\tau(T^{-2/3})\) term includes the mean and pin corrections already controlled by the reciprocal-row argument. Thus the proposed route would succeed if, uniformly for both signs,
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E(Fd)=o(1)}
\tag{B}
\]
along the simultaneous marked window, with the order of limits
\[
p\to\infty\ \text{through }p\equiv1\pmod4
\quad\text{at fixed }\tau>0,
\qquad\text{then }\tau\downarrow0.
\]

### What is proved

The actual-row estimates establish only the unweighted bounds
\[
\mathbb E d=O_\tau(T^{-2/3}),
\qquad
\mathbb E d^2=O_\tau(T^{-2/3}),
\]
using
\[
z=U+e,\qquad \mathbb E(1/U)=O_\tau(1),\qquad \mathbb E e^2=O_\tau(T^{-1}).
\]

These do not imply (B). The available pointwise bound for an arbitrary opposite optimizer is
\[
0\le F_i\le 1+\sqrt p,
\]
so the direct estimate is only
\[
\mathbb E(Fd)\le (1+\sqrt p)\mathbb E d
=O_\tau(\sqrt p\,T^{-2/3}),
\]
which is useless in the proved simultaneous window. Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\mathbb E(Fd)\le \|F\|_{2}\,\|d\|_{2},
\]
but no prime-uniform bound on \(\|F\|_2\) is known; assuming one would be precisely the unresolved uniform-energy input.

A useful decomposition makes the remaining arithmetic requirement explicit:
\[
d\le (2u-U)_+ +(-e-u)_+,
\]
hence
\[
\mathbb E(Fd)
\le
2u\,\mathbb E\!\left[F\,1_{\{U<2u\}}\right]
+\frac1{4u}\mathbb E(F e^2).
\tag{C}
\]
Therefore one needs a **weighted small-ball estimate** for the actual opposite optimizer and a weighted endpoint estimate. The existing reciprocal estimate controls \(\mathbb E(1/U)\), not
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[F\,1_{\{U<2u\}}\right],
\]
and the endpoint RMS estimate controls \(\mathbb E e^2\), not \(\mathbb E(F e^2)\).

Consequently, the claimed vanishing signed spill on ordinary primes is **not proved**.

## Square-control obstruction

The same inequality correctly detects a positive obstruction on the exact square controls, without treating squares as prime counterexamples.

For \(q=r^2\), the exact opposite optimum has normalized mark asymptotic \(1/\sqrt2\), whereas the actual fixed-depth marked average satisfies, for fixed \(\tau,T\) with
\[
\tau+\frac1T<1,
\]
the lower bound
\[
\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{z_{-,T}(0)}{\sqrt n}
\ge (1+\tau+1/T)^{-1/2}>\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]
Thus no nonnegative opposite-sector completion preserving the actual mean and mark can exist for all sufficiently large squares. Applying (A) to the exact square optimizer forces
\[
\liminf_{r\to\infty}\mathbb E(F_{\square}d)
\ge
\Delta_{\tau,T}-O_\tau(T^{-2/3}),
\]
where \(\Delta_{\tau,T}>0\) is the corresponding mark-budget gap. For fixed \(\tau>0\), choosing \(T\) sufficiently large makes the right-hand side positive.

This is a genuine obstruction to a **prime-insensitive** weighted-hinge estimate. It does not refute (B) on the actual prime family \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), because the square optimizer has different arithmetic support and the prime and square limits are not interchangeable.

## Evidence and remaining gap

Evidence supporting the route:

1. The unweighted hinge mass is already smaller than the reserve:
   \[
   \mathbb E d=O_\tau(T^{-2/3})=o(T^{-1/3}).
   \]
2. The pinned reflection identity isolates the only unresolved quantity as the positive weighted spill \(\mathbb E(Fd)\).
3. Broad LP information shows that a positive-cost dual witness must have substantial support; this is compatible with a prime weighted small-ball estimate, but does not prove one.
4. The finite prime tests and the \(p=97\) strict cone-gap example show that actual prime opposite optimizers can have nontrivial support leakage behavior, but they provide no asymptotic estimate.

The gap is therefore not local feasibility. It is the absence of a prime-uniform estimate coupling the **actual deficit field \(d\)** to the **actual opposite optimal row \(F\)**.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Weighted small-ball test for the actual opposite optimizer

For each actual prime \(p\), sign \(\sigma\), and depth \(T\) in the proved simultaneous marked window, compute or bound
\[
S_{p,\sigma}(u)
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
F_{p,-\sigma}\,
1_{\{U_{\sigma,T}<2u\}}
\right],
\]
where \(F_{p,-\sigma}\) is the genuine nonnegative opposite-sector optimizer, not a square control or a surrogate LP row.

The target is
\[
u\,S_{p,\sigma}(u)=o(1),
\]
together with
\[
\mathbb E(F_{p,-\sigma}e_{\sigma,T}^2)=o(u).
\]
This directly verifies the two terms in (C), preserves the physical marking, and uses the required prime-first limit.

**Why first:** it tests exactly the weighted quantity needed by (A), rather than attempting an unnecessary max-norm spill estimate.

### 2. Localized Jacobi-transform / support-saturation test

Use the zero set or near-zero set of the actual opposite optimizer to bound the signed Jacobi transform
\[
(Kd)(i)
\quad\text{or directly}\quad
\mathbb E(Fd),
\]
while retaining the true arithmetic support of \(F\). A useful finite target is a uniform estimate of the form
\[
\sum_{i\in B_{p,\sigma}}F_i d_i
\le
C_\tau T^{-2/3}+o_p(1),
\]
where \(B_{p,\sigma}\) is the actual low-value set of \(z_{\sigma,T}\), together with a separate estimate on the endpoint-error part.

**Why second:** the marked-kernel and support-saturation results indicate that the relevant obstruction is concentrated on the actual opposite support, but a localized Jacobi estimate is technically more demanding and must avoid replacing the actual optimizer by an ordinary LP relaxation.

No checked result currently establishes either test, so the ordinary-prime vanishing signed-spill question remains open.
