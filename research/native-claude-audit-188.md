### 2.1 The hypotheses (check 1)

- **\(\nu\ge0\) and \(\omega\ge0\): accepted.** \(\nu\) is the actual near-critical *probability* on \(\widehat Q\) ((H1), Rounds 163/173/178–182). With \(\lambda_\nu=\nu*\check\nu\ge0\) and \(\mathbf 1_Q\zeta=\frac12(\tilde\zeta+\tilde\zeta\chi_2)\) for a lift \(\tilde\zeta\), \(g=\sum_\alpha\omega(\alpha)\alpha\) on \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) with \(\omega(\alpha)=\frac12\lambda_\nu(\alpha|_Q)\ge0\), \(\sum\omega=1\). The Round 182 setup states exactly this ("\(\omega\) the actual law (a probability on the characters of \(Q\))").
- **(H3) inversion symmetry: true, and accepted.**
  - *Proof (one line).* \(f(1/x)=\sum_\eta\nu_\eta\eta(x)^{-1}=\overline{f(x)}\) because \(\nu\) is real, so \(g(1/x)=g(x)\) on \(Q\); \(Q^{-1}=Q\) and \(g=0\) off \(Q\) (including \(g(0)=0\)).
  - *Equivalently,* (H3) \(\Leftrightarrow\omega(\bar\alpha)=\omega(\alpha)\Leftrightarrow\omega\) real (given \(g\) real). So it is implied by \(\omega\ge0\).
  - *Where accepted.* Round 182 setup lists "(H3) Inversion symmetry \(g(1/z)=g(z)\)" among the hypotheses "accepted in Rounds 163, 173 and 178–180". The accepted Round 169 reduction of the four kernels to the single \(\alpha\) kernel uses \(w(t)=w(1/t)\), i.e. (H3).
  - *Replay.* \(|g(1/x)-g(x)|\le6\cdot10^{-15}\) for every law and prime in Part B of my script; \(\omega\ge-10^{-12}\), \(\omega=\lambda_\nu/2\) on both lifts, \(G=(p-1)\omega(1)\), \(\Gamma_{\rm tot}=(p-1)\sum\omega^2\).
  - *Load-bearing?* (H3) is used in Lemma A1 (\(\Lambda_0=X\)) and in Theorem A(i) (\(\sum_\alpha\omega(\alpha)\bar\alpha(\lambda)=g(1/\lambda)=g(\lambda)\)). Without (H3), \(\Lambda_0\ne X\) occurs (4 of 5 asymmetric integer weights at \(p=29\), Part A). This does not matter for the actual \(g\).
- **Verdict on the hypotheses: Theorem A is not conditional.** Both hypotheses hold for the actual \(g\) under the accepted definitions.

### 2.2 The identities, re-derived (check 2)

1. **\(m'(b)=c_h(b)\) on \(Q\setminus\{1\}\) (Lemma A1). ### 2.3 The covariance chain (check 3)

- **Normalization.** Rounds 169/173/178/182: the one-match covariance is \(O\bigl(p^{-1}\sum_{s\ne0,1,t}w(t)|H_\alpha(s,t)|^2+p^{3/2}\bigr)\) and the target \(O(p^{3/2})\) is \(\sum w|H_\alpha|^2=O(p^{5/2})\); the four kernels reduce to the \(\alpha\) kernel (Round 169, via \(w(t)=w(1/t)\)); \(0\le H_\alpha\le F_\mu h_t\) off \(s\in\{0,1\}\) with the endpoints priced at \(O(p^{5/2})\) (Round 173/178). BPP uses exactly these.
- **The step.** Dropping \(s\in\{0,1\}\) only lowers \(\sum_s|F_\mu h_t(s)|^2=M_1^2G^2/p+\|\pi\varphi_t\|^2\) (Round 182 Prop. 1). So the weighted sum is \(\le WM_1^2G^2/p+\sum_tw(t)\|\pi\varphi_t\|^2+O(p^{5/2})\), and
  - uniform branch: \(\sum_tw(t)\|\pi\varphi_t\|^2\le W\cdot pT_0\);
  - averaged branch: \(w\le\frac14\) and (ii) give \(\le\frac p4\mathcal T\).
  Dividing by \(p\): covariance \(\ll p^{3/2}+\min(WT_0,\mathcal T/4)\le p^{3/2}+\min(W,\Gamma_{\rm tot}/4)X\); the mean term is \(WM_1^2G^2/p^2\le G^7/p^2\ll p^{3/2}\) under the cap. *Replay:* the weighted sum over \(s\ne0,1\) against this right side: ratio \(\le0.84\) in every case.
- **Dimensions.** Both branches are (mass of size \(\le G\asymp\sqrt p\)) \(\times X\): \(W=\sum_tw(t)=G-\Gamma_{\rm tot}\) enters by summing a \(t\)-uniform bound against \(w\); \(\Gamma_{\rm tot}/4\) enters through \(w\le\frac14\) and the orthogonality over all \(t\). Logically and dimensionally right.

### 2.5 "Correction to the recorded obligation" (check 5): ACCEPTED WITH CORRECTION

- **What is right.** Round 187's record says reaching \(O(p^{3/2})\) "needs an input that is not an affine-energy bound **and** the generic six-word class below \(p^{9/2}\)". By Theorem A, \(X=O(p)\) alone gives covariance \(O(p^{3/2})\) (since \(\min(W,\Gamma_{\rm tot}/4)\le G\ll\sqrt p\)), with no six-word, affine-energy or (NC) input. So the six-word class is **not necessary**; it was the requirement of one route (operator norm, b1″/b-k3). Likewise "the Round 178 chain floors the covariance at \(p^{7/4}\)" remains true of the RS/point–plane chain, but \(7/4\) is no longer a floor for the one-match problem: through Theorem A every power saving in \(X\) (or \(T_0\)) moves the covariance exponent one-for-one down to \(3/2\).
- **Floors.** No contradiction with Round 184: on (NC), \(X\ge(2-o(1))p\) and \(W\asymp\sqrt p\), so Theorem A's bound is \(\gg p^{3/2}\): it sits **exactly at** the target scale when \(X=O(p)\). \(X=O(p)\) is borderline but possible: it says \(X\) is within a constant factor of its floor, with **no** \(p^{o(1)}\) or logarithmic loss. The floor \(\max_\xi W_\xi\ge(1-o(1))p\) concerns a different quantity (see §2.3) and does not enter.
- **What needs correcting.**
  1. \(X=O(p)\) is **one sufficient route among several**, not "the" one-match obligation. Others: \(T_0=O(p)\) (uniform branch), \(\mathcal T=O(p^{3/2})\) or \(\Gamma_{\rm tot}X=O(p^{3/2})\) (averaged branch), \(\Gamma_{\rm tot}=O(p^{1/4})\) (Cor. B1), \(E_{\ne1}(\mu)=O(p^2)\) (operator route), b1″ via the six-word class.
  2. \(X=O(p)\) is **strictly stronger** than the first-mass target \(X=O(p^{1+1/1280})\) and needs a logarithm-free, near-sharp shifted energy \(E_Q(h)\asymp G^4/p\) for the actual level sets; the Round 187 audit called the weaker first-mass target "a sum-product statement beyond current incidence technology". So the merge does not make the one-match target easier than the first mass; it makes **intermediate** savings in \(X\) (or \(T_0\)) valuable for the one-match exponent, which they were not before (gain was capped at \(1/24\)).
  3. Corollary A's "(any \(g\) with the cap)" should read "any \(g\) with the cap and (H3)" (via branch (ii)); the actual \(g\) satisfies both.
