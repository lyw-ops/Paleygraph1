## Result: exact signed packet identity, but no sub-budget physical estimate

Let \(p\) run through the eligible primes in the nominated family, with \(\chi(-1)=1\), and let
\[
G=\mathbb F_p^{\times 2},\qquad n=|G|=(p-1)/2.
\]
All identities below are at the actual height \(601\), on the original closed square, with the physical mark \(t_0=n\) retained. Let \(d,j\) be the inverse Fourier rows of the actual height-601 derivative and the pure Jacobi derivative, respectively, and put \(e=d-j\). Thus the marked entries are already included in \(e\) and \(j\); no unmarked surrogate is being used.

For \(x,y\in G\), write \(c=xy\). The mixed same-row term is
\[
\mathcal A(x,y)
=
s(x)\sum_{z\in G}\bigl(s(z)+s(c/z)\bigr)e(x/z)j(z/y),
\qquad s(u)=\chi(1-u).
\tag{1}
\]

### 1. Symmetrized whole-kernel pullback for \(c\neq1\)

Define
\[
F_{x,y}(z)
=
\frac12\Bigl(
e(x/z)j(z/y)+e(z/y)j(x/z)
\Bigr).
\]
Since \(c/z\) interchanges the two factors, \(F_{x,y}(c/z)=F_{x,y}(z)\). The complete mixed kernel is therefore
\[
\mathcal A(x,y)
=
s(x)\sum_{z\in G}\bigl(s(z)+s(c/z)\bigr)F_{x,y}(z).
\tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
z_c(r)=\frac{r^2-c}{r^2-1},
\qquad r^2\notin\{1,c\}.
\]
Using a symmetric actual trace extension \(\widetilde F_{x,y}\) to \(\mathbb F_p^\times\), the one-branch identity gives, for \(c\neq1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(x,y)
=
s(x)s(c)F_{x,y}(1)
+
s(x)s(c)
\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathbb F_p\\ r^2\notin\{1,c\}}}
\chi(r^2-1)\,
\widetilde F_{x,y}\!\left(z_c(r)\right).
}
\tag{3}
\]

This is a genuine signed packet-level identity for the complete two-step operator. The nonsquare cutoff has disappeared inside the summed kernel; it was not removed by separately pricing either inverse branch. Both trace lift pairs remain inside \(e\) and \(j\), and the physical mark remains inside the same rows.

The term \(F_{x,y}(1)\) is the actual \(z=1\) contribution:
\[
F_{x,y}(1)
=
\frac12\left(e(x)j(1/y)+e(1/y)j(x)\right).
\]
Thus the \(z=1\) contribution is retained explicitly and is not absorbed into an already-priced inverse operator.

### 2. The \(c=1\) inverse line is genuinely different

When \(c=1\), \(y=x^{-1}\), and because \(z\in G\) and \(\chi(-1)=1\),
\[
s(1/z)=s(z).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(x,x^{-1})
=
2s(x)\sum_{z\in G}s(z)e(x/z)j(zx).
}
\tag{4}
\]
Equivalently, after \(u=x/z\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(x,x^{-1})
=
2s(x)\sum_{u\in G}s(x/u)e(u)j(x^2/u).
}
\tag{5}
\]

There is no paired-sign cancellation on this line. The \(z=1\) term itself vanishes because \(s(1)=0\), but the remaining mask has
\[
\sum_{z\in G}\left|2s(z)\right|^2=4(n-1).
\tag{6}
\]
This is the exact inverse-line norm at the physical level. It is substantially different from the generic \(c\neq1\) mask, whose squared norm is
\[
2n-4-a_p(c),
\]
with \(a_p(c)\) the Legendre trace.

Consequently, the generic one-branch cancellation cannot by itself supply a uniform \(P_{5000}\) estimate: the inverse line must be priced separately as a whole operator.

## Local geometry

For \(c\neq1\), the pullback \(z_c(r)\) has the two branch loci
\[
r^2=1,\qquad r^2=c,
\]
and the exceptional target values \(z=1,c\). The symmetric trace extension is legitimate because the two branch contributions are exchanged by
\[
r\longmapsto \frac{\sqrt c}{r},
\]
while the full weight is invariant under \(z\mapsto c/z\).

At \(c=1\), these two branch loci coalesce and the rational parametrization degenerates to the inverse line. Therefore \(c=1\) is not a limiting generic case for the weighted estimate; it is a separate local geometry.

The known pure leading term also has a Legendre collision at the parameter \(x=-1\), corresponding to the unipotent point \(\lambda=1\) under \(x\mapsto-1/x\). That establishes a local-system collision for the pure comparator, but it does **not** prove a lower bound for the mixed difference \(e\cdot j\), nor does it show that the mixed contribution survives after the full signed packet sum.

## Physical estimate and failure point

The identity (3) is stronger than a pointwise Weil estimate because it sums the two inverse branches before absolute values. However, I cannot verify from the supplied data a weighted whole-operator price
\[
\|\mathcal A\|_{P_{5000}}
<
0.268468
\]
for the mixed anticommutator, nor the corresponding square-difference target
\[
\|(KD)^2-(KD^{\mathrm J})^2\|_{P_{5000}}
<
0.703184.
\]

The precise obstruction is the inverse line (5). Its mask has no sign saving and squared norm \(4(n-1)\). The one-branch identity controls the generic \(c\neq1\) fibers, but it supplies no decay for the translated packet
\[
u\longmapsto e(u)j(x^2/u)
\]
on \(c=1\). A counting estimate would be insufficient, and individual Weil bounds on the two trace factors do not control the \(P_{5000}\) norm of the complete operator.

Thus the proposal is **inconclusive, not refuted**:

- the required signed whole-kernel identity exists;
- the generic mixed geometry is compatible with the proposed pullback;
- the inverse line remains an unpriced physical component;
- no invariant confinement or half-step conclusion follows yet.

The order of limits is important. The identities hold for each finite eligible prime \(p\). A physical estimate would need to be proved for the exact finite-prime, physically marked \(P_{5000}\) norm, with analytic packet truncation or closure taken first at fixed \(p\), followed by a uniform \(p\to\infty\) limit through the eligible prime family. A finite numerical replay at fixed \(p\), or a counting decay estimate without the physical marking, would not settle the asymptotic target.

   Apply the actual \(P_{5000}\) packet representation directly to (5), retaining both trace lift pairs and the \(t_0=n\) mark. Separate the \(u=1\) local contribution, then price the remaining translated packet uniformly in \(x\). This is the first place where a genuine obstruction or a sub-\(0.268468\) estimate can appear.

2. **Local cohomology at the mixed collision \((x,u)=(-1,1)\).**  
   Analyze the two actual trace sheaves in
   \[
   s(x/u)e(u)j(x^2/u)
   \]
   with their Kummer factors retained. Determine whether the local monodromies cancel in the symmetrized mixed packet or produce a surviving unipotent component. A surviving component would be a precise representation-level obstruction; cancellation would justify a refined inverse-line packet identity.
