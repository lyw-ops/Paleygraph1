## Result

For ordinary primes \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\), with
\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
I do not obtain a prime-uniform \(o(T)\) bound for the actual oriented mask or signed terminal pair. The obstruction is structural: the physical mask
\[
S^+=\{i\neq0:x_T(i)\ge cT,\quad R_i\le \delta x_T(i)/T\},
\]
where
\[
x_T=s\sqrt n\,v_T=s\sqrt n\,v_1+sKH,\qquad H=\sqrt n\,g_{T-1},
\]
is score-dependent and need not have any presently controlled character complexity.

The exact terminal identity from the cited work remains valid:
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E(\lambda x_T)}{2T\sqrt n M}
 =\mathbb E(\lambda U_{<T})-1+\frac{\bar q(\Xi_0+1)}n
   +\frac{\mathbb E[\lambda(\Phi(v_0)+\mathbb E g_T)]}{2TM}. \tag{1}
\]
Here \(s=-\sigma\), \(F\) is a true \(s\)-optimizer or near-cap mixture, \(R\) is a true \(-s\)-optimizer, \(\lambda=1_{\rm bulk}\bar F\), and all expectations are normalized by \(n\).

If a prime subsequence satisfied
\[
\frac{q_s}{\sqrt n}\ge 1+\eta,
\]
then, after fixing \(\tau>0\) with \(\sqrt{1+\tau}<1+\eta\), taking \(T=T(p)\to\infty\) with \(T+1\) in the actual marked window, and then taking \(\epsilon=o(1)\), equation (1) gives
\[
\liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E(\lambda x_T)}{T}
\ge 2(1+\eta-\sqrt{1+\tau})>0. \tag{2}
\]
Thus an \(o(T)\) upper bound for the signed terminal pair would exclude the gap. I could not prove that upper bound.

## A restricted new estimate

Let \(A\subseteq G\) be a frequency set, \(X^\circ=1_{\{j\ne0\}}(F_j-L)_+\), and let \(\mu\) be any probability measure on shifts. Define
\[
X_\mu^\circ=\sum_h\mu_h\,\tau_hX^\circ.
\]
For the actual mask \(S^+\),
\[
\|1_{S^+}X_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}^2
=\frac1n\sum_{y,z}X_y^\circ X_z^\circ W_{S^+}(y,z),
\]
with
\[
W_{S^+}(y,z)=\sum_i1_{S^+}(i)\mu_{y-i}\mu_{z-i}. \tag{3}
\]

The following restricted decomposition is useful. Write
\[
1_{S^+}=\rho_S+e_S,\qquad \rho_S=\frac{|S^+|}{n},
\qquad \mathbb E e_S=0.
\]
Then
\[
\|1_{S^+}X_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}^2
\le \rho_S\|X_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}^2
 +\|e_S\|_{2,n}\,\|X_\mu^\circ\|_{4,n}^2. \tag{4}
\]
This is just Cauchy–Schwarz applied to
\[
\mathbb E(e_S|X_\mu^\circ|^2).
\]

The first term is genuinely small for a diffuse shift law. Since
\[
|S^+|\ll_{\tau,c}\frac{n}{T}
\]
by the energy bound \(\|x_T\|_{2,n}^2=O_\tau(T)\), one has
\[
\rho_S\|X_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}^2
\ll_{\tau,c}\frac1T\|X_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}^2. \tag{5}
\]
For a uniform law on \(a\) shifts and \(X^\circ\le F\), the diagonal contribution satisfies
\[
\|X_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}^2
\le \frac{\|X^\circ\|_{2,n}^2}{a}
 +\frac{nK(A)}{a^2},
\]
where \(K(A)\) is the largest off-diagonal overlap multiplicity. Hence the density term in (4) is \(o(T)\) whenever
\[
\frac{\sqrt n}{a}=o(T),\qquad K(A)=o\!\left(\frac{a^2T}{n}\right), \tag{6}
\]
recovering the useful diffuse-cap regime.

The new point is that all remaining difficulty is isolated in the centered mask term
\[
\mathcal E_{S^+}(X_\mu^\circ)
:=\mathbb E\!\left[e_S|X_\mu^\circ|^2\right]. \tag{7}
\]
A sufficient restricted estimate for the desired conclusion is
\[
\|e_S\|_{2,n}\,\|X_\mu^\circ\|_{4,n}^2=o(T). \tag{8}
\]
This is stronger than mere density control, but weaker than requiring pointwise or uniform cancellation of every \(W_{S^+}(y,z)\).

For a character-expandable mask, (7) becomes a genuine prime-sensitive sum. If
\[
e_S(i)=\sum_{\chi\ne1}\widehat e_S(\chi)\chi(i),
\]
then each term in (7) is a Jacobi-character correlation against the fixed weighted difference profile generated by \(X_\mu^\circ\). Weil estimates could control it provided the nonzero Fourier support of \(e_S\) is represented by bounded-complexity rational character expressions. The actual mask does not currently satisfy such a representation: its defining inequality involves the nonlinear orbit \(v_T\), the previous increment \(H\), and the true opposite optimizer \(R\).

The orientation does give the exact relation
\[
x_T=s\sqrt n\,v_1+sKH,
\qquad
\mathbb E(\bar F\,sKH)=\mathbb E(\bar F H)-\mathbb EH. \tag{9}
\]
The first term is \(O_\tau(1)\) by the physical Jacobi seed estimate, and the endpoint \(T-1\) cancellation is preserved. However, (9) controls the signed average against \(\bar F\); it does not control the centered indicator \(e_S\) in (7). In particular, it does not prevent \(S^+\) from aligning with a concentrated portion of \(X_\mu^\circ\).

## Why a direct Weil estimate is unavailable

For fixed algebraic sets \(S\), sums such as
\[
\sum_{i\in S}\chi(i-y)\chi(i-z)
\]
can be bounded by \(O(\sqrt p)\), with constants depending on the number of factors. That regime is not applicable here.

The actual \(S^+\) is not a fixed algebraic set. It depends on:

1. the zero-start orbit at the selected \(T=T(p)\);
2. the physical sign \(s=-\sigma\);
3. the inequality \(s\sqrt n\,v_T\ge cT\);
4. the opposite true optimizer through \(R_i\le\delta x_T(i)/T\);
5. the limits \(p\to\infty\), \(T=T(p)\to\infty\), and only subsequently \(\tau\downarrow0\).

Consequently, no bounded-degree character polynomial for \(1_{S^+}\) is known. The ambient estimates
\[
n2^{-k}+O(k\sqrt p)
\]
from simultaneous Jacobi masks cannot be applied: the relevant \(k\) would have to encode the orbit-dependent score, and the required \(k\sqrt p=o(T)\) scale is unavailable in the actual growing window.

The singleton case also shows why no unconditional estimate can follow from density alone. If \(\mu\) is supported on one shift, then \(X_\mu^\circ\) is a translate of \(X^\circ\), and the centered term in (7) can be as large as the entire masked collision whenever the cap support aligns with \(S^+\). The hypotheses \(R_y=R_z=0\) on the true opposite zero set do not by themselves prevent this alignment.

## Hypotheses and remaining gap

A prime-uniform proof would follow from either of the following quantified hypotheses for some selectable \(T(p)\to\infty\) with \(T+1\) in the true window:

1. **Centered-mask estimate**
   \[
   \|e_{S^+}\|_{2,n}\,\|X_\mu^\circ\|_{4,n}^2=o(T)
   \]
   for a near-cap optimizer and diffuse \(\mu\); or

2. **Direct weighted difference estimate**
   \[
   \sum_{y,z}X_y^\circ X_z^\circ
   \left(W_{S^+}(y,z)-\rho_S\right)=o(T).
   \]

Either would combine with (2), the exact opposite overlap
\[
\mathbb E(1_{\rm bulk}\bar F R)\le C\epsilon,
\]
and the physical orientation (9) to give the required contradiction to a fixed root gap.

Neither estimate is proved. Thus the present result is **inconclusive**, not a refutation of the prime-oriented terminal-mask proposal.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: actual centered-mask Fourier test

For ordinary primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), use the true zero-start orbit, fixed \(\tau\), the physical mark, and a selectable \(T+1\) in the certified window. Compute or bound
\[
\widehat e_{S^+}(\chi)
\]
only against the weighted frequencies appearing in
\[
|X_\mu^\circ|^2.
\]
The target is the restricted estimate (8), not a full \(\ell^1\) Fourier bound. This directly tests whether the orientation \(x_T=s\sqrt n v_1+sKH\) creates cancellation beyond absolute activation.

### 2. Singleton and sparse-cap stress test

Take \(\mu\) supported on one shift, then on \(a\) shifts with
\[
a\asymp \sqrt n
\quad\text{and}\quad
a\gg \sqrt n/T.
\]
For each actual prime and marked \(T\), measure
\[
\frac1T\|1_{S^+}X_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}^2
\]
and separately the centered contribution (7). If the singleton or sparse-cap contribution remains order \(T\), this precisely identifies the alignment obstruction and shows that a diffuse-cap hypothesis is essential. If it is \(o(T)\) uniformly, it supplies evidence for a genuine restricted prime estimate, though not yet an asymptotic proof.
