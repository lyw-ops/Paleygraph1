## Result: exact Fourier transform exists, but the present identities do not transfer cancellation

I work on the actual ordinary-prime family \(p\to\infty\) along the selected critical branch, with
\[
Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,\qquad n=|Q|=(p-1)/2,
\]
the true five-draw law \(w\), and the genuine five-variable pushforward
\[
P(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta)
=\sum_xw(x)w(\alpha x)w(\beta\alpha x)w(x/C)w(\delta x).
\]
No independent-ratio replacement is made. The physical marking remains the favorable \(\chi(1-t)=\rho\) marking, and the clean rank-two trace is the single \((q,i)=(0,0)\) trace.

After the checked \(O(p)\) removal of the nine clean-locus boundary pieces, the remaining quantity is
\[
\mathcal S
=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
J(\beta\alpha C,(\beta C)^{-1})\,
\mathscr K(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta)
\right]+O(p),
\]
where, for \(q=0,s=1\),
\[
\mathscr K
=\sum_{z,y}
 \beta(z)\,(\alpha\bar\delta\chi)(z+1)\,
 C(y)\,\delta(y+1)\,\chi(z-y).
\]
The \(O(p)\) here uses the previously checked algebraic relations on the exact resonance strata; it is not a cardinality estimate for a varying determinant locus.

### 1. Exact Fourier expansion of the true pushforward

Fix the standard multiplicative pairing on \(Q\), identifying \(Q\) with its character group. With the normalized Fourier convention,
\[
P(r)=n^{-4}\sum_{\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_4}
 \widehat P(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_4)
 \,\xi_1(\beta)\xi_2(C)\xi_3(\alpha)\xi_4(\delta),
\]
the exact coefficient is
\[
\widehat P(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,\xi_4)
=
f(\xi_1)f(\xi_2)f(\xi_3)f(\xi_4)
f\!\left((\xi_1\xi_2\xi_3\xi_4)^{-1}\right),
\]
where \(f\) is the actual Fourier transform of \(w\). Thus
\[
\mathcal S
=n^{-4}\sum_{\xi_1,\dots,\xi_4}
\prod_{j=1}^4 f(\xi_j)\,
f((\xi_1\xi_2\xi_3\xi_4)^{-1})
\,\widehat G(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_4)+O(p),
\]
with
\[
G(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta)
=J(\beta\alpha C,(\beta C)^{-1})\mathscr K(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta).
\]

This is an exact five-factor Fourier identity retaining the true overlap weights.

### 2. A nontrivial exact transform of the clean trace

Expanding the outer Jacobi sum gives
\[
J(\beta\alpha C,(\beta C)^{-1})
=
\sum_{t}
 (\beta\alpha C)(t)\,
 (\beta C)^{-1}(1-t).
\]
Substituting this and \(\mathscr K\), the dependence on the four ratio variables is
\[
\beta\!\left(\frac{tz}{1-t}\right),\qquad
C\!\left(\frac{ty}{1-t}\right),\qquad
\alpha\!\bigl(t(z+1)\bigr),\qquad
\delta\!\left(\frac{y+1}{z+1}\right).
\]
Therefore Fourier orthogonality gives the following exact transform. If \(\iota(x)\) denotes the dual character corresponding to \(x\in Q\), then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\widehat G(a,b,c,d)
={}&n^4
\sum_{\substack{t,z,y\\
\iota(\frac{tz}{1-t})=a\\
\iota(\frac{ty}{1-t})=b\\
\iota(t(z+1))=c\\
\iota(\frac{y+1}{z+1})=d}}
\chi(z+1)\chi(z-y).
\end{aligned}}
\]
All variables are restricted to the nonzero, nondegenerate locus inherited from the clean trace; the deleted boundary terms are already part of the \(O(p)\) contribution above.

Equivalently, the Fourier transform is supported on the three-parameter rational image
\[
(a,b,c,d)
=
\iota\!\left(
\frac{tz}{1-t},
\frac{ty}{1-t},
t(z+1),
\frac{y+1}{z+1}
\right),
\]
with the exact quadratic phase \(\chi(z+1)\chi(z-y)\).

This is a genuine trace-specific transform; it is not a transform of a smoothed or independent-ratio model.

### 3. Why this does not yet yield the required cancellation

Substitution into the Fourier expansion gives the equivalent identity
\[
\mathcal S
=
\sum_{t,z,y}
\chi(z+1)\chi(z-y)\,
\prod_{r\in\mathcal R(t,z,y)} f(r)
+O(p),
\]
where
\[
\mathcal R(t,z,y)=
\left\{
\frac{tz}{1-t},
\frac{ty}{1-t},
t(z+1),
\frac{y+1}{z+1},
\left(
\frac{t^3zy(y+1)}{(1-t)^2}
\right)^{-1}
\right\}.
\]
The fifth argument follows from
\[
\prod_{j=1}^4 r_j
=
\frac{t^3zy(y+1)}{(1-t)^2}.
\]

This identity is useful because it exposes the exact overlap geometry. However, it does not provide the desired \(O(p)\) bound for the following reasons.

1. **The transform is only a reparametrization of the same coupled sum.**  
   The phase \(\chi(z+1)\chi(z-y)\) is oscillatory, but the five \(f\)-factors are the actual critical Fourier coefficients. The near-unit part of \(f\) can concentrate precisely on rational images of the above map. No independent square-root cancellation is available for the weighted triple sum.

2. **The known overlap identity controls only an unweighted norm.**  
   The exact identity
   \[
   \sum_{\beta,C,\alpha,\delta}P(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta)^2
   =\sum_{d}R(d)^5,\qquad R=w*\check w,
   \]
   gives the collision mass of the true pushforward. It does not control
   \[
   \sum P(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta)\,
   J(\beta\alpha C,(\beta C)^{-1})\mathscr K(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta).
   \]
   Cauchy–Schwarz against \(\sum P^2\) loses the trace-specific phase and is too large on the critical branch.

3. **The low-to-middle Fourier modes are not perturbative.**  
   The exact coefficient contains
   \[
   f(\xi_1)f(\xi_2)f(\xi_3)f(\xi_4)
   f((\xi_1\xi_2\xi_3\xi_4)^{-1}),
   \]
   and the marked critical branch permits a growing set of \(\xi\)'s with \(|f(\xi)|\approx1\). Thus an \(L^2\) estimate for \(\widehat G\), or a generic \(p^{3/2}\) Weil bound for individual transform pieces, does not imply the required normalized \(p^{-1/2}\) cancellation.

4. **The exact transform does not commute with the favorable marking in a simplifying way.**  
   The physical marking survives as the factor \(\chi(z-y)\), while the outer Jacobi phase contributes the independent factor \(\chi(z+1)\). Their product is not a single Jacobi character after the five \(f\)-weights are inserted. Hence the previous one-variable pin and the overlap identity do not collapse the transform to a positive or diagonal form.

### Checked conclusion

For the actual ordinary-prime family, true five-draw law \(w\), physical marking, and the single clean \((q,i)=(0,0)\) trace:

- the exact Fourier transform above is valid;
- it preserves all five shifted weights and the correct overlap normalization;
- it gives a nontrivial three-parameter trace representation;
- but it does **not** presently imply the required \(O(p)\) estimate.

Thus the proposed transfer is **inconclusive**, with a precise failure: exact Fourier expansion plus the known overlap identity does not control the correlation of the five-factor coefficient
\[
f(\xi_1)f(\xi_2)f(\xi_3)f(\xi_4)f((\xi_1\xi_2\xi_3\xi_4)^{-1})
\]
against the transformed clean trace.

The obstruction is not total-variation smoothing; that obstruction remains valid and is avoided here. The missing input is a trace-specific estimate on the rational image above.

## Ranked next tests

### 1. Highest priority: a weighted three-variable trace estimate

Prove, on the actual critical ordinary-prime branch, a bound of the form
\[
\left|
\sum_{t,z,y}
\chi(z+1)\chi(z-y)
\prod_{r\in\mathcal R(t,z,y)} f(r)
\right|
\ll p.
\]
The relevant test is to split each \(f=f_{\mathrm{near}}+f_{\mathrm{rem}}\), where \(f_{\mathrm{near}}\) is supported on the near-unit set, and apply the marked phase relation only after fixing \(t\) or \(z/y\). A successful argument must retain the five rational arguments jointly; bounding them by separate \(L^2\) norms will lose the required scale.

### 2. Second priority: conditional overlap correlation

For
\[
P_{\beta,C}(\alpha,\delta)
=\sum_xw(x)w(\alpha x)w(\beta\alpha x)w(x/C)w(\delta x),
\]
test whether the conditional trace satisfies a variance estimate
\[
\sum_{\beta,C}
\mathbb E_{\alpha,\delta\mid\beta,C}
\left|
G(\beta,C,\alpha,\delta)
-\mathbb E_{\alpha,\delta\mid\beta,C}G
\right|^2
\ll p^{2+o(1)}.
\]
Combined with the exact collision formula \(\sum P^2=\sum R^5\), this could yield an \(O(p)\) aggregate bound without replacing \(P\) by a smoothed law. The test must preserve the common-shift fiber \(x\); discarding it would revert to the already ruled-out independent-ratio model.
