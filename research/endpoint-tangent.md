# One-sided theta penalties and the mark at the first support change

The [actual endpoint](endpoint-norm.md) of an ordinary theta LP is also the endpoint of a one-sided squared-norm penalty. A very small first-change penalty can occur while the witness norm is moderate and the mark is essentially constant over the first continuation. The thresholds and the initial mark response are different quantities. At $p=809$ both claims have strict interval certificates on the larger-theta side.

## Mark response on all support pieces

Write $V=\operatorname{ran}P_\sigma$, $\mathcal F=\{x\ge0:x-j\in V\}$, and $e=e_0$. Use inverse penalty $u=1/(2t)$ and set

$$x(u)=\Pi_{\mathcal F}(ue),\qquad a(u)=\langle e,x(u)\rangle,\qquad L(u)=\|x(u)\|^2.$$

This is the same optimizer as for $x_0-t\|x\|^2$. Put $L_*=\|X\|^2$ and $\alpha=X_0$ for the least-norm endpoint $X$. When $u\ge u_*=1/(2t_*)$, the solution is $X$, by the [tangent test](parallel-endpoint-10.md#the-finite-test-on-the-actual-endpoint). The projection is continuous and piecewise affine, with finitely many pieces. In fact, for each support $A$, project $ue$ onto the affine equations $x-j\in V$, $x_{A^c}=0$. The answer is affine in $u$. Its feasibility and projection normal conditions hold on an interval, as they are linear positivity and polyhedral cone conditions.

On every open support piece, let $T_A=V\cap\{d:d_{A^c}=0\}$. Differentiating the affine projection gives the exact response

$$a'(u)=\|\Pi_{T_A}e\|^2=:\eta(u),\qquad L'(u)=2u\eta(u). \tag{1}$$

The second identity uses orthogonality of $ue-x(u)$ to $T_A$. In even coordinates, with $N_A=\ker(P_{-\sigma}E_A)$, the mark slope is

$$\eta(u)=\|e_N\|^2-\frac{\langle e_N,j_N\rangle^2}{\|j_N\|^2},\tag{2}$$

where $e_N,j_N$ project the restricted mark and constant onto $N_A$. The denominator is nonzero because the current optimizer belongs to $E_A N_A$ and has mean one. This follows by dropping the one direction $j_N$ from the support kernel. For an opposite endpoint $Y$ with $Y_0=1/\alpha$, let $b=\alpha Y-e$. This vector is nonnegative off the mark, is zero on the endpoint support $S$, and has $b_0=0$. Since $Y$ is orthogonal to $V$,

$$\eta(u)=\|\Pi_{T_A} b\|^2\le\alpha^2\sum_{i\in A\setminus S}Y_i^2.\tag{3}$$

Equations (1) integrate exactly across switches. In particular $\alpha-a(U)=\int_U^\infty\eta(u)\,du$ and $L_*-L(U)=2\int_U^\infty u\eta(u)\,du$. If the fixed-width reciprocal quadratic values converge to one, the larger-theta sign satisfies $\limsup_p a(U)\le1$ at any fixed $U>0$. Indeed, comparison to $j$ gives $L(U)\le1+2U(\alpha-1/\sqrt n)$, a bounded norm for fixed $U$ since the [constant witnesses and reciprocity](fourier.md) bound $\alpha$ uniformly. The primal trial $x(U)/a(U)$ for the [reciprocal path](parallel-quadratic-limit-9.md) then gives $a(U)^2 M_r\le1+r(L(U)-1)$ for any $r>0$. Take the field limit at fixed $r$, then let $r\downarrow0$. Thus uniform decay of the tail of the **mark** integral as $U\to\infty$ would suffice. Equations (2) and (3) give two ways to measure the integrand on the actual support. On each support piece, $\Pi_{T_A}x(u)=u\Pi_{T_A}e$, so $\eta(u)\le (L(u)-1)/u^2$. A uniform bound $L(u)-1\le C u^{1-\varepsilon}$ for $u\ge1$ and some $\varepsilon>0$ would therefore also suffice, even with growing endpoint norm.

## First change on a strictly complementary support

Use orthonormal even Fourier orbit coordinates, which suffice by inversion symmetry and uniqueness. Let $D_i\in V$ be the even direction whose outside coordinates on $O=S^c$ are the unit vector at $i$. These directions exist uniquely when restriction $V\to\mathbb R^O$ is invertible, an assumption tested for the examples below. The tangent cone is then generated by the $D_i$. Assume strict complementarity $Y_i>0$ there, and set

$$\ell_i=\alpha Y_i=-\langle e,D_i\rangle,\qquad E_i=-\langle X,D_i\rangle.$$

The complementary equality for the mark holds in the orthonormal orbit coordinates, where the coordinate of a two-point orbit includes its factor $\sqrt2$. Assume $L_*>1$, so that the reduction direction $j-X$ ensures some $E_i>0$. The first-order inequality at $X$ gives

$$t_* =\min_{i:E_i>0}\frac{\ell_i}{2E_i},\qquad u_* = \max_i \frac{E_i}{\ell_i}.\tag{4}$$

Suppose the extremum occurs at a unique index $i$. The first affine continuation for $s=u_*-u>0$ is

$$ x(u_* -s)=X+s w,qquad w=\frac{\ell_i}{\|D_i\|^2}D_i.$$

Indeed, the projection gradient along $D_i$ is zero on this continuation. Along every other $D_j$ the gradient is affine in $s$ and strictly negative initially. Old positive coordinates start strictly positive. These conditions are sufficient for the projection and identify the end of this piece as the first time an old active coordinate or another multiplier hits zero, or $u=0$. The mark loss on this piece is exactly $s\ell_i^2/\|D_i\|^2$. Even up to $s=u_*$ it is at most $\ell_i\sqrt{L_*}$, since $E_i\le\sqrt{L_*}\|D_i\|$ and $\|D_i\|\ge1$.

## A whole penalty path on one side at square order

The constant trial gives $t_*\le(\alpha-1/\sqrt n)/(2(L_*-1))$ when $L_*>1$. Equality holds exactly if $X=j+u_*P_\sigma e$. In fact, equality makes the projection gradient at $X$ have zero pairing with $j-X$. Then the strictly positive point $j$ also maximizes this gradient on $\mathcal F$. It follows that the gradient is perpendicular to $V$, which gives the claimed formula. Conversely, if $X=j+vP_\sigma e$ for some $v>0$, then $X$ is the unconstrained affine projection at $u=v$. It is already an endpoint. For $u<v$, the affine projection follows the feasible segment from $j$ to $X$. For $u\ge v$, the additional gradient $(u-v)e$ supports the same endpoint. This gives $u_*=v$ and equality in the bound.

At $q=h^2$ for any odd prime $h$, the [least-norm minus witness](square-endpoint-uniqueness.md) is $Y=j+((h-1)/\sqrt n)P_- e$. Its entire one-sided path is

$$x_-(u)=j+\min\{u,(h-1)/\sqrt n\}P_-e,\qquad t_{*,-}\sqrt n=(h+1)/4.$$

Its mark slope is $1/2$ up to $u=(h-1)/\sqrt n$ and vanishes thereafter. Here $\|P_- e\|^2=1/2$. This gives an exact bounded-support-in-$u$ control for (1) on this side. On the plus side the endpoint is flat on the subfield annihilator and $L_*=h-1$, so the constant trial gives $t_{*,+}\sqrt n\le1/2$. In fact the preceding fixed-width argument, with fixed-width convergence at square orders, requires a nonvanishing mark tail there because $\alpha_+\to\sqrt2$.

## Interval enclosures away from the first change

The mark $a(u)$ at any fixed $u$ can be enclosed without finding its exact support. Put $b=j+uP_\sigma e$, so $x(u)$ minimizes $F(x)=\|x-b\|^2/2$ on $\mathcal F$. For an arbitrary nonnegative multiplier $y$ use $z=b+P_\sigma y$, the affine minimizer of $F(x)-y^Tx$. Repair its positivity by taking $x_c=(z+\delta\mathbf1)/(1+\delta\sqrt n)$ with $\delta\ge\max_i(-z_i)_+$. This stays on the same affine mean-one sector. For the Lagrange dual lower value $g(y)$ the gap is exactly

$$2(F(x_c)-g(y))=\|x_c-z\|^2+2y^Tx_c.$$

Strong convexity of the projection objective gives $\|x_c-x(u)\|^2$ at most this gap. Both vectors belong to $j+V$, so the corresponding mark error is bounded by $\|P_\sigma e\|$ times its square root. The [replay](../one_sided_penalty.py) proposes multipliers with an accelerated projected iteration and recomputes the repair and gap using Arb. It does not require convergence of the iteration or an active-set decision.

The [42 certified intervals](../data/one-sided-penalty.csv) cover both signs at four primes and three prime squares for $u=4,16,64$. At $u=64$, the plus mark for $p=9413$ is enclosed in $[0.9988072,1.0014847]$, while for $q=97^2$ it lies in $[1.1100953,1.1105379]$. The square mark can still rise to $1.3997084\ldots$ at the endpoint. The finite intervals compare mark drift across many support pieces. The required uniform tail estimate through primes remains open.
