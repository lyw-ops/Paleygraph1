## Result: inconclusive; complementary support alone does not presently give the required tail bound or clipped-window parity

Fix \(M>0\). Let \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\) range over the actual prime family, \(n=(p-1)/2\), and let \(i=0\) be the physical marked coordinate. Write \(\mathbb E_n\) and \(\|\cdot\|_{2,n}\) for normalized mean and normalized \(\ell _2\)-norm.

For the actual zero-floor ordinary optimizer, denote the activation by \(b\), the ordinary row by
\[
Y=Kb,
\]
and the ordinary complementary factors by
\[
F_\pm=\frac{b\pm Y}{\nu},\qquad \nu=\mathbb E_n b.
\]
On the bulk \(i\neq0\),
\[
b_i=|Y_i|,\qquad F_+(i)F_-(i)=0,
\]
while the physical mark is retained separately. The bulk seed is
\[
S_{\rm Jac}=2\sqrt n\,Ke_0,
\]
with
\[
(S_{\rm Jac})_0=-\frac2{\sqrt n},
\qquad
\|S_{\rm Jac}\|_\infty=2\sqrt{p/n}=2\sqrt2+O(p^{-1}).
\]

The exact bulk pairing is
\[
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}b_i(S_{\rm Jac})_i]
   =4\eta+O_M(n^{-1}),
\]
where \(\eta=Y_0/b_0\). Thus a signed tail estimate would indeed control the ordinary ratio.

### The proposed support argument does not close

For a fixed window \(L\), define
\[
T_{p,L}
 =\mathbb E_n\!\left[1_{\{i\ne0\}}(b_i-L)_+(S_{\rm Jac})_i\right].
\]
The desired prime-uniform statement is
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{\substack{p\to\infty\\p\equiv1(4)}}
 |T_{p,L}|=0
}
\tag{A}
\]
for each fixed cap \(M\), with \(p\to\infty\) taken before \(L\to\infty\).

The complementary representation gives only
\[
b_i=\frac{\nu}{2}\bigl(F_+(i)+F_-(i)\bigr)
\qquad(i\ne0),
\]
and hence
\[
|T_{p,L}|
\le
\|S_{\rm Jac}\|_\infty\,
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}(b_i-L)_+].
\]
Since \(\mathbb E_n b=\nu\le2\),
\[
\mathbb E_n[(b-L)_+]\le \mathbb E_n b\le2,
\]
which gives only an \(O(1)\) bound, not decay in \(L\). The disjointness
\(F_+F_-=0\) does not improve this: it identifies which sector carries a large value of \(b\), but gives no estimate for the correlation of that adaptive sector with the seed \(S_{\rm Jac}\).

A second-moment estimate would yield
\[
\mathbb E_n[(b-L)_+]\le L^{-1}\mathbb E_n b^2,
\]
but a uniform bound on \(\mathbb E_n b^2\) is precisely the unavailable optimizer-\(L^2\) input. The identities
\[
Y=Kb,\qquad \|Y\|_{2,n}^2=\|b\|_{2,n}^2-\nu^2
\]
do not bound \(\|b\|_{2,n}\). The pointwise bound \(b_i\le2\sqrt p\) is also insufficient uniformly in \(p\).

This is a genuine adaptive-support obstruction: a nonnegative row can have bounded mean while placing \(O(1)\) mass on a set of size \(O(L^{-1})\), and the seed has constant pointwise size on such a set. Fixed-complexity parity estimates do not apply to the moving support
\[
\{i:b_i>L\}.
\]

The bounded auxiliary profile
\[
b'_i=1+\frac14(S_{\rm Jac})_i
\]
on the bulk, with \(b'_0=0\), makes the same obstruction concrete:
\[
0\le b'_i\le2
\]
for large \(p\), but
\[
\mathbb E_n[b'S_{\rm Jac}]
 =1-\frac1n+\frac{2}{n^{3/2}}-\frac1{n^2}.
\]
Thus boundedness, positivity, and a seed-compatible support profile do not imply parity cancellation. This is not an optimizer counterexample, but it refutes the proposed sufficient principle based only on complementary support and boundedness.

### What bounded-window parity would require

For fixed \(M,L\), a sufficient arithmetic input would be a fixed-complexity negative-field approximation \(q_{p,L}\) satisfying, for every \(\rho>0\),
\[
\limsup_{\substack{p\to\infty\\p\equiv1(4)}}
\mathbb E_n\!\left[
1_{\{i\ne0\}}\,
|b_i^{[L]}-q_{p,L,i}|
\right]\le\rho,
\qquad
b_i^{[L]}=\min(b_i,L),
\tag{B}
\]
with the polynomial complexity and coefficients chosen before the prime limit. The fixed-expression trace estimate would then give
\[
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}q_{p,L,i}S_{{\rm Jac},i}]
   =O_{L,\rho}(p^{-1/2})+O_M(n^{-1}),
\]
while (A) would control the replacement of \(b\) by \(b^{[L]}\). The resulting estimate is
\[
|4\eta-T_{p,L}|
 \le 2\sqrt{p/n}\,\rho
      +O_{L,\rho}(p^{-1/2})
      +O_M(n^{-1}),
\]
with the order
\[
\text{choose }M,L,\rho\text{ and the polynomial first;}
\quad p\to\infty;
\quad \rho\downarrow0,\ L\to\infty.
\]
Neither (A) nor (B) follows from the complementary relation \(F_+F_-=0\).

## Remaining gap

The unresolved support-sensitive statement is one of the following genuinely stronger estimates:
\[
\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{p\to\infty}
\left|
\mathbb E_n[1_{\{i\ne0\}}(b_i-L)_+S_{{\rm Jac},i}]
\right|=0,
\]
or a sector-weighted version such as
\[
\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{p\to\infty}
\sum_{\epsilon=\pm}
\left|
\mathbb E_n\!\left[
1_{\{i\ne0\}}F_\epsilon(i)
(\epsilon Y_i-L)_+
\right]\right|=0.
\]
The latter uses the actual ordinary complementary pair, but still requires cancellation against an adaptive high-value support. No proof is available from the fixed-depth parity estimates.

## Ranked next tests

1. **Direct sector-weighted tail test.**  
   Prove or disprove, for fixed \(M\),
   \[
   \lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{p\to\infty}
   \mathbb E_n\!\left[
   1_{\{i\ne0\}}
   \bigl(F_+(i)(Y_i-L)_+
        +F_-(i)(-Y_i-L)_+\bigr)
   \right]=0.
   \]
   This is the most direct support-sensitive estimate and is aligned with the actual positive complementary pair. A finite-prime verification would only be evidence; the required prime-first asymptotic remains open.

2. **Clipped-window polynomial completion.**  
   For fixed \(M,L\), construct a bounded negative-field expression \(q_{p,L}\) satisfying (B), with complexity fixed before \(p\to\infty\). The key test is whether the actual optimizer’s clipped row, not merely fixed-depth iterates, admits such a bulk \(L^1\) approximation. Success would combine with (A) to yield true clipped-window parity; failure would identify the missing non-compactness of the ordinary optimizer.
