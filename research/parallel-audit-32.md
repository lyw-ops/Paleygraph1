## 1. A nonzero real trace modulo four

Let \(G=\mathbb F_q^\times\), \(\ell(a)=\chi(1-a)\), and
\(B=\ell*\ell*\ell\) with counting convolution. Write

$$h_q=B(1)=\sum_{abc=1}\ell(a)\ell(b)\ell(c).$$

Terms with a coordinate equal to one vanish, so restrict to triples
with \(a,b,c\ne1\). The Klein group generated by

$$I(a,b,c)=(a^{-1},b^{-1},c^{-1}),\qquad
T(a,b,c)=(b,a,c)$$

preserves their signs: inversion multiplies the product by
\(\chi(abc)=1\), since \(\chi(-1)=1\). The involution \(I\)
has no fixed triple in this domain. All coordinates would have to
be \(-1\), whose product is \(-1\). The involution \(IT\)
also has no fixed triple: it would give \(ab=1\) and hence
\(c=1\).

Thus every orbit has size four except those fixed by \(T\).
These have

$$a=b\ne\pm1,\qquad c=a^{-2}.$$

There are \(q-3\) such triples, two in each orbit, so there are
\((q-3)/2\) size-two orbits. Each contributes \(\pm2\), which
is two modulo four. Every size-four orbit contributes zero modulo
four. Therefore

$$\boxed{\quad h_q\equiv q-3\equiv2\pmod4,\qquad h_q\ne0.
\quad} \tag{1}$$

This conclusion uses no estimate or special value of a high-order
character sum. For comparison, when \(\chi(-1)=-1\), inversion
alone gives \(h_q=0\); that case is not used in the sign selection
below.

## 2. The rank-two stalk and its determinant

Let \(\mathcal H\) be the ordinary rank-three sheaf whose trace
is \(B\), before the \((3/2)[1]\) normalization. The accepted
hypergeometric description gives pure weight two and inertia at
one equal to \(1\oplus1\oplus\chi\). In particular the
middle-extension stalk

$$E_1=\mathcal H_1^{I_1}$$

has dimension two. Its trace is \(h_q\), and its two Frobenius
eigenvalues have absolute value \(q\). The latter is a purity
statement: inertia at this point is finite semisimple, with no
unipotent block of length greater than one, hence no local weight
loss on its invariant part.

The raw convolution value really is this middle-extension trace,
including at one. Here is an exact hypergeometric normalization
check that excludes a hidden lower-weight correction. Choose a
nontrivial additive character \(\psi\), write
\(g_\chi=\sum_{y\ne0}\chi(y)\psi(y)\), and set
\(\varepsilon=\chi(-1)\). The raw type-\((1,1)\) sum is

$$\operatorname{Hyp}_1(a)
=\sum_{x=ay}\psi(x-y)\chi(y)
=\varepsilon g_\chi\ell(a).$$

This holds also at one, where both sides vanish. The raw
type-\((3,3)\) sum is its third multiplicative convolution, so

$$\operatorname{Hyp}_3(a)=(\varepsilon g_\chi)^3 B(a).$$

The ordinary rank-three hypergeometric trace is
\(-\operatorname{Hyp}_3=\lambda^3B\), with
\(\lambda=-\varepsilon g_\chi\). The balanced trace formula
in [Katz, G2 and hypergeometric sheaves, Section 2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/g2hyper62finalcorr.pdf)
explicitly includes the middle-extension stalk at one. The
rank-one comparison fixes its compatible unramified scalar;
taking its third power fixes the scalar above. Since
\(|\lambda|=\sqrt q\), removing \(\lambda^3\) turns ordinary
weight five into weight two. Thus \(B(1)\), without any additive
correction, is the required pure rank-two stalk trace. This also
keeps the arithmetic normalization rather than calling the Gauss
constant harmless and discarding it.

Let the two eigenvalues be \(\alpha,\beta\). Their sum is the
nonzero real integer \(h_q\). Because
\(|\alpha|=|\beta|=q\), a nonzero real sum forces
\(\beta=\overline\alpha\): the alternative for two unit
complex numbers with real sum is that they are opposites, whose
sum is zero. Consequently (1) gives

$$\boxed{\quad\det(F_q|E_1)=\alpha\beta=q^2.\quad} \tag{2}$$

Reality of a total cohomological trace has not been used to isolate
an arbitrary top-weight term. Here the whole specified stalk is
two-dimensional and pure of one weight, with nonzero trace.

## 3. Arithmetic boundary comparison giving the phase exactly

Write \(r(a)=\chi(1-a)B(1-a)\), \(r(1)=0\), as in round 31,
and let \(F\) be its ordinary rank-three sheaf. It is normalized
as \(\mathcal R=F(3/2)[1]\), with perverse trace
\(-q^{-3/2}r\). Reflection and the Kummer twist show that
\(F_0^{I_0}=E_1\) arithmetically: the twist at zero has value
\(\chi(1)=1\). Let \(\lambda_\infty\) be the eigenvalue on
the one-dimensional \(F_\infty^{I_\infty}\).

The compact cohomology of \(F\) on \(\mathbb G_m\) is
concentrated in degree one and has dimension three. The two boundary
invariant dimensions already sum to three; the proper middle
cohomology is zero. The boundary exact sequence is therefore a
Frobenius-equivariant isomorphism

$$H_c^1(\mathbb G_m,F)\cong E_1\oplus F_\infty^{I_\infty}.$$

On the other hand, changing variables \(h=1-a\) gives exactly

$$\sum_{a\in G}r(a)
=\sum_{h\in G}\chi(h)B(h)-B(1)
=\left(\sum_{h\in G}\chi(h)\ell(h)\right)^3-h_q
=-1-h_q.$$

The last Jacobi sum is \(J(\chi,\chi)=-1\), because
\(\chi(-1)=1\). Lefschetz and the boundary isomorphism now give

$$h_q+\lambda_\infty=-\sum r=1+h_q,
\qquad\lambda_\infty=1. \tag{3}$$

This is the unramified constant that a purely geometric local table
would not identify.

Evaluate the determinant relation
\(\det\mathcal R=c_q^{\deg}\otimes\mathcal M^\vee\)
in Deligne's tensor-compatible fiber at the trivial character.
The relevant exact sequences are those used in
[Katz, Chapters 3 and 16](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin331.pdf),
printed pages 24--25 and 77--78. Since the proper middle cohomology
vanishes, this fiber consists of the two normalized eigenvalues
on \(E_1\) and the eigenvalue on
\(H^1(I_\infty,F(3/2))\).

For the size-three unipotent block with invariant eigenvalue one,
the ordinary Frobenius eigenvalues along its monodromy chain are
\(1,q,q^2\). Its inertia \(H^1\) is the top coinvariant with
the additional \((-1)\) twist, hence eigenvalue \(q^3\).
After normalization it is \(q^{3/2}\). Therefore

$$\det(F_q|\omega(\mathcal R))
=\frac{\det(F_q|E_1)}{q^3}\,q^{3/2}
=\frac{\det(F_q|E_1)}{q^{3/2}}.$$

For \(\mathcal M\), the trivial-character Deligne fiber is
its zero-boundary invariant, with eigenvalue \(q^{-1/2}\).
Tensor duality gives the eigenvalue \(q^{1/2}\) for
\(\mathcal M^\vee\). It follows that

$$\boxed{\quad c_q=\frac{\det(F_q|E_1)}{q^2}=1.\quad} \tag{4}$$

The comparison uses the nonunitary bad-character Deligne fiber,
not the raw compact Mellin value at that character. Substituting
the latter would be a normalization error. The argument also
does not assume that a geometric determinant identification is
automatically arithmetic: (3) and (2) supply its missing constants.
