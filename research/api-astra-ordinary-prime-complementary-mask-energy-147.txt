## Result

Let \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\) run through the ordinary primes, \(n=(p-1)/2\), and let \(T=T(p)\to\infty\) with \(T+1\) in the simultaneous marked window at fixed \(\tau>0\). Retain the actual zero-start trajectory, physical terminal activation
\[
G_i=\sqrt n\,g_T(i)\ge 0,
\]
the true ordinary optimizers \(F\) and \(R\) on opposite signs, and
\[
S=\{i\ne0:G_i\ge cT,\ R_i\le \delta G_i/T\},
\]
with fixed \(c>0\), \(\delta\to0\), \(\epsilon=o(\delta)\), and \(L=o(T)\).

For
\[
X_j^\circ=1_{\{j\ne0\}}(F_j-L)_+,\qquad
X_\mu^\circ=\sum_h\mu_h X^\circ_{i+h},
\]
the exact masked shifted energy is
\[
\mathcal E_S(\mu)
:=\mathbb E[1_S(X_\mu^\circ)^2]
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in S}
   \left(\sum_h\mu_hX^\circ_{i+h}\right)^2.
\]

### Lemma: a quantified bounded-difference class

Let \(A=\operatorname{supp}\mu\), \(|A|=a\), and first take \(\mu\) uniform on \(A\). Define
\[
K_S(A):=
\max_{\substack{y\ne z\\X_y^\circ X_z^\circ>0}}
\bigl|S\cap (y-A)\cap(z-A)\bigr|.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal E_S(\mu)
\le \frac{q_s}{a}
+\frac{K_S(A)\,n}{a^2},
\quad q_s=F_0.
\quad} \tag{A}
\]

Indeed, expanding the square gives
\[
\mathcal E_S(\mu)
=\frac1{na^2}\sum_{y,z}X_y^\circ X_z^\circ
 |S\cap(y-A)\cap(z-A)|.
\]
For \(y=z\), the intersection has size at most \(a\), while
\[
\sum_y (X_y^\circ)^2\le q_s\sum_yX_y^\circ\le q_s n,
\]
because \(0\le X^\circ\le F\le q_s\) and \(\mathbb E F=1\). Thus the diagonal contributes at most \(q_s/a\). For \(y\ne z\), use the definition of \(K_S(A)\) and
\[
\sum_yX_y^\circ\le n
\]
to obtain the second term in (A).

The same argument for a nonuniform probability \(\mu\) gives
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal E_S(\mu)
\le q_s\|\mu\|_{\ell^2}^2
+\frac{K_S(\mu)\,n}{1},
\quad}
\]
where
\[
K_S(\mu):=
\max_{y\ne z:X_y^\circ X_z^\circ>0}
\sum_{h}\mu_h\mu_{h+y-z}\,
1_S(y-h)\,1_S(z-h).
\]
The uniform form (A) is more useful for a concrete class.

Since the ordinary endpoint cap gives \(q_s\ll\sqrt n\), (A) yields
\[
\mathcal E_S(\mu)=o(T)
\]
whenever
\[
a\gg \frac{\sqrt n}{T}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
K_S(A)\ll \frac{a^2T}{n}
\]
with both ratios tending to the required limits. In particular, if \(a\asymp\sqrt n\) and \(K_S(A)=o(T)\), then the masked energy is \(o(T)\).

This is a genuine nontrivial lemma for a precisely quantified class: shift sets whose score-dependent mask intersections have bounded multiplicity.

### Prime-sensitive version for algebraically majorized masks

There is also a prime-sensitive estimate, but only under an additional algebraic containment hypothesis. Suppose the actual mask satisfies
\[
S\subseteq B_{\mathbf a,\boldsymbol\sigma}
:=\bigcap_{\ell=1}^k
\{i:\chi(1-a_\ell t_i)=\sigma_\ell\},
\]
where the \(a_\ell\in Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2\) are distinct, the signs \(\sigma_\ell\in\{\pm1\}\) are fixed, and the frequency coordinate \(i\) corresponds to \(t_i\in Q\). Then the Weil estimate used in the filtered-support argument gives, uniformly for \(y\ne z\),
\[
\bigl|B_{\mathbf a,\boldsymbol\sigma}
 \cap(y-A)\cap(z-A)\bigr|
\le 2^{-k}n+O(k\sqrt p).
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal E_S(\mu)
\le \frac{q_s}{a}
+\left(2^{-k}n+O(k\sqrt p)\right)\frac{n}{a^2}.
\quad} \tag{B}
\]

For \(k\le \frac14\log_2p\), the error is lower order relative to the main mask size. Thus (B) proves \(o(T)\) for, for example,
\[
a\asymp\sqrt n,\qquad
2^{-k}n=o(T),\qquad
k\sqrt p=o(T),
\]
which is a nonempty quantified algebraic class.

The prime input is explicit: \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), \(n=(p-1)/2\), and the error is \(O(k\sqrt p)\), coming from multiplicative Weil bounds. No assertion is made for square orders \(p=r^2\), since that is not the requested prime family.

## Relation to the actual complementary mask

The complementary-overlap identity remains valid:
\[
\mathbb E[1_{\rm bulk}\bar F R]
=1-\frac{\bar F_0q_{-s}}n
\le C\epsilon.
\]
Together with the definition of \(S\), this gives the already established reduction
\[
\frac{\mathbb E[1_{\rm bulk}\bar F G]}T
\le c+\frac{C\epsilon}{\delta}
+\frac{C_\tau}{\sqrt T}
\left(\sqrt L+\|1_SX_\mu^\circ\|_{2,n}
+\|\mu\|_{\ell^2}\right).
\]
Therefore, if the actual mask satisfied either hypothesis of (A) or the algebraic majorization hypothesis of (B), then the order-\(T\) lower bound forced by the near-cap terminal comparison would be contradicted.

For a diffuse near-cap law with
\[
\|\mu\|_{\ell^2}^2=o(T/\sqrt n),
\]
the equal-shift term is already \(o(T)\). The remaining distinct-shift term is controlled by (A) whenever the actual score-dependent intersections obey
\[
K_S(A)=o(a^2T/n).
\]

## Precise failure of the unconditional prime route

The missing statement is not a prime-counting estimate for ordinary primes alone. The actual set
\[
S=\{G\ge cT,\ R\le\delta G/T\}
\]
depends simultaneously on:

1. the physical marked orbit at time \(T\);
2. the chosen true optimizer \(R\);
3. the pointwise score \(G\);
4. the chosen optimizer \(F\) through the shifted energy.

None of the supplied ordinary-prime character-sum estimates shows that this set is contained in a bounded-complexity quadratic-character mask, nor that its intersections with translated cap supports satisfy \(K_S(A)=o(a^2T/n)\). Unmasked difference counts for \(A\) do not imply this score-dependent intersection bound.

Indeed, at the purely combinatorial level, a set \(S\) of density \(O(1/T)\) can be arranged to contain many simultaneous intersections
\[
S\cap(y-A)\cap(z-A)
\]
for a structured \(A\), even when \(A\) has acceptable unmasked difference counts. Thus the prime estimate cannot be applied until an algebraic or dynamical description of the actual score mask is proved.

This leaves the central asymptotic target open:

\[
\boxed{\text{No unconditional prime-uniform }o(T)
\text{ bound for the actual }\mathcal E_S(\mu)\text{ is proved.}}
\]

The result does establish a checked \(o(T)\) bound for the quantified bounded-intersection class (A), and a genuinely prime-sensitive \(o(T)\) bound for the algebraically majorized class (B). It does not prove that the actual complementary terminal mask belongs to either class.

## Ranked next tests

1. **Highest priority: prove score-mask pseudorandomness.**  
   For the actual \(S\), test whether one can prove
   \[
   K_S(A)\ll 2^{-k}n+O(k\sqrt p)
   \]
   for cap-support classes \(A\) arising from true optimizers, perhaps after conditioning on \(G\ge cT\). This would connect the physical marking to the ordinary-prime Weil estimate without assuming that \(S\) itself is algebraic.

2. **Second priority: classify the exact-cap obstruction.**  
   Analyze true optimizers whose cap set \(A\) is periodic or nearly periodic. For such \(A\), compute the actual weighted intersections
   \[
   \sum_{y\ne z}X_y^\circ X_z^\circ
   |S\cap(y-A)\cap(z-A)|
   \]
   rather than replacing them by unmasked difference counts. Either a uniform \(o(T)\) bound here or an explicit family with \(K_S(A)\asymp a^2T/n\) would decide whether periodic cap structure is the remaining obstruction.
