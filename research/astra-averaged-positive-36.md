# Exact positive averaged witnesses and the endpoint horizon

Round 36. Ordinary theta for ETH Problem 26 remains open. The two requested
prime witnesses at depth 128 now have exact Fourier inputs and strict Arb
positivity certificates. Their minima need only exceed zero; the reserve
parameter `kappa=1/1000` is not an output-floor requirement here.

The uniform result below is an explicit **growing-depth** construction:
the actual, unrounded projected average is positive at all sufficiently
large depths, with a proved horizon of order `n^(3/2)/tau`. It preserves
the original mean, sector, and mark without any repair. This statement
also holds on square orders. **Marked cancellation at that horizon is
not proved**, and the fixed-depth arithmetic theorem is not used there.
It does not settle the requested prime-uniform fixed-depth completion.

There is also a precise limitation of decreasing averaging weights:
uniform weights minimize both the available mean-budget penalty and the
absolute endpoint estimate under the uniform orbit cap used here. Merely
tapering the final weights moves the endpoint cost into the interior.
This is a theorem about that estimate, not an impossibility theorem for
weighted prime constructions or for signed cancellation.

## 1. Strict finite certificates

Use the conventions of [round 35](averaged-mark-35.md), and the pinned
projection of [round 34](astra-conic-primal-34.md):

\[
 P_\sigma=(I-J+\sigma K)/2,\quad
 \ell_\sigma=P_\sigma e_0,\quad
 a_\sigma=(n-1-\sigma)/(2n),\quad
 L_\sigma=P_\sigma-\ell_\sigma\ell_\sigma^T/a_\sigma.
\]

For an exact even input `z in 1+V_sigma`, define

\[
 d_0=0,\qquad d_i=(2\kappa-z_i)_+\ (i\ne0),\qquad
 \widehat z=z+2L_\sigma d.                                      \tag{1}
\]

Each saved input consists of integers divided by `2^90`, with DC exactly
one, exact evenness, and exact zero coefficients on the forbidden
Fourier support. The actual signs are generated by modular arithmetic,
including a primality check and primitive-generator check. Floating
zero-Picard averaging over times 1 through T proposes these coefficients;
**no exact-orbit-prefix assertion is made**.

Replay starts from the saved integers. Arb decides every clipping branch
strictly, applies (1), and checks every output coordinate. It also checks
the mean, marked value, evenness, reflection energy, and an independent
DFT of the output. Exact mean/sector/mark follow algebraically from
`L_sigma 1=L_sigma e_0=0` and its range, rather than from interval
containment alone. The output need not have rational coefficients.

## 2. Weighted endpoint identity, with the full budget

These statements concern the **actual** zero-Picard orbit, not rounded
proposals. Let 0<tau<n, c=tau/n, w0=1, wi=c off the mark, and W=sum w.
Set

\[
 v_0=0,\quad v_{t+1}=K\phi_t,\quad
 \phi_t=\sqrt{v_t^2+4w},\quad \mu_t=\mathbb E\phi_t/2,
 \quad A_{\sigma,t}=(\phi_t+\sigma v_t)/2.
\]

Take decreasing nonnegative weights alpha1,...,alphaT with sum one,
and put

\[
 M_\alpha=\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t\mu_t,\qquad
 X_{\sigma,\alpha}=M_\alpha1+\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_tP_\sigma A_{\sigma,t}.
\]

The exact decomposition is

\[
 X_{\sigma,\alpha}=
 \underbrace{\tfrac12\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t(\mu_t-\mu_{t-1})1}_{C_\alpha1}
 +\tfrac12\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t(A_{\sigma,t}+A_{\sigma,t-1})
 +\tfrac\sigma4\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t(v_{t+1}-v_{t-1}).          \tag{3}
\]

It follows by expanding the projection and using
`Kv_t=phi_(t-1)-2mu_(t-1)1`. Since phi_t>=phi_0 pointwise, mu_t>=mu_0;
summation by parts gives

\[
 2C_\alpha=\alpha_T(\mu_T-\mu_0)
 +\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}(\alpha_t-\alpha_{t+1})(\mu_t-\mu_0)\ge0.    \tag{4}
\]

Write E_t=||v_t||_2^2. The exact energy telescope and Jensen yield

\[
 nM_\alpha^2\le W+\tfrac14\left[
 \alpha_1 E_1+\sum_{t=2}^T(\alpha_t-\alpha_{t-1})E_t
 -\alpha_T E_{T+1}\right]
 \le W+\alpha_1(1-\sqrt c)^2(1-1/n).                         \tag{5}
\]

Means and sectors are exact. For each fixed weight vector and horizon,
the inherited marked theorem, through time T+1, gives
`X_(+,alpha)(0),X_(-,alpha)(0) -> 1`. No claim is made for a
field-dependent horizon without its own marked hypothesis.

For T>=2 the terminal vector in (3) is exactly

\[
 -\alpha_2v_1+
 \sum_{j=2}^{T-1}(\alpha_{j-1}-\alpha_{j+1})v_j
 +\alpha_{T-1}v_T+\alpha_Tv_{T+1}.                          \tag{6}
\]

The interior sum is empty at T=2. For decreasing weights all coefficients
except that of v1 are nonnegative, and their sum is alpha1+alpha2.
Consequently, if `||v_j||_infty <= D` for j>=2 and `||v_1||_infty <= b`,

\[
 \left\|\tfrac14\sum\alpha_t(v_{t+1}-v_{t-1})\right\|_\infty
 \le\tfrac14[D\alpha_1+(D+b)\alpha_2].                     \tag{7}
\]

For uniform weights this is `(2D+b)/(4T)`. Triangular decreasing
weights make the final coefficients small but introduce the interior
positive coefficients in (6); their bound is approximately twice as
large. This cost must not be discarded as a boundary cancellation.

**Optimality within this estimate.** If T>=3 and 0<=b<=D, uniform
weights minimize (7) over all decreasing probability weights. Indeed
the decreasing-weight simplex is the convex hull of uniform prefixes
of lengths k=1,...,T: the coefficients in this convex combination are
`k(alpha_k-alpha_(k+1))`. For k=1 the true terminal is v2, with bound D.
For k>=2 its bound before division by four is `(2D+b)/k`. The minimum
is `(2D+b)/T`, since `2D+b <= 3D`. Uniform weights also minimize alpha1
in (5), because alpha1>=1/T. This does not optimize the actual signed
terminal values or prove that tapering can never help a particular prime.
The constants in (8) satisfy b<=D for every n>=2, since
`b/R <= Q^(1/4)/n <= 1`.

## 3. An unconditional actual-orbit positivity horizon

Here is a uniform construction on actual Paley spaces, with an explicitly
large horizon. It is useful for separating the positivity question from
the unproved marked-arithmetic window; it is not a prime-specific
cancellation theorem.

Let Q=2n+1 be the field order and use the actual bound
`|K_ij| <= sqrt(Q)/n`. Define

\[
 R=2\sqrt W\,Q^{1/4},\qquad
 A=2\sqrt{QW/n},\qquad D=R+A,\qquad
 b=2(1-\sqrt c)\sqrt Q/n,
\]
\[
 a_D=\frac{2c}{\sqrt{D^2+4c}+D},\qquad
 H_{Q,\tau}=\frac{2D+b}{4a_D}.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\]

**Theorem.** At every integer T>H_(Q,tau), both actual unrounded rows
`X_(sigma,T)` are strictly positive, with

\[
 \min_i X_{\sigma,T}(i)\ge a_D-\frac{2D+b}{4T}>0.             \tag{9}
\]

Thus `z_(sigma,T)=X_(sigma,T)/M_T` itself is a completion, retaining
exactly its mean one, sector, evenness, and mark. Its original sharp
budget `n M_T^2 <= B_(n,T)` is unchanged. There is no clipping, additional
mean, modified marked target, or stationary solver in the construction.
At fixed tau,

\[
 H_{Q,\tau}\sim \frac{2(1+\tau)n\sqrt Q}{\tau}.
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]

**Proof, including the stationary bound used only for analysis.** A
strictly positive stationary pair exists by the following finite
variational argument. Maximize `sum_i w_i log p_i` over the compact
polytope `p>=0`, `p in 1+V_+`. It contains the positive constant vector,
and every boundary point has objective tending to minus infinity, so
the maximizer is strictly positive. The first variation gives
`P_+(w/p)=0`. For B=w/p, therefore B belongs to `R1+V_-` and
`mean B=W/n`, by pairing with p. Scale p and B reciprocally to get
positive A*,B* of common mean `sqrt(W/n)` and product w. Then
`v*=A*-B*` satisfies `v*=K(A*+B*)=K Phi(v*)`.

It follows directly that

\[
 \|v^*\|_1\le2\sqrt{nW},\qquad
 \|v^*\|_\infty\le2\sqrt{QW/n}=A,\qquad
 \|v^*\|_2^2\le4W\sqrt Q=R^2.                             \tag{11}
\]

The map `F(v)=K Phi(v)` is nonexpansive in counting l2: each scalar
activation is 1-Lipschitz and `||K||_(2->2)<=1`. Starting at zero gives
`||v_t-v*||_2 <= ||v*||_2 <= R` at **every** time. Hence
`|v_t(i)| <= A+R=D` for every coordinate and time. Also the explicit
first step gives `||v_1||_infty <= b`.

For every real u with |u|<=D and every w_i>=c,

\[
 \frac{\sqrt{u^2+4w_i}+\sigma u}{2}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{D^2+4c}-D}{2}=a_D.
\]

The positive middle term of the uniform version of (3) is therefore
at least a_D. Its scalar term is nonnegative by (4), and its terminal
absolute value is at most `(2D+b)/(4T)`. This proves (9). Since
`D~2sqrt(1+tau)Q^(1/4)`, rationalizing a_D proves (10). No marked
arithmetic or unproved stationary uniform l2 bound was used. QED.

The Arb horizon records evaluate (8), round the threshold upward to an
integer, and verify the positive lower bound (9). They do not execute
these enormous trajectories. The known stationary cap and
nonexpansiveness are not claimed as new; the displayed endpoint horizon
and the decreasing-weight comparison are the results extracted here.

**Scope of a possible finish.** One could now take any T_n exceeding
this explicit horizon. To use round 35's direct theta squeeze one still
needs

\[
 \frac1{T_n}\sum_{t=0}^{T_n+1}|v_t(0)|^2\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{through primes}.                               \tag{12}
\]

Nothing in (8)--(11) proves (12). These horizons are far beyond a
fixed-depth theorem. They work equally on square orders, where the
known theta obstruction forces (12) to fail for tau<1. Consequently
this construction cannot be advertised as an arithmetic-window
extension, a prime/square distinction, or a solution of ETH26.

## 4. Other routes examined and their exact stopping points

1. **Signed deficit from the endpoint.** Formula (3) isolates the only
   potentially negative contribution. It bounds the support and size
   of a deficit by a terminal exceedance over a positive raw average.
   However clipping this comparison does not commute with K; a bound
   on the terminal alone gives no bound on `K(2kappa-z)_+` with the
   required sign. Replacing it with the round-35 quadratic-profile law
   would be an unjustified change of the actual profile. No such
   replacement, rank-three closure, or higher-field law is used.
2. **Absolute coherence for the actual clipped vector.** It incurs
   `Gamma ||d||_1`, with Gamma of order n^(-1/2). If the normalized
   deficit mass has a positive field-first limit, this grows like
   sqrt(n). The exact positive finite witnesses do not remove that
   loss; their signed DFT checks are essential.
3. **Decreasing endpoint taper.** Equations (5)--(7) show why reducing
   just the final coefficient does not improve the currently proved
   uniform bound. No extra primes were scanned to support this fact.
4. **Including time zero.** Uniform averaging over times 0,...,T
   eliminates the energy penalty: `n M^2 <= W`. It also changes the
   fixed-depth marked limit to `1-1/(2(T+1))`, since the time-zero
   projected mark tends to 1/2, not one. The resulting asymptotic
   squared bracket constant is
   `(1+tau)/(1-1/(2(T+1)))^2`, rather than `1+tau+1/T` for times 1,...,T.
   At tau=.05,T=128 the latter is smaller. The zero-time choice wins
   this comparison only if `tau < (T+1)/(T(4T+3))`. Positivity remains
   separate; omitting the half-mark loss would be incorrect.
5. **Taking the proved large horizon.** This genuinely proves positive
   completion, but leaves (12) unproved. It does not prove eventual
   failure of the T=128 rule either. The main-line large-prime stress
   is a separate finite test, not a replacement for this missing limit.
