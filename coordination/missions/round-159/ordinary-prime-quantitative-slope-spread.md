## Question
Can the genuine near-unit slope exclusion be quantified and extended to a nontrivial portion of the actual marked phase-arc moment?

## Why this call is worth making
A large near-unit set forces one bounded-diameter subgroup and a forbidden finite-coset concentration. A quantitative interval of amplitudes or explicit smoothing rate can test whether this mechanism reaches a useful weighted slice.

## Current proof obligation
Connect the prime-only slope exclusion or convolution smoothing to moderate actual amplitudes or a real coupled upper on the marked arc. Pointwise trace geometry does not control the true weight law.

## Scope
Retain the actual short block, cap, quantitative pin, mass, marked phase sign and original reflected rows. Ordinary-prime incidence thresholds and varying subgroup size must be explicit.

## How the result changes the next decision
- If supported: Prove a genuine prime-only upper for a nontrivial weighted band of the actual moment, or derive a quantitative spread that contributes at the necessary scale.
- If refuted: Identify a sharp obstruction to lowering the amplitude threshold by a stated argument.
- If inconclusive: Obtain a uniform explicit modulus of the near-one slope count and show exactly which slice or convolution law it controls.
