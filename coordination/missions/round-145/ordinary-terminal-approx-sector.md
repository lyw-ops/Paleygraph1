## Question
Can prime-specific Fourier smoothing construct nonnegative near-root rows with small full forbidden-sector norm and small actual activation?

## Why this call is worth making
The near-root mark no longer requires exact optimal-face membership. A uniform forbidden-sector RMS at the T^(-1/2) scale suffices with the actual first moment.

## Current proof obligation
Construct a genuinely prime-uniform sequence of approximate rows with both low terminal activation and small positive signed residual. Try real clipped and approximate sector constructions separately.

## Scope
Work at fixed tau in the same real growing marked window. Keep mean one, positivity, q=q_s+o(sqrt n), weighted terminal cost o(T) and forbidden RMS o(T^(-1/2)) on each sign. Use a strategy distinct from clipping an exact optimizer and control the near-root loss. Take the floor down last.

## How the result changes the next decision
- If supported: Construct such a row on all eligible primes at selectable times using specific prime arithmetic.
- If refuted: Give a genuine obstruction to a named smoothing or Fourier interpolation mechanism at the stated precision.
- If inconclusive: Prove a prime-sensitive sufficient condition or exact smoothing tradeoff at the actual near-root normalizations.
