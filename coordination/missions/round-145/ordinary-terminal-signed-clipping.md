## Question
Can clipping a true optimum at a growing but sub-square-root terminal scale preserve the terminal signed criterion?

## Why this call is worth making
Clipping can give a deterministic sublinear activation bound. The new exact residual can be paired directly with the true cutoff tail without an entire forbidden RMS estimate.

## Current proof obligation
Construct a genuinely prime-uniform sequence of approximate rows with both low terminal activation and small positive signed residual. Try real clipped and approximate sector constructions separately.

## Scope
Use ordinary primes p=1 mod 4, fixed positive tau, exact true sign-s optimizer, original mark and growing selectable T with T+1 inside the simultaneous window. On the bulk clip F at L=o(sqrt T), keep F0, and restore mean on the bulk. Then take tau down last.

## How the result changes the next decision
- If supported: Prove a prime-uniform signed bound on the actual cutoff tail against the exact score in the residual, or an alternative clipped construction satisfying the same law.
- If refuted: Prove a scope-specific obstruction or forced signed tail consumption for the actual prime optimizer; retain the square diagnostic as control.
- If inconclusive: Derive an exact mass restoration and signed tail comparison, and identify a genuinely arithmetic remaining estimate.
